# Bittek Sarn: The Little Man

## The One Ronas Was Sitting On

Ronas Dermir spent twenty years telling anyone who would listen that the Warrens produced psychotic filth and
that his boot on its neck was the only thing standing between that filth and everybody else. It was true. Sarn
is what he meant.

He is a Slaver lieutenant of no particular rank and no particular use, who has survived two decades in an
organisation that eats men like him because he is frightened of exactly the right person and cruel only to
people who cannot answer. He beats slaves and stops short of permanent damage. He defers upward with an
enthusiasm that embarrasses the men receiving it. He has never in his life entered a contest whose outcome was
in doubt, and he believes himself hard.

**He is the one character in this series written with nothing redeeming in him**, and that is a deliberate
choice rather than an oversight. See the guardrails, which are the point of this sheet.

---

## Paradigm Analysis

**Merchant → Truth Seeker → Spirited Competitor → Founder → Engineer → Creator → Caretaker**

**This is the Joffrey Baratheon Counterpoint from `The Seven Paradigms.md`, and that section is effectively
his sheet already** — Merchant primary with nothing balancing it, *"not a shrewd accumulator, but a petulant
one,"* a drive for *"more deference, more cruelty he can inflict, more whatever's in front of him"* with no
terminal state. The doc's own summary of the type is the summary of him: **he wants the world handed to him
and throws tantrums when it isn't.**

**Read only the top and the bottom.** For most of this cast the interesting part of a stack is the middle —
the collisions, the drive that gets surrendered second-to-last. Sarn has no middle. Everything from fourth
down is negligible and the ordering between them is close to arbitrary. **That flatness is the
characterization**, not a gap in the analysis.

### Merchant (Primary, and effectively the whole stack)

Appetite without a terminal state, and — the part that matters for Book 6 — **appetite disconnected from
acquisition.** He wants Ronas's position and will not do an hour's work to earn it. He wants to be feared and
has only ever frightened people who were already restrained by someone else. He wants things **given**.

**This is what makes him unmodellable, and it is a precise mechanism rather than a mood.** A model does not
need a man to want things; it needs him to *do the work that gets him the thing*. That assumption is the one
he does not satisfy, which is why Melina Valanar's profile of him is not blank but **confidently wrong** —
full detail on the Book 6 outline, Act III, where it is load-bearing for her arc rather than his.

### Truth Seeker (Second, and purely instrumental)

**His one genuine competence: he reads power accurately.** He knows precisely how much cruelty he can inflict
before it costs him, he knows exactly who in a room can hurt him, and he is never wrong about it while Ronas
is alive. There is nothing intrinsic in it — he has no curiosity about anything — and it is better understood
as twenty years of fear producing a very narrow expertise.

**It fails him exactly once, and it kills him.** See the guardrail on Tiberian below.

### Spirited Competitor (Third, and counterfeit)

From outside, and from inside his own head, this looks like his second drive. It is not one. **He avoids every
contest whose outcome is in doubt** — the Paradigms doc's phrase for the type is that he *"wants the
opposition disarmed first"* — and what he has instead is the *appearance* of a fighting man, assembled
entirely out of beating people who could not fight back.

**He believes the counterfeit.** That is the single most important thing about him after the appetite, and it
is what walks him into the last scene of his life.

### Founder, Engineer, Creator, Caretaker (the rest)

**Founder:** none. He does not want the cartel, he wants no one above him, and he would not rebuild anything.
**Engineer:** near-zero, and it shows on the page — he seizes the water distribution in Book 6 and cannot
operate it, so the taps run worse every month he holds them and he blames whoever is nearest. **Creator:**
absent. **Caretaker: last, and absolutely.** There is no floor under him. This is the precise inversion of
Ronas, and the inversion is the point.

## What He Is For

**He is the perpetrators' face, on exactly the terms the dead child is the victims'.** Book 6's own brief for
that child is *"not a statistic. A name, a face"* — and the book applied that principle only to the people
being killed, leaving the people doing the killing as three category nouns across four acts. Sarn finishes a
device the book already relies on.

**And Book 6 is the greyest book of the eight** — Melina's delay kills, Tiberian's intervention kills, nothing
in it has a clean answer — which is exhausting by design. **Sarn is the relief valve**: someone the reader may
hate without having to think about it, whose death is the one unqualified satisfaction in the book.

### What he does to Ronas Dermir, without either of them knowing it

**He is what Ronas spent twenty years claiming to be.** Ronas's whole self-presentation is the pure apex
predator — *damaged product sells for less*, *the Warrens has no place for weakness* — and his own sheet is
explicit that this is a Caretaker drive wearing Competitor armour, denied because his world would kill him
for admitting it.

**Sarn is that claim with nothing underneath it, and he is pathetic.** The reader who meets him understands,
without one character saying so, that Ronas was lying about himself for two decades. `The Seven Paradigms.md`
has the structure already: a Merchant on top with nothing else, standing beside a Competitor primary with
terminal states, in the same organisation, demonstrating the doc's own line that **a Merchant in the stack
and a Merchant on top are radically different people.**

**No character ever articulates this.** It is a fact the reader assembles and the cast cannot.

## Guardrails — the point of this sheet

**1. He is complete as designed. Do not deepen him later.** *(Nothing in this series forbids a character like
him. A search of the whole repository for any rule requiring an antagonist to be sympathetic, grey, or
possessed of redeeming features returns nothing, because none was ever written — and the framework
**provides** for him, per the Joffrey Counterpoint above. The real rule 2 test is *does the text charge them
for it*, and it barely engages: **he never acts against his own stack once.** He is the most internally
consistent character in the book, and the book charges him in full, exactly once, at the end.)*

**What this guards against is a later pass "deepening" him** because he reads thin beside Rosik, Ronas and
Kalden. He is not thin — he is shallow on purpose. **Give him no wound, no reason, no moment of unexpected
tenderness, no glimpse of the boy he was.** A sheet this short is correct for him; padding it would be the
first symptom of the drift it exists to prevent.

**2. Nobody credits him with a plan.** He does not scheme. He pounces on whatever falls into his lap and then
**narrates it to himself afterward as a masterstroke** — and he is the only source in the book for that
reading. Not Melina calling him cunning, not one of her analysts crediting him with strategy, not Tiberian
conceding him one at the end. **The moment a competent character describes him as clever he acquires a
stature this whole design exists to deny him.** The reader should see the seam between what he did and what
he says he did.

**3. Write him small.** Pettiness, repeatedly — not atrocity. A man who commits one enormous evil is a plot
device; a man who commits forty small ones is a person, and the accumulation is what makes the reader's hatred
real. This is why he is placed across all four acts rather than introduced as the thing Tiberian kills.

**4. His restraint under Ronas is fear and nothing else, and Book 2 supplies its source.** He was in the
organisation when Rosik first ordered the leash off, and **he was not among the ones who ran wild** — his only
governor is fear of the man who punishes him, and in Book 2 Ronas was still standing, and proved it by
executing the lieutenant who murdered Melina Kemvimore, immediately, to re-establish control. Sarn watched
that and drew exactly one lesson.

**The one who stayed in line is the one who spent twenty years storing it up.** *(Never dated on the page, no
character refers to it, and it is not a recognition for the reader to have — **Book 2's prose never
individuates a single one of Ronas's lieutenants**, so there is nobody there to recognise. It is here so a
drafter knows the fear is specific and has a source.)*

**5. He badly misjudges Tiberian, and the mechanism is not ignorance.** He knows the Tiger Prince's reputation
perfectly well and dismisses it as propaganda **because he manufactures his own the same way** — he built his
standing on beating people who could not fight back and letting the story do the rest, so he assumes
Tiberian's standing is the identical con with better tailoring. **A fraud assumes everyone else is a fraud.**
That assumption kills him, and it is characterization rather than a plot hole to be patched.

**6. He is not the lieutenant who killed Melina Kemvimore.** Ronas executed that man immediately in Book 2.
The revenge frame it would import would wreck both this design and Book 6's Act IV.

**7. Specimen, never source.** He is fairly typical of what Ronas was sitting on; the narration never calls
him the worst of them, and the Warrens is still collapsing when he dies. *(This one is enforced structurally
in `book6_outline.md`, along with the rule that he never touches the dead child — both are constraints on the
book's architecture rather than on him, and the outline is their home.)*

## Personal Characteristics

### Voice — not yet locked, and a warning about locking it

**He has no designed register yet**, and one should not be invented casually. Two constraints exist already.
**He is not to be given Ronas's** — that register is locked to Ronas on his own sheet and is load-bearing
there. And the obvious instinct, giving him a *distinctive* voice, is the wrong instinct: the truthful choice
is that **he talks like a bigger man than he is**, using vocabulary and cadence borrowed from people he has
watched, which fits a man whose entire self is assembled from other men's reputations.

If Book 6 drafting produces something worth pinning, pin it here.

### Behavioral Patterns

- **Kisses up, kicks down** — the whole of him, and the reason he survived. His deference is excessive enough
  to be uncomfortable to receive, and it costs him nothing, because he does not experience it as humiliation.
- **Calibrated cruelty** — under Ronas, always at the permitted maximum and never past it. He has no internal
  limit; he has a very accurate map of an external one.
- **Betrays whoever is nearest whenever it is easy**, and is measurably less safe afterward every time.
- **Blames the person closest to hand** for any failure, including failures of machinery he does not
  understand.
- **Escapes by putting other people between himself and the danger**, and reads each escape as evidence of his
  own brilliance.

### The Central Tension

There isn't one, and that is the design. Every other antagonist in this series carries a genuine internal
collision — Rosik's denied grief against his Merchant, Ronas's Caretaker against the armour he cannot take
off, Kalden's real claim against his real vanity. **Sarn has no collision, because he has nothing to collide
with the appetite.** He wants, he takes what is nearest, he is frightened of whoever can hurt him, and when
the last person who could hurt him is dead he becomes, immediately and completely, everything he was already
prepared to be.

He dies begging — and what he begs with is another betrayal, selling somebody else, mid-sentence. **He never
gets the dignity of a genuine surrender**, which is the last fact about him and the reason his death costs
Tiberian nothing.
