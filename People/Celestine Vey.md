# Celestine Vey — The Lady Who Knew the Rules

## Paradigm stack

Unsettled.

## Function

Celestine is the daughter of the Marquis of Vey and Lucien's elder sister. The collapse of the Grain
cartel cost House Vey a substantial share of its wealth and much of its old influence. It remains a
rich and well-connected house. Celestine sees the restored dynasty as the order in which her family
will have to prosper, and she intends to put it close to the throne by marrying Tiberian. She wants
him for herself as well. He is handsome, well liked and a good prospect by every measure she trusts.

Celestine would make an able royal hostess. She can receive a difficult guest, put him at ease and
seat him where he can do the least harm. She enjoys knowing who can be invited together and which
favor can be asked without making its recipient feel used. She also tends to take a person's place
in the room as a measure of what that person is worth.

She has patronized Irene since they were both about sixteen and Irene first came to the balls. Before
there was any question of rivalry, Irene's poverty was reason enough for Celestine to make a little
display of noticing it. The blue dress was a favorite subject and far from the only one.

**Rank is how she reads a room.** Her circle is for the daughters of dukes, marquises and counts. A
baron's daughter barely warrants a glance, which is why she spent years not noticing Nessa Halcin. The
moment she understood that Nessa and Melina were genuine friends, Nessa was invited in and treated as
roughly a count's daughter's equal, Aurelia's level, and Celestine considered that generous of her. She
has been scrupulously civil to Nessa ever since. Nessa does not care, and Celestine has not noticed.

Tiberian mostly sees the manners Celestine uses around him, and Elara thinks her a very nice young
woman. **She is sweet to her date and screams at the waiter**: lovely to the people she needs, careless
with the ones she does not. It is Nessa who tells Tiberian the difference. Irene knows exactly how those
same manners sound when they are being used against her.

Celestine loves Lucien and has better political judgment than he does. She sees what his plan could
cost the family, refuses to help him and believes him when he promises to stop. By the time she knows
better, he is in prison and her father will not let her see him. The conventions she can usually turn
to her advantage give her very little room to act when her own family is the one drawing away.

See [The Marriage of the Tiger Prince](../Offstage.md#the-marriage-of-the-tiger-prince) for the
courtship, Lucien's arrest and the blue dress.

## Guardrails

- Her manners and ceremonial competence are genuine. Give her something she can do well in a room
  where she has every advantage.
- She chooses targets who have little room to answer. Her insults should survive being repeated
  aloud as compliments.
- She believes Lucien's promise until his arrest. She refuses to fund or join the conspiracy. **The
  belief is not naive and it is not free.** It is a fair inference, since Lucien was the circle's
  money, and it is also the only belief that let her keep her brother. Never write it as simple
  trust.
- Lucien never names her, and nobody outside House Vey knows she was asked. She can never confess
  her silence and will never be accused of it. That is what it costs her.
- **House Vey was a hunting house**, and her father hunted before Elara's ban. She was too young to
  remember it and never saw the prey. What she thinks of it now is open; do not settle it in passing.
- Her grief is real. Let it coexist with the vanity, affection and social cruelty she brought into
  the story.
