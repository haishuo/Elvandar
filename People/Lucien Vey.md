# Lucien Vey — The Marquis's Son Who Wanted to Rule

## Paradigm stack

Unsettled.

## Function

Lucien is Celestine's younger brother and a son of the Marquis of Vey. He grew up hearing how much
more his family had mattered when the Grain sat on the Coterie. He knows that world from the stories
of people who did well in it. He wants the Coterie back and expects to be among the men who govern
through it.

Lucien turns a circle of disaffected young nobles into a group capable of acting. He puts his own
money into it and takes on work that the others had been content to discuss. He can find people who
share his grievance, commit resources and press a decision to a conclusion. Once he has paid for
something, he assumes everyone involved owes him obedience. The peers he needs hear themselves
addressed as dependents. He admires Rosik's power and has little sense of the work that held Rosik's
alliances together.

His personal purse has limits, which is what brings him to Celestine. He expects sympathy from
someone who understands what House Vey lost. After she refuses, he gives her the promise she wants
to hear and keeps going. He borrows against what he expects to inherit, from lenders who will
advance money on a promise. The careful houses on Vessel Street will not. He has put enough of himself into the venture that abandoning it would mean
giving up the position he already thinks of as his. His conviction leaves him slow to see how little
loyalty he has purchased.

[The Children of the Great Hunt](../Offstage.md#the-children-of-the-great-hunt) holds his part in the
conspiracy and his execution. [The Marriage of the Tiger Prince](../The%20Marriage%20of%20the%20Tiger%20Prince.md)
holds his dealings with Celestine and the consequences for her.

## Guardrails

- He is the principal financier and an organizer. The other conspirators have money, ideas and
  contacts of their own.
- His practical initiative is part of what makes his domineering manner costly. He has real work
  to his credit and reads far too much obligation into it.
- His resentment comes from family memory. He cannot speak as a witness to the Coterie's government.
- **House Vey was a hunting house.** The Marquis hunted before Elara's ban, and the ban is part of the
  grievance Lucien inherits. He was too young to remember it and never saw the prey; he knows the Hunt
  as a privilege his family lost, not as what it was.
- **He never names Celestine**, under questioning or at trial. It is the one loyalty in the venture
  he did not buy, and the only one that holds.
- His pressure makes the wavering men susceptible to an approach. Melina and Nessa do the work
  that draws them away.
