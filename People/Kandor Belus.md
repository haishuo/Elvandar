# Kandor Belus: The War Provost

## The Soldier Who Wanted No War

Kandor Belus is the War Provost of Uratha and head of the Ruby Tower, which is responsible for the nation's
defense and martial training. He is often seen as the most practical-minded member of the Council of
Provosts (`Places/Uratha/The Government of Uratha.md`). **He is the man who tried to remove Leowin Draskin
lawfully, failed, and then tried to remove him by force with the smallest strike he could design, and failed
again.** The second failure started the civil war he had spent everything trying to avoid.

**He wants to avoid war, and that is expertise, not softness.** He is not only a soldier who has seen war; he
is the War Provost of a country that is bad at it. Ruby is not fully equipped to fight a land war, and in the
Compact War its limits were partly covered by Terinok's presence in the Alliance, which Uratha's own record
calls *both true and embarrassing* (`Places/Uratha/Uratha_ The Land of Scholars.md`). Nobody in Uratha knows
better what a civil war would cost, because nobody knows better how badly Uratha fights.

## Paradigm Analysis

**Founder → Caretaker → Spirited Competitor → Engineer → Truth Seeker → Creator → Merchant**

*Proposed 2026-11-21, checked against the decisions the author has already given him rather than derived from
them; the author may reorder it.*

### Founder (Primary)

**Will this endure?** What he defends is Uratha's civic culture: Council decisions by consensus, position
papers in advance, ritualised debate, recorded votes, recall by a Tower's own faculty, magistrates who can
order a scholar released on a valid licence. **That culture was real.** The Globehall was a crime committed in
secret by an inner circle inside it, not what it was. Belus fights for *the Uratha he remembered*, and it is
not a lie he is telling himself.

**He sacrifices his own position to it.** He knows the strike exceeds his authority as War Provost and expects
to lose his seat when it is over, and he goes anyway. The institution comes before his place in it. **The
Founder's shadow is in him too**: the word *remembered* is a conservative's word, and he is defending a
country against change as much as against Draskin.

### Caretaker (Second)

**Minimise bloodshed.** It governs his means, not his end. It is why he began with a vote, why the strike is
aimed at one man, and why the force is small. **It is also why the strike fails**: a force small enough to be
humane is too small to overwhelm the defenders.

### Spirited Competitor (Third)

**He believes the contest should be fair.** Draskin won the vote within the rules and rigged it: lawful notice,
timed for Sapphire's absence, with no proxy permitted. Belus cannot name a rule Draskin broke and can see that
the game was never fair. This is the drive that is outraged. It is third, not first: he does not want to beat
Draskin, he wants Draskin gone and tried.

### Engineer (Fourth)

**Practical-minded.** He plans operations, sizes forces, and knows what Ruby can and cannot do. It serves the
drives above it.

### Truth Seeker (Fifth)

**He did not ask.** He knew how long the Crown had been silent, as a Provost would, and credited Uratha's
technological lead to its scholarship, because that is what Uratha believes about itself. A higher Truth
Seeker would have wondered what replaced the Crown. He did not, and that is not complicity.

### Creator, Merchant (the rest)

**Merchant is last**, and the strike shows it: he gives up his career without bargaining for anything in
return.

## What He Knew — author's ruling, 2026-11-21

**He did not know about the Globehall.** Ruby is among the Towers that were *variously uninvolved or
horrified*, and Belus was horrified. He knew the Crown was silent; knowing the Crown was dead is not knowing
what replaced it. **What Book 8 costs him is horror, not guilt.** If his own Tower benefited from the
Globehall's knowledge without his knowing, he may carry that, but it must never be written as complicity.

---

## What He Did

**He started the no-confidence motion.** The other four opposing Provosts joined him, so the lawful route was
his, and so was its failure. He wanted Draskin out of office so that the immunity would end, Sapphire could
prosecute him, and the nation could have justice. **Draskin defeated it lawfully and in bad faith**, and the
failed vote barred another motion for two years.

**Then the Ilhamori came to ask him to reaffirm his loyalties**, and Draskin began moving toward the unanimity
amendment. Belus read it as preparation for a purge: accusations, suspensions, replacement Provosts, and an
amendment passed by the replacements. Draskin permanent, and permanently immune. **He concluded that if he
did nothing soon, Uratha as he knew it would vanish**, and that every lawful route was closed.

**So he chose the minimum intervention he could see**: a small, fast strike from Ruby aimed at the Ivory
Tower, to take Draskin into custody, in irons if that was what it took. He knew Onyx and Amethyst were
implicated too and might retaliate even if he succeeded. He hoped the speed of the strike would put Draskin
under arrest before they could react. **He did not know how it would play out in the courts**, and he knew he
was exceeding his office. He believed he was acting as a patriot, and if it cost him his career, so be it.

**It failed.** The Ilhamori fought the Ruby force off, and Draskin declared Belus in rebellion.

## Guardrails

**1. He did not know about the Globehall.** Never imply he suspected it. His residue from Book 8 is horror.

**2. His wish to avoid war is a soldier's judgment, not a pacifist's scruple.** Do not write him as reluctant
to fight in general, and do not write him as a hawk. He knows exactly what war would cost Uratha.

**3. He prices the strike himself.** He never believes it lawful, and he never expects to keep his seat after
it. The page must not treat it as free, and it does not need anyone else to tell him what it cost.

**4. The strike fails on its merits.** The Ilhamori are the service that arrests powerful mages who do not want
to be arrested, and in Book 8 they overpowered Xion in the Globehall. Taking the Ivory Tower is close,
room-by-room work on their ground, and Ruby trains for the field. **No leak or betrayal is needed**, and none
is to be added unless the author places one.

**5. His restraint and his failure are the same choice.** Do not separate them: he sized the force to his
conscience, and the size lost.

**6. Nobody states the title.** Not Belus, not the narration. *This was never a fair game* is what the reader
works out from the vote's timing, the Ilhamori visit and the strike. If the words ever appear, once, flat, and
not from him as a thesis.

**7. He does not articulate what he cannot recognise.** What he sees is Draskin using the Ilhamori openly
against a sitting Provost. That the Ilhamori silenced the Globehall's discoverers in secret, and are now doing
the same thing to the Council in the open, is a connection the reader may make. **Nobody points at it.**

**8. His patriotism is not self-deception.** The Uratha he remembers is the civic culture, and it was real. Do
not write it as a lie he refuses to see.

## Relationships

### Leowin Draskin

His opponent, and the man he holds responsible. Draskin *enjoys politics*; for him it has always been a game.
Belus's grievance is not that it is a game but that it is not fair.

### The other opposing Towers

Pearl, Topaz, Emerald and Sapphire joined his motion. **His strike puts them in an impossible position**: they
voted to remove Draskin, and now they must choose between him and a man who attacked the seat of government.

### Balisha

He has no relationship with the Valanars to speak of. If Book 9 is written from his side, what Balisha does is
a foreign power's choice, seen from outside.

### The Central Tension

**Every step he took was in character, and every step made the next one worse.** He started with the lawful
route; Draskin defeated it lawfully and in bad faith; he chose the smallest intervention he could; it failed
because of the restraint that made it decent. **The man who wanted to avoid war is the one who started it.**
