# Leowin Draskin: The Scholar-King

## The Man Who Cannot Leave

Leowin Draskin is the Scholar-King of Uratha, elected from the Department of Cosmological Studies. He took
office as a compromise candidate, about five years into his first ten-year term when the series begins, and
by Book 8 he is serving an almost unheard-of third term (`Offstage/Draskin's Three Terms.md`). **He sits at
the center of the Globehall**, in which Ivory is deeply implicated (`Offstage/The Failed Vote Against
Draskin.md`), and his government held the Empress of Balisha and her Emperor Consort prisoner.

**After Book 8, everything he does is driven by one fear: execution.** *(Author's ruling, 2026-11-21.)* He
enjoys politics, and he likes the office. Both are true and neither is the reason. **The reason is that if he
leaves office, they will pin everything on him, he will be executed, and he does not want to die.** It is
one of the oldest and strongest fears there is, and there is nothing to bargain him down to.

## Paradigm Analysis

**Merchant → Spirited Competitor → Founder → Engineer → Truth Seeker → Creator → Caretaker**

*Proposed 2026-11-21, checked against Book 3 ch12 and his post-Book 8 decisions rather than derived from them;
the author may reorder it.*

### Merchant (Primary)

**He is a trader of terms.** In B3 ch12 he negotiates the Crown compact himself, *precise, unhurried, entirely
without vanity, conceding small things quickly and holding the two or three that mattered*, and Xion watches
him *price* the sealed chamber in about three seconds. After Book 8 he prices his own survival the same way:
a plea deal against immunity, Amethyst and Onyx as the goods he could hand over, two years as the time he has
bought. **The Merchant's shadow is the one that matters in Book 9**: what it cannot keep, it can burn down. He
does not intend a civil war, and he will pay for one rather than give up the office.

### Spirited Competitor (Second)

**He enjoys the game.** The bargains and rivalries of the Council are *work he wants to do*
(`Places/Uratha/The Government of Uratha.md`), and he stood uninvited for a third term because he liked the
work. Rigging the vote within the rules is the competitor's pleasure in his own skill.

### Founder (Third)

**He maintains.** His administration favoured the status quo, and he has a feel for institutions and how
to use them. It is not a commitment to them: he will bend the constitution to stay alive.

### Engineer, Truth Seeker, Creator (the middle)

**Truth Seeker is low.** The Globehall survived on euphemism, and in Book 8 Xion shreds the ones Draskin's
Council hides behind. He reads everything correctly and believes what is useful.

### Caretaker (Last)

**He presided over the Globehall.** Whatever he feels about the minds in it, it was never what decided anything.

## Why He Does Not Give Up

**The obvious question is why he does not surrender, accept arrest, and let Uratha survive.** The answer is the
arithmetic as he sees it:

- **The immunity only postpones prosecution.** It covers conduct under claimed official authority while he holds
  the office, investigators may keep preserving evidence the whole time, and it ends the day he leaves. **Serving
  out his term does not save him.** His only safe position is permanent office.
- **A plea deal might save his life.** If he turns over the people in Amethyst and Onyx, he *might* get
  imprisonment rather than execution. *Might.*
- **Staying in office is certain, for now.** The failed vote bars another no-confidence motion for two years.
  **Two years to work out how to survive when they run out**, and that is a better offer than *might*.

**So every move he makes extends what the office protects:**

- **The vote**, timed with full formal notice for Sapphire's absence, knowing no proxy was possible.
- **The amendment** requiring all seven Provosts to vote for removal: removal impossible while one Provost is his.
- **The purge**, through the Ilhamori and the suspension clause. A purged Council also elects the Scholar-King,
  and terms are renewable; if he can never leave alive, he needs a fourth term, and a fifth.

**Underneath the fear of death is the fear of losing control of his fate.** *(Author's ruling, 2026-11-21.)*
If he ever loses the office, **his entire fate is in the hands of others.** A plea deal is not only uncertain;
it hands the decision to prosecutors, magistrates and the Towers he would be betraying. In office, every outcome
still runs through him. A man who has spent his life setting terms cannot bear to be the one terms are set for.

**How he gets past the two years is tomorrow's problem.** In Book 3 he is patient: he holds the few things that
matter and waits. Here he buys two years knowingly, without a plan for the third. **That is what fear does to a
patient man**: the horizon shrinks to *stay alive this year*. On the page it reads as a change in him, a man who
used to think in terms now thinking in reprieves, never as a different personality.

**His guarantee is only as good as everyone else's willingness to keep the rules.** He rigs them and depends on
them at the same moment. The office protects him only while the people around him choose to obey it, and
Belus's strike shows that the guarantee was never guaranteed. **The man who cheated within the rules needs
everyone else to play fair.** Nobody says so.

**He does not see it, and that is not an oversight: it is his skill seen from behind.** *(Author's ruling,
2026-11-21.)* Cheating while everyone else plays fair is close to the job description. Every bargain he has
made, every vote timed with proper notice, every small thing conceded fast to hold the two that mattered, works
only because the other players stay at the table. In his whole career nobody in the Council has overturned it,
and a politician who planned for opponents sending soldiers would be a worse politician. **Assuming everyone
plays fair is the ground his competence stands on**, so rigging the rules while relying on them does not feel
like a contradiction to him. B3 ch12 says *he read everything correctly*; inside the room, he does. The room is
the assumption.

*A reading, not a ruling:* Book 8 may have taught him the wrong lesson. He has met one man who refused to play,
Xion, who locked the Globehall and would not open it; but Xion was a prisoner and Draskin held the force. What
he could take from it is that a refusal can be outlasted if you hold the Ilhamori. **Belus is the first refusal
with force behind it**, and nothing in Draskin's experience prepares him for that. Book 8 does not need to change
for this.

**His coalition is a hostage arrangement.** The implicated Ilhamori leadership, Onyx and Amethyst have their own
interest in his survival, because his fall exposes them. They know he could sell them to save himself. He keeps
them close because they are the only thing he has to sell. **Everyone around him is held in place by the fear
he has.**

## In Escaping Execution, He Guarantees It

*(Author's ruling, 2026-11-21.)* **Had he sued for leniency at once** and turned state's evidence on Onyx,
Amethyst and the mages he knew were involved in the Globehall and in holding the Valanars, **he might genuinely
have avoided execution.** Might: nothing guaranteed it, but the chance was real. **After the civil war, with
thousands dead, there is no chance.** Once he is brought before a Sapphire court, his fate is sealed.

**His one real error is a pricing error, and he is a Merchant.** He weighed a plea deal that *might* save him
against an office that was *certain* for two years, and he priced both wrong:

- **He overvalued certainty.** The guarantee held only while everyone else kept the rules (above).
- **He let the deal lose its value.** At first he had something to sell: Onyx, Amethyst, the Globehall's mages,
  the people behind holding the Valanars, and his own crime was concealment and complicity in a conspiracy with
  many hands. **Every move he made afterward shrank what he had to sell and grew what he owed**, until the
  worst thing against him was no longer anyone he could hand over. It was his own war.

**The trader dies of a bad trade: he held a position while it fell.** Nobody in the book says so.

## How He Ends

*(Author's design, 2026-11-21. Full placement: `Book 9 - Fair Game/book9_outline.md`, *The Ending*.)* **His side
loses the war, and he runs**, because he will not die. Too famous to be sheltered as himself, he goes incognito,
with as much liquid wealth as he can carry after one last "official" act, liquidating the Ivory Tower's
treasury. **He goes to Kaha'an**, where nobody would expect him. His money is coin, so he must deposit it, and to
an Urathan a bank is a strongbox; he avoids the large houses as too discerning and picks a small one eager for
his business. **It is House Umberlow.** He does not know it is Irene Valanar's.

**He never meets her.** A clerk takes the deposit, asks nothing, and writes everything down, including where he
is staying. **He takes *no questions* to mean *no knowledge*.** Irene reads the ledger, stops on the line, and
tells Tiberian. **Elara and Xion extradite him through proper procedure; a Sapphire court tries him and
sentences him to death.**

**Every part of it is him.** He flees because he will not die. He chooses the least expected city because he never
told anybody what he expected. He reads a foreign money house by Urathan rules, as he read the Council by the
assumption that everyone plays fair. **His last mispricing is which house to trust with his money**, and he makes
it with a better trader, whose primary is also Merchant. **What kills him is procedure honestly followed.**

## A Politician, Not a General

**He is a bureaucrat, and he thinks in procedure.** When Belus's strike fails, Draskin declares him in rebellion
and **does not understand that he has started a civil war.** He expects the Ilhamori to arrest Belus and that to
be the end of it, because that is how it would be handled *bureaucratically*. To him a rebellion is a warrant.

**The Ilhamori go to make the arrest, and Ruby soldiers stop them.** That is the first time he begins to
understand that events are moving in a direction he can no longer control
(`Book 9 - Fair Game/book9_outline.md`).

**It mirrors Belus's mistake.** Belus sent soldiers to do a police action; Draskin sends police to put down a
rebellion. Each uses his own instrument on the other man's problem, and each fails for it. **Nobody in the book
points this out.**

## Guardrails

**1. The fear of death is the reason.** Never write him as a man who wants power for its own sake. *He loves
the office* is the easy reading and the wrong one. He does enjoy it; that is a footnote.

**2. He is very good at the game.** B3 ch12 is the standard: precise, unhurried, without vanity, reading
everything correctly, and he *never in his life told anybody what he expected*. Do not make him a fool so that
his opponents look clever. His mistakes after Book 8 come from fear and from fighting outside his instrument,
not from stupidity. **Like Belus's, they come from his competence**: each man fails at the edge of what he is
good at, never through weakness.

**3. He did not intend a civil war.** He thinks in warrants and votes. Do not let him plan the war or welcome
it; he finds it out when the arrest fails.

**4. His words are chosen.** *Do you reaffirm your loyalty to the Scholar-King Leowin Draskin?* is how he writes
when he is in control. **When his control slips, the precision slips with it**, and from Belus's side of the
page that may be the only way the reader sees it. The narration never explains his letters.

**5. The plea deal is real.** He has weighed it honestly, and turning on Amethyst and Onyx is genuinely
available to him. Do not write his allies as loyal to him or him to them.

**6. He has no on-page interior in Book 9.** The book is Belus's. Draskin is seen from outside: through his
letters, his orders, and what happens when they are carried out. **In Kaha'an he is a line in Irene's ledger**, and
the scene is hers, not his.

## Relationships

### Kandor Belus

His opponent. Belus wants him gone and tried; Draskin wants to live. **Neither is wrong about the other.** Draskin
reads Belus's refusal to reaffirm exactly right. He misreads what Belus's Tower can do. See `People/Kandor
Belus.md`.

### Irene Valanar, born Umberlow

He has never met her and never does. He chooses her house to hide his money in without knowing it is hers, and
she finds him in her books.

### Onyx, Amethyst, and the implicated Ilhamori

Fellow hostages. See *Why He Does Not Give Up*.

### Elara and Xion Valanar

He negotiated the Crown compact with them in Book 3 and was gracious at the end, *and the warmth in it was
real*. His government later held them prisoner. They will not lift a hand for him. **When he is found in their city,
they extradite him through proper procedure** rather than try him themselves.

## Personal Characteristics

### Physical Presence

**Not yet set.** B3 ch12 gives none, and none is to be invented in a draft.

### Behavioral Patterns

- Enters a room before protocol requires it, when it suits him (B3 ch12).
- Adjusts to new information *without any part of his face admitting that an adjustment had taken place*.
- Concedes small things fast and holds the few that matter.
- Never states what he expects.

### The Central Tension

**The most skilled politician in Uratha, fighting for his life with the only instrument he has**, against a
problem that instrument cannot solve. Every move he makes to stay safe makes the war he did not plan more
certain. **He clung to the office so that his fate would never be in other people's hands, and the war it
produced puts it in the hands of soldiers**, the one kind of people he cannot manage. The blocked arrest is not
only the moment he sees events slipping; it is his worst fear arriving by the route he took to avoid it. **And
the execution he fled is the one he makes certain.**
