# Offstage

Things that happened in this world and are not shown in any book.

**These are canon.** Not drafts, not speculation, not maybes — settled authorial history that simply
never got a scene. The nearest comparison is the way a legendarium sits underneath the novels written
from it: this is the material the books stand on, told as account rather than as scene. A later pass may
dramatise any of it, and until then a contradiction with what is written here is a **bug**, on the same
terms as a contradiction with a character sheet.

---

## What belongs here

Three tests. An entry needs all three, and the third is the one that decides it.

1. **Settled and authorial.** Not an idea being tried out. If it is still a question, it goes in the
   relevant outline as an open question instead.
2. **Not depicted anywhere.** If a book's prose or beat drafts stage it, it belongs to that book and this
   file only points at it.
3. **Something in the books depends on it being true.** This is the real filter. If nothing downstream
   rests on it, it is worldbuilding trivia and does not need a home here.

**One source, and everything else points at it.** The full account lives here. Character sheets carry only
the consequence for that character; book outlines carry only what that book needs. Do not restate an
entry in three places — that is how the ch8 beat draft came to describe a chapter that no longer existed.

**Register:** chronicle, not scene. Tell it. If it wants dialogue and staging, it wants to be a chapter,
and the decision to make it one is a decision about a book.

## The Taking of the Tiger Prince

**When:** Between Book 6 and Book 7, during the population of the Green Cities.
**Who:** Tiberian Valanar, Kess Ashwood, Melina Valanar, the last of the Slavers.

The relocation was underway and going well, which is what made it dangerous. Sixty thousand people were
being moved out of the failing tunnels into cities built on reclaimed desert, and the men who had run the
Warrens under Ronas Dermir were watching the one asset they had ever possessed — a captive population, and
a monopoly on what that population needed to live — being carried off in wagons a district at a time.
What was left of the Slavers made one last grab at the power they had lost when Ronas went down.

They used the Crown Prince's own habit against him. Tiberian inspected the construction personally, and
made a point of being seen doing it: walking the sites, in front of the people being rehoused, in the open,
often. It was not vanity. He believed the people being moved should be able to look at the man moving them.

Kess Ashwood was not there. She still went off for stretches, to wherever it was she went, and Tiberian
still never asked and never sent anyone after her. He was simply certain she would come back.

They took him in a tight space, which was the only sort of ground on which it could have worked. He fought.
It did not matter.

The demand was the Slavers restored to what they had been, in exchange for the prince. The answer came from
Melina, with her parents behind it and no daylight between the three of them: return him unharmed and you
will be imprisoned rather than executed. He was recovered.

**What it did to Kess is the reason this entry exists.** Standing outside it, unable to affect any of it,
she worked out two things in an order that mattered. The first was that she could leave — right now, in
the middle of it, and he could not have stopped her. The second was that this had been true every single
day since he cut her out of the pit. **Nobody had ever contested it.** The freedom she had been holding in
reserve, the thing the distance was for, had never once been under threat.

And the day it finally cost something was a day she had not been there. On a watch nobody had ever assigned
her, that she had never agreed to keep, and that she was therefore the only person who could be held to.

She decided it would not happen again.

---

**On what this is not.** It is not gratitude and it must never be written as gratitude. She is not rescued
into loyalty — she was rescued twice already and it produced a woman who ran and then shadowed. **She is
furious with herself over a duty she appointed herself to and then failed.** She becomes his protector by
her own decision, unprompted and unrequested, which is the only mechanism available for a character whose
entire definition is that she takes no orders and owes nobody anything.

**On Tiberian's half of it**, which is easy to miss because he does nothing. He never asks where she has
been. He extends the freedom first, and keeps extending it, for as long as it takes — and that, rather than
either rescue, is what she had been gathering evidence about. The taking did not create the trust. It
finished a case she had been building for years.

**What depends on this being true.** By Book 7, *she goes where Tiberian goes* is a pattern reliable enough
for Rosik Kemvimore to build a plan on — and it is reliable because of this. **So the Slavers' last grab at
relevance is what welded Kess to Tiberian's shoulder, and therefore what guaranteed she would ride out of
Kaha'an with him rather than stand in the palace.** That is precisely the condition Rosik needed. Their
final attempt to matter served the one man who was using their whole world as scenery, and not one of them
ever knows it.

**Open:** whether Kess acts during the crisis or only after it. The turn happens in her head, about her own
conduct, so it requires her to do nothing at all.
