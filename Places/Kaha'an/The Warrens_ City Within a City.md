# **The Warrens: Overview**

## **What Is The Warrens?**

The Warrens is an underground refugee settlement beneath Kaha'an, home to approximately 60,000 people living in an ancient network of tunnels and chambers. What began twenty years ago as a desperate escape from desert heat has become a permanent city-within-a-city—a sprawling, three-dimensional maze of passages and carved chambers where the displaced have carved out a brutal existence in darkness.

The name "Warrens" is grimly appropriate. The tunnels form a rat's warren of passages connecting carved chambers at multiple depths. Residents are sometimes called "Warren rats" or "tunnel rats" by surface dwellers—a slur some have reclaimed with bitter pride.

**Total Population:** \~60,000  
 **Founded:** Twenty years ago, following the Rending  
 **Location:** Beneath Kaha'an's northern district, extending under much of the city  
 **Governance:** None. Fragmented control by various factions  
 **Official Status:** Doesn't exist (according to Kaha'an's government)

## **Historical Context**

### **Before the Rending: The Old Slums**

Pre-Rending Kaha'an had a typical urban slum in its northern district—known plainly as the Slums District, a rough neighborhood housing perhaps 3,000-5,000 people in poverty. Overcrowded tenements, narrow streets, high crime, but ultimately manageable for an imperial capital of 200,000-300,000 people. That same neighborhood is what residents today call the **Surface Warrens**—the name "Warrens" having crept upward, over twenty years, to swallow the district that sat atop the tunnel-mouths.

Beneath the city lay something else entirely: an extensive network of ancient tunnels, built thousands of years ago by unknown builders. The surface world called them the **Arol Rhutan**, **AH-rol ROO-tən**, /ˈa.rol ˈru.tən/, and gave them little more thought than the name. The phrase means **“Old Tunnels,”** but its two words reached imperial speech differently: *arol* is the learned Halauri word “old, belonging to the former order,” while inherited *rhutan* is a lexical collective for an interconnected tunnel system. It descends regularly from Late Open *re-hutan*; *hutan* by itself names one tunnel, and historical **rh** records the coalescence of /r + h/ after the weak prefix vowel disappeared. Post-sealing, pre-imperial Marresonnian record-keepers formed the hybrid phrase to classify something already ancient; the empire later retained their name. It was never the builders' own name for the system. The tunnels had stood empty for millennia. To anyone who recalled they existed at all, they were unstable ruins, possibly haunted, and plainly never meant for habitation: the proportions were wrong, the dark was total, and nothing about them invited a living soul. The one exception was the Long Knives, the city's old assassins' guild, who guarded the deepest approaches by an obligation older than their surviving history and sent recruits into the empty Arol Rhutan to train. No one else had any use for the place. The guild does not remember that it began as the human custodial order Hauren placed over the Brass Door; see `Offstage.md`, “The First Charge of the Long Knives.”

Kaha'an's underworld in those days included slavers—but not *the* Slavers. Slaving was the trade of a scatter of disconnected gangs who fought one another over territory and victims nearly as often as they fought the city guard that hunted them. There was no Slaver cartel, and no Slaver King. That would come later—and it would come from below.

### **The Rending: Catastrophe**

Twenty years ago, the Rending transformed the fertile lands surrounding Kaha'an into harsh desert. Outlying villages, towns, and even smaller cities were destroyed or rendered uninhabitable. Hundreds of thousands of refugees flooded into Kaha'an—the only remaining major settlement in the region.

The northern slums became catastrophically overcrowded almost immediately. A few thousand permanent residents found themselves sharing space with tens of thousands of desperate newcomers. The city's resources strained beyond breaking point. People slept in streets, in doorways, in collapsed buildings—anywhere they could find shelter.

### **The Descent: Going Underground**

Two factors drove refugees into the tunnels:

**Overcrowding:** The surface simply could not contain the influx. Even the sprawling ruins of the northern district weren't enough. People were dying of exposure, disease, and violence in the packed streets.

**Desert Heat:** Kaha'an, now a desert city, became unbearably hot. Summer temperatures soared above 110°F (43°C). The surface slums, already miserable, became nearly unlivable. Children and elderly died from heat exposure.

Desperate refugees began exploring the tunnels below. They discovered large chambers with smooth walls, high ceilings, and—incredibly—some still-functioning water and ventilation systems. It was dangerous, it was dark, it was claustrophobic, but it was *livable*. And crucially, it was *cooler*.

### **The Growth: From Hundreds to Thousands**

What started as a few hundred desperate people became thousands within months, then tens of thousands within a few years. The underground population exploded as more refugees arrived and surface conditions remained intolerable.

Over twenty years, the Warrens developed its own economy, power structures, and culture. What was meant to be temporary became permanent. A generation has now been born underground, children who have never seen the sun.

The Warrens is no longer an emergency shelter. It's a city.

### **The Slavers: From Gangs to Cartel**

The refugee flood swept everyone underground—including Kaha'an's criminals. The slaver gangs that had once squabbled in the surface alleys found themselves displaced into the same tunnels as everyone else, in a lawless dark where the city guard would not follow.

It was perfect soil for consolidation, and one figure understood that faster than the rest: Ronas Dermir. He had come up through one of the larger gangs—whether as its leader or merely its most ambitious lieutenant is a detail the Rending's chaos swallowed—and in the years that followed he did what no slaver before him had managed. One by one he absorbed the rival crews: by alliance, by force, by simply outlasting them, until the scatter of warring gangs had become a single organization answering to a single master.

That organization is the Slaver cartel, and it is unlike the others. Grain, Water, and Iron all trace their power to the old imperial order; the Slaver cartel was *born of the Rending*, forged underground from refugee-era gangs barely twenty years ago. It is the youngest of Kaha'an's four ruling cartels, and the only one with no roots in the world above—a fact the other three have never let Ronas forget. He rules the Depths as the Slaver King, sits among the cartel lords as the newest and least respected of them, and remembers every slight.

## **The Warrens Today**

### **Population Breakdown**

**Total: \~60,000 residents**

**Surface Warrens (8,000-10,000):** The gateway population, living in the old Slums District—the pre-Rending northern slum, now half-ruined and absorbed into the Warrens in name and in fate. They control access to the tunnels below and manage trade with surface Kaha'an. More privileged than underground residents—they have sunlight, easier access to surface goods, and can grow limited crops in the ruins.

**The Tiers (15,000-18,000):** Upper tunnels closest to the surface. Successful merchants, skilled craftspeople, and those with faction protection live here. Better air quality, easier access to surface, and larger living spaces. This is the "middle class" of the Warrens.

**The Midlevels (25,000-30,000):** Where the majority live. Working poor, laborers, families struggling to survive. They make enough to live but cannot escape. Most have given up hope of returning to the surface.

**The Depths (8,000-10,000):** The desperate. People fleeing something, people with nothing, people who have given up. The air is hot and stale, the spaces cramped and dangerous. Easy prey for slavers and criminals.

### **Demographics**

**Origin:** Most residents are refugees from the Rending—farmers, villagers, townspeople displaced when their homes became desert. Some are from Kaha'an's original poor population. A few fled here from elsewhere, hiding from the law or from debts.

**Age Distribution:**

* Infant mortality is high (30-40% don't survive first year)  
* Children are common but many don't reach adulthood  
* Adults in their 40s are considered old  
* The elderly (60+) are rare and respected as survivors  
* Average life expectancy: mid-30s

**Tunnel-Born Generation:** Children born underground now number in the thousands. They've never seen the surface, navigate by sound and touch, and consider darkness normal. They're developing into a distinct subculture.

### **Relationship to Surface Kaha'an**

**Official Position:** The Warrens doesn't exist. City government records don't acknowledge the underground settlement. This willful ignorance serves multiple purposes:

* Don't have to provide services (expensive)  
* Don't have to address the refugee crisis (politically difficult)  
* Can claim ignorance of crimes committed there  
* Maintain fiction that Kaha'an successfully absorbed all refugees

**In Practice:** Everyone knows it exists. City guards occasionally patrol surface entrances (halfheartedly). Criminals flee there knowing pursuit is unlikely. Escaped slaves hide there with tacit official acceptance.

**Economic Connection:** Despite official denial, significant trade flows between surface and tunnels:

* Surface merchants sell food and goods to Warren merchants (at markup)  
* Salvaged artifacts from the tunnels sell to surface collectors  
* Black market goods flow both directions  
* Some surface businesses quietly employ Warren labor (cheap, desperate workers)

**Political Reality:** The Warrens' existence is politically convenient:

* Acts as pressure valve for refugee crisis  
* Keeps desperate people out of sight  
* Allows government to ignore problem  
* Creates black market economy that some officials profit from  
* If destroyed, 60,000+ people would need actual housing

The unspoken agreement: Warren residents stay underground (or in the ruins), surface dwellers pretend they don't exist, and everyone maintains the fiction.

## **Daily Life in the Warrens**

### **The Darkness**

Most of the Warrens operates in near-total darkness. This is not metaphorical—it's the default physical state. Natural light only reaches the first 20-30 meters down from surface entrances. Below that, complete darkness.

Residents adapt:

* Navigate by memory, touch, and sound  
* Count steps and remember three-dimensional paths  
* Recognize people by footsteps and breathing  
* Conduct entire conversations without seeing faces  
* Children grow up learning to navigate blind

Lighting (lanterns, torches, candles) is expensive. Most families can't afford constant illumination. They ration light carefully, spending most of their time in darkness. This fundamentally changes how people interact—you hear tone and words but miss all facial expressions and body language.

### **Water and Food**

**Water:** The most precious resource. Ancient pumping stations still function, drawing water from underground aquifers. These are controlled by a faction called the Well Mothers, who distribute water throughout the network.

Daily ration: \~2 liters per person for drinking (free with water token). Additional water for cooking or washing costs premium prices. Water theft is one of the few crimes that unites all factions in punishment.

**Food:** All food comes from the surface, making it expensive. By the time goods reach the Midlevels, prices have been marked up 2-3 times through multiple middlemen.

Most residents eat poorly—enough to survive but not thrive:

* Cheap: Preserved grains, dried meat, root vegetables, stale bread  
* Moderate: Fresh bread, beans, dried fish  
* Expensive: Fresh produce, quality meat, cheese  
* Luxury: Anything from before the Rending, foreign goods, spices

Malnutrition is common, especially in children. Stunted growth and chronic hunger are normal.

### **Shelter**

Residents live in the ancient carved chambers scattered throughout the tunnel network. Since the walls are impossibly hard to modify, they build wooden platforms and fabric partitions *inside* the chambers, creating cramped living spaces.

**Living conditions:**

* 2-3 square meters per person (similar to worst urban slums)  
* Fabric walls provide visual privacy but no sound insulation  
* Everyone hears their neighbors' conversations, arguments, and intimate moments  
* Multi-level platforms in larger chambers utilize vertical space  
* The gap between ramshackle human construction and perfect ancient walls creates eerie visual contrast

**Prime real estate:**

* Near ventilation openings (fresh air, can cook without choking on smoke)  
* Near water sources  
* Upper platforms (better air quality)  
* Multiple exits nearby (escape routes)

### **Health**

Disease is rampant. Crowded conditions, poor sanitation, limited clean water, and malnutrition create perfect conditions for illness:

* Respiratory infections (smoke, poor air, mold)  
* Waterborne illnesses  
* Tuberculosis (close quarters, poor ventilation)  
* Skin infections (crowding, limited washing)  
* Injuries from violence, cave-ins, and accidents

Medical care is minimal:

* A surface temple (the Sanctuary) provides basic care (overwhelmed, inadequate supplies)  
* The Well Mothers know some herbalism  
* Folk remedies (mostly ineffective)  
* Black market medicines (often fake)

Life expectancy is significantly lower than surface Kaha'an. Violence, disease, accidents, and malnutrition take constant toll.

### **Time and Rhythm**

Without sunlight, the Warrens has no natural day/night cycle. Residents create artificial rhythms:

* "Day" \= when markets are active, most people awake  
* "Night" \= when most people sleep, fewer lights burning  
* Time measured by market cycles (major market every seven days)

Some measure time by their own body rhythms. Others track shifts if they work for factions. Children grow up with no instinctive sense of time passing.

### **Navigation**

Most people know their immediate area (their home chamber and connecting tunnels within a few hundred meters) but rely on professional guides (called Lantern Boys) for anything beyond.

Getting lost is extremely common and potentially fatal. In total darkness, side passages look identical, sounds echo confusingly, and panic sets in quickly. Without landmarks or a guide, you can wander until you collapse from exhaustion or dehydration.

Children learn practical navigation skills:

* "If lost, find a ventilation shaft and follow air current upward"  
* Count steps and remember turns  
* Listen for water sounds to orient yourself  
* Warm passages lead down, cool passages lead up

## **Culture and Identity**

### **Warren Identity**

Twenty years has created a distinct identity separate from surface Kaha'an:

**Pride in Survival:** "We endure when others would break." Warren residents take grim pride in surviving impossible conditions. They see surface dwellers as soft, spoiled, and weak.

**Resentment of Surface:** "They threw us away like garbage." Deep anger at the government that ignores them and the nobles who pretend they don't exist. Many have given up on ever returning to respectable society.

**Dark Humor:** Constant jokes about their conditions. "At least it's not as hot as the surface" (said in 90-degree passages). Gallows humor is survival mechanism.

**Reclaimed Slurs:** "Warren rat" as badge of honor. "Tunnel rat" said with pride. If surface dwellers think they're rats, they'll be the toughest, most resilient rats imaginable.

**Unique Language:** Entire vocabulary developed underground:

* "Deep as Dead Man's" \= impossibly complicated  
* "Gone to the tunnels" \= disappeared/died  
* "Surface soft" \= someone who can't handle Warren conditions  
* "Walking the dark" \= navigating life's difficulties

### **Generational Divide**

**Surface-Born (Adults):** Remember life before the Rending. Remember sunlight, open spaces, growing things. They mourn what was lost and dream of escape (though most have given up hope).

**Tunnel-Born (Children/Young Adults):** Never seen the surface. The tunnels are their entire world. They navigate better in darkness, have heightened hearing, and find enclosed spaces comforting. Surface world seems alien and frightening to them.

This divide is growing. In another twenty years, most Warren residents will be tunnel-born. The Warrens is becoming its own society, not just refugees waiting to leave.

### **Community**

Despite brutal conditions, strong community bonds form:

* Neighbors watch each other's children  
* Communities pool resources during crises  
* Information sharing about dangers and opportunities  
* Mutual defense agreements  
* Cultural celebrations (rare moments of joy)

Each major chamber develops its own character, customs, and informal leadership. People identify with their chamber-community as much as with the Warrens generally.

### **Oral Culture**

Without light to read by or materials to write on, culture is oral:

* Stories told in darkness (tales of the old world, Warren history, legends)  
* Songs echo beautifully through tunnels  
* Children learn through listening and memorization  
* Storytellers are respected profession, preserving cultural memory

The acoustics of the tunnels make music haunting and beautiful—perhaps the one aesthetic advantage of the Warrens.

### **Religion and Spirituality**

Various belief systems coexist:

* Traditional faiths from refugees' home regions  
* Imperial religious tradition (maintained by the Sanctuary)  
* Practical animism (belief the tunnels themselves are alive)  
* Spiritualist movement (Ghostlights) believing in ancient ghosts  
* Widespread fatalism (we're all doomed anyway, might as well endure)

## **Power and Governance**

### **No Unified Government**

The Warrens has no central authority. Instead, multiple factions compete for control of territory, resources, and influence. Power is measured in:

* Controlled chambers  
* Access to water  
* Ability to extract tribute from residents  
* Control of key passages

### **Major Factions**

**The Long Knives:** The largest and most organized faction, controlling the Tiers and key passages. Led by The Mistress, they are no Warren-born group but Kaha'an's old assassins' guild—one of the few powers that knew the Arol Rhutan before the Rending, having long used its empty passages to train recruits in the dark. When the refugees flooded down, the Long Knives already held the deep tunnels. Rather than be displaced, they extended their grip into a role of protection and order over the new population, and have become the closest thing the Warrens has to a government. They extract tribute but genuinely provide protection and maintain order. Many residents view them as legitimate authority.

**The Slavers:** Universally hated but powerful. They kidnap vulnerable residents (especially from the Depths) and sell them to surface buyers or keep them for forced labor. Led by Ronas Dermir, the Slaver King—who welded the old surface slaver gangs into a single cartel in the years after the Rending (see *The Slavers: From Gangs to Cartel*, above)—they control the Depths through terror. They persist because the Slaver cartel is itself one of Kaha'an's four ruling powers: the other cartels find its services too useful, and its reach too long, to move against it.

**The Well Mothers:** Control all major water sources. Led by Grandmother Shira, they distribute water based on need and protect vulnerable populations (especially children). Near-universally respected. Even other factions don't attack them—cutting off water would unite everyone against you.

**The Lantern Boys:** Professional guides who know the tunnels intimately. They maintain absolute neutrality, guiding anyone who pays. Essential infrastructure for a city where most people can't navigate beyond their immediate area. Attacking a Lantern Boy is taboo.

**Gate Clans:** Control surface access points, charging tolls and managing trade flow between surface and underground. Multiple competing clans specialize in different goods or control different entrances.

### **How Power Works**

**Territory:** Factions control specific chambers and passages. Residents in those areas pay tribute for protection (or out of fear). Different factions have different styles:

* Long Knives: \~10% of income, actual protection provided  
* Slavers: Variable extraction through fear  
* Well Mothers: Payment for water (but fair rates)

**Tribute and Protection:** Most residents pay multiple forms of tribute:

* To faction controlling their chamber (protection/tolerance)  
* To Well Mothers (for water)  
* To Gate Clans (if trading with surface)  
* To Lantern Boys (for guidance)

Those who cannot pay face eviction to worse locations, or worse.

**Violence:** The ultimate arbiter. Factions maintain power through demonstrated ability to use force. Occasional territorial wars erupt, with residents caught in crossfire.

## **The Ancient Tunnels**

### **What Are They?**

The tunnel network was built thousands of years ago by unknown builders. It consists of:

* Ventilation shafts (some with still-functioning mechanical fans)  
* Water access tunnels (with pumping stations still drawing from aquifers)  
* Maintenance corridors  
* Emergency escape routes  
* Carved chambers at intervals throughout the network

Everything is built to proportions that feel slightly wrong for humans—slightly too tall, slightly too narrow. The walls are perfectly smooth, harder than any known material, impossible to modify or damage with available tools.

### **What Was Their Purpose?**

No one knows. The sophistication of the system suggests it served something significant, but what? The tunnels seem designed as infrastructure supporting something below, but the deepest passages lead only to confusion and danger.

Residents call the creators "the builders" and marvel at their engineering, but know nothing else about them.

### **Legends and Myths**

Some surface scholars speak of "Hauren," a mythical lost city of elves supposedly somewhere underground, somewhere in the region. Like Atlantis or El Dorado—probably legend, possibly metaphor, maybe (maybe) real?

Most Warren residents have never heard the name. They just know the tunnels are ancient, impossibly well-built, and that some systems still work despite millennia of abandonment.

### **The Deep Mystery**

The deepest tunnels (called Dead Man's Passage) form a lethal labyrinth. It's not monsters that kill—it's the maze itself. Passages wind, loop back, and dead-end unpredictably. In complete darkness, getting lost is inevitable and fatal.

Somewhere deep in that maze lies a sealed brass door. Few have seen it. No one knows what's beyond it. No one knows how to open it.

The Long Knives guard the route to this door and keep its location secret. Whether it leads to something significant or is just an ancient sealed vault, no one knows.

## **Why Don't They Just Leave?**

With 60,000 people living in darkness and poverty, why don't they simply leave the Warrens?

### **Surface Kaha'an Won't Accept Them**

The city officially denies the Warrens exists. If 60,000 people suddenly demanded housing, food, and services, the government would face the crisis they've been ignoring for twenty years. They have no plan, no resources, and no political will to address this.

Surface authorities would likely respond with force—driving people back underground or out into the desert.

### **The Desert Makes Migration Impossible**

The Rending transformed the surrounding lands into harsh desert. Traveling to other settlements requires weeks of dangerous desert crossing. Residents lack resources for such journeys:

* No money for supplies  
* No camels or desert-adapted animals  
* No survival skills for desert travel  
* No certainty other settlements would accept them

Attempting to walk out is likely death.

### **Many Were Born Here**

A generation has grown up underground. Thousands of young adults have never seen the surface, never felt sunlight, never known any other life. Where would they go? How would they adapt?

The surface world is alien to them now.

### **No Resources to Relocate**

Residents are desperately poor. They lack:

* Money for travel or relocation  
* Skills valuable in legitimate economy  
* Social connections outside the Warrens  
* Legal identity (many have no papers)

Starting over somewhere else requires resources they don't have.

### **Faction Control**

For some, leaving would mean abandoning faction protection or breaking obligations. Others are hiding from surface authorities. Some are trapped by debt or contracts.

And for a few—the faction leaders, successful merchants, established residents—the Warrens is actually preferable to being poor on the surface. Better to be powerful underground than powerless above.

### **Adaptation and Acceptance**

After twenty years, many have simply adapted. The Warrens is horrible, but it's *their* horrible. They know how to survive here. They have community, identity, and purpose (even if that purpose is just survival).

Leaving would mean abandoning everything they've built from nothing.

## **The Future**

### **Status Quo (Most Likely)**

The Warrens continues as:

* Underground slum for Kaha'an's poorest  
* Ignored by imperial government  
* Controlled by factions  
* Gradually expanding as population grows  
* Slowly deteriorating conditions

This is sustainable in the short term but not indefinitely. Eventually something will break—population growth, resource depletion, major disaster, or political change.

### **Integration (Optimistic)**

If Kaha'an's government changes or power structure evolves:

* Official recognition of Warren residents as citizens  
* Infrastructure investment (improving water, ventilation, lighting)  
* Legal framework for governance  
* Integration with surface economy  
* Gradual improvement in conditions

This would require admitting twenty years of neglect, spending enormous resources, and acknowledging nearly 60,000 people living in tunnels beneath the capital. Politically difficult but possible.

### **Destruction (Pessimistic)**

If surface authorities decide to act:

* Military operation to "clear" the Warrens  
* Forced relocation of residents  
* Sealing tunnel entrances  
* Potentially catastrophic humanitarian crisis

But where would 60,000 people go? This would likely trigger a refugee crisis worse than the original problem. Violence and chaos would be inevitable.

### **Discovery (Unknown)**

If whatever lies beyond the brass door is revealed:

* Ancient technology becomes accessible  
* Secrets of the builders discovered  
* Fundamentally changes everything about the Warrens and Kaha'an

What's down there could help or harm. No one knows.

### **Expansion (Concerning)**

If Warren population continues growing:

* Exploration of deeper, more dangerous tunnels  
* Discovery of new sections  
* Potential unintentional contact with whatever is sealed below  
* Unknown consequences

The Warrens is pushing deeper into the tunnel network. What they might find—or awaken—is uncertain.

## **Summary**

The Warrens is a city of 60,000 people living in ancient tunnels beneath Kaha'an. Born from the refugee crisis following the Rending, it has evolved from desperate emergency shelter into permanent underground settlement with its own economy, power structures, and culture.

It exists in darkness, poverty, and danger—yet people endure, adapt, and even find meaning in their struggle. A generation has now been born knowing only the tunnels, creating a distinct society separate from the surface world that ignores them.

The Warrens is:

* A failure of governance (60,000 people officially don't exist)  
* A triumph of human resilience (building community in impossible conditions)  
* A powder keg of political tension (how long can this status quo hold?)  
* A mystery (what lies in the depths below?)

Twenty years old and still growing, the Warrens represents both the worst of human society and the best of human spirit. In the bones of an ancient city, in passages meant for purposes now forgotten, thousands endure in darkness.

And they call themselves Warren rats with pride.
