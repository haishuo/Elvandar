# **The Warrens: Geography and Physical Layout**

## **Overview**

The Warrens is an extensive network of ancient tunnels beneath Kaha'an, now inhabited by approximately 60,000 refugees. The tunnel system was built thousands of years ago as infrastructure—ventilation shafts, water access tunnels, maintenance corridors, and emergency escape routes serving some unknown purpose far below.

**Total Population:** Approximately 60,000 residents  
 **Surface Warrens:** 8,000-10,000 (ruins, entry points)  
 **Underground Warrens:** 50,000-52,000 (tunnel network)

---

## **Geographic Position**

### **Location Within Kaha'an**

**Surface Presence (The Surface Warrens):**

* Located in the northern district of Kaha'an, furthest from the harbor  
* The old imperial slums that became catastrophically overcrowded after the Rending  
* A sprawling area of collapsed tenements, improvised scaffolding, and makeshift shelters  
* Dozens of tunnel entrances scattered throughout the ruins  
* Covers approximately 2-3 square kilometers of surface area

**Underground Extent:** The tunnel network extends beneath much of Kaha'an:

* Most populated chambers lie directly beneath the northern district  
* Some tunnels extend beneath the Imperial Quarter (though few know this)  
* Scattered access shafts surface in abandoned buildings throughout the city  
* The deepest sections lie beneath the oldest part of Kaha'an  
* Total tunnel network spans an estimated 15-20 square kilometers across multiple depths  
* The full extent is unknown; some passages remain unexplored  
  ---

  ## **Physical Structure**

  ### **The Tunnel Network**

The tunnel system consists of passages at multiple depths connecting carved chambers. Tunnels vary widely in size and purpose:

**Major Ventilation Shafts:**

* Diameter: 3-5 meters  
* Run vertically or at steep angles  
* Some contain slowly-turning mechanical fans (still functional after millennia)  
* Create air circulation throughout the network  
* These are the "highways" of the tunnel system  
* Most contested and valuable passages

**Water Access Tunnels:**

* Width: 2-3 meters  
* Run horizontally toward underground aquifers  
* Contain ancient pumping stations (still functional, mechanism unknown)  
* Critical infrastructure for water supply  
* Connect to six major water stations throughout the network

**Maintenance Corridors:**

* Width: 1.5-2.5 meters  
* Most common type of passage  
* Narrow, winding tunnels connecting everything  
* Built to proportions that feel slightly wrong for humans (too tall, too narrow)  
* The primary navigation routes between chambers

**Emergency Escape Routes:**

* Width: 4-6 meters  
* Designed for mass movement  
* Clearest, most direct routes between depths  
* Wide enough for multiple people to pass simultaneously  
* Most structurally sound passages

**Dead Man's Passage:**

* The deepest section of tunnels (-500+ meters and below)  
* A confusing labyrinth where passages wind, loop back, and dead-end unpredictably  
* Many sections are flooded with dark water  
* Structural instability common  
* Toxic gas seepage in certain areas  
* Without a map, entering is effectively a death sentence

  ### **Architectural Features**

**Wall Construction:**

* Perfectly smooth surfaces (impossible for humans to replicate)  
* Unknown building material (harder than any known stone)  
* No visible tool marks or construction seams  
* Walls meet at precise angles

**Symbols and Markers:**

* Incomprehensible symbols cover walls at regular intervals  
* Likely navigational aids in an unknown language  
* Geometric patterns and mathematical notations  
* Some residents have learned practical associations ("this symbol usually means water nearby")  
* No one understands their actual meaning

**Lighting Systems:**

* Faint blue ambient glow in some areas (still-functioning ancient systems)  
* More common in deeper, less-inhabited passages  
* No visible light source—walls themselves seem to glow  
* No one knows how to control or maintain these systems  
* Creates eerie, cold illumination

**Fixtures and Controls:**

* Brass fixtures at intervals (purpose unknown)  
* Control panels with buttons and levers (residents avoid touching them)  
* Conduits running through walls (carrying what, no one knows)  
* Occasional humming or vibration from walls  
* Everything built to slightly wrong proportions

  ## **The Carved Chambers**

Large chambers carved from solid rock serve as the primary living spaces. These range from small alcoves to massive halls. Residents simply call them "chambers" \- they have no idea what the original builders intended them for.

### **Size Classification**

**Major Chambers:**

* Diameter: 40-60 meters  
* Ceiling height: 6-10 meters  
* Population capacity: 300-500 residents each  
* Multiple tunnel connections: 5-8 passages at different heights  
* Usually have functioning ancient systems (water, ventilation, sometimes lighting)  
* Found primarily in upper levels and key Midlevel locations  
* Estimated number in Warren territory: 15-20

**Medium Chambers:**

* Diameter: 20-35 meters  
* Ceiling height: 4-6 meters  
* Population capacity: 80-150 residents each  
* Tunnel connections: 2-4 passages  
* Most common type throughout the network  
* Mixed functionality (residential, workshops, storage)  
* Estimated number: 150-200

**Small Chambers:**

* Diameter: 8-15 meters  
* Ceiling height: 3-4 meters  
* Population capacity: 15-40 residents each  
* Often dead-ends or minor junction points  
* Single-family or small group occupancy  
* Minimal infrastructure  
* Estimated number: 300-400

  ### **Chamber Features**

**Ceiling Structure:**

* High ceilings allow vertical construction (residents build multi-level platforms)  
* Some chambers have domed ceilings  
* Perfect acoustic properties in certain chambers (sound carries strangely)  
* No visible support structures—chambers seem impossibly stable

**Ventilation Connections:**

* Most chambers connect to the main ventilation network  
* Openings at various heights around chamber walls  
* Fresh air flows through connected passages  
* Air quality varies by proximity to active ventilation shafts

**Water Systems:**

* Some chambers have functioning water taps  
* Water flows from brass fixtures in walls  
* Six major chambers serve as primary water distribution points  
* Ancient purification systems still function (blue-lit chambers deeper in the network)

**Multiple Entrances:**

* Tunnel openings at different points around chamber perimeter  
* Some openings at different heights (upper and lower levels)  
* Makes chambers natural gathering places and marketplaces  
* Easy to defend (multiple escape routes)

  ### **Human Habitation**

Residents cannot easily modify the ancient walls, so they build *inside* the chambers:

**Construction Methods:**

* Wooden structures salvaged from surface refuse  
* Fabric partitions creating "rooms" and privacy  
* Multi-level platforms utilizing vertical space (2-3 levels in major chambers)  
* Rope and scrap metal holds everything together  
* Small shops and stalls around chamber perimeters  
* Central communal areas left open

**Structural Gap:** There's usually a gap between human construction and the ancient surfaces. The ramshackle wooden platforms don't touch the perfectly smooth walls, creating a visual contrast between desperate human habitation and alien perfection.

## **Vertical Stratification**

The tunnel network operates on clear vertical hierarchy based on depth:

### **The Surface Warrens (Ground Level)**

**Depth:** Surface to \-20 meters  
 **Area:** \~2-3 square kilometers  
 **Population:** 8,000-10,000

**Physical Description:**

* Ruins of old Kaha'an's northern slums  
* Collapsed tenements from pre-Rending era  
* Improvised scaffolding connecting ruined buildings  
* Makeshift shelters built into and atop ruins  
* Dozens of tunnel entrances scattered throughout  
* Some entrances are obvious (large openings), others hidden (under collapsed buildings)

**Natural Light:**

* Full sunlight exposure  
* Exposed to weather (sandstorms, rare rain, desert heat)  
* Temperature extremes: 110°F+ (43°C) in summer, 40-50°F (4-10°C) in winter nights

**Entrances to Underground:**

* Major entrances: 8-12 large tunnel mouths (3-5 meters wide)  
* Minor entrances: 30-40 smaller openings (1-2 meters wide)  
* Hidden entrances: Unknown number in collapsed basements and ruins  
* Some entrances have been deliberately concealed or secured

  ### **The Tiers (Upper Chambers)**

**Depth:** \-20 to \-100 meters  
 **Population:** 15,000-18,000  
 **Density:** 10-15 major chambers, 40-60 medium, 80-100 small

**Physical Characteristics:**

* Closest to surface with easiest access  
* Larger chambers (the builders created bigger spaces near surface)  
* Better air quality from proximity to surface ventilation  
* Some natural light penetrates from surface (first 20-30 meters)  
* Passages tend to be wider and more numerous

**Temperature:**

* Summer: 70-80°F (21-27°C) when surface reaches 110°F+  
* Winter: 50-60°F (10-16°C)  
* Temperature fluctuates with surface conditions  
* Noticeably cooler than surface in summer (why people moved underground)

**Access:**

* Relatively easy navigation to/from surface  
* Well-traveled routes between major chambers  
* Multiple connections to surface exits  
* Shortest travel time to proper Kaha'an

  ### **The Midlevels**

**Depth:** \-100 to \-300 meters  
 **Population:** 25,000-30,000  
 **Density:** 5-8 major chambers, 80-120 medium, 150-200 small

**Physical Characteristics:**

* Where the majority of residents live  
* Mix of residential and commercial chambers  
* Air quality depends heavily on proximity to ventilation shafts  
* No natural light—complete darkness except for resident lighting  
* Passages more irregular and confusing than upper levels

**Temperature:**

* Relatively stable year-round: 65-70°F (18-21°C)  
* Minimal seasonal variation  
* Most comfortable temperature range  
* Always feels slightly damp and cool

**Navigation:**

* More complex three-dimensional maze  
* Requires guides for unfamiliar routes  
* Easy to get lost beyond immediate area  
* Travel between distant chambers can take hours

**Water Access:**

* Four major water stations in the Midlevels  
* Most residents travel significant distances for water  
* Some chambers have functioning taps, many don't

  ### **The Depths**

**Depth:** \-300 to \-500 meters  
 **Population:** 8,000-10,000  
 **Density:** 2-3 major chambers (strongholds), 20-30 medium, 40-60 small

**Physical Characteristics:**

* Fewer and smaller chambers  
* More isolated and difficult to reach  
* Passages narrower and more unstable  
* Some sections show structural damage  
* Strange ancient technology more active here (humming walls, warm conduits)

**Temperature:**

* Progressively warmer approaching geothermal sources  
* Range: 80-95°F (27-35°C)  
* Near functioning power conduits: 95-105°F+ (35-40°C)  
* Oppressive atmosphere combined with poor air quality

**Hazards:**

* Structural instability common  
* Toxic gas seepage in certain areas  
* Flooding risk (water seeks lowest point)  
* Some passages too hot to traverse safely  
* Ancient systems behave unpredictably

  ### **Dead Man's Passage**

**Depth:** \-500+ meters  
 **Population:** 0 (no permanent residents)

**What it is:** **natural caverns — not Lathionese construction, and not a designed maze.** Water, faulting and mineral precipitation made this network long before anybody built anything above it. A cut Lathionese freight road once descended through it to the Brass Door; that road was deliberately collapsed when the city sealed itself two thousand years ago, and what survives is the raw geology it had been driven through. Every lethal quality below is geological, not architectural. See `Places/Kaha'an/Lathion_ Physical Layout and Geography.md`.

**Physical Description:**

* Deep natural cavern network descending below the engineered service tunnels  
* Three-dimensional maze where passages wind, loop, and dead-end  
* Some sections flooded with dark water (depth unknown)  
* Complete darkness (no functioning lighting systems)  
* Air grows warmer and thinner with depth

**Why It's Deadly:**

* Passages branch at odd angles (sometimes 3-4 directions)  
* Routes that descend suddenly curve back upward  
* No sense of direction or time in perfect darkness  
* Flooded sections where tunnel continuation is unclear  
* Even attempting to backtrack, can anyone remember every turn?  
* Structural instability and rockfall risk  
* Toxic gases pool in low areas

**Navigation:**

* Requires perfect map and careful navigation  
* Takes a full day to traverse even with guidance  
* Only one known safe route through (closely guarded secret) — found by the Long Knives at great cost, **not** a rediscovered Lathionese path and not marked on the walls  
* Without map, survival time measured in days (dehydration, getting lost)

**The Brass Door:** At the end of one specific route through Dead Man's Passage lies a small antechamber:

**Chamber Features:**

* First true constructed space since the caves began (Lathionese construction resumes here)  
* Perfectly preserved despite millennia  
* Faint blue lighting (functioning ancient systems)  
* Temperature: 68°F (20°C) — a **sharp drop** from the cave heat behind you, and cold to anyone raised in Kaha'an. The climate system treats this chamber as Lathion proper.  
* Deep rhythmic sound from beyond door (like breathing or machinery)  
* Perfect silence except for that sound

**The Door Itself:**

* Massive: 5 meters tall, 3 meters wide  
* Made of brass or brass-like metal (perfectly preserved)  
* Covered in intricate script and geometric patterns  
* No visible lock, handle, or opening mechanism  
* Feels significant—weight of presence  
* Seals something beyond (what, no one knows)

  ## **Environmental Conditions**

  ### **Air Quality**

**Near Active Ventilation Shafts:**

* Relatively fresh air  
* Slight breeze from ancient fans  
* Smells of surface (dust, heat) mixing with underground (stone, damp)  
* Breathable without difficulty  
* Premium real estate value

**Mid-Level Tunnels:**

* Stale but breathable air  
* Humid from condensation  
* Smells of bodies, cooking, waste  
* Where most residents live  
* Extended exertion causes shortness of breath

**Dead-End Sections:**

* Dangerously poor air quality  
* High carbon dioxide from breathing  
* Smoke accumulation from cooking fires  
* Causes headaches and dizziness  
* Only the desperate live here

**Deep Tunnels:**

* Toxic gases occasionally seep up  
* Warm air rising creates strange convection patterns  
* Disorientation and confusion common  
* Lethal without protection or knowledge of safe routes

**Ventilation System:**

* Ancient mechanical fans in major shafts still turn  
* No visible power source—they simply work  
* Air circulation pattern too complex to fully understand  
* Some fans work, others don't—no pattern discernible  
* Residents recognize "good air" areas instinctively

  ### **Temperature Distribution**

**Upper Tunnels (The Tiers):**

* Summer: 70-80°F (21-27°C)  
* Winter: 50-60°F (10-16°C)  
* Temperature fluctuates with surface conditions  
* Thermal gradient visible—warmer near surface exits

**Mid-Level Tunnels:**

* Year-round: 65-70°F (18-21°C)  
* Most stable temperature zone  
* Minimal seasonal variation  
* Comfortable for habitation

**Deep Tunnels (The Depths):**

* Range: 80-95°F (27-35°C)  
* Near power conduits: 95-105°F+ (35-40°C)  
* Progressively warmer approaching geothermal sources  
* Oppressive when combined with poor air quality

**Dead Man's Passage:**

* Starts at \~85°F (29°C)  
* Gets warmer with depth  
* Brass door chamber: \~80-85°F (27-29°C)  
* Below brass door: Unknown

  ### **Water Systems**

**Ancient Pumping Stations:**

* Six major stations throughout the network  
* Draw water from underground aquifers  
* Still functional after millennia  
* Mechanism completely unknown  
* Water quality varies by station (some purification systems work better than others)  
* Capacity sufficient for 60,000+ people if properly rationed

**Pumping Station Locations:**

* One in the Tiers (-60 meters)  
* Four in the Midlevels (-150 to \-250 meters)  
* One in the Depths (-380 meters)

**Water Flow:**

* Some chambers have functioning taps  
* Water pressure varies (some stations stronger than others)  
* Blue-lit chambers deeper in network seem to purify water  
* Pipes run through walls (visible as slight bulges or different textures)

**Natural Seepage:**

* Small underground streams in some tunnels  
* Unpredictable and unreliable  
* Often contaminated with minerals or surface runoff  
* Pools collect in low areas

**Flooding:**

* Rare heavy rains cause flooding  
* Water seeks lowest point through network  
* Lower tunnels and chambers can flood rapidly  
* Dead Man's Passage becomes completely impassable  
* Floodwater can rise 1-2 meters in minutes  
* Takes days to weeks to drain completely

  ### **Lighting**

**Natural Light:**

* Only near surface exits  
* Penetrates 20-30 meters down major shafts  
* Gone entirely by mid-morning in most places  
* Upper Tiers get indirect sunlight in well-positioned chambers

**Ancient Lighting Systems:**

* Faint blue ambient glow in some passages  
* Walls themselves seem to emit light  
* No visible light source  
* More common in deeper, less-inhabited areas  
* Cold, eerie illumination  
* Cannot be controlled or turned off/on by residents

**Darkness:**

* Most of the tunnel network is in near-total darkness  
* This is the default state  
* Residents provide their own light or navigate without it

  ## **Major Locations**

  ### **The Crossroads**

**Type:** Large junction chamber  
 **Depth:** \-150 meters (Midlevels)  
 **Size:** Roughly circular, 45 meters diameter, 6-meter ceiling

**Physical Structure:**

* Six significant tunnels meet at different levels  
* Two tunnels enter at floor level  
* Three tunnels enter 2-3 meters up the wall  
* One tunnel enters near ceiling level  
* Ancient brass walls show faint technical diagrams and symbols  
* Unusually good air circulation from multiple ventilation connections

**Chamber Features:**

* Three levels of wooden platforms built by residents  
* Ramps and ladders connecting levels  
* Central plaza kept clear (15 meters diameter)  
* Tunnel openings at different heights create interesting sight lines  
* Echo effects from chamber shape

**Acoustics:**

* Sound carries well throughout the space  
* Multiple conversations create background hum  
* Louder than most chambers

  ### **The Well of Whispers**

**Type:** Ancient water monitoring station  
 **Depth:** \-200 meters (Midlevels)  
 **Size:** Multi-level chamber, 30 meters diameter with central shaft

**Physical Structure:**

* Central shaft extends both upward and downward  
* Chamber surrounds the shaft on multiple levels  
* Ancient catwalks at three different heights  
* The shaft itself is a water distribution node  
* Multiple taps at different levels

**The Shaft:**

* Diameter: 3 meters  
* Extends up \~50 meters (to \-150m depth)  
* Extends down \~100 meters (to \-300m depth)  
* Visible water flow through transparent sections  
* Faint blue lighting from active purification systems below

**Unique Feature (Acoustics):**

* Sound carries strangely throughout the chamber  
* Echoes and whispers seem to come from nowhere  
* Speaking at one point can be heard clearly across the chamber  
* Private conversation is impossible  
* Water sounds create constant background noise

**Temperature:**

* Noticeably cooler than surrounding tunnels  
* 60-65°F (16-18°C)  
* Mist sometimes rises from the shaft

  ### **The Breach**

**Type:** Catastrophic collapse  
 **Depth:** Cuts through multiple levels (-100 to \-400 meters)  
 **Size:** Irregular vertical shaft, 15-30 meters across at various points

**Physical Description:**

* Massive vertical shaft from structural failure  
* Exposed construction techniques visible in walls  
* Layers of different materials revealed  
* Conduits and structural elements never meant to be seen  
* Walls rough and unstable (unlike smooth ancient surfaces)

**The Collapse:**

* Occurred during the Rending (twenty years ago)  
* Tore through multiple chamber levels  
* Some chambers partially destroyed (half-chambers opening onto void)  
* Rubble piles at various levels  
* Some sections still crumbling

**Depth Profile:**

* Top: \-100 meters (The Tiers)  
* Bottom: \-400 meters (The Depths)  
* Total drop: 300 meters  
* No one has survived falling into the Breach

**Bridges:**

* 3-5 makeshift bridges at different heights  
* Constructed from salvaged wood and rope  
* Rickety and poorly maintained  
* Swing and sway when crossed  
* Some have partial collapses

**Environmental Effects:**

* Mist rises from depths below  
* Carries warmth and strange smells  
* Steam from geothermal sources  
* Strange sounds echo up (machinery? breathing? both?)  
* Air currents create unusual wind patterns

  ### **The Arena**

**Type:** Large circular chamber repurposed for blood sport  
 **Depth:** \-180 meters (Midlevels)  
 **Size:** Roughly circular, 50 meters diameter, unusually high ceiling (10 meters)

**Physical Structure:**

* One of the largest chambers in the network  
* Originally some kind of distribution hub or depot (residents speculate)  
* Perfect circular shape  
* Open floor plan  
* Multiple tunnel entrances around perimeter (6-7 openings)

**Current Modifications:**

* Crude wooden platforms built in tiers around perimeter (3-4 levels)  
* Seating capacity: 500-800 people  
* Central space serves as fighting pit (20 meters diameter)  
* Sand covers ancient flooring (poorly)  
* Ancient brass walls visible above platforms

**Ceiling:**

* 10 meters high (unusually tall for a chamber)  
* Some ancient fixtures still visible  
* Good acoustics for crowd noise  
* Ventilation openings near top

**Lighting:**

* Torches in sconces around walls  
* Smoky atmosphere from torches and cooking fires  
* No ancient lighting systems functioning

**Temperature:**

* Warmer than surrounding passages from body heat and torches  
* 75-80°F (24-27°C) during events

  ### **The Sanctuary**

**Type:** Former imperial temple, now underground refuge  
 **Depth:** \-20 meters (partially above ground)  
 **Size:** Temple complex 40x60 meters above ground, lower chamber 35 meters diameter

**Surface Structure (The Temple):**

* Built centuries ago at edge of old city  
* Stone construction (rare in the Warrens)  
* Partially collapsed but main structure intact  
* Bell tower still standing (visible landmark)  
* Main hall, side chambers, and prayer rooms

**Underground Connection:**

* Temple crypts connect to a large chamber below  
* Original ancient chamber repurposed as temple space  
* Stone stairs descend from temple to chamber  
* Multiple tunnel entrances in chamber connect to network

**The Chamber Below:**

* 35 meters diameter, 7 meters ceiling  
* Better preserved than most (protected by temple above)  
* Functioning water tap (ancient system)  
* Good ventilation from multiple connections  
* Divided into sections by fabric walls

**Transition Space:**

* Acts as literal threshold between surface and underground  
* Natural light filters down from temple above  
* Temperature gradient—cooler below, warmer near surface  
* Acoustic transition—temple echoes meet tunnel sounds

  ### **Dead Man's Passage (Region)**

**Type:** Deep tunnel labyrinth  
 **Depth:** \-500+ meters  
 **Size:** Extensive network spanning several kilometers

**Physical Characteristics:**

* Not a single location but a region  
* Three-dimensional maze of passages  
* Passages wind, loop back, branch unpredictably  
* Dead ends without warning  
* Some sections flooded (dark water, depth unknown)  
* Complete darkness (no functioning lighting)

**Tunnel Features:**

* Narrower than upper passages (1-2 meters wide)  
* Lower ceilings (2-3 meters)  
* More irregular surfaces (less finished than upper tunnels)  
* Occasional ancient fixtures (purpose unknown)  
* Strange echoes and acoustic properties

**Environmental Hazards:**

* Structural instability (loose rock, unstable walls)  
* Toxic gas pockets (various types)  
* Flooded sections (can't see if tunnel continues underwater)  
* Temperature increases with depth  
* Air quality deteriorates rapidly  
* No ventilation circulation

**The True Path:**

* One specific route through the maze leads to the brass door  
* Requires following specific sequence of turns  
* Marked by subtle ancient symbols (if you know what to look for)  
* Takes full day of careful navigation even with map  
* Any deviation from path leads to being hopelessly lost

**The Brass Door Chamber:** Located at the end of the true path:

**Antechamber:**

* Small chamber, 8 meters diameter  
* Perfectly smooth walls (true ancient construction, not tunnels)  
* Faint blue lighting (still functioning)  
* Temperature: 80-85°F (27-29°C)  
* Ceiling: 4 meters high  
* Single entrance passage, single brass door opposite

**The Door:**

* 5 meters tall, 3 meters wide  
* Brass or brass-like metal (perfectly preserved)  
* Covered in intricate script (unknown language)  
* Geometric patterns and mathematical symbols  
* No visible lock, handle, or hinges  
* Appears to be solid piece of metal  
* Deep rhythmic sound from beyond (frequency: \~1 per 3 seconds)

**Chamber Atmosphere:**

* Perfect silence except for rhythmic sound  
* Sense of weight, of presence  
* Air slightly warmer than passage leading in  
* No dust, no debris—chamber is pristine  
* Feels like standing before something significant

  ## **Structural Integrity and Hazards**

  ### **Stable Sections**

**Ancient Construction:**

* Main tunnels and chambers are remarkably stable  
* No visible deterioration despite millennia  
* Unknown building materials exceed modern engineering  
* Designed to withstand enormous pressures  
* Original construction shows no signs of failure

**Most Reliable Routes:**

* Major ventilation shafts  
* Emergency escape routes  
* Water access tunnels  
* All designed with safety margins far exceeding apparent need

  ### **Unstable Sections**

**Human-Modified Areas:**

* Anywhere residents have tried to expand chambers  
* Sections where walls have been chipped or damaged  
* Around makeshift construction  
* Near heavy wooden platforms

**Natural Damage:**

* The Breach (collapse during the Rending)  
* Sections damaged by ancient seismic activity  
* Areas with water damage and erosion  
* Passages where roots have penetrated from surface

**Deep Sections:**

* Dead Man's Passage has inherent instability  
* Some passages show stress fractures  
* Evidence of ancient repairs (different materials visible)  
* As if something down there caused damage long ago

  ### **Cave-In Risk Indicators**

**Warning Signs:**

* Rumbling sounds from walls  
* Dust falling from ceiling  
* New cracks appearing in surfaces  
* Strange echoes (sound of structure shifting)  
* Sudden temperature changes  
* Changes in air flow patterns

**High-Risk Areas:**

* The Breach and immediately surrounding passages  
* Sections with visible cracks  
* Areas where ceiling shows stress  
* Passages with loose rock  
* Anywhere human construction has damaged walls

  ### **Flooding Patterns**

**Flood-Prone Areas:**

* All of Dead Man's Passage  
* Depths chambers below \-350 meters  
* Low-lying passages throughout network  
* Areas with poor drainage

**Flood Behavior:**

* Water enters from surface during heavy rains  
* Follows gravity through tunnel network  
* Seeks lowest points  
* Can rise 1-2 meters in lowest areas  
* Creates dangerous currents in passages  
* Takes days to drain completely

**Safe Areas:**

* Upper Tiers (above typical flood levels)  
* Chambers with elevated platforms  
* Passages with good drainage  
* Chambers with multiple exits to higher ground

  ### **Toxic Gas Zones**

**High-Risk Areas:**

* Dead Man's Passage (various pockets)  
* Certain sections of the Depths  
* Dead-end passages with poor ventilation  
* Areas near warm conduits

**Gas Types Encountered:**

* Carbon dioxide (heavy, settles in low areas)  
* Sulfur compounds (distinctive smell)  
* Unknown gases from ancient systems  
* Various combinations depending on location

**Recognition:**

* Rotten egg smell (sulfur)  
* No smell but sudden dizziness (carbon dioxide)  
* Unusual metallic taste (unknown compounds)  
* Dead rats or other animals (warning sign)  
* Ancient warning symbols (if you recognize them)

  ## **Navigation and Wayfinding**

  ### **Landmarks and Route-Finding**

**Named Routes:** Major passages acquire names based on distinguishing features:

* "The Hot Passage" (near geothermal vents)  
* "Echo Hall" (naturally amplifying acoustics)  
* "The Fan Tunnel" (ancient ventilation shaft with working fan)  
* "Rust Route" (old iron fixtures on walls)  
* "Blue Light Way" (section with functioning ancient lighting)  
* "The Squeeze" (particularly narrow passage)  
* "Water Way" (passage with stream running through it)

**Ancient Markers:**

* Symbols cover walls at regular intervals  
* Likely navigational aids in unknown language  
* Geometric patterns and mathematical notations  
* Some patterns repeat (possibly indicating distance or direction)  
* Residents have learned some practical associations without understanding meaning

**Natural Navigation:**

* Air currents indicate direction to ventilation shafts  
* Temperature gradients (cooler \= upward, warmer \= downward)  
* Sound of water indicates proximity to water tunnels  
* Slope of passage indicates general depth change  
* Humidity levels correlate with proximity to water sources

  ### **Distance and Travel Time**

**Within Same Level:**

* Adjacent chambers: 5-15 minutes  
* Same district: 30-60 minutes  
* Cross-district: 1-3 hours

**Vertical Movement:**

* One level up/down: 30-90 minutes  
* Surface to Tiers: 15-30 minutes  
* Tiers to Midlevels: 1-2 hours  
* Midlevels to Depths: 2-4 hours  
* Depths to brass door: 8-12 hours (with map and guidance)

**Factors Affecting Travel:**

* Lighting conditions (slower in darkness)  
* Passage conditions (obstacles, flooding)  
* Vertical climbing (slower than horizontal travel)  
* Navigation knowledge (faster with familiar routes)  
* Load carried (water, goods, etc.)

  ## **Spatial Characteristics**

  ### **Total Volume**

**Estimated Tunnel Network:**
