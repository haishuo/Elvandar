# The Noble Political Alignments of Post-Rending Kaha'an

## What this document covers, and what it does not

**This is noble politics.** It describes how the surviving houses aligned themselves after the Rending and
what each alignment believes it is doing. It is not a map of the city.

Four things operate on axes this document cannot measure, and each is noted in its own section below: the
**Tor'anar**, who reject the entire frame; the **Warrens**, which is not noble politics at all; the
**cartel bureaucracy**, whose clerks and magistrates hold real power without holding a house; and the
**population of Kaha'an**, most of whom have no stake in anybody's genealogy and considerable stake in the
price of water.

**Rebuilt 2026-08-01.** The previous version predated the settled account of how the Coterie formed
(`Offstage.md`, "The Forming of the Coterie") and described a conventional contested monarchy — loyalists
awaiting restoration, pragmatists backing the regime, conspirators backing a rival. **That framing does
not survive the legal architecture.** What follows is the same political landscape re-read through it.

**On the house names below:** they are documented in `Places/Kaha'an/houses_of_kahaan.md`, which is their
source; this file only records where each one stands. Do not invent new ones here.

## Historical Context: The Great Collapse

Before the Rending, Kaha'an was the heart of Balisha, successor to the original Balishan Empire. Multiple
noble houses controlled agricultural estates, managed trade networks, and maintained ancestral seats in
both Kaha'an and the old imperial capital of Vartonne.

The catastrophe destroyed that order in hours:
- Most agricultural estates became desert wasteland
- Noble families caught outside the city's protective wards perished
- Traditional sources of wealth and influence vanished overnight
- The emperor and empress died, leaving no clear succession
- Refugees flooded the protected city, creating chaos and opportunity

The surviving houses faced a stark choice: adapt to the new reality or fade into irrelevance. What none of
them could do was contest the succession, because within a few years the succession had been placed beyond
contesting.

## The question everyone is actually arguing about

**It is not whether the Valanars are legitimate.** Legally they are, and the Coterie has spent twenty years
writing that answer onto every decree it issues. The Coterie is **officially loyalist**. It does not claim
the dynasty was replaced; it claims to govern in the dynasty's name until the dynasty returns, on the
strength of the ruling that the Valanars are **missing rather than dead**.

So a faction cannot be defined by believing House Valanar is the rightful dynasty. **Everyone in
respectable Kaha'ani politics believes that, or is at minimum required to behave as though they do.**

The actual disagreement is narrower and much sharper:

> **What does loyalty to an absent dynasty require of you while it remains absent?**

Three answers, and each is a genuine constitutional position rather than a disposition. Everybody speaks
the language of imperial legitimacy. **They mean radically different things by it.**

---

## 1. The Continuationists

**Position:** The Coterie is not merely an emergency caretaker. It is the proper and lawful expression of
Valanar authority during the interregnum, and disturbing it before a verified Valanar returns endangers
the very inheritance it is holding.

They would consider the accusation of being anti-imperial outrageous, and they would have an argument.
Their doctrine is **continuity**: the Valanars remain sovereign, the Coterie administers on their behalf,
and stability and preservation are themselves acts of loyalty.

**Rosik in particular insists he has spent twenty years preserving the Valanars' city for them, and the
irritating thing is that this is not wholly false.**

**Base**
- Cartel-integrated houses — **Valorian**, **Thessarian**, **Malthorn** (Grain); **Darfi**, **Corvallen**,
  **Thessian** (Water); **Issamil**, **Belgrand**, **Vorthak** (Iron)
- Administrators and magistrates whose authority derives from Coterie appointment
- Commercial beneficiaries of the cartel order
- Ordinary conservatives who do not love the monopolies and fear reopening the succession far more

**Internal divisions** — and they matter, because this is not one opinion:
- **Sincere custodians** who believe every word of the trusteeship
- **Profiteers** for whom the doctrine is a convenience
- **Gradualists** who want the system reformed rather than ended
- **Those who privately hope no Valanar ever returns**, and who will discover in Book 1 that they were the
  largest group

**Methods:** economic integration, administrative office, marriage networks, and the plain fact that the
city's daily operation depends on their cooperation.

*Do not call these people pragmatists. Pragmatism is their temperament. Continuity is their doctrine.*

---

## 2. The Restorationists

**Position:** The Coterie's authority is derivative and conditional. It was granted to preserve, not to
transform — and it has quietly converted temporary guardianship into permanent oligarchy.

Their critique is dangerous precisely because it uses the Coterie's own premise:

> **If you truly govern only until the family returns, why have you built a system that cannot survive
> their return?**

They point at property confiscated after the Rending, at hereditary offices, at monopolies, at old law
allowed to lapse — and argue these are abuses of a trust, not the exercise of a mandate.

**Base**
- Dispossessed old houses — **Vex** (informal coordination), **Altamiris** (former court musicians turned
  information brokers), **Lady Cordelia Ashworth** (widowed diplomat, contact with foreign powers)
- **Thessalar**, former imperial archivists, who maintain the genealogical records
- **Valoreth**, descended from the old palace guard families
- Minor merchant families who remember when trade required imperial licenses
- The **Vartonne Connection** — relatives of Kaha'ani nobles who fled to the old capital
- An underground of servants, clerks and minor officials serving cartel masters with quiet sympathies

**Internal divisions**
- **Principled constitutionalists**, who mean it
- **Nostalgic reactionaries**, who romanticise an empire whose costs they never paid
- **Property claimants**, dressing restitution as doctrine
- **Active searchers**, who chase rumours, correspond with surviving branches and investigate genealogy
- **Passive preservers**, who keep the regalia, the records and the protocols and wait

**They are not uniformly passive and should not be written that way.** Some obstruct measures they regard
as irreversible damage to the imperial inheritance. Some pursue every rumour of a survivor. That the
Loyalist underground has spent twenty years maintaining records proving a princess's legitimacy is not
sentiment — **it is the one act that turns out to matter.**

**They do not have unquestioned moral authority and must never be written as though they do.** They may
hold the strongest constitutional argument, particularly after Elara appears. Their motives are mixed,
their nostalgia is selective, and a good number of them want their estates back. **The cause can be valid
while the motives are not pure.**

## 3. The Erulian Claimants

**Position:** The interregnum has to end. Stewardship does not become permanent government merely by
refusing to declare the obvious, and Kalden Erulius is the nearest credible blood claimant.

*The previous version called them Secessionists, which was wrong — they are not trying to secede Kaha'an
from anything. They are trying to resolve a suspended succession in a particular direction.*

**The sophisticated version of their case concedes a great deal**, which is what makes it dangerous: the
original emergency arrangement was lawful and necessary, and the men who made it were not villains. It
simply cannot be indefinite. Twenty years is not an emergency. **A living heir of collateral blood is
preferable to permanent government by commodity monopolies.**

**Kalden's supporters do not think they are traitors.** They think they are the only people serious enough
to finish a succession crisis everyone else has profited from postponing.

**Base**
- **House Morthen** — themselves distant Valanar cousins, backing Erulian claims rather than advancing
  their own
- **Master Garrett Caldris**, disgraced former cartel administrator
- **The Circle of Seven**, anonymous nobles coordinating through coded correspondence
- Families with Erulian connections and relatives who fled there after the Rending
- Displaced nobles excluded from cartel wealth; merchants crushed by the monopolies; ideological
  dissidents; residents with ties to other successor states

**Note what that base is made of.** Every one of those is a real grievance and a reliable recruit, and
**none of them can deliver an institutional ruling**, because the ruling would have to be issued by the
people who won. Twenty years on, it is still a fringe.

**Program**
- Build Kalden's legitimacy and cultivate Kaha'ani clients
- Delegitimise indefinite stewardship
- Present Erulius as the ready-made successor state
- **Turn the eventual succession debate into the ratification of an accomplished fact**

### Kalden's building programme is the physical half of this faction

The Hall of Ancestors, the Amber Palace, the new coinage, the Imperial Chancellery and the Aspirant Guard
are not decoration and not vanity. **They are evidence manufactured in advance**, and they belong in this
document because they are aimed at a decision that would be taken in this city.

- **The Hall of Ancestors** says Kalden belongs to history
- **The coinage** says Erulius is already sovereign in practice
- **The Amber Palace** says the principality has a court capable of becoming an imperial court
- **The Aspirant Guard** says he is not merely guarded by soldiers but served by an institution of
  dynastic dignity
- **The improvements to Erulius** say the collateral heir has not merely inherited blood but demonstrated
  fitness

**The project, stated plainly: the construction of an alternative imperial centre.** He is trying to make
the succession *feel* settled before it is legally opened, so that when Kaha'an finally admits that
*missing* cannot mean *possibly returning forever*, only one candidate looks inevitable.

**It is patient, symbolic, architectural and politically intelligent**, and it is why his later ruin cuts
as deep as it does. He did not only lose money and prestige. **He watched the material proof of his
inevitability decay around him.**

### And the other half, which is not constitutional at all

Sabotage placement, emergency caches through the city, fifth-column assets inside cartel organisations,
economic disruption, and assassination contacts running to the Long Knives.

**He built both halves and the coercive one is further developed**, which is characteristic — and it is
why the pivot to force after Book 1 is so fast. When the legal route dies, nothing has to be built. See
`People/Kalden Erulius.md`.

## Outside the noble frame

### The Tor'anar

**Not a faction. A fourth constitutional position, and the only one that never had to be argued.**

The war-magic corps is sworn to Valanar blood specifically, holds that the Coterie has no jurisdiction over
it whatsoever, and **never consented to the caretaker fiction in the first place.** Their answer to the
question this document is organised around is that they do not recognise the question:

> *We do not accept that authority flows from noble coalition-building. We know whom we serve.*

Rosik tried in the early years to bring them to heel by leverage, debt and pressure, and could not, because
his toolkit requires that a person need something he controls. **They are living proof the Coterie's
authority was never total** — and they give Elara's return immediate practical force, because she does not
have to build an imperial military organ from nothing. One has been standing outside Coterie jurisdiction
for twenty years, waiting to be asked. Full account:
`Places/Kaha'an/The Tor'anar_ Balisha's War-Magic Corps.md`.

### The Warrens

**Not noble politics, and the Slavers do not exhaust it.** The Warrens contain their own factions, gangs,
intermediaries and arrangements, over which Ronas Dermir exercises coercive dominance without holding all
of it.

**Ronas's seat was not extorted under threat of war and this document previously said otherwise.** He
consolidated the independent gangs, demanded a seat, was refused, and **withdrew slave labour from the
surface for two months** — the deep scavenging, the corpse retrieval, the tunnel maintenance. The surface
economy seized. The Slavers absorbed the loss without breaking. The Coterie blinked. See
`People/Ronas Dermir.md` and `Offstage.md`.

**He did not threaten conventional war. He demonstrated dependency**, and the distinction is the whole of
his political intelligence: the surface treats the Warrens as disposable while relying on the work it
refuses to look at, and **his strike made invisible labour visible by removing it.**

**Nor is it true that he has no political infrastructure and only leverage.** He has no *noble* 
infrastructure. He has a consolidated cartel, labour control, tunnel knowledge, territorial authority,
client relationships, arrangements with gangs, a reputation for enforcing bargains, and twenty years of
institutional presence at the Coterie. **That is politics. It is simply not aristocratic politics.**

**Do not write authorially that he took his seat rather than earning it.** The other three say it
constantly and will never stop; objectively he earned it by proving the city cannot function without the
population and the labour system he represents, which is a stronger claim to material relevance than most
hereditary houses can make.

**The irony to keep:** every noble faction finds him vulgar, and he is the only member of the Coterie whose
seat was won by a successful collective action from below — which he then converted into personal
domination.

### The cartel bureaucracy, and the city

The clerks, magistrates, inspectors and functionaries who actually administer Kaha'an hold power that no
house confers and no faction can quite direct. Most are Continuationist by employment rather than
conviction.

**And most of Kaha'an is not in this document at all.** Merchants outside noble houses, labourers,
refugees, artisans, cartel employees, former tenants of estates that no longer exist, and everybody born
after the Rending with no memory of an empire. **Many of them do not care who has the cleanest genealogy.**
They care about grain, water, work, safety, and the price of all four.

This produces a distinction worth holding: **the Restorationists have ceremonial legitimacy and the
Continuationists have lived legitimacy.** People have survived twenty years under the system, which is an
argument. Conversely, resentment of the monopolies can produce popular sympathy for Elara — or for Kalden —
with no aristocratic ideology behind it whatsoever.

## The balance of power

**No percentages.** The previous version gave 60/25/15 of "noble influence," which implied a precision
nothing supports and a single measure that does not exist. Houses, wealth, votes, access to officials,
retainers, prestige and the ability to move a crowd are different quantities, and different factions lead
on different ones.

Qualitatively:

- **Continuationists** control most functioning institutions and nearly all commercial administration
- **Restorationists** retain prestige, records, old networks and pockets of bureaucratic sympathy
- **Erulian Claimants** are small, covert, and concentrated among the dispossessed and the aggrieved
- **Ronas's power is orthogonal to all three** and cannot be measured on this axis at all

### Factional frictions

**Continuationist vs Restorationist** — the live one, and it is fought entirely in the language of
loyalty. Property seized after the Rending; hereditary office; the handling of imperial ceremony; whether
a trustee may reform what it holds in trust.

**Restorationist vs Erulian** — both invoke imperial legitimacy and mean different bloodlines by it. They
share an enemy, which makes tactical cooperation possible and permanent alliance impossible.

**Continuationist vs Erulian** — open hostility. The cartels run active counterintelligence against
Erulian sympathisers, and Erulian success would end the cartel order outright.

## What Elara's return actually does

**It does not strengthen one faction. It detonates the legal grammar under all three**, because every
faction has spent twenty years arguing about what an absent dynasty is owed, and the dynasty just walked
in.

**For the Continuationists** she is the principal whose authority they have claimed to exercise. **They
cannot reject her without confessing the caretaker story was a fraud** — and that confession voids twenty
years of decrees, which run through every contract and judgment in the city.

**For the Restorationists** she is vindication, and then a complication. Some imagine she will restore
their estates and their offices and discover that she is not a ceremonial vessel for their nostalgia but a
living ruler with her own judgment and a Caretaker's priorities. **Some will be elated by her return and
some will be appalled by the actual woman.** That is politics, and it should be written as politics.

**For the Erulian Claimants** she is catastrophic. She closes the exact question they needed opened.
**And she does something worse than beat Kalden's claim: she proves the Coterie's fiction was literally
true.** The missing Valanar was missing. She came back.

**The fracture in Kalden's faction is one of the major consequences of Book 1**, and it should run in
several directions at once — genuine conversion to Elara; denial of her identity; insistence that absence,
upbringing, marriage or foreign influence disqualifies her; the argument that Kalden has earned
prescriptive legitimacy through twenty years of preparation; the theory that Xion manufactured her; and,
for some, the abandonment of legal argument altogether in favour of force.

**For Ronas**, very little changes immediately. Even an empress needs the labour, and he knows it.

## The Role of Xion's Circle

**The circle is not a cross-factional coalition and the previous version was wrong to frame it as one.**
All four are heirs or affiliates of the cartel order. Their disagreements are internal to an emergent
ruling class, not a balanced sample of the city's politics. **What makes them powerful is that they are
inheritors of a system they do not yet understand they inhabit.**

**It is also not Xion's group. Silvanno Larannas started it** — he noticed where Xion was spending his
time when Xion was sixteen and asked him directly instead of telling anyone, and that conversation is the
group's origin. Kael came two years later, brought in by Silvanno. Farleen was embedded the year after
that. By Book 1 they have five years behind them. See `People/Silvanno Larannas.md`.

- **Xion Kemvimore** (Grain heir) — alienated from cartel domination, and **not a Restorationist.** Before
  Elara he has no position on the succession at all; his objection is to what the system does to people in
  the lower wards. His relationship to restoration arrives through her, and through a refusal to treat
  legitimacy as cartel property.
- **Silvanno Larannas** (Water heir) — the group's founder, and the one who concluded after five years
  that charity is futile and the system itself is the problem. His position is about Water, the Coterie and
  political order, not about dynasties.
- **Kael Issamil** (Iron affiliate) — reform from within, sincerely meant.
- **Farleen Darfi** (Water affiliate) — **do not categorise her as a Cartel Faction member who betrayed the
  group.** She was embedded, by Rosik, before there was anything to betray. See `People/Farleen Darfi.md`;
  flattening her to "the betrayer" loses the entire arc.

## Open, and worth resolving

- **The Thess- collision.** *Thessalar* (Restorationist archivists), *Thessarian* (Grain-integrated) and
  *Thessian* (Water-integrated) are three separate houses in three different alignments with nearly the
  same name. On the page it is confusing; aloud it is worse. Renaming two of them would touch
  `houses_of_kahaan.md` and **Book 3's chapter skeleton and the ch1 beat draft**, which both use
  *Valorian* and *Thessarian*, so it is a decision rather than a cleanup.
- **"Erulian Military Success: Kalden's victories elsewhere might embolden the Claimants"** appeared in the
  previous version's tipping points. **Kalden has no victories** — he loses the Compact War and is blocked
  at Silaris. Cut here; recorded in case it was meant as a conditional.
- **Whether Kalden's Kaha'ani supporters know they are also an assassination network**, or whether the
  constitutional and coercive halves of his faction are compartmented from each other. It bears on how many
  of them can convert to Elara after Book 1.
