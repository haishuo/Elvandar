# The Bloodsworn: Warriors of Red Mountain

## Origins and Philosophy

Within Terinok's already martial society exists a canton so devoted to warfare that even other warriors view them with a mixture of respect and unease. They call themselves the *Krev'han Thuum* — the Blood Sworn — though outsiders often refer to them simply as "the Zealots" or, more commonly, "the Red Mountain." Their canton occupies the slopes and high valleys surrounding Mount Karagh, an active volcano that dominates the upper Sentinel Mountains on Terinok's eastern border with Gunastran — at the convergence where the Sentinels meet the Frost Spine in the high north. The mountain is officially named for an ancient warlord; in common usage, everyone calls it the Red Mountain, a name earned through both the volcanic stone that bleeds from its flanks and the extraordinary amount of blood spilled upon its slopes over the centuries.

The Bloodsworn predate the Balishan Empire. Their ancestor clans refused imperial integration even when the rest of Terinok served as the empire's military backbone, retreating to the Red Mountain and continuing their practices undisturbed. When the confederation formed, they were recognized as a canton — not because the other eleven wanted them as peers, exactly, but because denying them recognition would have meant fighting them, and no one wanted to do that.

They are, in the collective opinion of the other cantons, a great deal.

### Core Philosophy: Dhuum Dhar Zhuhala

The Bloodsworn live by a singular, uncompromising motto: "**Dhuum Dhar Zhuhala**" — "To fight or to die," the only two activities they consider worthy of a warrior. All other aspects of life — eating, sleeping, even reproduction — are viewed merely as necessary maintenance to enable fighting. Their philosophy rests on several extreme principles:

- The body exists solely as a weapon, and should be treated as such
- Pain is merely weakness leaving the body
- Death in battle is the only honorable conclusion to life
- A warrior who can no longer fight has no purpose and should seek death
- Blood spilled in combat feeds the strength of future generations
- The line between battle and ritual is nonexistent

Most extreme is their belief that the purpose of victory is not conquest or glory, but simply to create opportunities for more combat. Peace is viewed as stagnation of the warrior spirit.

Other Terinok warriors generally agree with the *spirit* of these principles. The debate is one of degree. The other cantons consider the Bloodsworn position on, say, sleeping to be somewhat hysterical. They recognize the Bloodsworn as genuine warriors — perhaps the most fearsome in the confederation — while also finding their specific implementation of these shared values to be excessive, exhausting, and not entirely sane.

The Bloodsworn regard this view with total indifference. They consider the other cantons insufficiently committed.

### The Creation of the Demon's Edge

When the Titan's Heart — the divine artifact that originally blessed Terinok's warriors with gradually increasing strength through patient discipline — grew silent, most of Terinok's leaders sought alternatives that would preserve their martial advantage. However, the Bloodsworn saw opportunity rather than crisis.

According to recovered accounts, seven Bloodsworn elders performed a ritual atop Mount Karagh, where they sacrificed one hundred warriors who volunteered for the honor. The ritual lasted seven days and seven nights, with the volunteers fighting to the death in various combinations. The final survivor was then sacrificed upon a blade forged from meteoritic iron that had been quenched in the blood of the previous ninety-nine, then plunged into the volcanic stone of the mountain itself.

This blade became the Demon's Edge — a weapon that gains power with every life it takes, but whose hunger is never satisfied. The corrupted artifact embodies the antithesis of the Titan's Heart's virtues: impatience instead of discipline, immediate power instead of gradual growth, consumption instead of cultivation. The other cantons consider the Demon's Edge a deeply unsettling object and are quietly relieved that it stays on Red Mountain.

## Canton Status and Relations with the Confederation

### Recognition and Representation

The Bloodsworn Canton sends a representative to the War Table — the *Krev'mahl* Speaker, drawn from their Memory Keepers — and participates in confederation votes as any other canton does. In practice, this participation is minimal. The Bloodsworn vote yes to anything involving war and abstain on everything else. They have never, in the confederation's history, voted against a military campaign.

Their Voice at the War Table is traditionally the most formally dressed representative present, armored in full crimson-stained plate with ritual scarification visible where skin shows. Other representatives find this either impressive or deeply unnecessary, depending on their canton. The Bloodsworn consider both reactions irrelevant.

### The Management Problem

The other eleven cantons maintain a careful, mutually agreed policy toward the Bloodsworn that no one writes down officially:

- Bloodsworn territory is positioned at the most volatile border regions, where skirmishes with Gunastran across the Sentinel passes are frequent and the Bloodsworn's need for combat is reliably met by geography
- When confederation forces assemble for major campaigns, Bloodsworn contingents are integrated into the vanguard, given the most dangerous assignments, and kept physically separated from other canton forces during downtime
- After major battles with high enemy casualties, Bloodsworn units are immediately dispersed to different sectors rather than quartered together
- When external conflicts go quiet for extended periods, neighboring cantons have been known to arrange "training disputes" with each other in territory adjacent to Bloodsworn lands — incidents that escalate just enough to require the Bloodsworn to "intervene," providing the violence they need without anything formally escalating
- Two cantons maintain dedicated liaison warriors assigned to Red Mountain full-time, whose job is to detect when Bloodsworn restlessness is approaching dangerous levels and communicate this to the confederation before the problem resolves itself in a less convenient direction

None of this is codified in War Table law. It is simply understood. The phrase "managing the mountain" is canton shorthand for this entire problem.

### Why the Other Cantons Tolerate This

The answer is military value that is, frankly, difficult to argue with. Bloodsworn warriors anchoring a battle line do not waver. They do not retreat. They do not surrender. They do not get tired in ways that affect their fighting. Deploying them against an enemy position is, as one Takama Canton commander reportedly observed, "less like ordering an assault and more like opening a door." The Bloodsworn go through it, and what was on the other side is no longer relevant.

In mercenary terms, the Bloodsworn are rarely contracted independently — their philosophy makes sustained campaigns impractical, they take too many casualties, and clients find them "difficult to brief" — but they are sometimes attached to confederation contracts as a specialized element, with significant price premiums and detailed liability clauses that the other cantons have learned to include.

Several foreign powers have attempted to hire the Bloodsworn directly. The standard outcome involves the foreign emissary delivering their offer in person, as a show of good faith. The Bloodsworn receive this offer. There is a ritual duel. The emissary or their bodyguard is killed. This is considered a compliment and a provisional acceptance. Most foreign powers have learned to send their offers in writing, through Takama Canton intermediaries, with fees paid to Takama for the translation service.

### Canton Independence and the Containment Agreement

The Bloodsworn's status as a full canton means they cannot simply be directed. They can be asked. They can be offered incentives — primarily war, which they already want. They can be pressured through the Warrior's Code, which they do take seriously, though their interpretation of several key provisions differs from the mainstream.

The unspoken agreement of the other cantons amounts to: *we give them an active border, a steady supply of enemies, and the respect their status demands; they give us the most terrifying vanguard in the known world and do not do whatever they would do if we tried to constrain them.* It is not a comfortable arrangement. It is one that has held.

## Organization and Structure

The Bloodsworn are organized into sanctuary-based units called Blood Packs, each consisting of approximately sixty warriors. Seven major sanctuaries exist throughout Red Mountain's slopes and surrounding highlands, each maintaining its own traditions while following the fundamental Bloodsworn philosophy.

### Sanctuaries

Each sanctuary serves as both training ground and spiritual center for its Blood Pack. Located on and around the active volcanic slopes of Mount Karagh, the sanctuaries are environments that non-Bloodsworn find either symbolically appropriate or literally too hot:

- Training grounds designed for extreme conditions, including volcanic terrain
- Ritual chambers where ceremonies and initiations occur, several carved directly into cooled lava flows
- Walls inscribed with the names and death-tales of fallen members
- Forge facilities that use volcanic heat directly — Bloodsworn weaponry is worked in conditions other smiths cannot or will not attempt
- Memorial halls housing relics from particularly glorious deaths

The volcanic environment is not merely aesthetic. The mountain's activity is considered sacred; eruptions are read as omens, and the Bloodsworn have a sophisticated practical knowledge of volcanic behavior that allows them to live there safely. They also use this knowledge tactically, and more than one incautious force has learned what it means to chase Bloodsworn warriors through terrain the Bloodsworn understand and they do not.

### Hierarchy

The Bloodsworn maintain a minimal hierarchy, valuing combat prowess over formal rank:

- **Memory Keepers** — Elders who preserve the names and deeds of fallen warriors; the closest thing the Bloodsworn have to administrators
- **Voice of the Blood Pack** — A designated representative who speaks for the sanctuary
- **Black Eyes** — Warriors who have replaced an eye with obsidian after losing it in worthy combat
- **Silver Spikes** — Veterans who have undergone extensive bone modification rituals
- **Blood Brothers/Sisters** — Standard Bloodsworn who have completed all initiation rites

Unlike conventional military organizations, leadership is fluid and contextual. The warrior most suited to a particular battle or ritual may lead that specific endeavor, regardless of their standing in other contexts. The Memory Keeper who represents them at the War Table is not the Bloodsworn's "leader" in any meaningful sense — they are simply the one whose current role requires surviving long enough to attend twice-yearly meetings.

## Rituals and Practices

### The Blood Tithe

Perhaps their most infamous ritual, performed every seven years. Each Bloodsworn warrior contributes a significant amount of their own blood to a communal vessel, then battles in a weakened state. Those who survive are deemed worthy to continue; those who fall are fully exsanguinated to complete the tithe. The collected blood is used in various rituals, including the forging and maintenance of their distinctive crimson-tinted weapons.

### The Marking

Bloodsworn warriors undergo extensive scarification, creating intricate patterns that tell their battle history. These scars are infused with red mineral pigments — sourced from Red Mountain itself — that make them stand out vividly against their skin. The most devoted members also engage in bone modification, having small metal spikes or blades permanently attached to their knuckles, elbows, or other striking surfaces.

### The Waking Battle

Bloodsworn never fully sleep. Instead, they practice a technique called "battle meditation," where they remain partially conscious, ready to fight at a moment's notice. They train by having fellow members attempt to kill them while they rest, creating warriors who can transition from sleep to combat instantly. This practice tends to shorten their lifespans through exhaustion, which they consider an acceptable trade for constant battle readiness.

### The Final Walk

When a Bloodsworn becomes too injured or aged to fight effectively, they undertake the Final Walk — a journey into enemy territory with the sole purpose of dying while killing as many opponents as possible. This ritual suicide is considered the most honorable end, and successful Final Walks (measured by enemy casualties) are commemorated in their mountain sanctuaries. Other cantons observe the Final Walk tradition with a mixture of genuine respect and pointed silence.

### The Rite of Respect

A ceremony conducted to honor fallen warriors from both allied and enemy forces. The ritual involves:

- Creating a sacred circle with stones marked with ritual symbols
- Placing clay bowls representing each fallen warrior in a circular pattern
- Filling the bowls with krev'harl (ritual blood meal) mixture
- Speaking the names and deeds of the fallen
- Adding blood offerings from living warriors who respected the deceased
- Creating memorial pouches containing mixture from each bowl

This ritual acknowledges the shared bond between all who have shed blood on the same battlefield, regardless of which side they fought on. Other canton warriors who have witnessed it have generally found it unexpectedly moving, which they tend not to mention.

### The Cleansing

A rare and dreaded ritual reserved for those who violate what the Bloodsworn consider the sacred laws of battle — particularly those who take captives rather than granting death, harm children or the defenseless, or treat combat as mere commerce. During the Cleansing:

- Bloodsworn warriors enter a trance-like state where they move with unnatural synchronization
- They advance relentlessly, ignoring wounds and continuing to fight despite mortal injuries
- They maintain complete silence throughout the killing, performing it as ritual rather than battle
- No quarter is given, no surrender accepted
- The ritual culminates in the "breaking of the chain" — a ceremonial execution of the leader using the krev'harl mixture, which causes a particularly painful death to those who have "profaned" the sanctity of battle

The other cantons are broadly supportive of the Cleansing's stated targets. They are less supportive of the Bloodsworn's tendency to conduct it without consulting anyone first. This is a recurring agenda item at the War Table.

## The Krev'dhuum: Blood-Battle Ecstasy

The pinnacle of Bloodsworn achievement is reaching the Krev'dhuum — a transcendent berserker state achieved in their final moments of life. This is considered the ultimate spiritual experience, the moment when a warrior transcends the boundaries between life and death, becoming one with battle itself.

Signs of a warrior entering the Krev'dhuum include:

- Maniacal laughter that continues even as they sustain mortal wounds
- A complete cessation of defensive movements, focusing entirely on offensive strikes
- A visible change in their eyes — pupils dilating fully until almost no iris is visible
- Rhythmic chanting or singing, often growing louder as wounds accumulate
- Increased speed and strength despite catastrophic injuries
- Continuing to fight despite injuries that would incapacitate ordinary warriors

The Bloodsworn believe each warrior's Krev'dhuum produces a unique "battle song" — a specific pattern of movements, strikes, and vocalizations that emerges spontaneously in their final moments. Chroniclers record these patterns, which are then taught to young initiates as sacred forms.

Other canton warriors who have witnessed the Krev'dhuum firsthand tend not to discuss it much afterward. The accounts that exist are consistent: it is not something that reads as human.

## Theology: The Heart of the Mountain

The Bloodsworn's religious belief is entirely their own — distinct from mainstream Terinok faith, growing from the volcanic ground of Red Mountain itself, and regarded by other cantons as another variety of their general excess.

### The Eternal Army and Its Limits

Mainstream Terinok religion holds that warriors who die honorably join the *Eternal Army* — a permanent host of ancestor-warriors who watch over the living and stand ready for some final, apocalyptic battle at the end of the age. It is a comfort. It is a reward. The dead rest, watch, remember.

The Bloodsworn consider this insufficient.

Rest is what happens when you can no longer fight. Watching is a substitute for doing. And an army that never marches, never bleeds, never wins or loses anything — however eternal — is not an army the Bloodsworn particularly want to join. The Eternal Army, in their view, is a fine afterlife for warriors who have run out of fights. The Bloodsworn intend to find more.

They do not say this to other Terinok warriors directly. They say it in the way they live, which is enough.

### Krev'thuum Karagh: The Heart's Reforging

The Bloodsworn believe that Mount Karagh is alive — not metaphorically, not as a spiritual presence dwelling in the stone, but *literally*. The mountain breathes. Its eruptions are exhalations. Its lava is blood of a kind older and more patient than any warrior's. At the mountain's core — in the furnace-heart they call *Krev'thuum Karagh*, the Blood-Heart of the Mountain — souls can be reforged as iron is reforged: burned clean of what they were, made ready to be something new.

The theology works as follows:

A warrior who dies *well enough* — in genuine combat, against a worthy opponent, having fought until the body could no longer sustain the fight — leaves behind more than a corpse and a memory. They leave a *battle-essence*, the distilled heat of everything they were as a fighter. This essence descends into Red Mountain through channels that run beneath all of Terinok, drawn by the mountain's hunger for worthy dead. There, in the Heart's fire, it is unmade and remade: the self burned away, the warrior-nature purified, the capacity for violence and courage and hunger refined into something almost elemental.

When the mountain is ready — when it has need, when conditions are right, when some balance the Bloodsworn do not fully understand has been satisfied — it releases this reforged soul back into the world. It enters a child being born somewhere on or near Red Mountain's slopes: not the same warrior, not carrying memories or identity, but carrying the *fire*. A child born of the mountain's choosing arrives in the world already inclined toward battle, already restless, already in some sense *old* in ways that have nothing to do with years lived.

This is why the Bloodsworn have always produced warriors of extraordinary quality from a relatively small population. Or so they believe.

### What Earns Reforging

The theology is precise about what qualifies a death for reforging, and what does not.

**Deaths the mountain accepts:**
- Death in genuine combat, still fighting when the end comes
- Death achieved in the Krev'dhuum — this is considered the highest possible death, the surest path back; the battle-song a warrior produces in those final moments is believed to be the soul's signature writing itself into the mountain's memory
- Death on the Final Walk, provided the walk ends in actual fighting rather than a quiet collapse
- Death defending the sanctuaries or the mountain itself
- Death at the hands of a warrior one genuinely respected

**Deaths the mountain refuses:**
- Death from age, illness, or injury sustained outside of battle
- Death in captivity — including by one's own hand if the alternative was surrender rather than a fighting end; the *krev'thalan* (voluntary heart-stop) occupies an uncomfortable theological grey area, discussed at length among Memory Keepers
- Death performing the Cleansing, oddly — the ritual is necessary, but it is execution, not battle, and the mountain distinguishes
- Death in service of commerce rather than genuine warfare — a mercenary who dies protecting a trade caravan occupies complicated theological ground; the Bloodsworn have ongoing internal debate about this

The *Zhur'thalak* — "the lingering" — carries its full weight here. A warrior who grows old without achieving a worthy death is not merely shamed socially. They are facing the extinction of something. Not punishment, not hell — just ending. The fire goes out. The mountain does not call for embers.

### The Mountain Keeps Its Counsel

The Bloodsworn do not claim to know which of their warriors have returned before. The mountain does not announce it. A child born on Red Mountain's slopes might carry a reforged soul from a warrior who died a century ago — or might not. There is no reliable sign, no certain marker.

Memory Keepers watch. They observe children who show unusual aptitude, instincts that seem older than their years, particular affinities for specific weapons or fighting styles that echo documented predecessors. They note these patterns. They do not make pronouncements. The memory of a warrior is a living thing in their records, and they will sometimes observe, quietly, that a young Blood Brother or Sister moves in ways that recall someone specific — but this is hypothesis, not declaration.

The mountain decides. The mountain does not explain itself.

This uncertainty is, to the Bloodsworn, theologically correct. If the mountain announced who had returned and who had not, warriors might begin fighting for recognition rather than for the fight itself. The ambiguity keeps the motivation pure: you die well because dying well is what you are, not because it will guarantee you something. The fire either calls to you or it doesn't, and you will not know which until after.

### Relation to the Demon's Edge

The Demon's Edge occupies a complicated place in this theology. Its creation involved the deaths of one hundred warriors in ritual combat, all deaths that should — by the theology's logic — qualify for reforging. The Bloodsworn accept this, which means they believe the souls of those hundred are somewhere in the mountain, waiting or already returned.

Some Memory Keepers theorize that the Edge itself is a kind of bottleneck — that the weapon's hunger traps a portion of each soul it takes, holding it in a state between death and reforging. This would explain Kraag Vos'hal's peculiar relationship with the weapon: he may be, in some theological sense, carrying company. The hundred first sacrifices, plus every soul taken since, accumulated in the blade, waiting.

This theory is not discussed with Kraag Vos'hal. He has not been asked.

### Other Cantons' Views

The other cantons regard the Red Mountain theology the way they regard most Bloodsworn beliefs: as intense, probably sincere, and not something they intend to argue with directly. The specific belief that the Eternal Army is an insufficient afterlife for a real warrior is noted and not responded to, in the same way one does not respond to a statement made by someone holding a very large axe in a very small room.

A small number of warriors from other cantons, late at night, after campaigns where they have fought alongside Bloodsworn, have privately expressed something that sounds like envy — not of the Bloodsworn's life, which most find untenable, but of the possibility of a death that *means something going forward*, rather than a death that simply concludes. The Eternal Army is restful. But some warriors, if they are honest, are not sure they want rest.

They do not say this to the Bloodsworn. The Bloodsworn would take it as an invitation to help them find out sooner.

### The Krev'zhulaar: The Final Fire

Underpinning everything — the relentless training, the death-seeking philosophy, the refusal to doubt, the Proving, the brutal unanimity of their faith — is the Bloodsworn's central eschatological belief: the world will end in fire, and they must be ready when it does.

They call it the **Krev'zhulaar** — the Blood-Fire-End, the Final Burning. Where mainstream Terinok religion speaks of the Eternal Army gathering for a final apocalyptic battle, the Bloodsworn's version is more specific and considerably more literal. The world does not end through war between men and gods. It ends through the mountain. Mount Karagh is not merely a volcano. It is a contained catastrophe — a force of world-ending magnitude that waits, patient as geology, for the call.

The Bloodsworn believe they are the ones who answer that call.

When the Krev'zhulaar comes — when the age ends and the world burns clean — it will be the Bloodsworn who summon it. Not passively, not as witnesses. As participants. The Final Fire is not something that happens to them. It is something they *do*.

This belief is the reason for everything.

### The Final Chant: Vel'thuum'krev'zhal

The mechanism of the Krev'zhulaar is a ritual called the **Vel'thuum'krev'zhal** — the Final Oath-Fire, or in common speech, the Final Chant. The Chant, performed in full by all living Bloodsworn simultaneously, calls the mountain to its work. The words are old enough that their linguistic origins are disputed even within the community; the Memory Keepers preserve them as they have always been:

*"Mother Mountain hear our plea,*
*rend the earth and boil the sea!*
*Mother Mountain hear our cry,*
*burn the land and scorch the sky!"*

The Chant has never been performed in full. It has never come close. Every Bloodsworn alive knows this and finds it theologically correct: the Final Chant is not for ordinary times. It is for the end. Performing it prematurely — before the Krev'zhulaar has truly come — would be the greatest possible desecration of the mountain's purpose.

But the mechanics of the Chant are where the theology becomes most consequential, and most dangerous:

**The Chant requires unanimity.** Every living Bloodsworn who has taken the blood-oath must participate, and must participate with genuine belief. The Chant is not a performance. It cannot be coerced. A warrior who mouths the words while holding doubt in their heart does not contribute — they *break* the Chant. One doubter among ten thousand and the mountain does not answer. One doubter among a hundred and the mountain does not answer. The threshold is absolute: all, or none.

This is why the Bloodsworn cannot afford doubt. It is not merely a cultural or social prohibition. Doubt is a structural failure condition. A Bloodsworn warrior who loses faith does not weaken the canton's commitment to their philosophy — they *nullify the entire mechanism*. They become the single person standing between the mountain and its purpose when the end truly comes.

The other cantons, when they grasp this theology fully, understand "managing the mountain" in a new way. The Bloodsworn must be kept fighting, kept purposeful, kept *believing* — not because the other cantons share their faith, but because a Bloodsworn driven to despair, who looks at the world and concludes that the end is now, is the trigger that cannot be untriggered. The Krev'zhulaar theology is the reason Terinok ensures the Bloodsworn are never truly desperate. Bored, occasionally. Restless, often. Never without enemies or purpose. Never without something to fight for while they wait.

The gray wolves' position on this is simple and practical: they live on the same mountain. They are particularly motivated to ensure the Bloodsworn remain purposeful.

### The Krev'zhulaar and The Rending

When The Rending struck — when the world visibly, catastrophically broke, fertile lands becoming desert in hours, forests dying, rivers vanishing — the Bloodsworn believed it had begun.

The Memory Keepers' records from that period are the most emotionally intense documents they have ever produced. Scouts were sent out from Red Mountain's sanctuaries. They returned with accounts of devastation on a scale that no living warrior had ever witnessed. The mountain's activity increased sharply in the days following the Rending — eruptions more frequent, the smoke column higher and darker.

The elders convened. The Final Chant was raised as a question, formally, for the first time in centuries. The argument for initiating it: the signs are here, the world is ending, this is what we have prepared for. The argument against: the Rending's fire is not volcanic. The world has not ended — it has changed. The mountain has not called. The correct response is to fight what survives, not to end what remains.

The debate lasted nine days. The against-faction prevailed, narrowly, on the grounds that the Krev'zhulaar, when it comes, will be unmistakable — the mountain's own signal, not a signal from elsewhere that is interpreted as the end. The Rending was a catastrophe of another kind. It was not the Final Fire.

The Rending generation of Bloodsworn is remembered as the most fervent in the canton's history, having faced the question directly and chosen to continue. Their battle-songs are the most complex in the memorial archives.

Outside Terinok, this history is not widely known. Within the confederation, the knowledge that the Bloodsworn came within a narrowly-argued theological debate of initiating a world-ending chant during a recent catastrophe is part of why "managing the mountain" is treated with the seriousness it receives.

### The Vel'dhuum: The Proving

The unanimity requirement creates a problem that the Bloodsworn have solved in a specific and unusual way: voluntary exit.

At the age of fourteen, every Bloodsworn-raised child undergoes the **Vel'dhuum** — the Unbloodied's Choice, commonly called the Proving. This is the moment when a child who has been raised in the sanctuaries, trained in Bloodsworn ways, immersed in Bloodsworn philosophy their entire life, is given a genuine choice: take the blood-oath and become Bloodsworn, or leave.

The choice must be genuine. This is the point. An oath extracted by coercion or social pressure from someone who doubts is worse than no oath — it creates exactly the structural failure the theology cannot tolerate. Better to know your doubters clearly, release them cleanly, and maintain the purity of those who remain, than to maintain numbers that include hidden uncertainty.

The ceremony is simple. The child stands before the assembled sanctuary. The Memory Keeper asks, once: *"Do you believe?"* If the answer is yes — if the belief is present in their body and their voice and their eyes — the oath is taken, the scarification begins, and they are Blood. If the answer is no, or the Memory Keeper reads hesitation beneath a yes, the child is told: *"You are free. We do not hold you."*

This is true. The Bloodsworn do not harm those who leave. They do not pursue them. They feel, in some complex way, that releasing a doubter is an act of service to the mountain — removing a weakness before it can corrupt the whole.

What they do not do is help.

The child who says no at the Vel'dhuum must descend Red Mountain alone. No guide, no supplies beyond what they arrived with, no farewell beyond a nod from the Memory Keeper. The sanctuaries are in the high volcanic slopes; the descent to habitable land is several days of difficult terrain. Arrow hawks nest along the most practical routes. The Bloodsworn consider this an appropriate final test: if you are going to leave the blood, prove you can survive without it.

Most do. The training has been thorough, regardless of what the Proving decides.

### The Vel'thuum: Those Who Left

Those who leave the Bloodsworn at the Vel'dhuum are called the **Vel'thuum** — the oath-less, or more literally, those who are outside the sworn. They are not enemies. They are not shamed in any formal sense. They are simply no longer counted.

In the theology, they are invisible. When the Final Chant comes, the Vel'thuum neither strengthen nor weaken it — they are not Bloodsworn, their belief or disbelief is irrelevant, and the mountain does not know their names. They walked out of the accounting freely, and the accounting does not follow them.

In practice, the Vel'thuum are a small and particular population. Children raised in Bloodsworn sanctuaries to age fourteen emerge from the Proving — if they leave — carrying:

- Bloodsworn combat training, which is extraordinary
- Bloodsworn cultural knowledge, which is intimate
- No clan, no oath, no community
- The experience of having descended Red Mountain alone

They arrive, typically, at the edges of the gray wolf territories or the nearest Terinok canton settlements, which in the northern reaches of the Sentinel range means Vharak or Moru. Both communities have developed informal protocols for receiving them. The gray wolves' response is typically practical: if you survived the descent, you're useful; we will not ask what you chose, and you will not volunteer it unless asked. The Terinok warrior cantons receive them as free persons of demonstrated capability, though the Bloodsworn training and the circumstances of their arrival are generally known and noted.

The Vel'thuum rarely speak of what they left. Some integrate fully into Terinok canton life; others remain in a permanent in-between, recognized everywhere and fully belonging nowhere. A few have made their way further — to Balisha, to Uratha — carrying knowledge of Red Mountain's interior that no other outsiders possess.

What the Vel'thuum privately think about the Final Chant varies. Some left precisely because they did not believe the world was ending and found the belief oppressive. Some left because they believed it entirely and found the inevitability of it more than they could carry at fourteen. Some simply could not sustain the absolute certainty required, and had the self-knowledge to say so.

The Bloodsworn, if asked, will say they bear the Vel'thuum no ill will. This is true. What they feel is something closer to the sentiment one has toward a fire that has gone out: not anger, not grief, exactly. Just the awareness of what is no longer present.

## Cultural Elements

### Krev'harl (Blood Meal)

A ritual concoction prepared from dried blood, herbs, and volcanic mineral compounds from Red Mountain. Different variations exist for different purposes:

- **Initiate's Mixture** — Created from the first blood drawn in a warrior's initiation
- **Battle Preparation** — Consumed before combat to enhance focus and reduce pain
- **Memorial Blend** — Used in ceremonies honoring fallen warriors
- **Cleansing Formula** — A special mixture used in the ritual punishment of those who violate battle ethics

The krev'harl is believed to connect warriors to their "battle-truth" and the collective strength of those who have fallen before them. When consumed by worthy warriors, it brings clarity and power; when forced upon those who have "profaned" battle, it causes excruciating death.

### Blood Tokens

Metal discs etched with patterns similar to Bloodsworn scarification, containing a small obsidian stone at their center. These tokens are given to non-Bloodsworn who have demonstrated exceptional valor or honor in battle. Possessing a Blood Token marks one as "blood-worthy" — a status that ensures no Bloodsworn will ever consider them an unworthy opponent deserving of "the Mercy" (quick death without glory).

Within the confederation, receiving a Blood Token from a Bloodsworn is treated by the other cantons as a meaningful honor, even by those who find the Bloodsworn excessive in all other respects.

### Death Songs

Unlike most warrior cultures that celebrate victories, Bloodsworn songs focus almost exclusively on glorious deaths. Their most revered ballads describe final stands against impossible odds, warriors achieving the Krev'dhuum while surrounded by enemies, or particularly creative ways of ensuring a memorable death. These songs are not mournful but celebratory, treating each perfect death as the culmination of a warrior's purpose rather than a tragedy.

### The Bloodsworn's Position on Slavery

This is one area where the other cantons and the Bloodsworn differ in ways that occasionally cause friction. The Bloodsworn's core philosophy holds that captivity in any form is a violation of the natural order — a warrior either wins and kills, or loses and dies. Taking slaves is, to them, a corruption of battle's sacred resolution.

They do not keep slaves. They do not trade slaves. They regard the broader Terinok practice of ritualized slave hunting as philosophically incoherent — if young warriors are training by killing slaves, they are training against opponents who cannot genuinely threaten them, which is not training, it is performance. The Bloodsworn initiate their young warriors against each other, with live weapons, and accept the casualties this produces.

Other cantons find this position alternately principled and inconvenient, particularly since it occasionally leads Bloodsworn warriors to "liberate" war captives in field situations, which creates complications.

## Notable Individuals

### Kraal of the Red Mountain

A prominent Voice of the Blood Pack, recognized by his obsidian eye implant and extensive scarification. Serves as a representative to outside groups and leads a Blood Pack of sixty warriors. Known for his formal ritual knowledge and a slightly more diplomatic approach than most Bloodsworn — which, in context, means he gives outsiders a full sentence to make their point before responding.

### The Memory Keeper

An elderly Bloodsworn whose entire body is covered in scar patterns and whose skull is reinforced with a network of embedded metal bands. Serves as the repository of ritual knowledge and keeper of the names of fallen warriors. Conducts major ceremonies including the Rite of Respect and the Cleansing. Appears, disconcertingly, to be waiting to die with great patience and no particular urgency.

### Kraag Vos'hal

The current wielder of the Demon's Edge, a figure so deeply transformed by the weapon that some question whether he remains human. His body is a topography of battle scars and ritual markings, with eyes that have turned completely black. He hasn't spoken in over a decade, communicating only through battle signals and ritual gestures. The Edge is never sheathed in his presence — he sleeps with it gripped in his hands, cuts his food with its edge, and has been observed seemingly in conversation with the weapon. The other cantons' representatives at the War Table give the seat next to his a wide berth when he bothers to attend.

## The Ultimate Insult and Honor

To a Bloodsworn, being told they are "not worth killing" is the ultimate insult. To them, a duel to the death is the greatest gift one could offer — especially if they end up being killed, as this provides the perfect death they seek.

Elder Bloodsworn who have survived too many battles carry a profound shame. They call this condition *"Zhur'thalak"* — "the lingering" — and view it as a spiritual affliction. These veterans become increasingly desperate in their attempts to find a worthy death. The confederation has developed informal protocols for managing *Zhur'thalak* veterans: assigning them to the most dangerous forward positions, ensuring they never run short of enemies, and under no circumstances posting them to garrison duty.

The greatest honor one can pay to Bloodsworn warriors is not to mourn their deaths but to envy them — particularly if they achieved the Krev'dhuum in their final moments.

## Phrases and Terminology

- **Dhuum Dhar Zhuhala** — "To fight or to die" (core philosophy)
- **Krev'han Thuum** — "Blood Sworn" (their name for themselves)
- **Krev'dhuum** — "Blood-Battle Ecstasy" (transcendent berserker state achieved in final moments; considered the surest path to reforging)
- **Krev'harl** — "Blood meal" (ritual concoction consumed before battles and ceremonies)
- **Zhur'thalak** — "The lingering" (shameful condition of surviving too long; carries theological weight — the risk of a death that the mountain will not accept)
- **Krev'mahl** — "Blood Council" (gathering where elders evaluate each other's worthiness for death)
- **Dhuum'kar Vhaal** — "The Final Dance" (formal death challenge between Bloodsworn)
- **Krev'zhal dum Orvash** — "May you die in bed, forgotten" (worst possible curse; implies a death the mountain will refuse)
- **Krev'halla** — "The Perfect Ending" (ideal death in glorious battle)
- **Krev'thalan** — Method of voluntary death through muscle contractions that stop the heart (theologically contested — whether the mountain accepts this kind of death is a matter of ongoing debate among Memory Keepers)
- **Krev'thuum Karagh** — "The Blood-Heart of the Mountain" (the volcanic core of Red Mountain, where the Bloodsworn believe worthy souls are reforged and returned)
- **Dhuum'krev'hal** — "The Reforging" (the theological process by which a worthy soul is unmade and returned to the world through the mountain; sometimes used colloquially to mean any radical transformation)
- **Krev'zhulaar** — "The Blood-Fire-End" / "The Final Burning" (the Bloodsworn's apocalyptic theology; the world-ending event they are prepared to summon and survive into)
- **Vel'thuum'krev'zhal** — "The Final Oath-Fire" (the ritual Final Chant that calls the mountain to its world-ending work; requires total unanimity among all living Bloodsworn who have taken the blood-oath)
- **Vel'dhuum** — "The Unbloodied's Choice" (the Proving at age fourteen, when Bloodsworn-raised children choose to take the oath or leave)
- **Vel'thuum** — "The oath-less" (those who left at the Vel'dhuum; no longer counted in the Final Chant, no longer Blood, but not enemies; sometimes used more broadly for any outsider who was once within)
- **Vel'keth** — "Sky-hunter" (the Bloodsworn name for the arrow hawks that nest on Red Mountain's volcanic cliffs; considered the mountain's own predators)
