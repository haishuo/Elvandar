# Languages of Elvandar

A reference for the linguistic layers underlying Elvandar's names, titles, and ceremonial vocabulary. This
began as a naming-consistency guide rather than a conlang and now contains two kinds of material that must
not be confused:

- **Story practice and historical/cultural constraints**, including second-name grants, political events,
  speaker communities, register needs, and collision tests
- **Inherited linguistic reconstruction**, including proposed family relationships, phonological
  signatures, morphemes, etymologies, and corpus-fitting names from the superseded first pass

History, culture, characterization, and narrative function remain binding inputs. Existing non-protected
sound-forms do not. The linguistic reconstruction in this file has no authority over a new design plate;
if an approved language contradicts it, this file and the dependent canon change.

The reconstruction inventory is now in [`Attested Forms Register.md`](Attested%20Forms%20Register.md).
That register controls **protection, exposure, and conformance cost**. P0 forms are mandatory historical
outcomes. P1 prose forms carry a strong presumption of survival but may change before the language acquires
one-off machinery to rescue them. P2–P4 forms do not constrain design.

---

## The Core Premise

The deep Halauri/Balishan relationship was settled on 2026-08-22. The exact sound systems, grammars,
roots, and subsidiary family assignments remain to be reconstructed. In particular, a learned written
standard may be conservative after the sealing, but the surface communities teaching, pronouncing,
copying, and reusing it do not literally stop changing.

Hauren's 3,000-year open period (5,000–2,000 years ago) gave surface humans extended contact with the most
advanced civilization on the continent and made a deep scholarly and technical influence possible. The
inherited model treats Late Open Halauri as the basis of a learned surface tradition after the sealing.
Its copied standard may remain conservative, while pronunciation, pedagogy, interpretation, and new human
coinages continue to change. Halauri inside the sealed city develops separately for another two
millennia. How intelligible the two traditions remain is not fixed until their speakers, institutions, and
sound changes are designed.

The spoken contact varieties were also nativized by human communities. Their descendants lead to
Proto-Balishan and Imperial Balishan, while an older human language or family survives within them as the
**Ancestral Continental** substrate. The sustained Great Shaft interface and the cultural mechanism of
that shift are settled in `Halauri Culture.md`; the genealogy is in `Language Families.md`.

This gives five layers or strata relevant to surface civilization.

## The Five Layers

### **1. Halauri (proper)**

The living elven language history of Hauren, spoken continuously from the city's founding ~5,000 years ago
until its end twenty years ago. It contains multiple open and sealed stages rather than one unchanging
tongue. No native speakers survive. The relationship between Terminal Halauri and the learned surface
tradition is not yet quantified; do not assign an intelligibility analogy until the intervening changes
exist.

### **2. Old Halauri (Hieratic Halauri)**

The proposed learned standard descended from the Halauri used with surface civilizations late in the
open period. After the sealing, its textual norm is treated as inherited authority: studied, cited, and
used for some titles, inscriptions, treatises, formulae, and ceremonial names. The communities carrying it
continue to develop their own pronunciations, teaching traditions, meanings, and compositions. Its actual
reach within the later Balishan Empire — who could read it, write it, or merely recognize set forms — must
be established from literacy and institutional history.

This is the language behind almost every "ancient" or "imperial" name on the continent.

### **3. Surface Halauric**

The human-nativized descendants of the Halauri contact varieties used during the open period. These
became ordinary community languages: learned by children, reshaped by human speech habits, and carried
forward after Hauren sealed. Proto-Balishan is a late heartland stage of this branch, not a separate
family.

### **4. Ancestral Continental substrate**

The older human language or related group of languages spoken before and during the shift to Surface
Halauric. It survives in sounds, constructions, names, and vocabulary, particularly ordinary cultural and
landscape domains. Its internal branches and modern independent descendants are not yet assigned.

### **5. Imperial Balishan**

The institutional koine standardized from the living Surface-Halauric heartland continuum, especially
Vartonnian speech with Marresonnian commercial and scribal influence. Its high register also borrows
afresh from Hieratic Old Halauri. After imperial collapse, regional descendants of Imperial Balishan
diverge. **Modern Balishan** is the national and administrative descendant centered on the capital;
**Kaha'ani** is its urban dialect and cultural adjective. Vartonnian continues the western branch, while
Urathan descends from the imperial language of the academy community and overlays its vernacular with a
deliberately re-Halaurized Collegiate register. Full transmission: `Imperial Balishan.md`.

The first productive daughter morphology now makes that scope difference regular. Imperial ***-an***
marks polity or territorial affiliation, deriving *Balishan* and *Urathan* after final-vowel coalescence.
Eastern ***-i*** marks settlement or urban affiliation, deriving *Kaha'ani* from the whole title. It is
not LOL bound *-'an* inside *Kaha'an*: the full stack is *kaha + -'an + -i*. Similar endings in
*Terinoki, Gunastran,* or *Erulian* remain other-language material.

## Beyond Balisha

The above describes the imperial-heartland linguistic situation. The continent has other language
families used beyond the original Balishan/Halauri contact zone. Their deepest relationships to
Ancestral Continental remain unassigned until their peoples are developed:

- **Urathan** — a national descendant of Imperial Balishan, not a separate language family. Differentiation
  began while the academy was still an imperial institution and continued after Uratha's secession in 695
  BR, giving it at least 349 years of independent development by Book 1. Its scholarly register preserves
  and **deliberately imports** unusually heavy Old Halauri vocabulary: scholars revive old roots, prefer
  learned doublets, and coin new forms in an ancient register because antiquity signifies dignity and
  precision. Urathan is nevertheless not frozen court Balishan; its ordinary speech has its own
  innovations, pronunciations, and social varieties. Modern Urathans pride themselves on speaking what
  they consider the "purest" descendant of Imperial Balishan, which the Vartonnese find ridiculous and the
  Kaha'ani simply ignore. Full register model: `Languages/Urathan.md`.
- **Terinoki** — northern, the language of Terinok and the wolf-clan peoples. No Halauric genealogy is
  assumed. Hard consonants and *-ok / -ama / -an* endings mark its current naming register. Later military
  and trade contact can produce loans without making it a Halauric daughter.
- **Old Gunastran** — currently treated as a distinct family, with heavy consonant clusters and a harsh
  phonological register. The Gunastrans inherited Halauri magitech without shifting as deeply toward the
  prestige language. Whether Old Gunastran is distantly Ancestral Continental remains open.
- **Erulian** — Latinate-feeling, possibly a distant cousin of Old Halauri or possibly a parallel-developed prestige tongue. Linguistic affinity unclear.
- **Far Eastern (working name)** — the language of peoples beyond the empire's old reach. Distinctly monosyllabic feel (e.g. *Sa Ko Ren*). Rare in current Elvandar; speakers are typically foreign-born or descended from displaced eastern minorities.

These are sketched here only enough to keep new names in-register. The deep work is in the Halauri/Balishan side.

## Phonological Signatures

### Late Open Halauri public contact register

The first actual phonological stage is established in `Late Open Halauri.md`:

- five stable vowels **a, e, i, o, u**, with hiatus and a small set of diphthongs;
- default penultimate stress and little public-register vowel reduction;
- ordinary **p b t d k g**, so the old claim that *b, d, g* were nearly absent is retired;
- **th** /θ/, **sh** /ʃ/, rare learned/name-register **x** /ɕ/, and phonemic **'** /ʔ/;
- predominantly `(C)(G)V(V)(C)` syllables, with public speech avoiding dense non-glide onset clusters;
- clear linking vowels in long derived words and compounds.

The apostrophe is a real consonant in this stage, but it is not a universal family marker: Terinoki
romanization may use the same punctuation for an unrelated sound or boundary. Nor is every word ending in
*-an, -ion, -ara,* or *-eth* automatically Halauri; endings require a demonstrated derivation.

### Ancestral Continental substrate

The minimal profile in `Ancestral Continental.md` has the same five vowel qualities but initial root
stress, unstressed centralization, broader onset and coda clusters, and no shared /θ/, /ɕ/, or /ʔ/
contrast. Its speakers adapt those Late Open sounds differently, disfavor hiatus, reduce linking vowels,
and sometimes lose final vowels. Those pressures create the raw material for Surface-Halauric clusters,
shorter forms, and inherited/learned doublets.

### Surface Halauric and later Balishan stages

`Early Surface Halauric.md` now orders the shared changes and the first Marresonnian/Vartonnian split:
root-initial stress, vowel reduction and loss, hiatus repair, regional consonant mergers, syncope, stronger
codas, and boundary assimilation. `Imperial Balishan.md` carries those channels through the capital
transfer and first daughter split. Modern Balishan is still not to be projected backward: actual roots and
closed lexical tests must demonstrate word-level outcomes before additional etymologies are accepted.

### The Long Knives: a lost name and a modern one

**Long Knives** is the reader-translation of the guild's current Modern Balishan name. It is not a
translation of its founding title. The order began at Hauren's sealing as human custodians of the Brass
Door approach; it almost certainly received a Late Open Halauri institutional name, but two thousand
years erased that name along with the explanation of its mandate. The modern compound remembers the
weapon and assassination craft the order retained, not the threshold it was created to guard.

Do not coin the lost title as decorative Halauri. Its semantic burden is known—trusted human custodians
charged to deny the final approach—but its form must wait for institutional vocabulary tested through the
grammar.
Full historical account: `Offstage.md`, “The First Charge of the Long Knives.”

## Who Gets a Second Name

*Author's ruling, 2026-08-21. **This is a Balishan custom, not a law of the world** — see the scope note at the end before applying it anywhere else.*

**A second name is a grant, and only the throne grants one.** In Balisha a house name is a thing conferred — on a family, for service or for money, by an emperor or by the Coterie acting in an emperor's absence — and it is conferred rarely enough that carrying one is itself the statement. Nobles have them. **Commoners have one name and no more.**

The native legal category is mixed Imperial ***seldal***, **SEL-dahl**, /ˈsel.dal/: LOL *sel-* “enter or
constitute in authoritative public record” before CON ***dal***, **DAHL**, /dal/, “the functioning
household maintained under one provision, roof, and domestic authority.” A *dal* can contain blood kin,
married-in members, wards, servants, and dependants and need not be noble. A *seldal* is the registered,
chartered body reader-facing English capitalizes as **House**. Neither word names the physical building.

**Five things remain separate:** blood, House membership, the second name carried in records, heirship,
and present office. Marriage can change membership and name without changing blood. Disinheritance can
remove heirship without making a name vanish from every ledger that day. Bearing a name does not itself
make someone head of the House. Marriage instruments decide which House receives a spouse and recognized
children; individual charters decide succession. Balisha therefore has no universal paternal, maternal,
male-preference, or firstborn rule to which Rosik, Tania, and Xion must be exceptions.

House names share the legal slot, not one suffix. They can preserve opaque native names (*Kemvimore,
Hadris*), fossil patronymics (*Larannas*), neo-Halauri prestige formations (*Valanar*), recent purchased
grants (*Darfi*), complete foreign names recognized in Kaha'ani law (*Sa Ko Ren*), or a stolen house-shaped
name (*Dermir*). Semantically transparent House names may be translated for the reader, as *Greystone* is;
opaque names remain in their in-world sound-shapes.

This is not a rule the books had to be bent to fit. **They were already written this way** and nobody noticed: *Cullen, Korvin, Jorik, Varris, Vex, Rykan, Sela, Deshan, Deshi, Old Taren, Grandmother Shira, Brennek, Ormuth, Lira, Garrin, Bittek* — every commoner in the register goes by one name. The rule was reverse-engineered from the practice, not imposed on it.

**How commoners identify each other: bynames.** A trade, a street, a parent, a feature — *Tam, twelve, the boy with the bandaged hand*. A byname is not a name and does not descend; it is how you find the right person in a crowd when the crowd has forty Tams. Slavers, bailiffs and debt-writs therefore have to **describe** people, which is a texture worth using: the writ that cannot name you has to point at you.

**Honorific plus one name is the commoner shape** — *Master Fen, Mistress Janice, Mistress Keya, Master
Keelen* — and it is why Xion's alias worked for eight years. The native honorific is genderless CON
***sar*** /sar/, **SAHR**, placed before the name or byname. English supplies *Master* for a man and
*Mistress* for a woman; Balishan does not change the word. When the referent is unknown, English may keep
the established public *Master*, as Elara does in “the original Master Fen” before she knows who taught
Xion. The title recognizes an established adult or someone acting in a respected skilled capacity. It is
neither a second name nor a professional licence.

The English words are broader than this one native title. Noble *young master*, *master of the Grain*,
*master of the Shadow Corps*, achieved mastery grades, and the Long Knives' unique *Mistress* do not
establish uses of *sar*; their Balishan forms remain uncoined.

### Ronas Dermir took his

*Dermir* is a house name on a man with no house. He was a slaver lieutenant in the pre-Rending Slums who took a name the way he took the title of **Slaver King** — `People/Ronas Dermir.md`: *"The title was not granted by anyone — there was no one to grant it. He declared it, and then he enforced the declaration by being who he was."* Both are the same act, and the name is the earlier one. Nobody in the Coterie has ever said so out loud, which is its own kind of acknowledgement.

**The one line that already knew.** B1 ch18, Ronas to Xion: *"Also heard you been callin' yourself Master Fen down here. Patchin' people up. **Got yourself a name off it.**"* Read it literally. The only two men in Kaha'an who took their names off the work are in that room, and one of them is pointing it out.

### Fen was never a surname

**It is the word for the work.** Mira had no house name because no servant does. *Fen* is what you call a woman who grinds poultices, and *Mira Fen* is *Mira the herb-woman* — so **"Master Fen" was never a name at all. It was a job**, which is what made it the most anonymous thing a hunted man could hide behind, and why B3 ch1's undersecretary has to spell it (*"F, e, n"*): he knows the word perfectly well; he has simply never had to write it in the column where houses go.

The historical derivation is now controlled. CON *fen* /fen/ is a compact practical-work root meaning
“prepare plant remedies; herbal medicine as skilled work.” Its stressed CVC shape survives the
Surface-Halauric sound changes unchanged. Zero derivation lets the same form name the activity and one
practitioner. It has no gender: “herb-woman” is an explanatory English gloss, not a feminine suffix.

**Do not widen it to all medicine.** *Fen* is the herbal/apothecary trade. Mira was both a *fen* and a
***len*** /len/, a healer; Xion learned *fen* from her and extended the practice into wounds, infections,
surgery, and broad care. A *len* need not be a *fen*. Learned ***sathen*** /ˈsa.θen/ is the diagnostic
practitioner translated *physician*: a competence category rather than a crown licence. Xion is all three;
Mira is securely *fen* and *len* without being assigned the learned title.

Practical-site ***-um*** distinguishes people from workplaces. ***Lenum*** /ˈle.nəm/ is a clinic—a
continuing arrangement that receives patients, not merely a room—while ***fenum*** /ˈfe.nəm/ is an
apothecary shop or herb-work room. Thus Mira is the estate *fen*, Keelen works from a *fenum*, and Master
Fen's back rooms constitute a *lenum* only while the care arrangement survives. Master Keelen uses his
personal name, proving that *Fen* is an available occupational byname rather than every apothecary's
compulsory title.

**Master is separate.** It translates genderless *sar*, the ordinary respect title. Xion supplies no
personal name, so native *sar Fen* has the structure “respected [the] Herb-worker.” Janice's bare *Fen* is
intimate reanalysis from the woman who watched him choose the working identity; it does not make the
public trade-word a birth name.

**This is what B3 ch1 is actually doing.** Xion does not swap one house for another. He asks the imperial record to enter **a common noun where a house name goes** — a dead servant's trade, in the register of the empire, in a margin, because the First Counselor asked and no clerk was going to argue. Nobody in the room can read it. Elara can. Neither says anything.

**And it is why Melina is the only one who ever finds Mira.** You cannot look up *Fen* any more than you can look up *smith*; there were other Fens. There is no record to search, so the route is servants and witnesses and twenty-year-old estate business — which is exactly what `People/Melina Valanar.md` already says it is. A trade-word is *hidden in plain sight* in the literal sense.

**One consequence, permanent:** *Fen* is not a family. There is no Fen line, no Fen cousin, and there never can be.

### Scope — where this rule does not reach

- **Erulius.** Retainers and commoners are Erulian in both halves (see the Erulian section below). The Principality apes Balisha's given names and not its grants, which is a difference worth having.
- **Gunastran, Uratha, Terinok, the Far East, bakarn Silaris.** Their own customs. *Perrin Halmore* is a bakarn dockworker and keeps her name.
- **Clan names are a different category.** *Ashwood* is a timber wolf clan, not a house — `People/Kess Ashwood.md`. Kess is unaffected.
- **Nouveau houses count as houses.** *House Darfi* was bought inside the last twenty years and is looked down on for it, and Farleen is still a noblewoman with a noble's name. Bought and granted are both grants; that is the point of the resentment.

## Naming Conventions by Culture

When coining new names in-register, use these rules of thumb.

### Balishan (ceremonial / imperial / aristocratic)
- Use Old Halauri roots for deliberately learned, royal, ceremonial, or prestige formations—not for
  every aristocratic second name
- Native and mixed charter-names can retain harder substrate shapes and opaque compounds; legal House
  status comes from recognition as a *seldal*, not from a noble-sounding suffix
- Open vowels, soft consonants, and *-an / -ion / -ara / -eth / -anar* remain characteristic of the
  learned prestige layer
- Glottal stop remains reserved for the highest-prestige names, royal titles, sacred sites, and ancient
  artifacts
- Examples of the learned layer: *Tharen, Velana, Korin'al, Selathan, Marianor*; examples of native House
  shapes: *Kemvimore, Larannas, Hadris, Darfi, Issamil*

**Say it aloud before adopting it.** The *-anar* suffix is productive and will be reused, so a new coinage
sharing it must differ from *Valanar* in **consonant, vowel and coda** — not in one of the three. The
war-magic corps was originally **Ver'anar** and had to be renamed to **Tor'anar** in 2026 because *VAL*
against *VER* differs only in the liquid, **which is the least reliable distinction there is in speech**;
on the page it scanned fine and aloud the two collapsed together. Names that read as distinct are not
necessarily names that *hear* as distinct, and the V-initial group is the standing hazard in this cast —
*Valanar, Vartonne, Valor, Valan, Velana* and *Vesk* — and it once held **four unrelated bearers of
*Vex***. All but one were cleared on 2026-08-01: House Vex became **Sedranne**, and the two Gunastrans
became **Ondra Kelgrav** and **Field Commander Bregan Haldorn**, who were additionally off-register,
since Old Gunastran runs to heavy clusters and *-stran / -grav / -dorn* endings. **The survivor is the
Warrens operator in Book 1**, who keeps the name: he is in finished prose, and the text introduces him as
*"Calls himself Vex,"* which marks it in-world as an alias — a short hard name is right for what he is.

**Two renames have already been forced by this, and the second was worse than the first.** *Ver'anar* →
**Tor'anar** fixed a corps whose name differed from *Valanar* only in the liquid. Then *Vrek* → **Draskin**
fixed the Scholar-King of Uratha, whose name was a single syllable away from **Vesk**, the Arol Batae
commander: */vɛsk/* against */vrɛk/*, both V-initial, both four letters, both ending in *-k*, differing
only in the medial cluster. **Short names are the dangerous case**, because there is less material in them
to carry a distinction. **Prefer a non-V initial for any new name**, and say every monosyllable aloud
beside every other monosyllable already in the cast before adopting it.

**Book 4's L/K cluster, cleared 2026-08-02 — and the operative test is co-occurrence, not similarity.**
The book had *Lira* (Harkim's sister), *Kira Voss* (bakarn resistance organizer) and *Lydia Karven*
(Gunastran Chief Acquisitor) at once. What made it a bug rather than an inconvenience is that **Lira and
Kira appear in the same sentences** — `book4_outline.md` had *"when Harkim and Lira are separated… when
he finds her through Kira Voss's network."* Two names being similar in the abstract is survivable; two
names a single sentence has to carry are not. **Check co-occurrence first when triaging a collision, and
rename the one with the smaller footprint** — Lira carried 44 mentions and reaches into Book 8's outline,
Kira carried 8.

Three things fell out of that pass, and they are the pattern to look for:

- ***Kira Voss* → *Perrin Halmore*.** The surname was the worse half of the collision and nobody had
  noticed it: *Kaiden Voss* was then the Book 3 engineer, so two characters one book apart shared a
  surname *and* an initial, which reads as a deliberate family relation that does not exist.
  *(**Voss is now retired entirely.** On 2026-08-04 that character became **Brennek**, the Balishan
  crown surveyor of ch2 — see `Names.md`. *Voss* was itself a collision nobody had caught: one
  syllable, V-initial, sharing an expedition with **Vesk**. Two independent flaws in one unwritten
  name is the argument for keeping the register.)*
  Rename both halves or you fix the smaller problem and keep the larger one. *P* was chosen because it
  was unused anywhere in the series; *Halmore* is one of the Balishan-common examples above.
- ***Lydia Karven* → *Wilka Zarnstran*.** *Karven/Kalden* is the Vesk/Vrek error again — two syllables,
  same initial, and **both Compact-side**, so they can share a paragraph. *Lydia/Lira* was a third L in
  the same book. The old name was also simply off-register: nothing in *Lydia Karven* is Gunastran.
  *Zarnstran* takes the third of the three endings (*Kelgrav* has *-grav*, *Haldorn* has *-dorn*), and
  *Z* is unused by any person in the series — the only Z forms in the canon are Terinok institutional
  terms (*Zhum'kara*, *Zhuhala*), a different register entirely with no bearer.
- ***Lira* and *Lyra Valanar* stay as they are, deliberately.** They are homophones, which is technically
  a worse collision than either of the above — **and it is still not worth fixing.** Lyra is Elara's
  mother, dead before Book 1, five mentions in total, all of them in backstory files, and she never
  shares a scene, a book, or a sentence with Lira. **Recorded here so that it is not "discovered" later**
  and used to argue for renaming Lira, which would be the exact wrong move now that Book 4 has been
  rebalanced around her.

**The general rule this produced: initials are a scarce resource, and a book is the unit to audit.** Book
4 alone had three names competing for *L* and *K* while *P, G, W* and *Z* sat unused across the entire
series. Before coining, list the initials already spoken in that book — not just the series-major cast —
and take one that is free.

**Regnal-name convention:** Valanar monarchs do not reign under their birth names. Upon coronation, the new emperor or empress *takes* a throne-name from a small fixed pool of Old Halauri dynastic names — *Urath, Valan, Tharen, Sera, Mariath, Korin* — and is numbered in sequence with every prior bearer (*Urath I–IV, Valan I–VII, Tharen I–II, Sera I–VIII*, and so on). The numeral asserts continuity with the entire line of that name; the dynasty's legitimacy is partly constructed out of those sequences. The birth name is set aside at coronation and survives only in family records.

**The pool accumulated; it did not predate the throne.** **Valan I Valanar** founded the Balishan Empire in
0 BR and made *Valan* the first imperial throne-name. Later rulers added names to the recognized pool, and
reuse turned those names into numbered lines of continuity. By Elara's age the pool feels immemorial, but
its oldest sequence begins with the empire itself.

This is the convention behind **Emperor Urath IV Valanar**, founder of Uratha: born under some other name now of interest only to genealogists, he took the throne-name *Urath* upon ascension because it was his turn in a sequence — the fourth emperor of that name in the Valanar line. The throne-name choice itself was an imperial statement (he could have picked any name then due in rotation; he picked the one that signaled "*the High One, returning*").

The Vartonnese imperial tradition treats throne-name taking as part of what *constitutes* a valid coronation — not a decorative flourish but a load-bearing ritual act. By that reading, **Empress Elara** has not quite performed the rite. She was raised by the Arol Batae and never taught the convention; she takes the throne under her birth name, with no Old Halauri throne-name and no numeral, because that is who she is and no one ever told her there was anything else to do. Vartonne reads this not as a chosen snub from a pool of throne-names (there *is* no Elara in the pool — the construction is incoherent in their tradition, the equivalent of *King Joe of England*) but as a procedural defect: a coronation that omitted one of its essential ritual elements. The fact that she calls herself *Empress* at all is, in their view, a kind of polite fiction the rest of the continent is going along with. See [Elara Valanar.md](../People/Elara Valanar.md) for the full beat.

### Balishan (common / native human / non-noble)
- **One name only.** Second names are grants and commoners do not receive them — see **Who Gets a Second Name**, above. Coin one name, not two, and reach for a byname (trade, street, parent, feature) when a scene needs to tell two of them apart
- Draw from Proto-Balishan roots
- Harder consonants, consonant clusters, V-initial common, *-onne / -ore / -ek / -in / -annas* endings
- No glottal stop
- Examples: *Brennek, Tovanne, Halmore, Doric, Vannas*

### Halauri (when surface characters encounter Halauri inscriptions or, in extraordinary circumstances, living Halauri)
- Establish the historical stage first: Foundational, Open, Sealed, and Terminal Halauri are not one
  interchangeable naming language
- Use the phonology in `Late Open Halauri.md` only for late open public/contact forms; archaic and
  terminal forms require their own changes
- Compounds may be long while roots remain short, and linking vowels keep public forms syllabically clear
- No universal Halauri personal-name length has been established; household, civic, ritual, and
  documentary forms may differ

### Urathan
- Old Halauri ceremonial register for institutions, scholarly titles, the Tower system
- Urathan vernacular, descended from Imperial Balishan, for ordinary personal and place names
- "Old Urathan" means an earlier stage of this Urathan branch, not a sister language of Proto-Balishan
- Collegiate Urathan deliberately revives and coins Old-Halauri-style forms; not every learned word is
  genuinely ancient

### Erulian
- Latinate feel throughout — this is the register, not just the prestige layer
- Open syllables, liquid consonants (*l, r*) doing much of the work, no consonant clusters heavier than two
- **No glottal stop.** An Erulian name with a `'` in it is wrong
- Signature endings: *-ius / -us / -ian / -us / -ine / -anus*
- Examples to coin from: *Salvian, Ostrus, Verrian, Tarrus, Aventine, Corvinus, Naevius, Marcian*

**Given names are frequently Balishan, surnames Erulian**, on the evidence available — *Kalden* is Proto-Balishan (*"The Steady One"*) carried by the house of *Erulius*. A principality whose entire identity is a claim on the **Balishan** imperial succession has an obvious reason to name its sons in the language of the throne it wants, and the pattern should be assumed to run through the nobility. Retainers and commoners are Erulian in both halves.

**Worked example.** *Salvian Ostrus* — the last captain of the Aspirant Guard (`Offstage.md`, "The Recruitment of Kalden Erulius"). *Salv-* is an Erulian root for *keeping, preserving, holding in trust*, which is an ordinary and slightly old-fashioned thing to name a child, and which the man's life makes quietly ironic: he preserved nothing. Nobody in the books remarks on it and nobody should.

### Gunastran
- Heavy consonant clusters, *-stran / -grav / -dorn* endings
- Examples: *Korstran, Belgrav, Hadorn, Ungost*

### Terinoki
- Hard consonants, *-ok / -ama / -an / -in* endings
- Examples: *Takama, Shizan, Vorinok, Daman*

### Far Eastern
- Monosyllabic compounds, three or four short syllables strung together
- Examples: *Sa Ko Ren, Tan Wei, Mi Ko*

## Open Threads

A few questions left deliberately open. Decide as the prose demands.

- **Human-Halauri ancestry.** The 3,000-year open period was long enough that genuine ancestral mixing is possible. Whether any Balishan noble bloodlines carry distant Halauri ancestry — and whether anyone alive knows it — is undecided
- **Ancestral Continental boundaries.** The human substrate is settled; its relationship to Terinoki, Old
  Gunastran, Erulian, and other languages is not. Do not collapse them into one family for neatness before
  their speakers and histories exist
- **Modern Halauri drift.** Two thousand years of separate development make late inscriptions a genuine
  translation problem, but the degree of difference is not yet fixed. Establish it from the historical
  stages rather than choosing an analogy in advance
- ~~**The forgotten name of modern Kaha'an.**~~ *Resolved:* **Marresonne**, “Harbor Settlement,” from LOL
  *marreth + onna*. It shares the settlement head with mixed CON–LOL *Vartonne*, “Settlement at the
  Confluence.” “Sea-place” and “Heart-place” remain the traditional broad translations. See
  `Halauric Core Lexicon.md`.
- **Archaic Halauri.** The form of Halauri spoken in Hauren's founding centuries (5,000–4,000 years ago) — almost certainly different from the form transmitted to humans much later. Survives only in the oldest inscriptions inside Hauren itself. Probably never needs to surface in the prose
