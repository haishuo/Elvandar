# Balishan

**Status:** Historical and sociolinguistic foundation, begun 2026-08-21. The Vartonne refounding, realm
and capital distinction, founding coalition, 500 BR population transfer, imperial register split, and
first daughter-language trajectories are settled. Phonology and grammar live in the family-stage
documents; `Imperial Balishan.md` governs the imperial hinge and `Halauric Core Lexicon.md` the controlled
roots.

## Scope

*Balishan* belongs to the Halauric family but currently covers several things that must eventually be
distinguished:

- the related or interacting human varieties spoken around pre-imperial Vartonne and Marresonne;
- the administrative, military, legal, and trade standards built by the empire after 0 BR;
- the court language before and after the capital moved in 500 BR;
- regional descendants across the successor states;
- modern Balishan, centered on Kaha'an and transformed by the Rending.

Calling all of these one language would conceal the history the conlang is meant to recover. Until the
family tree exists, **Pre-imperial Heartland**, **Early Imperial Balishan**, **Late Imperial Balishan**, and
**Modern Balishan** are period labels rather than claims that every speaker used one homogeneous form.

## The pre-imperial settlements

### Vartonne

Vartonne existed before the empire as an inhabited river settlement at the convergence of three waterways
on exceptionally fertile plains. Its location supported farming, fishing, river transport, irrigation, and
seasonal flood management. The surrounding culture preserves sacred groves, agricultural rites, river
traditions, and practical communities later absorbed into the imperial capital.

The surviving form *Vartonne* is older than the capital built over it. It derives regularly from CON
*vart* “confluence; meeting-center” plus borrowed LOL *onna* “established settlement”: **“Settlement at
the Confluence.”** Traditional **“Heart-place”** is a legitimate poetic development of “meeting-center,”
later made irresistible by the city's imperial role, but it is not the narrow word-for-word gloss.

### Marresonne

The coastal settlement later called Kaha'an was Marresonne, a deep-water port above the sealed city of
Hauren. It was already an established trade city before the imperial court arrived in 500 BR. Its people
occupied the one surface location with the clearest route to direct Halauri contact during Hauren's
open period, although that contact ended roughly a millennium before the Balishan Empire was founded.

The name combines LOL *marreth* “sheltered deep-water sea-harbor” with *onna*: **“Harbor Settlement.”**
Traditional **“Sea-place”** is an accurate broad translation. The shared settlement head makes Vartonne
and Marresonne members of one old toponymic layer, while their stems come from different sources. That
supports a contact and naming corridor; it does not imply one state, ethnicity, or uniform language.

### The corridor between them

The two settlements were not adjacent: Vartonne lies in the west and Marresonne farther east at the
future site of Kaha'an. Calling them sister-settlements therefore describes an old commercial and naming
relationship, not twin towns on the same river. The eventual reconstruction must explain how people,
marriage, goods, religious forms, and words moved between the river heartland and the coastal port.

This corridor is the route by which the LOL settlement head *onna* and Marresonnian documentary spelling
**-onne** reached western communities. It also carried other Surface-Halauric speech, contact-period
texts, and later learned forms toward the empire eventually founded at Vartonne. That one demonstrated
route does not decide the ancestry of every heartland word.

## The imperial refounding of Vartonne — settled 2026-08-21

Vartonne was not created from empty ground by Valan I Valanar. At the empire's founding in 0 BR, he established
a permanent military and administrative camp **at the older settlement**, choosing a defensible river
convergence capable of feeding and moving an imperial population. The camp absorbed the town and was
rebuilt outward as the planned concentric capital.

Both inherited accounts are therefore true at different scales:

- the settlement and the name *Vartonne* are pre-imperial;
- the monumental ring-city, imperial institutions, and capital called *Kaha'an* begin with Valan I.

Imperial histories call Valan I the city's founder because the city they value is the planned capital.
The absorbed riverside quarters, farming communities, sacred groves, and the name itself preserve the
older layer that official history minimizes. When the throne moved in 500 BR, the title *Kaha'an* moved
with it and the old name surfaced again.

This also calibrates House Kemvimore's history. The family can be a founding house whose lineage "predates
the city" because *city* there means Valan I's imperial capital. Its recorded six centuries of agricultural
dominance date the mature estate system, not necessarily the first existence of the family.

## The founding dynasty — settled 2026-08-22

**Valan I Valanar** founded the Balishan Empire in 0 BR. House Valanar then held the imperial line through
the empire's seven centuries and continued ruling the remnant afterward. The overview's former reference to
"several dynasties" describes successive reigning branches within House Valanar: senior lines ended,
marriages and cadet lines carried succession onward, and different branches changed the character of the
court without breaking the house's claimed continuity.

The regnal-name pool grew out of that history. Valan I made *Valan* the first imperial throne-name; later
monarchs added *Urath, Tharen, Sera, Mariath, Korin* and reused earlier names with numerals. The fixed pool
known to Vartonne and missed by Elara is the end state of centuries, not a complete institution that existed
before the founding emperor.

## The founding compact — settled 2026-08-22

Valan's empire began as a coalition rather than a finished conquest state. His own following supplied
command, arbitration, and the new throne; Vartonne's river and agricultural communities supplied the first
capital's food, transport, and daily institutional base; Marresonne's merchants and scribes supplied
commercial networks, record-keeping, and transmitted Halauri learning.

Terinok joined on deliberately different terms. Valan demonstrated martial worth recognized across its
fiercely independent cantons, which swore their blades to him without surrendering the Warrior's Code,
canton law, or internal command. Their oath made Terinok the empire's military arm while preserving the
sovereignty that later emperors would mistake for provincial privilege. The edict placing the Warrior's
Code beneath imperial appeal therefore broke the founding relationship; it did not merely provoke a
restive province.

This supplies the cultural base for a Vartonnian river-and-market koine broadened through campaigning and
trade, then formalized in part through Marresonnian scribal practice and learned vocabulary. Terinoki
companies could retain their own speech and command structures while lending martial terms to the shared
imperial language. The full foundation is in `Early Balishan Culture.md`.

## Realm and capital are different names — settled 2026-08-22

There is no contradiction in a Balishan Empire governed from Vartonne. *Balisha* names the realm, people,
country, or an earlier polity; *Vartonne* names a city; *Kaha'an* is the transferable title borne by the
city holding the throne. France was not required to call itself Paris because its kings ruled from Paris,
and Balisha is not required to call itself Vartonne because its first emperors ruled there.

The title's route now supports that distinction. LOL *Kaha'an* combines *kaha*, sovereignty understood as
an office, with bound *-'an*, the seat in which that authority is instantiated. The Valanar court borrowed
the whole word through Hieratic instruction and applied it to Vartonne; moving the throne in 500 BR moved
the title. This is learned vocabulary doing constitutional work, not an inherited city-name accidentally
shared by two places.

`Design Plates/Balishan Realm and Province Names - Plate 1.md` gives the approved answer without making
the capital and realm the same referent. It derives pre-imperial ***Balisha***, “share-country,” from the
Vartonne river-and-field communities' system of apportioned water, land, yield, and labor. The name widens
from western country to Valan's polity and then to the portable imperial state. It separately derives the
eastern province containing Marresonne/Kaha'an as ***Litrava*** from Halauri ***Litarava***,
*lita + rava*, “the Community of the Opening.” The inherited “Green Country” analysis and the briefly
approved *Koyara/Koira* suite are retired.

Do not infer that the founding dynasty came from Marresonne merely because the empire is called Balishan.
That remains possible, but it requires independent cultural evidence.

## Historical stages

### Pre-imperial heartland varieties

The evidence currently consists of place names, geography, surviving customs, and later institutional
claims. At least a western river variety and an eastern coastal variety should be assumed possible. Both
belong to the human Surface-Halauric continuum descended from open-period contact speech, with an older
Ancestral Continental substrate. Their degree of separation remains open.

Direct Halauri contact belongs to this period, principally through Marresonne. Ordinary inherited forms,
substrate vocabulary, and borrowings entering at different dates across Hauren's three-thousand-year open
period may already have looked unrelated by the time the empire formed.

After the sealing and before the empire, Marresonnian record-keepers classify the ancient tunnel network
as ***Arol Rhutan***, “Old Tunnels.” Learned *arol* modifies the community's inherited *rhutan*, regularly
descended from LOL collective *re-hutan*. The later imperial administration inherits this local registry
name with the city; it is not the lost name used by the builders below.

### Early Imperial Balishan, 0–500 BR

The capital at Vartonne brings together the older local population, Valan I's military following,
administrators, merchants, nobles, laborers, and subjects from incorporated regions. Imperial Balishan
emerges from institutions rather than appearing fully formed in Year 0. Its likely grammatical center is
Vartonne's Surface-Halauric river-and-market speech, broadened into a trade and campaign koine.
Marresonnian merchants and scribes help shape its commercial and written registers and borrow afresh from
Hieratic Old Halauri. Terinoki canton companies remain internally autonomous and need not abandon their
own languages in order to fight beside imperial forces.

This is the period that coins ***Kaha Batae***, “Imperial Guard,” from learned LOL *kaha* and *batae* <
*bat-ae*, “appointed guard; commissioned guarding body.” Its full vowels and retained hiatus are court
choices, not the ordinary outcome of Surface Halauric inheritance. The later guard-title therefore
already carries a millennium of human reinterpretation before the Rending changes its first word.

The future reconstruction must distinguish:

- household speech;
- river and market speech;
- army command;
- chancery and law;
- court ceremony;
- learned Old Halauri traditions;
- provincial use by people whose first language remains local.

These registers feed the ancestor of the modern national language, but they do not begin identical and do
not immediately displace provincial household languages.

### Late Imperial Balishan, 500–750 BR

The transfer begins as a nominally temporary or seasonal eastern court and becomes permanent across
successive reigns. The imperial household, Kaha Batae, resident envoys, portable treasury, maritime and
foreign offices, ambitious noble branches, financiers, clerks, artisans, servants, and suppliers follow
the throne. They encounter an established port population rather than empty land. Families and offices
split across the two cities rather than moving as indivisible blocks.

Vartonne retains the Hall of Records, land and grain administration, old courts, cathedral establishments,
hereditary offices, estate machinery, and most of its ordinary population. The move therefore creates two
prestige centers before political dissolution begins: innovative, commercial Kaha'an and conservative,
procedural Vartonne.

The resulting Transferred Court Balishan keeps the common imperial SVO and prepositional grammar but
acquires a coastal center: Marresonnian /s/ outcomes, fuller weak vowels, harbor vocabulary, and local
speech enter prestige usage beside compact western forms already distributed by the koine. Hieratic
renewal intensifies at court. Vartonne preserves old formulas and verb-final legal structures in writing
and ceremony while its living spoken language continues western syncope and final-vowel loss. Full model:
`Imperial Balishan.md`.

### Modern Balishan

After dissolution, Kaha'an's variety develops under trade, court continuity, and the shrinking political
meaning of *Balisha*. **Modern Balishan** is the national and administrative descendant; **Kaha'ani** is
the urban dialect and cultural adjective of the capital. The terms overlap for many speakers but differ in
scope.

The difference is grammatical as well as political. Early Imperial ***-an*** marks relation to a polity
or territorial people: *Balisha + -an > Balishan*. Eastern local ***-i*** marks relation to a settlement
or urban community: *Kaha'an + -i > Kaha'ani*. A Kaha'ani house is locally characteristic of the capital;
a Balishan law belongs to the realm. The same person can be both. Native *Kaha'ani* is
**kah-HAH-nee**, /kaˈha.ni/; instructed historical **kah-HAH-ah-nee**, /ka.ˈha.ʔa.ni/, preserves the
transparent suffix boundary and learned glottal stop.

Native Kaha'ani pronounce the written title ***Kaha'an*** as **kah-HAN**, /kaˈhan/. The city-name is used
too constantly for the older three-syllable form to remain ordinary local speech. Historical
**kah-HAH-an**, /ka.ˈha.ʔan/, survives in fixed ceremony and learned citation; using it in everyday
conversation sounds book-taught and can expose an outsider. The spelling does not contract with the
speech.

The Rending then destroys the surrounding towns and concentrates survivors inside the capital within one
generation. This produces dialect leveling, not a new language in twenty years. Adults retain regional
habits; children and mixed neighborhoods favor widely understood alternatives; the Warrens develop dense
occupational and neighborhood varieties. Court and noble speech remain more continuous because the city's
institutions, records, and tutors survive inside the shield.

The surviving *Kaha Batae* then rename themselves ***Arol Batae***. They do not translate the institution
into ordinary Modern Balishan: they replace learned *kaha*, the authority of a living empire, with learned
*arol*, the old or former order, and keep *batae*. The language makes the claim exactly—the commissioned
body remains, but what it guards has become memory.

The current prose does not need conspicuous dialect spelling to acknowledge this. Differences can live in
word choice, address, syntax, professional vocabulary, and what a speaker regards as formal.

### Canonical lost-province names

`Design Plates/Modern Balishan - Plate 2.md` establishes the bounded canonical suite for pre-Rending
Litrava. The lost settlements are **Talrava, Gonmer, Yelava, Unava, Tirun, Feshber,** and **Durcai**. The
principal landmarks and works are **Dunyelas** forest, **Dunkargar** fortress, **Limel Mar** Great Plain,
**Gonmel Mar** Great Grainlands, **Yeltal** river, **Yelpas** forest passage, **Unpel Dun** northern
aqueduct, **Maulom** harbor basin, and the **Maukaras** reef coast.

This is a bounded approval within the otherwise working Modern Balishan proposal. The names are historical
canon and the forms required to derive them are canonical in that use. They do not require the reader to
receive a lost atlas, and they do not make the rest of Modern Balishan Plate 1 canonical by implication.

## Halauri ancestry and prestige

Balishan receives Halauri material through several distinct routes:

- ordinary descent through human-nativized Surface Halauric;
- direct pre-imperial borrowings entering at different times and places;
- Ancestral Continental substrate retained through the language shift;
- surface scholarly and religious traditions maintained after the sealing;
- inherited technical vocabulary whose original source has been forgotten;
- deliberate imperial use of old forms for titles, legitimacy, law, and ceremony;
- later Urathan re-Halaurization feeding learned terms back into the wider world.

These routes should produce doublets and competing pronunciations rather than one clean layer of "elven
words." A farmer's ordinary word may be the regularly changed descendant of a Halauri root; a royal
title may borrow the older form from a manuscript; an Urathan academic may later coin from it again.
Terminal Halauri can preserve a collateral cognate changed inside the sealed city.

The first actual sets are demonstrated in `Halauric Core Lexicon.md`: LOL *onna* yields coastal *onne*,
western *onn*, and imperial written **-onne**, while the two city stems retain their different histories;
the imperial cluster then separates inherited, Hieratic, and human learned routes. `Imperial Balishan.md`
turns the older sound channels—western /t/, coastal /s/, learned /θ/; inherited root stress versus learned
penultimate stress; ordinary hiatus repair versus careful retention—into the first controlled
correspondence set across the imperial language and its daughters.

## First ordinary word: *fen*

CON ***fen*** /fen/, **FEN**, means “prepare plant remedies; herbal medicine as skilled work.” The root
survives unchanged because its stressed CVC shape has no weak vowel, hiatus, or unfamiliar consonant for
the Surface-Halauric changes to alter. Its practical plant-and-body meaning belongs exactly where human
substrate vocabulary is strongest.

Modern Balishan uses zero derivation: ***fen*** can be the work or one practitioner according to syntax.
It is number-neutral when context or a numeral supplies number, and it carries no gender. Capital *Fen*
in *Mira Fen* or *Master Fen* is author-facing treatment of a byname or title, not different spoken
morphology.

The semantic limit matters. A *fen* is an herb-worker or apothecary, not every healer. Mira combined that
trade with broad care; Xion began in it and extended his practice much further. *Master Fen* is native *sar
Fen*, using the ordinary respect title established below; Master Keelen may practice the same trade under
his personal name, while Xion's anonymous form means “respected [the] Herb-worker.”

The three story forms are consequently different grammatical acts: *Mira Fen* is name plus occupational
byname; *Master Fen* is honorific plus trade used as a complete working identity; *Xion Fen* is the crown
allowing a common trade noun to occupy the house-name field. None creates a Fen lineage.

## Ordinary respectful address: *sar*

Modern Balishan ***sar*** /sar/, **SAHR**, is an invariant title placed before the one personal name or
occupational byname of a socially established adult. Age, household standing, or recognized skilled work
can make the title appropriate; it does not certify a profession or confer nobility.

The native language does not distinguish *Master* from *Mistress*. Both are English translations of the
same genderless form. If the referent is unknown, English can retain the established public form *Master*;
this is why Elara can infer “the original Master Fen” before learning anything about Mira.

| Balishan analysis | Reader-facing form | Why the title fits |
|---|---|---|
| *sar Fen* | **Master Fen** | a recognized healer acting under an occupational byname |
| *sar Janice* | **Mistress Janice** | an older skilled weaver and shop proprietor |
| *sar Keelen* | **Master Keelen** | an elderly apothecary addressed by his personal name |
| *sar Keya* | **Mistress Keya** | an older patient accorded ordinary adult respect |

*Sar* sits outside the name, carries no agreement, and may be omitted in intimate, hurried, or hostile
speech. It is not the native word behind noble *young master*, a cartel master, the master of the Shadow
Corps, a mastery grade, or the Long Knives' unique *Mistress*. Those English senses remain uncoined and
must not be collapsed into this ordinary commoner title.

## The medical register

Modern Balishan separates what English often lets overlap:

| Form | Pronunciation | Reader translation | Core distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***len*** | **LEN**, /len/ | heal; healing; healer | broad treatment of living bodies; verb, practice, or practitioner by syntax |
| ***sathen*** | **SAH-then**, /ˈsa.θen/ | physician | learned diagnostic knowledge and practiced medical competence |
| ***lenum*** | **LEN-əm**, /ˈle.nəm/ | clinic | continuing site or practice arranged to receive patients |
| ***fen*** | **FEN**, /fen/ | herb-work; herb-worker/apothecary | plant-remedy craft or practitioner |
| ***fenum*** | **FEN-əm**, /ˈfe.nəm/ | apothecary shop/workroom | continuing site arranged for herb-work |

*Len* is substrate vocabulary and zero-derives its practitioner just as *fen* does. *Lenum* and *fenum*
add reduced substrate service-site suffix *-um*; they classify recurring organized work, not architecture.
Master Fen's back room is therefore a *lenum* while the practice operates and merely two rooms after it
ends. Keelen is a *fen* and *len*; “Keelen's apothecary” as a place is his *fenum*.

*Sathen* is learned LOL *sath-en*: “one practiced in discerning a body's internal condition from signs.”
It retains formal /θ/ through technical and Hieratic teaching. It is competence-based, not a crown licence
or palace office. Xion can truthfully be both *len* and *sathen*; the imperial household's robes mark the
appointment of its physicians, not the existence of the category.

These are overlapping axes rather than ranks. Xion is *fen, len,* and *sathen*. Mira is securely *fen*
and *len*; nothing requires placing her in the formal *sathen* register. The native terms for medicine as
a mass category, surgery, midwifery, hospitals, and other specialties remain uncoined.

## Household and chartered House

Modern Balishan distinguishes ordinary ***dal***, **DAHL**, /dal/, from legal ***seldal***,
**SEL-dahl**, /ˈsel.dal/. A *dal* is the functioning household maintained under one provision, roof, and
domestic authority; it can include blood kin, married-in members, wards, servants, and dependants. It is
not the building. A *seldal* is that social category transformed by authoritative recognition into the
continuing legal body reader-facing English capitalizes as **House**.

The word is historically mixed: learned LOL *sel-* “enter or constitute in authoritative public record”
precedes inherited CON *dal*. That mirrors the institution. Households predate the empire; the chancery
act that gives one a second name, charter, property, and succession rules belongs to imperial government.
The throne grants that standing, and the Coterie grants it only by claiming to act in the throne's absence.

Five associated facts remain separable:

1. **blood** — biological descent;
2. **membership** — recognized inclusion in the *seldal*;
3. **name** — the second name carried in public records;
4. **heirship** — the right or expectation of succession;
5. **office** — current authority to head or act for the House.

Marriage settlements decide which House receives a spouse and recognized children; there is no universal
paternal or maternal default. Each charter can govern succession differently. Rosik therefore enters
Kemvimore by marriage and later heads it; Xion can lose heirship while records still call him Kemvimore;
and his later marriage can enter him and his children into reigning House Valanar without changing his
ancestry.

House names share a legal slot, not a linguistic suffix. *Kemvimore* and ***Hadris*** /ˈhad.ris/ are
opaque native charter-names; *Larannas* preserves fossil patronymic *-annas* “descendants of Lar”;
*Valanar* is a human neo-Halauri prestige dynasty; *Sa Ko Ren* is a foreign name recognized whole;
*Dermir* is an ungranted name Ronas forced into social fact; and *Greystone* is a reader-translation whose
native transparent compound remains uncoined.

## Names and social structure already settled

- In modern Balisha, a second name is a grant of the throne or its acting authority. Commoners use one name
  and descriptive bynames.
- An ordinary household is *dal*; a crown-recognized chartered House is *seldal*.
- Noble house names are therefore political artifacts as well as linguistic forms, but do not share one
  productive noble suffix.
- Blood, membership, name, heirship, and office are separate axes.
- Marriage settlements, not a universal gender rule, decide House entry for spouses and children;
  individual charters govern succession.
- Fossil *-annas* explains *Larannas* as “descendants of Lar” without remaining productive.
- *Hadris*, **HAD-riss** /ˈhad.ris/, is the compact native House name borne by Lord Merchant Tobias.
- *Fen* is a trade-word, not a family name.
- *Sar* is a genderless respect title outside the name, translated as *Master* or *Mistress*.
- *Len* “healer” and learned *sathen* “physician” overlap; neither is a rank above the other.
- Practical-site *-um* distinguishes *lenum* “clinic” and *fenum* “apothecary workplace” from the people
  who practice there.
- *Dermir* is a house name Ronas took without grant.
- The protected forms **Xion, Elara, Valanar, Kemvimore, and Rosik** must survive the reconstruction.
- Their inherited etymologies remain testable; protecting a form does not protect a speculative root.
