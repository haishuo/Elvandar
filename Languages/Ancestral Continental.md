# Ancestral Continental

**Status:** Minimal substrate sound profile, established 2026-08-22. `Grammar Foundations.md` supplies
the corresponding structural profile. This is an author-facing reconstruction label, not a known
endonym and not yet proof of a single uniform language.

## Scope

Ancestral Continental names the older human speech underlying the communities that adopted Open
Halauri contact varieties. It may eventually resolve into several close languages rather than one
parent. The profile below identifies shared pressures strong enough to shape Surface Halauric; it does
not assign Terinoki, Old Gunastran, Erulian, Silarian, or any other independent modern language to this
family.

The goal is deliberately minimal. A substrate is not a sack of “hard sounds” poured into Halauri. It is
the speech system carried by adults and children through a language shift: what contrasts they hear, what
they merge, where they place stress, which syllables they preserve, and which native naming habits remain
socially important.

## Vowels

The shared reconstruction has five phonemic vowels:

| Front | Central | Back |
|---|---|---|
| /i e/ | /a/ | /o u/ |

Unstressed vowels centralize toward **[ə]**. That central vowel is initially predictable rather than a
separate phoneme. Repeated reduction and vowel loss can later make it contrastive in particular daughter
varieties.

Vowel length is not reconstructed as a stable common contrast. Diphthongs exist, but adjacent vowels are
generally disfavored across syllables. Speakers resolve hiatus by contraction, glide insertion, or loss.

## Consonants

|  | Labial | Alveolar | Postalveolar | Velar | Glottal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stops | /p b/ | /t d/ | — | /k g/ | — |
| Affricates | — | /ts/ | /tʃ/ | — | — |
| Fricatives | /f v/ | /s z/ | /ʃ/ | /x/ | /h/ |
| Nasals | /m/ | /n/ | — | /ŋ/ | — |
| Liquids | — | /l r/ | — | — | — |
| Glides | /w/ | — | /j/ | — | — |

The common reconstruction has no /θ/, /ɕ/, or phonemic /ʔ/. Individual ancestral varieties may possess
one of them, but the populations most important to the Marresonne–Vartonne corridor do not reliably hear
those Late Open distinctions.

## Phonotactics

The usual root syllable is:

```text
(C)(C)V(C)(C)
```

Permitted initial clusters favor **stop + liquid** (*pr, br, tr, dr, kr, gr, pl, bl, kl, gl*) and
**s + stop** (*sp, st, sk*). Codas freely include stops, nasals, liquids, and **-s**. Clusters across
morpheme boundaries are ordinary.

Roots tend to be compact: **CVC, CCVC, CVCC**. Productive suffixes are usually unstressed. Compounds put
two recognizable roots directly together more readily than Late Open Halauri, whose public register
prefers linking vowels.

This profile does not mean every Ancestral Continental word was monosyllabic or harsh. It means speakers
were comfortable preserving more consonantal information and less unstressed vocalic material than the
Halauri public register.

## Stress and rhythm

The common default is **initial root stress**. Prefixes may remain unstressed; suffixes normally do.
Secondary stress falls on a later compound root.

Consequences during language shift include:

- Late Open penultimate stress is frequently reinterpreted as root-initial stress;
- vowels after the stressed syllable centralize;
- medial unstressed vowels can disappear, creating clusters that did not exist in the contact register;
- suffixes become shorter and less transparently connected to their Halauri sources;
- inherited forms diverge sharply from later Hieratic reborrowings, which restore full vowels.

## Adaptation of Late Open sounds

| Late Open input | Substrate response | Possible later result |
|---|---|---|
| /θ/ | merged with /t/ or /s/ by region | inherited doublets can disagree while learned **th** returns later |
| /ɕ/ | merged with /s/ or /ʃ/; sometimes voiced near sonorants | several legitimate descendants of written **x** |
| /ʔ/ | not reliably perceived | loss, contraction, or inserted /j, w/; careful Hieratic speech may restore it |
| /V.V/ | treated as one nucleus or repaired | diphthong, glide, or vowel loss |
| unstressed final vowel | reduced, then optionally lost | new final consonants and shorter names |
| /h/ after an unstressed vowel | weakened or lost | adjacent syllables contract |
| intervocalic voiceless consonant | variably voiced in some communities | regional alternations later leveled by the koine |
| linking vowel in compounds | reduced or deleted | clusters at root boundaries |

These are contact pressures, not a single ordered sound-change list. Early Surface Halauric formed in
more than one community across centuries. `Early Surface Halauric.md` selects and orders their common
effects, then establishes different Vartonnian and Marresonnian outcomes.

## Native material that survives language shift

Even communities that shift almost completely to Surface Halauric retain Ancestral Continental material
most strongly in:

- personal and clan names;
- rivers, hills, fields, and old settlement names;
- kinship and household terms;
- crops, animals, fishing, herding, and seasonal work;
- body vocabulary and ordinary insults;
- local religious rites and beings;
- intimate particles, nursery language, and discourse habits.

Halauri material is initially strongest in contracts, instruction, trade, measurement, public worship,
records, and skilled work. Over generations that social division erodes: inherited Halauric words become
ordinary household vocabulary, while some old substrate words acquire ceremonial or regional value.

## Compatibility with protected and old forms

### Established substrate roots and strong candidates

***Fen*** /fen/ is now the second established CON lexical root after *vart*: “prepare plant remedies;
herbal medicine as skilled work.” Its compact form and practical plant-and-body domain support survival
through the language shift. Early Surface and Imperial Balishan preserve it unchanged and use zero
derivation for the craft and its practitioner. Full social grammar is in `Halauric Core Lexicon.md`.

***Sar*** /sar/ is the third established CON item: an invariant preposed title recognizing an established
adult or a person acting in a respected skilled capacity. Its ordinary social domain, compact CVC shape,
and compatibility with the substrate's modifier-before-head order support unchanged survival. English
*Master* and *Mistress* are gendered translations of this one genderless form in *Master Fen, Mistress
Janice, Master Keelen,* and *Mistress Keya*.

***Len*** /len/ is the fourth established CON root: “treat or mend a sick or injured living body.” Zero
derivation supplies the practice and practitioner translated as *healing* and *healer*. It is broader than
*fen* and does not encode a technique, successful cure, institutional credential, or Xion's ethical rules.

Unstressed ***-um*** /um/ is the first established substrate practical-service site suffix. Surface
Halauric reduction produces Modern Balishan *lenum* /ˈle.nəm/ “clinic” and *fenum* /ˈfe.nəm/ “apothecary
shop/workroom.” It classifies a site by recurring organized work rather than by architecture alone.

***Dal*** /dal/ is the fifth established CON root: a social collective meaning the household maintained
under one continuing provision, roof, and domestic authority. Its compact form and intimate household
domain make unchanged survival expected. It includes kin, married-in members, wards, servants, and
dependants where they belong to the functioning household; it does not mean a physical building or a
chartered noble House. Imperial mixed *seldal* adds learned LOL *sel-* “enter into authoritative public
recognition” to create the legal category translated as capitalized *House*.

Fossil patronymic collective ***-annas*** /an.nas/ is established only inside *Larannas*, historically
“descendants or issue of Lar.” Its deeper substrate analysis remains open, and it is not productive in
Modern Balishan.

- **Rosik** has the compact **CVCVC** shape, initial stress, and final stop expected of a surviving human
  name. No claim is made yet about either syllable's meaning.
- **Kemvimore** can contain several old elements joined across **m-v**, then preserved as an opaque house
  name after their meanings faded. Its length is not an obstacle: compounds can be long while their roots
  remain compact.
- **Vartonne** is now the first controlled mixed form: CON *vart* “confluence, meeting-center” + borrowed
  LOL *onna* “established settlement.” **Marresonne** shares the settlement head but uses the LOL coastal
  stem *marreth*; it is not assigned a substrate root merely because the ending matches.
- **Tam, Jorik, Brennek, Vesk, Dermir,** and other Balishan-common forms remain legitimate candidates for
  substrate inheritance, ordinary Surface-Halauric development, or mixtures of both. Their period label
  does not decide the route.

### Forms probably entering by another route

- **Elara** and **Valanar** are better treated as learned or neo-Halauri prestige formations than as
  untouched substrate names.
- **Kaha'an** can remain a careful Hieratic/imperial title even if everyday descendants lose /ʔ/.
- **Xion**, **ZY-on** /ˈzaɪ.ən/, follows the learned Marresonnian–Hieratic route established in `Early
  Surface Halauric.md`; it is not ordinary substrate inheritance.
- **Balisha** may be a contact-period regional name inherited through ordinary speech, a learned name
  repeatedly refreshed from texts, or both. Its locked form does not settle the route.

## The first toponymic hypothesis

The shared **-onne** in *Vartonne* and *Marresonne* is a **Surface-Halauric settlement element**, not a
pure Proto-Balishan root meaning generic “place.” `Halauric Core Lexicon.md` establishes its structural
history:

1. LOL *on-* “dwell permanently” plus the result/place nominalizer *-na* produces *onna*, “established
   habitation”;
2. coastal speech weakens the final vowel while western speech deletes it;
3. Marresonnian documentary practice writes historical *onna* as **-onne**;
4. the spelling becomes the imperial convention for the old settlement-name class.

The western stem ***vart*** is the first established Ancestral Continental lexical entry: “confluence,
meeting-center,” with later figurative “heart” senses. *Vartonne* is therefore the mixed *vart + onna*,
“Settlement at the Confluence.” The shared ending proves a naming corridor; it does not make the coastal
*marreth* stem Ancestral Continental.

## What remains open

- whether this profile represents one language, a dialect chain, or a reconstruction across several;
- the endonyms of any ancestral communities;
- exact distribution west of Marresonne and around Vartonne;
- exact grammatical exponents, pronouns, and inflectional paradigms beyond the minimum reconstruction;
- which protected names contain substrate roots;
- which modern independent languages, if any, descend from the family;
- whether the spelling conventions behind doubled consonants originate before or during the empire;
- the regional distribution of substrate features beyond the shared Early Surface tendencies;
- all root meanings except the controlled *vart, fen, sar, len, dal, -annas,* and *-um* entries.

## Constraint carried into the daughter history

Early Surface Halauric must look like a language natively acquired by human children, not “Halauri
spoken badly.” Its ordered sound history now makes the changes regular within communities. Children
generalize the new stress, cluster, and vowel patterns; they do not preserve an eternal learner's
approximation. Different communities regularize different adaptations, allowing the later heartland koine
to contain regional variants and doublets from its beginning.
