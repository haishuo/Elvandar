# Halauric Core Lexicon

**Status:** Controlled lexical and derivational reconstruction, established 2026-08-22. The settlement
layer, confirmed imperial cluster, first Imperial-Balishan affiliation suffixes, first ordinary substrate
family, first ordinary social honorific, first medical register, and first household/chartered-house
register are complete; only entries explicitly
listed here are established. Every entry must
identify its historical stage, grammatical class, meaning, and derivation; resemblance to one of these
forms does not create another cognate automatically.

This lexicon begins with the **-onne** settlement layer because *Vartonne* and *Marresonne* provide a
closed test. Their locations and histories are already known, the shared ending is real, and the sound
changes in `Early Surface Halauric.md` have to produce both forms without exemptions.

## Status labels

- **LOL** — Late Open Halauri public/contact register
- **CON** — Ancestral Continental reconstruction
- **ESL** — Early Surface Halauric common or mixed formation
- **MAR** — Marresonnian coastal outcome or documentary tradition
- **VAR** — Vartonnian western outcome
- **HIE** — Hieratic or deliberately learned transmission
- **EIB** — Early Imperial Balishan
- **LIB** — Late Imperial Balishan or its emerging daughter usage

An asterisk marks a reconstructed historical form, not uncertainty about whether the established entry
exists. IPA gives the author-facing pronunciation at the stated stage; it does not claim the original
script used an alphabet.

## Established entries

### LOL *on-* — dwell; establish permanent habitation

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***on-*** |
| Stage | LOL |
| Class | verbal root |
| Pronunciation | /on/ |
| Core meaning | to dwell as an established inhabitant; to make a place one's continuing habitation |

The root denotes settled residence, not merely being located somewhere or spending a night there. In
institutional use it can describe the establishment of a continuing inhabited site. No finite verb,
imperative, or modern independent descendant is coined yet.

### LOL *-na* — bounded result or place of an action

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***-na*** |
| Stage | LOL |
| Class | derivational suffix |
| Pronunciation | /na/ |
| Core meaning | the concrete result, bounded locus, or established site produced by the root |

This is a derivational nominalizer, not the ordinary locative role marker and not a general word meaning
“place.” It can form a concrete result noun where the root's semantics allow one. Nothing here makes every
name ending in *-na* Halauri; the morpheme must be demonstrated inside a regular derivation.

### LOL *onna* — established habitation; settlement

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***onna*** < *on-na* |
| Stage | LOL |
| Class | derived noun; productive final head in settlement names |
| Pronunciation | /ˈon.na/ |
| Core meaning | an established inhabited place; a continuing settlement |

The adjacent **n** sounds come from the root and suffix boundary. They are syllabified /on.na/ in careful
Late Open speech. This is the settlement head required by `Grammar Foundations.md`:

```text
LOCAL OR FOUNDING STEM + onna
```

Its meaning is narrower than generic “place” and broader than “city.” A village, port, river town, or
planned colony can be an *onna* if it is understood as a continuing habitation. A temporary camp cannot.

***Onna*** is not *Kaha'an*'s bound head ***-'an*** in another spelling. *Onna* classifies an inhabited
settlement; *-'an* identifies a governing seat or official locus. Their similar vowels and nasals do not
establish a historical relationship, and no metathesis between them is reconstructed.

### CON *vart* — confluence; meeting-center

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***vart*** |
| Stage | CON, western corridor |
| Class | noun and place-name classifier |
| Pronunciation | /vart/ |
| Core meaning | a confluence or junction; a center made by waters, roads, or communities meeting |

The physical sense is primary. The root naturally acquires the figurative senses “center,” “heart,” and
“gathering point,” but it does not yet establish the ordinary anatomical word for a heart. At Vartonne it
describes the convergence of the Imperial Vein, Silverflow, and Verdant Stream—the reason the settlement
could farm, move goods, and defend itself before an emperor arrived.

### LOL *marreth* — sheltered deep-water sea-harbor

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***marreth*** |
| Stage | LOL coastal/contact vocabulary |
| Class | noun and place-name classifier |
| Pronunciation | /mar.reθ/ |
| Core meaning | a protected deep-water anchorage open to the salt sea; a sea-harbor |

By Late Open this is one lexical stem. Its older internal history is not reconstructed, and **-eth** is not
a linking morpheme. The word is appropriate to the surface interface above Hauren: the defining human
feature was not abstract proximity to water but a harbor deep and sheltered enough to sustain durable
maritime trade.

## The settlement-head transmission

The productive Late Open head *onna* passes through the shared Surface Halauric changes regularly:

| Stage | Form | Development |
|---|---|---|
| LOL | /ˈon.na/ | Root *on-* plus result/place nominalizer *-na* |
| Nativizing ESL | /ˈon.nə/ | SL-2 and SL-7 weaken final unstressed /a/ |
| MAR | /ˈon.nə/ | Coastal speech retains the weak final vowel longer |
| VAR | /ˈonː/ > /ˈon/ | Western speech deletes the final weak vowel; the old boundary may surface as a long or firm /n/ before later simplification |
| Imperial spelling | **-onne** | Marresonnian documentary practice writes the weak historical final vowel as **e** and preserves **nn** from *on-na*; the spelling is generalized even where the western ending is pronounced /on/ |

The final **e** is therefore a historical spelling convention, not a separate suffix and not necessarily a
spoken modern vowel. Written **nn** has a real morphological source in this word, although other doubled
consonants can still arise later through assimilation as described by SL-8.

This gives the first controlled learned/inherited set:

```text
Late Open head:       *onna   /ˈon.na/
coastal descendant:  *onne   /ˈon.nə/
western descendant:  *onn    /ˈon/
imperial spelling:    -onne
```

## Vartonne

### Formation

Pre-imperial western speakers combine the local Ancestral Continental root with the borrowed Halauri
settlement head:

```text
CON vart     “confluence; meeting-center”
LOL onna     “established settlement”
ESL *Vartonna “settlement at the confluence”
```

The compound is deliberately mixed. That is evidence of a human community making Halauri material its
own, not evidence that its speakers lacked a language before contact.

### Derivation

| Stage | Form | Development |
|---|---|---|
| Mixed ESL | */ˈvartˌon.na/* | Native compact stem plus borrowed settlement head; initial root stress is already regular |
| Early VAR | */ˈvartˌon.nə/* | Final /a/ weakens |
| Spoken VAR | */ˈvart.onː/ > /ˈvar.ton/* | Final weak vowel deletes; syllables resettle across the transparent boundary; long/firm /n/ simplifies |
| Documentary/imperial | **Vartonne** | The commercial spelling **-onne** is applied to the recognized settlement-name class |

The established modern author-facing pronunciation is **VAR-ton**, /ˈvar.ton/. The final **e** is silent,
and the doubled **n** need not remain geminate.

### Meaning

The strict etymological gloss is **“Confluence Settlement”** or **“Settlement at the Meeting of Waters.”**
The traditional **“Heart-place”** remains legitimate as an old poetic and later imperial interpretation:
*vart* already extends from a physical meeting-center to the heart of a network, and the empire then made
Vartonne its political heart. It is not the narrow word-for-word gloss.

This order matters. The town was not named because later emperors considered it central. Valan chose it
because the three-river confluence had already made it a human center worth absorbing.

## Marresonne

### Formation

The coastal name begins inside the Late Open contact sphere:

```text
LOL marreth       “sheltered deep-water sea-harbor”
LOL onna          “established settlement”
LOL *Marrethonna  “settlement at the deep-water harbor”
```

The name passes into natively spoken coastal speech and undergoes the same changes as ordinary inherited
vocabulary. The result—not the Late Open source form—is the human city's name.

### Derivation

| Stage | Form | Development |
|---|---|---|
| LOL | */mar.reˈθon.na/* | Default Late Open stress falls on the penultimate syllable |
| Nativizing coastal | */ˈmar.reˌθon.na/* | SL-1 moves primary stress to the first lexical root; the settlement head retains secondary stress |
| Early MAR | */ˈmar.rəˌson.nə/* | SL-2 reduces unstressed vowels; coastal SL-5 changes /θ/ to /s/; final /a/ weakens |
| Ordinary MAR | */ˈmar.rəˌson.ə/* | The inherited vernacular simplifies the internal long/firm /n/; medial schwa survives because deleting it would create an alien cluster |
| Established MAR | **Marresonne**, /ˈmar.ə.son/ | Final weak /ə/ is lost late; inherited /r.r/ and secondary stress simplify; the spelling directly records coastal /θ/ > /s/ and the established **-onne** settlement head |

The earlier coastal form sounded approximately **MAR-rə-son-ə**. Late loss of the final weak vowel gives
established **Marresonne**, **MAR-reh-son**, /ˈmar.ə.son/. This is the ordinary human name of the city
before *Kaha'an* replaces it after the capital moves—not a learned archival respelling. Much older
Halauri records may preserve source *Marrethonna*, but later Vartonnese archives and Urathan indices
preserve the human form *Marresonne*. Neither source nor descendant is available to Xion merely because
the author knows it.

### Meaning

The strict gloss is **“Harbor Settlement”** or **“Settlement at the Deep-Water Harbor.”** Traditional
**“Sea-place”** is an accurate broad translation but loses the practical distinction carried by
*marreth*: this is a usable, sheltered maritime threshold, not simply land beside salt water.

That distinction fits the city's historical role. Marresonne connected surface communities to Hauren,
then connected the western heartland to overseas trade, and eventually drew the imperial court east. The
name describes the instrument by which the city mattered.

## The imperial cluster

This cluster does not come down by one route. Its roots are Late Open Halauri, but the surviving
phrases were assembled, preserved, or renewed by human institutions at different dates. That difference
is part of their meaning: the Valanar court uses learned Halauri to claim continuity, while ordinary
Surface Halauric remains audible inside the same register.

### LOL *kaha* — sovereignty as office

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***kaha*** |
| Stage | LOL |
| Class | institutional noun; attributive classifier before a head |
| Pronunciation | /ˈka.ha/ |
| Core meaning | crown or sovereign authority understood as an office and institution |

***Kaha*** does not name the physical crown. Before another institutional noun it means “of the Crown,”
“royal,” or, in Valanar usage, “imperial.” Its full vowels and audible /h/ are repeatedly restored in the
human learned tradition; an ordinary inherited cognate, if one survives, has not been identified.

### LOL *-'an* — seat in which authority is instantiated

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***-'an*** |
| Stage | LOL |
| Class | bound institutional head |
| Pronunciation | /ʔan/ |
| Core meaning | the official seat or locus in which an office or governing authority is made present |

This is not an ordinary word for “place,” a locative ending, or a settlement classifier. Working Late Open
Halauri Plate 2 treats it as productive institutional-locus morphology: it converts a public office,
delegated authority, or regulated competence into the recognized seat where that authority is legally
present and able to act. The proposed family includes ***veda'an*** “governing seat,” ***esha'an***
“tribunal,” ***netha'an*** “certifying chair,” ***ketha'an*** “standards tribunal,” ***vela'an***
“licensing authority,” ***riena'an*** “archival custody office,” ***bata'an*** “guard command,” and
***thuma'an*** “system control seat.” These family members remain working until that plate is approved.

The sovereign member is the LOL title ***Kaha'an***, /ka.ˈha.ʔan/, literally **“Seat of Sovereignty”**
and conventionally **“Royal Seat.”** The Valanar court borrows the whole title through the Hieratic
tradition and makes it transferable: whichever city holds the throne is *Kaha'an*. The glottal boundary,
penultimate stress, and full vowels belong to careful title pronunciation.

Ordinary daughter-language speech need not reproduce that citation form. Regular loss of /ʔ/ exposes the
two adjacent vowels; contraction and later shortening produce two-syllable ***Kaha'an***,
**kah-HAN**, /kaˈhan/:

```text
/ka.ˈha.ʔan/ > /ka.ˈha.an/ > /ka.ˈhaːn/ > /kaˈhan/
```

Both pronunciations name the same title, but they are not socially equal inside the city. Contracted
**kah-HAN** is the native Kaha'ani default, including among educated residents. The full form is an
instructed historical pronunciation retained in fixed ceremony, Hieratic citation, and some foreign
reading traditions; Collegiate renewal has additionally made it the ordinary standard Urathan name for
the city. A local can reproduce it, but using it in ordinary Kaha'ani conversation sounds book-taught and
may mark a speaker as non-Kaha'ani. ***Ka'han*** may be used as a pronunciation guide; the canonical
written form remains ***Kaha'an***.

The head remains historically distinct from ***onna*** “established settlement.” Nothing in the present
reconstruction licenses a common source merely because both words contain open vowels and **n**.

### LOL *bat-* and *-ae* — appointed guard or guarding body

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***bat-*** |
| Stage | LOL |
| Class | verbal root |
| Pronunciation | /bat/ |
| Core meaning | to stand watch over; to ward or protect a person, threshold, or charge |

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***-ae*** |
| Stage | LOL |
| Class | duty-holder and commissioned-body nominalizer |
| Pronunciation | /a.e/ |
| Core meaning | the appointed holder or organized body constituted by performance of the root's duty |

Together they form LOL ***batae*** < *bat-ae*, /ba.ˈta.e/, “appointed guard; commissioned guarding body.”
The noun does not itself decide whether English needs singular *guard* or collective *guard corps*;
context and syntax do that work. The three audible syllables and penultimate stress of official imperial
***batae*** identify a learned Hieratic form. Ordinary Surface Halauric would move stress toward the root
and repair the hiatus, but no vernacular descendant is yet established.

### LOL *arol* — old in institutional time

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***arol*** |
| Stage | LOL |
| Class | adjective and pre-head quality term |
| Pronunciation | /ˈa.rol/ |
| Core meaning | old, long-established, or belonging to the former order in contrast with the new |

The word does not inherently mean elderly, decrepit, or ruined. It is particularly apt for offices,
structures, customs, and names whose age gives them standing. Ordinary inheritance would reduce the
post-tonic vowel and could produce *arəl* or western *arl*; neither is established in the surviving
corpus. Full ***arol*** is a learned form kept available by Hieratic instruction.

### LOL *hutan* and *re-* — a tunnel and its connected system

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***hutan*** |
| Stage | LOL |
| Class | count noun |
| Pronunciation | /ˈhu.tan/ |
| Core meaning | one purpose-built underground passage; a tunnel |

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***re-*** |
| Stage | LOL |
| Class | old non-stressing collective prefix |
| Pronunciation | /re/ |
| Core meaning | an interconnected aggregate whose members function as one system |

This limited prefix is older and less productive than Late Open suffixation; it does not make the language
generally prefixing. LOL ***rehutan*** < *re-hutan*, /re.ˈhu.tan/, means “interconnected tunnel system” or
“tunnels as one network.” Unlike the other visible members of the cluster, it enters ordinary Surface
Halauric speech:

| Stage | Form | Development |
|---|---|---|
| LOL | */re.ˈhu.tan/* | Collective prefix plus count noun; root stress remains on *hu-* |
| Nativizing ESL | */rə.ˈhu.tən/* | The weak prefix vowel and post-tonic /a/ reduce |
| Early ESL | */r.ˈhu.tən/* | SL-6 deletes the reduced prefix vowel |
| Heartland ESL | */ˈr̥u.tən/* | Boundary /r + h/ coalesces to a voiceless rhotic; **rh** records that sound |
| Imperial Balishan | **rhutan**, /ˈru.tən/ | The rhotic later voices in ordinary speech while the historical spelling remains |

Author-facing **Rhutan** is therefore **ROO-tən**, /ˈru.tən/, not a Late Open word beginning with an
otherwise unexplained **rh** cluster. The individual/count noun is ***hutan***; ***rhutan*** is the
inherited lexical collective. Neither form licenses treating **r-** as a living Modern Balishan plural
prefix.

## The four established imperial formations

| Formation | Date and route | Careful author-facing pronunciation | Exact force |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kaha'an** | LOL title borrowed whole through HIE; adopted as the transferable Valanar capital-title | careful **kah-HAH-an**, /ka.ˈha.ʔan/; contracted **kah-HAN**, /kaˈhan/ | **Royal Seat**; literally “Seat of Sovereignty” |
| **Kaha Batae** | human imperial formation from two learned LOL elements | **KAH-hah bah-TAH-eh**, /ˈka.ha ba.ˈta.e/ | **Imperial Guard** |
| **Arol Batae** | post-Rending learned substitution of *arol* for *kaha* in the existing guard-title | **AH-rol bah-TAH-eh**, /ˈa.rol ba.ˈta.e/ | **Old Guard** |
| **Arol Rhutan** | post-sealing, pre-imperial Marresonnian registry hybrid: learned *arol* plus inherited collective *rhutan*; later adopted unchanged by the empire | **AH-rol ROO-tən**, /ˈa.rol ˈru.tən/ | **Old Tunnels** |

The phrases are Imperial Balishan uses of Halauric material, not proof that native Late Open speakers used
all three exact combinations. ***Kaha Batae*** makes the empire sound continuous with Halauri
sovereignty. Renaming it ***Arol Batae*** after the Rending is not a translation into ordinary Balishan;
it is a solemn lexical replacement inside that same learned title, turning the guard from the body of a
living empire into the body that remembers one. ***Arol Rhutan*** has a different texture and an older
human history: after Hauren sealed, Marresonnian record-keepers used a learned age word to classify the
ordinary inherited name of the system beneath their city. The later empire kept the existing phrase.

## Imperial Balishan affiliation suffixes

### EIB *-an* — of a polity or territorial people

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***-an*** |
| Stage | EIB, substrate-derived |
| Class | invariant relational suffix |
| Pronunciation | /an/ |
| Core meaning | belonging to or associated with a polity, territorial community, or its people |

By Early Imperial Balishan the suffix can create an adjective, inhabitant term, collective people-name,
or language-name. Those are contextual uses of one invariant relation, not four homophonous suffixes.
Its deeper Ancestral Continental form remains uncoined; establishing ordinary Imperial productivity does
not require pretending that the surviving realm names preserve the substrate unchanged.

After a realm stem ending in /a/, /a + an/ coalesces to /an/:

| Base | Derivation | Established result | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Balisha** | *Balisha + -an* > *Balishaan* > **Balishan** | adjective, person/people, national language | Balisha as polity and territorial community |
| **Uratha** | *Uratha + -an* > *Urathaan* > **Urathan** | adjective, person/people, national language | Uratha as polity and territorial community |

The rule establishes the suffix, not the internal etymology of either base. *Balisha* may still prove
inherited, learned, or mixed; *Uratha* remains Urath IV's imperial foundation-name.

### MAR–LIB *-i* — of a settlement or urban community

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***-i*** |
| Stage | MAR, productive in transferred and late imperial capital speech |
| Class | invariant local-affiliation suffix |
| Pronunciation | /i/ |
| Core meaning | belonging to or associated with a particular settlement, its inhabitants, or its local speech and culture |

The first canonical result is ***Kaha'ani***:

```text
Kaha'an + -i  >  Kaha'ani
/ka.ˈha.ʔan/ + /i/  >  /ka.ˈha.ʔa.ni/
```

The suffix is outside the inherited title. It resyllabifies the title's final /n/ but neither deletes the
internal /ʔ/ nor moves the title's stress. Careful author-facing pronunciation is
**kah-HAH-ah-nee**. Native city speech normally contracts this to **kah-HAH-nee**, /kaˈha.ni/. In current
usage *Kaha'ani* can name an inhabitant, the urban cultural adjective, or the capital's dialect/accent. It
does not replace national *Balishan*.

The same vernacular reduction can apply after derivation:

```text
/ka.ˈha.ʔa.ni/ > /ka.ˈha.a.ni/ > /kaˈha.ni/
Kaha'ani                              kah-HAH-nee
```

This is the native local outcome. The fuller form remains available in the same instructed registers as
full *Kaha'an*.

The deeper source of *-i* remains open. Its presence in Marresonnian and later capital usage is fixed;
its possible relationship to an older relational or inhabitant form is not. No additional city demonym
is coined merely to exhibit the suffix.

### Three endings that must not collapse

| Ending | Language and stage | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| LOL ***-'an*** /ʔan/ | working productive institutional-locus head, preserved inside *Kaha'an* | governing seat or locus of instantiated authority |
| EIB ***-an*** /an/ | ordinary human suffix in *Balishan, Urathan* | polity or territorial affiliation |
| MAR–LIB ***-i*** /i/ | eastern local suffix in *Kaha'ani* | settlement or urban affiliation |

Thus ***Kaha'ani*** is morphologically *kaha + -'an + -i*. Its visible **an** is not the national suffix
found in *Balishan*.

## The first ordinary Balishan lexical family: *fen*

### CON *fen* — prepare plant remedies; herbal medicine as skilled work

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***fen*** |
| Stage | CON > ESL > Imperial and Modern Balishan |
| Class | practical-work root; verb and noun through zero derivation |
| Pronunciation | /fen/, author-facing **FEN** |
| Core meaning | to select, prepare, and compound plant material for remedies; the skilled work of doing so |

***Fen*** is the second established Ancestral Continental lexical root after *vart*. Its compact stressed
CVC shape passes unchanged into Surface Halauric because it contains no weak syllable, unfamiliar
consonant, or hiatus for the daughter sound changes to alter. Its survival also fits the substrate's
strongest semantic domain: ordinary plants, bodies, households, and practical work.

Ancestral Continental and Early Surface Halauric both permit **zero derivation**: syntax can use the same
unchanged root as an activity, craft, or practitioner without adding an audible suffix. Modern Balishan
therefore has one number-neutral form:

| Use | Meaning | Reader-facing example |
|---|---|---|
| activity or discipline | herb-work; practical preparation of plant remedies | “she learned *fen*” |
| count occupation | a herb-worker or apothecary | “a *fen*”; “three *fen*” in unmarked-number syntax |
| attributive trade term | associated with the craft, its tools, stock, or workplace | “*fen* work”; an untranslated native compound remains uncoined |
| occupational byname | the person identified by that work | ***Mira Fen***, “Mira the herb-worker” |

The common noun is lower-case ***fen***. Capital ***Fen*** appears when the reader-facing orthography
places it inside a byname, title, alias, or official personal-name entry. English may render a plural as
*Fens*, but no Modern Balishan plural suffix is established by that translation; ordinary number remains
recoverable from context, numerals, or other uncoined number marking.

The word has no grammatical gender. “Mira the herb-woman” explains her identity in English; it does not
translate a feminine ending. A man, woman, or constituted workshop can use the same root where syntax
makes the referent clear.

### What *fen* does not mean

***Fen*** is not the general word for every healer. It names the herbal and apothecary trade from which
Mira and Xion began. A midwife, bonesetter, wound-worker, surgeon, or other practitioner may be a healer
without being a *fen*; a *fen* may principally compound remedies rather than perform every kind of direct
care.

The broader medical register is established below. **Healer** is substrate *len*, the practical category
defined by treating sick or injured bodies rather than by one technique. **Physician** is learned *sathen*,
foregrounding diagnostic competence without implying a legal licence. **Clinic** is *lenum*, a continuing
care arrangement and site. It is not derived from *fen*: a *fen* may work from an estate, *fenum*
apothecary workplace, market room, or *lenum* clinic.

Likewise **Master** is the reader-translation of ordinary Balishan ***sar*** /sar/, the genderless respect
honorific established below. ***Master Keelen*** demonstrates that an apothecary can be addressed by his
personal name rather than the trade. Xion withholds a personal name and supplies the occupation itself,
so ***Master Fen*** is structurally *sar fen*, “respected [the] Herb-worker.” Its genericness is why the
alias works.

### The three fixed personal uses

| Form | Grammatical analysis | Narrative consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Mira Fen** | personal name + occupational byname | Mira is identified as the estate's herb-worker; *Fen* is neither house nor inherited family name |
| **Master Fen** | translated honorific + occupational byname used as the whole public identity | Xion claims Mira's work without revealing a birth name; patients can address him normally without knowing who he is |
| **Xion Fen** | personal name + a common trade noun deliberately entered in the imperial house-name slot | the record makes this occurrence a personal name by fiat; it does not create a Fen family or make the trade hereditary |

Janice can shorten ***Master Fen*** to ***Fen*** because she witnessed the alias being chosen and treats
the occupational byname as his working name. That intimate usage does not change the word's public
grammar. When Xion later requests ***Xion Fen***, the undersecretary spells a familiar common noun because
its placement—not its sound—is anomalous.

## The first ordinary Balishan social honorific: *sar*

### CON *sar* — respectful recognition of an established adult

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***sar*** |
| Stage | CON > ESL > Imperial and Modern Balishan |
| Class | invariant preposed social honorific |
| Pronunciation | /sar/, author-facing **SAHR** |
| Core meaning | respectful recognition of a socially established adult or a person acting in a recognized skilled capacity |

***Sar*** is the third established Ancestral Continental item after *vart* and *fen*. Like *fen*, its
stressed CVC form survives the Surface-Halauric shift unchanged. Its semantic domain is equally resistant
to replacement: ordinary address, neighborhood standing, household adulthood, and respect between people
who do not possess house names.

The honorific precedes the name or byname it scopes, matching the inherited modifier-before-head order:

```text
sar Fen       → Master Fen
sar Janice    → Mistress Janice
sar Keelen    → Master Keelen
sar Keya      → Mistress Keya
```

The Balishan form has **no grammatical gender and does not agree with its referent**. English uses
*Master* for a man and *Mistress* for a woman because that is the chosen reader-facing convention; no
sound, suffix, or inflection changes inside Balishan. A speaker need not know or announce a person's
gender in order to use *sar*. When the referent is unknown, English may retain the established public
form *Master*; Elara's “the original Master Fen” does exactly this before Xion has told her who taught him.

The title marks ordinary respect, not a guild examination or legal licence. Age and household standing
can license it, as with Keya; recognized independent work can license it even for a younger adult, as with
Fen. Janice qualifies both as an older adult and as the proprietor and skilled weaver of her shop; Keelen
qualifies both by age and by his apothecary practice. Its use is socially meaningful but optional:
intimates, peers, hurried speakers, and hostile speakers may omit it without changing the person's name.
Janice's bare ***Fen*** is therefore intimate and relationship-specific, not ungrammatical.

***Sar*** is not a second name, house grant, occupational byname, noble rank, or claim of authority over
the person addressed. A commoner still has one personal name; the honorific sits outside it. It can also
scope an occupational byname, which is precisely what allows *sar Fen* to function without revealing any
personal name at all.

### English *master* and *mistress* outside this test

The English prose reuses those words for several relationships that do **not** establish another use of
*sar*:

| Reader-facing phrase | Linguistic status |
|---|---|
| **young master** addressed to noble Xion | a noble-house or status courtesy; native form uncoined |
| **master of the Grain**, a cartel master | headship or control of an institution; native form uncoined |
| **master of the Shadow Corps** | command of an institution; native form uncoined |
| **the Mistress / Mistress of Knives** | the unique office at the head of the Long Knives; native modern title uncoined |
| master artist, craft grade, magical master, or owner | domain-specific rank or ordinary lexical sense; no relation to *sar* established |
| **Master Garrett Caldris** and other P3 titles | insufficiently exposed to assign; no automatic relation to *sar* |

English polysemy is not Balishan etymology. In particular, the Long Knives' leader is not called *sar*
merely because Xion addresses her as “Mistress” in translation.

## The first ordinary Balishan medical register

### CON *len* — treat or mend a living body

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***len*** |
| Stage | CON > ESL > Imperial and Modern Balishan |
| Class | living-care root; verb, activity noun, and practitioner noun through zero derivation |
| Pronunciation | /len/, author-facing **LEN** |
| Core meaning | to attend, treat, or mend a sick or injured living body; the practice or practitioner doing so |

***Len*** is the broad word translated as **heal, healing,** or **healer** when deliberate care is meant.
Like *fen*, it is a compact substrate root preserved unchanged and available through zero derivation:

```text
len              treat or mend a living body; healing practice
one len          a healer
three len        healers, with number supplied by the numeral
```

The word does not promise a cure and is not limited to herbs, surgery, wounds, or any one technique. A
midwife, bonesetter, wound-worker, *fen*, or learned physician can also be a *len* when that person treats
living bodies. The exact Balishan terms for those narrower specialties remain uncoined.

The corpus also gives *healer* an ethical weight greater than its dictionary meaning. Xion's “healers
aren't judges” treats *len* as a vocation whose practitioner accepts the body in front of him. That is his
and Mira's professional ethic, not a lexical claim that every person called *len* shares it. A practitioner
can violate the ethic without causing the language itself to become incoherent.

### CON *-um* — recurring practical-service site

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***-um*** |
| Stage | CON > ESL > Imperial and Modern Balishan |
| Class | unstressed practical-service site suffix |
| Pronunciation | CON /um/; Modern Balishan /əm/ |
| Core meaning | a place regularly arranged for the root's practical work to be performed or received |

The suffix reduces regularly after root stress:

```text
CON *len-um* /ˈle.num/ > ESL /ˈle.nəm/ > Balishan lenum /ˈle.nəm/
CON *fen-um* /ˈfe.num/ > ESL /ˈfe.nəm/ > Balishan fenum /ˈfe.nəm/
```

Modern ***lenum***, **LEN-əm**, is the ordinary word translated as **clinic**: not simply a room containing
medical supplies, but a continuing arrangement in which people are received for treatment. A back room can
be a *lenum* and a handsome storefront can fail to become one if no care is actually organized there. This
is the lexical distinction inside Janice's “A room the city can point at isn't a clinic.” When Master Fen's
practice ends, the two rooms remain while the *lenum* does not.

Modern ***fenum***, **FEN-əm**, is an apothecary workplace, shop, or regularly maintained herb-work room.
English **apothecary** is ambiguous between person and place; Balishan is not:

| English phrase | Balishan analysis |
|---|---|
| Mira was the estate apothecary | Mira was its ***fen***, the practitioner |
| Master Keelen's apothecary | Keelen's ***fenum***, the shop/workplace |
| Master Fen's clinic | *sar Fen*'s ***lenum***, organized for receiving patients |

The suffix is established only in this practical-service family. It does not license analyzing every word
ending in **-um** or coining arbitrary buildings without a cultural need.

### LOL *sath-* and *-en* — examine bodily signs; learned practitioner

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***sath-*** |
| Stage | LOL technical/public register |
| Class | diagnostic verbal root |
| Pronunciation | /saθ/ |
| Core meaning | to examine the signs of a living body and distinguish its internal condition accurately |

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***-en*** |
| Stage | LOL technical/public register |
| Class | habitual skilled-practitioner nominalizer |
| Pronunciation | /en/ |
| Core meaning | a person whose recognized practice is the specialized work named by the root |

Together they form LOL ***sathen*** < *sath-en*, /ˈsa.θen/, the technical practitioner translated as
**physician**. The public and Hieratic medical tradition keeps the learned form in Modern Balishan as
***sathen***, **SAH-then**, /ˈsa.θen/. Ordinary inheritance would permit coastal *sasen* and western
*saten*, but neither descendant is established in the current corpus.

***Sathen*** identifies diagnostic knowledge and practiced medical competence. It does not itself encode
a crown licence, academy degree, court appointment, gender, or moral character. The imperial household's
physicians are institutionally appointed *sathen* and can be recognized by robes and bearing; that does
not make every *sathen* a palace officer. Xion truthfully counts himself one in Book 3 because his knowledge
and practice meet the term, although Mira taught him privately and no institution certified him.

### The category map

| Reader translation | Native form | What it foregrounds |
|---|---|---|
| **healer** | ***len*** /len/ | the act and vocation of treating living bodies |
| **physician** | ***sathen*** /ˈsa.θen/ | diagnostic knowledge and recognized technical practice |
| **herb-worker / apothecary person** | ***fen*** /fen/ | preparation and use of plant remedies |
| **clinic** | ***lenum*** /ˈle.nəm/ | a continuing care practice/site that receives patients |
| **apothecary shop/workroom** | ***fenum*** /ˈfe.nəm/ | a continuing site organized for herb-work |

These are overlapping axes, not a rank ladder. Xion is *fen*, *len,* and *sathen*; his rooms are a
*lenum*. Mira is securely *fen* and *len*; the corpus does not require calling her *sathen*. Keelen is
securely *fen* and *len*, while “Keelen's apothecary” names his *fenum*. Palace physicians are *sathen*
and are probably also *len* in ordinary description, but the institutional term foregrounds their
technical standing rather than Xion's ethical vocation.

**Medicine, medical, surgeon, midwife, bonesetter, hospital, healing center,** and other specialist or
institutional translations do not acquire native forms from this test. Terinoki healers and foreign
institutions likewise require their own language histories.

## The first Balishan household and chartered-house register

English *house* collapses a building, a domestic group, a noble lineage, a legal corporation, and a
dynasty. Balishan does not. The controlled distinction begins with one ordinary substrate word and one
mixed imperial legal formation.

### CON *dal* — household under one provision

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***dal*** |
| Stage | CON > ESL > Modern Balishan |
| Class | social collective noun |
| Pronunciation | /dal/, **DAHL** |
| Core meaning | the people maintained together under one continuing roof, provision, and domestic authority |

***Dal*** names a household as a functioning social body. Blood kin often form its center, but servants,
wards, dependants, and married-in members can belong to the same *dal*. The word does not require noble
standing and does not name the physical building. A family can leave one residence and remain the same
*dal*; unrelated lodgers under one roof do not automatically become one.

Its compact CVC form and domestic domain make unchanged survival through the Surface-Halauric shift
regular. The word is older than the empire and belongs to the substrate layer most resistant to
replacement during language shift.

### LOL *sel-* — enter into authoritative recognition

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Form | ***sel-*** |
| Stage | LOL public/legal register |
| Class | verbal root; pre-head institutional classifier in human use |
| Pronunciation | /sel/ |
| Core meaning | to enter, recognize, or constitute something in an authoritative public record |

***Sel-*** is narrower than generic “write” or “name.” It describes an entry whose record changes public
standing: recognition, charter, appointment, or constitution by an authority capable of making the entry
effective. No ordinary word for writing, ink, book, or personal naming is inferred from it.

### EIB *seldal* — a chartered House

Early Imperial Balishan combines learned legal *sel-* with ordinary household *dal*:

```text
LOL sel-  “enter into authoritative recognition”
CON dal   “household under one provision”
EIB seldal /ˈsel.dal/  “registered household; chartered House”
```

Modern ***seldal***, **SEL-dahl**, /ˈsel.dal/, is the institution reader-facing English capitalizes as
***House***. It is not merely a family with money. A *seldal* is a continuing legal body recognized by the
throne—or, during the interregnum, by the Coterie acting in the throne's absence—with a granted second
name and whatever property, offices, obligations, and succession rules its charter carries.

The mixed word records the institution's history. The domestic body is human and older than the empire;
the public act that turns one into a House comes through the learned chancery register. A newly purchased
House such as Darfi and an ancient House such as Kemvimore are equally *seldal*. Their prestige differs;
their legal category does not.

### Five things a House does not collapse

| Axis | What it means | Canonical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **blood** | biological descent | Valanar succession can care about blood even when ordinary House membership does not prove it |
| **membership** | recognized inclusion in the *seldal* | marriage can enter a spouse into a House without changing that person's ancestry |
| **name** | the granted second name borne in public records | it normally marks membership, but a record can lag, be stolen, or accept an exception such as *Fen* |
| **heirship** | expectation or right to succeed to property or authority | Rosik can disinherit Xion without the sound *Kemvimore* vanishing from every record that day |
| **office** | present authority to act for or head the House | being born to a House does not make every member its head or agent |

Marriage settlements decide which *seldal* receives a spouse and recognized children. Balishan has no
universal rule that the husband's or wife's House must prevail. House charters likewise differ on
succession: headship is an office, not a grammatical consequence of sex, birth order, or bearing the
name. This permits Rosik to marry into Kemvimore and later head it, Tania to head Larannas, and Xion to
enter Valanar at marriage without creating three exceptions to one imaginary paternal rule.

### Fossil *-annas* — descendants of a named forebear

House ***Larannas*** preserves old patronymic collective ***-annas*** /an.nas/, “the descendants or issue
of the named forebear”: historically *Lar-annas*, approximately **“the descendants of Lar.”** The element
is fossilized by the imperial period. Modern speakers can hear the old lineage shape, but they do not
productively make new House names by attaching *-annas* to a parent's name, and ordinary children do not
receive patronymic second names from it.

The deeper segmentation and independent pre-house history of *-annas* remain open. Establishing its force
inside *Larannas* does not make every written **-anas, -annas,** or **-nas** ending cognate.

### Existing houses test different routes

| Form | Route through the system |
|---|---|
| **Kemvimore** | old opaque native or mixed charter-name; Rosik enters it by marriage; Xion loses heirship before he stops bearing it |
| **Larannas** | fossil patronymic charter-name; Tania and Silvanno share membership without implying male headship |
| **Valanar** | human neo-Halauri prestige dynasty; blood legitimacy and *seldal* membership are related but not identical |
| **Darfi** | recent purchased grant; socially resented and legally genuine |
| **Issamil** | inherited charter-name carried from Kael to Kai |
| **Hadris** | compact native-Balishan charter-name, **HAD-riss**, /ˈhad.ris/; historically opaque |
| **Sa Ko Ren / House Ren** | foreign personal-name structure recognized whole inside Kaha'ani House law; not Balishan morphology |
| **Dermir** | house-shaped second name Ronas took without grant and made the city accept in practice |
| **Fen** | common trade-word exceptionally entered where a House name normally goes; never thereby made a hereditary family |
| **Greystone** | reader-translation of a semantically transparent Balishan charter-name; native sound-form remains uncoined |

House names therefore do not share a productive ending. A *seldal* is created by recognized legal
standing, not by attaching a “noble suffix.” Founder names, old patronymics, prestige coinages, imported
names, purchased grants, and translated transparent names can all occupy the same second-name slot.

The native terms for mother, father, sibling, spouse, adoption, dowry, legitimacy, entail, and individual
inheritance instruments remain uncoined. So do the ordinary word for a physical house and the native
sound-form translated as *Greystone*. This test establishes the social and legal distinctions required by
the story without inventing a complete kinship dictionary around them.

## What this test establishes

- **-onne** descends from LOL *onna*, not from an unanalysed Proto-Balishan suffix meaning generic
  “place.”
- The old naming layer can be shared without the two city stems coming from the same source language.
- *Vartonne* is a mixed substrate–Halauri compound; *Marresonne* is the regular coastal descendant of a
  Halauri compound.
- The paired modern spellings preserve the shared settlement class while their stems expose different
  histories.
- “Heart-place” and “Sea-place” survive as traditional translations, but the strict meanings are
  Confluence Settlement and Harbor Settlement.
- The imperial cluster has multiple regular transmission routes rather than one undifferentiated “Old
  Halauri” register.
- *Kaha'an* is the sovereign member of a working productive *-'an* institutional-locus family and is
  inherited whole; *Kaha Batae* and *Arol Batae* are human learned formations;
  *Arol Rhutan* is a learned–inherited hybrid.
- *Batae* is a duty-holder or commissioned-body noun rather than a hidden plural, while inherited *rhutan*
  is a lexical collective derived from LOL *re-hutan*.
- **Rh** in *rhutan* records the regular coalescence of /r + h/ after prefix-vowel loss.
- Imperial *-an* regularly derives *Balishan* and *Urathan* from realm stems ending in **a**.
- Marresonnian–Late Imperial *-i* derives city-local *Kaha'ani* from the whole learned title.
- LOL *-'an*, Imperial *-an*, and city-local *-i* remain historically and semantically distinct.
- Substrate *fen* survives regularly as an unchanged stressed CVC root and expands through zero
  derivation into a craft, practitioner term, and occupational byname.
- *Fen* remains narrower than general “healer,” and none of its personal uses creates a hereditary family.
- Substrate *sar* survives as an invariant preposed respect title and explains the commoner honorific
  cluster without importing English grammatical gender into Balishan.
- The ordinary title remains distinct from noble courtesy, institutional headship, achieved grades, and
  the office of the Long Knives' Mistress.
- Substrate *len* supplies the broad practice and practitioner translated as *healing/healer*, without
  making one technique or institutional credential definitional.
- Substrate site suffix *-um* regularly distinguishes *lenum* “clinic” and *fenum* “apothecary workplace”
  from the practitioners *len* and *fen*.
- Learned *sathen* identifies diagnostic medical competence without requiring a palace appointment or
  legal licence; healer, physician, and herb-worker overlap rather than form a rank ladder.
- Substrate *dal* distinguishes a functioning household from a building, a bloodline, and a noble House.
- Mixed Imperial *seldal* combines authoritative-register *sel-* with *dal* to name a crown-recognized
  chartered House.
- Blood, membership, public name, heirship, and office can diverge without contradiction.
- Fossil patronymic *-annas* explains *Larannas* as “descendants of Lar” without creating a productive
  modern surname suffix.
- House names can enter the same legal slot through native, learned, foreign, stolen, exceptional, or
  reader-translated routes rather than one sound shape.

## What remains open

- older morphology inside *marreth*;
- any independent modern descendant of *on-* or *-na*;
- whether other established names truly contain *onna*;
- the original scripts and the exact in-world spelling reforms represented by the author-facing
  romanization;
- Sealed and Terminal Halauri cognates;
- any ordinary inherited descendants of *kaha, batae,* or *arol*;
- the former productivity and wider lexical family of collective *re-*; approval and human descendants
  of the working institutional *-'an* family;
- the deeper substrate source of Imperial *-an* and the deeper source of eastern local *-i*;
- the native sound-forms behind *medicine, surgeon, midwife, bonesetter, hospital,* and other specialties
  or institutions not established above;
- the ordinary inherited descendants, if any, of learned *sathen*;
- wider productivity of practical-site *-um* and specialist *-en* beyond the controlled medical forms;
- the deeper history of *-annas* and any living independent descendant;
- native kinship terms, physical-house vocabulary, marriage instruments, and detailed inheritance law;
- the native sound-forms behind semantically translated House names such as *Greystone*;
- honorifics beyond ordinary *sar*, including noble courtesy and the Long Knives' leadership title;
- any biological-heart, generic-sea, river, road, or city roots beyond the meanings fixed above.

No new **-onne** name should be coined merely to demonstrate productivity. Two ancient settlements are a
historical layer; a third would need a place, a people, and a reason to belong to it.
