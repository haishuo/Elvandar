# Urathan

**Status:** Sociolinguistic foundation, settled 2026-08-21 and connected to the imperial daughter history
2026-08-22. Genealogy, register structure, the first vernacular/Collegiate correspondence channels, the
**Theraris / Vela Theraris** service-name suite, and the Modern Urathan numeral system are canon. Full
phonology, grammar, historical sound changes, and general vocabulary remain to be built.

## Genealogy and age

Urathan descends from **Imperial Balishan** and therefore belongs to the human Surface-Halauric branch.
Its ordinary vocabulary can contain both inherited Halauric forms and the older Ancestral Continental
substrate carried through Proto-Balishan. Its distinct history began while Urath IV's academy was still an
imperial institution and continued as the academy became a permanent community, city, province, and
finally an independent state in 695 BR. Book 1 opens in 1044 BR, giving sovereign Uratha 349 years of
separate development. The academy's founding date remains unset, so the total age of the branch is older
than 349 years but not yet fixed.

Modern Urathan and modern Balishan are ordinarily mutually intelligible. They are not interchangeable.
Accent, idiom, etiquette, technical vocabulary, and judgments about educated speech have developed under
different institutions for centuries.

The name of the language itself preserves that shared genealogy. Productive Early Imperial ***-an***
marks polity-scale affiliation, so *Uratha + -an > Urathan* by the same final-vowel coalescence as
*Balisha + -an > Balishan*. The derivation is ordinary Imperial Balishan morphology applied to Urath IV's
foundation-name; it does not make the base *Uratha* an ancient native Halauri word.

Until the academy's foundation site and local population are settled, Urathan is not assigned exclusively
to either the Vartonnian or Kaha'ani subbranch. It descends from the Imperial Balishan used by the academy
community. That source already contained a common koine, regional forms, and a learned register; deciding
which local variety contributed most requires the missing population history. See `Imperial Balishan.md`.

## The register system

Urathan does not have one uniform relationship to Old Halauri. It has a vertical register system.

### Vernacular Urathan

The ordinary daughter language spoken in homes, markets, workshops, farms, docks, and daily administration.
It descends from Imperial Balishan and has its own innovations; it is not merely old Balishan preserved.
Regional, occupational, and class varieties belong inside this register and remain to be mapped.

### Collegiate Urathan

The formal language of scholarship, examinations, institutional ceremony, legal findings, technical
classification, and the Tower hierarchy. It is **deliberately re-Halaurized**. Urathan scholars reach for
Old Halauri because they consider it older, more exact, more dignified, and therefore more appropriate
to serious thought.

This is not only inheritance. Across generations the academies actively import roots, revive obsolete
forms, build new compounds from old material, translate ordinary concepts into learned calques, and prefer
Halauri-derived alternatives where vernacular Urathan already has a serviceable word. The result plays
a role like ecclesial Latin, learned Latin and Greek in European institutions, or any prestige language
whose antiquity is used to authorize a modern elite.

Collegiate Urathan may eventually develop recognizable habits in syntax and rhythm as well as vocabulary,
but those must be derived from the actual languages rather than represented by ornamental word order.

The first controlled habits are now established:

| Vernacular Urathan | Collegiate Urathan |
|---|---|
| inherited root-initial stress | Late Open penultimate stress restored in learned words |
| ordinary loss of /ʔ/ and repair of adjacent vowels | /ʔ/ and separate vowels restored in citations and formal coinages |
| ordinary SVO clauses with prepositions | verb-final clauses and old relational patterns cultivated in definitions, findings, and ceremony |
| inherited Imperial vocabulary | revived roots, semantic calques, and new compounds built from Hieratic material |

These are register correspondences, not a claim that every Urathan sentence has two mechanical versions.
They explain why a scholar can sound deliberately antique without ordinary Urathan ceasing to be a living
Imperial-Balishan daughter.

***Kaha'an*** is the culturally decisive exception. Standard Urathan does not use the inherited
contracted **kah-HAN** as its ordinary name for the capital. The academies repeatedly renewed the title
from Hieratic pronunciation, and modern Urathans consequently call it **kah-HAH-an**,
/ka.ˈha.ʔan/, even in otherwise vernacular speech. They articulate the hiatus and glottal stop
deliberately; careful enunciation demonstrates that the speaker knows what the written apostrophe means.

An Urathan can imitate Kaha'ani **kah-HAN**, /kaˈhan/, when accommodating a local interlocutor, but that
is code-switching toward the city rather than the unmarked Urathan form. To native Kaha'ani ears, the
Urathan default sounds book-taught, emphatic, and faintly determined to pronounce the city's own name
more correctly than its inhabitants do. That social effect is not an accident. It is exactly how Urathan
prestige culture uses old language.

### Cited Old Halauri

Fixed phrases, titles, inscriptions, definitions, canonical passages, and technical roots presented as Old
Halauri itself. Urathan pronunciation belongs to a human scholarly tradition transmitted for centuries
after Hauren sealed. It may be internally standardized and historically prestigious without matching any
native Halauri period exactly.

An Urathan scholar can therefore pronounce a citation "correctly" by every rule of the academy and still
sound foreign to a terminal Halauri system. This is accumulated history, not scholarly incompetence.

### Bakarn varieties

Bakarn speech is not defective Urathan. It develops under restricted education, inherited status,
segregated labor, and intense contact across practical trades. Its relationship to Collegiate Urathan is a
question of access and power, not intelligence.

The High Exam can weaponize this register difference. A child raised without sustained access to
Collegiate Urathan is tested partly through the language of the institution and then classified as though
the result measured inherent capacity. The language does not create the bakarn system, but it can make the
system look objective to the people administering it.

Do not make every bakarn speaker sound the same. Dockworkers, domestic servants, technicians, rural laborers,
and hidden scholarly families will not share one uniform sociolect.

## What the high register does culturally

Choosing Collegiate Urathan can carry several meanings at once:

- disciplinary expertise;
- institutional membership;
- deference to precedent;
- a claim of precision;
- a performance of dignity;
- social distance from a vernacular speaker;
- deliberate obscurity disguised as exactness.

None is automatic. A scholar may use the high register because a technical distinction genuinely requires
it, because ceremony requires it, because he wishes to impress a colleague, or because he does not want an
outsider to understand. The same linguistic instrument serves knowledge and exclusion; that tension is
Urathan rather than an exception to it.

## Purity as institutional mythology

Urathans may describe Collegiate Urathan as the purest survival of Imperial or Halauri speech. That is a
claim about legitimacy, not a neutral linguistic fact. Their tradition contains authentic old material,
later human pronunciation, revived archaisms, new compounds, semantic drift, and forms coined recently in
an ancient style.

The distinction to track in the eventual lexicon is therefore:

- ordinary inherited Surface-Halauric vocabulary;
- Ancestral Continental substrate inherited through Proto-Balishan;
- genuinely attested later Open or Hieratic Halauri borrowings;
- inherited Imperial Balishan learned vocabulary;
- Urathan revivals from old texts;
- neo-Halauri coinages created by Urathan scholars;
- vernacular Urathan words with no learned origin.

Treating all five as simply "Old Halauri" would reproduce Uratha's ideology rather than describe its
language.

## Narrative consequences

- A Balishan and an Urathan can normally converse without translation.
- Mutual intelligibility falls with **register**, not merely distance. A Provost's formal address may be
  substantially harder for a Kaha'ani listener than an Urathan merchant's conversation.
- Kaha'ani speakers may hear Collegiate Urathan as archaic, ceremonious, exacting, or pompous. Urathan
  scholars may hear modern Kaha'ani as agile and expressive but careless or commercially abbreviated.
- Recognition of learned roots does not grant comprehension of Terminal Halauri.
- Rediscovering Hauren can expose false archaisms, changed meanings, and confident pronunciations that
  belong entirely to Urathan history.
- Elara's involuntary impressions remain categorically different from academic mastery; neither replaces
  the other.

## Canonical numeral standard

Modern Urathan uses the approved decimal system in `Design Plates/Modern Urathan - Plate 3.md`. Its digits
are **nu, an, du, sei, ko, pem, vek, zhu, tae, nor** for zero through nine. The ordinary rank words are
**des** “ten,” **hek** “hundred,” and **mir** “thousand”; coefficients precede ranks, so 125 is **an hek du
des pem**, literally “one hundred two ten five.” Values group in triads from the right. There is no
ten-thousand rank: 10,000 is **des mir** and 100,000 **hek mir**.

Large scales are generated rather than memorized. Inherited **kel**, “count; measure,” links a base to its
exponent: **mir kel du** is 1,000² or one million, **mir kel sei** is 1,000³ or one billion, and any numeral
can serve as the exponent. **Nu** also marks a run of omitted positions; **koi** “boundary” is the decimal
point; **tir** “divide cleanly” forms exact fractions; inherited **=ya** makes the complete numeral phrase
ordinal.

The standard is genuinely efficient and socially weaponized. Tower-trained speakers articulate explicit
coefficients and triad boundaries; examiners can mark intelligible regional or bakarn forms “imprecise”
because they do not follow the mandated parse. Reader-facing prose continues to translate quantities into
ordinary English words or digits unless the Urathan form itself matters in the scene.
