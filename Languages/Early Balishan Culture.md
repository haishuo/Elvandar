# Early Balishan Culture

**Status:** Founding cultural dossier, established 2026-08-22. This document defines the communities and
institutions from which Early Imperial Balishan can develop. It does not yet establish phonology, grammar,
a script, or a root inventory.

## The founding was a compact, not a completed conquest

Year 0 BR marks Valan I Valanar's founding compact, his imperial refounding of Vartonne, and the beginning
of expansion. It does not mean that every later province became culturally or linguistically Balishan in a
single year. The empire grew because several groups with different strengths accepted a shared system
without becoming one people overnight.

Valan's achievement was therefore coalition-building as much as conquest. He created an arrangement that
could feed armies, move goods, keep accounts, adjudicate disputes, and extend itself into regions where
household speech and local law remained distinct.

## The constituencies

### Valan I and his following

Valan supplied military leadership, arbitration between communities, and the personal legitimacy around
which the new imperial office formed. He was not merely a figure placed over an already finished state:
the throne, the planned capital, and the institutions called imperial begin with him.

His birthplace, mother tongue, pre-imperial title, and the precise origin of House Valanar remain open. His
martial ability is settled through Terinok's response to him, but the deed by which he demonstrated it — a
duel, a battle, a campaign, or something else — has not been chosen.

### Vartonne's river and agricultural communities

The older settlement supplied the material base of the first capital: grain, fish, irrigation, river
transport, flood knowledge, markets, and people accustomed to coordinating work across the three-river
convergence. The imperial camp absorbed these communities; it did not replace them.

Vartonne's established landholders and practical families included the ancestors of houses later counted
among the empire's founders, including House Kemvimore. That does not project Kemvimore's mature estate
monopoly backward into Year 0. The house's later six centuries of agricultural dominance grew from an
older relationship with land and food rather than existing fully formed at the founding.

### Marresonne's merchants and scribes

The eastern port supplied maritime trade, commercial capital, long-distance relationships, record-keeping,
and learned traditions carrying material inherited from the open centuries of Hauren. Marresonne did not
need to be the first capital to shape the first imperial institutions: merchants, clerks, contracts, and
texts could travel the corridor to Vartonne.

This gives Early Imperial Balishan more than one form of its own ancestry. Vartonne can supply the living
Surface-Halauric speech of the capital and ordinary administration while Marresonnian practice shapes
commercial language, formal records, and renewed borrowing from Hieratic Old Halauri.

### Terinok's cantons

Valan did not conquer Terinok into obedience. He demonstrated martial worth legible across its fiercely
independent cantons, and they swore their blades to him. The oath gave the new empire its decisive military
arm without placing canton law, the Warrior's Code, or internal command beneath an imperial governor.

The oath was first earned by a person, not extracted from a province. Later Valanar rulers inherited the
relationship only conditionally: the house could claim Valan's precedent, but each emperor remained
answerable to the standard that made the cantons recognize him. This is why a later emperor's attempt to
place the Warrior's Code beneath imperial courts did more than create a legal dispute. It proved the throne
no longer understood the compact and was therefore unworthy of Terinok's swords.

Terinok's secession in 680 BR ends that old oath rather than liberating a people who had surrendered their
sovereignty in Year 0. Four centuries later, Elara wins a new blood oath by the same underlying route:
martial worth demonstrated before the cantons, followed by an alliance between equals. Book 3's recurrence
restores Valan's bargain; it does not recreate ordinary imperial subjection.

The exact number and identity of the cantons that witnessed the first oath remain open. The modern twelve
and the modern War Table must not be projected backward unchanged without a separate history.

## What the compact creates

The founding arrangement joins four functions without erasing their sources:

| Function | Principal source | Linguistic pressure |
|---|---|---|
| Food, river transport, and the capital's daily life | Vartonne | Its living Surface-Halauric river-and-market variety has the strongest claim to the grammatical base of the emerging koine |
| Trade, credit, record-keeping, and inherited learning | Marresonne | A related coastal variety contributes commercial vocabulary and formal written habits; texts supply learned Halauri forms afresh |
| Central command, arbitration, and court | Valan and his following | Titles, oath formulas, commands, and court conventions become portable across regions |
| Organized military force | Terinok's participating cantons | Martial loans and shared campaign terminology spread while canton units preserve their own speech and command structures |

The result is an empire able to operate before it possesses a uniform imperial culture. A person can use
the shared market, obey an imperial judgment, or serve beside another region's soldiers without adopting
Balishan as a household identity.

## Consequences for Early Imperial Balishan

Early Imperial Balishan should emerge as a **koine made institutional**, not as Valan's private language
simply imposed on the continent.

- Its likely grammatical center is the Surface-Halauric river-and-market speech used around Vartonne,
  because that is where the first capital, supply system, and daily administration meet. That speech
  already carries an Ancestral Continental human substrate.
- Trade and campaigning level strongly local pronunciations and expressions enough for strangers to work
  together, without eliminating regional varieties.
- Marresonnian scribes help formalize chancery and commercial registers. They also borrow afresh from
  Hieratic Old Halauri into law, religion, measurement, learning, and ceremony, sometimes beside an
  ordinary inherited cognate that has changed beyond recognition.
- Valan's court creates portable titles and oath language. These forms can be more conservative than
  ordinary speech because repeating them exactly carries political force.
- Terinoki speech need not supply the grammar of Imperial Balishan. Independent canton companies retain
  their own internal languages and commands while lending prestigious military terms to the shared koine.
- Later provinces can learn the koine for administration, military coordination, or trade while retaining
  unrelated or distantly related household languages.

Speaking Imperial Balishan initially means *we can trade, march, account, and be judged within one system*.
It does not yet mean *we are one ethnicity*. Centuries of schools, courts, migration, marriage, prestige,
and state service may make it a native identity for later populations.

## Questions still open after the first reconstruction

- Valan's mother tongue and House Valanar's geographic origin before Year 0
- What *Balisha* denoted before it became the name of the empire
- The relationship between Vartonnian and Marresonnian household speech
- The precise form and date of the first Terinok blade-oath
- The scripts used in Vartonne and Marresonne and which one the chancery selected
- Pre-imperial religion, oath practice, and the custodians of transmitted Halauri texts
- Which early territories joined by treaty, conquest, purchase, marriage, or administrative absorption

These questions can be answered independently. None requires changing Valan's founding, Terinok's
conditional oath, or the four-part institutional base already settled here.
