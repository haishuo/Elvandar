# Late Open Halauri — Design Plate 1

**Status:** WORKING — not canon<br>
**Created:** 2026-08-22<br>
**Historical focus:** Metropolitan civic speech, the Crown/documentary register, and the surface contact
register near the end of Hauren's open period

This plate designs the last broadly surface-facing stage of living Open Halauri. It is not the language
of Hauren's founders five thousand years ago, the language spoken at the end of the two-thousand-year
sealing, or the later human manuscript tradition called Hieratic Old Halauri. Those stages descend from,
preserve, or reinterpret material represented here; they are not identical to it.

The plate must naturally produce two P0 outcomes:

- **Kaha'an**, the transferable title “Royal Seat,” with careful /ka.ˈha.ʔan/;
- **Arol Batae**, “Old Guard,” with *arol* meaning “old.”

**Lathion was a disposable working label, not a protected outcome.** This plate does not derive it and was
not shaped to preserve it. The city's replacement name belongs to a separate name-history plate that
begins in Foundational Halauri, passes through three millennia of native change, and only then enters a
surface receiving language. The other protected forms are tested for possible historical routes rather
than forced into native Halauri.

## 1. The intended voice

Late Open Halauri is **measured, resonant, and continuous**. It likes clear vowels, audible morpheme
boundaries, and clauses that gather their detail before landing on the verb. A speaker can compress it in
the market or workshop, but the civic standard sounds as if each part has been placed where another person
can recover it later.

That is a linguistic texture, not a claim that elves always speak formally. Household speech clips,
teases, overlaps, swears, and relies on context. The Crown and contact registers become explicit because
their words must survive strangers, archives, and disputes.

The target sound:

> **An ei navam zenatri.**<br>
> *an ei nava-m zena-t=ri*<br>
> I this compact-OBJ record-CPL=RECORD<br>
> “I entered this compact into the record.”

> **Arol Batae Kaha'ani suan.**<br>
> *arol bata-e Kaha'an-i sua-n*<br>
> old guard-BODY Royal.Seat-LOC be.present-STAT<br>
> “The Old Guard stands at the Royal Seat.”

### How to read the examples

The plate keeps three different things separate:

1. The **bold first line** is normal author-facing romanization of the spoken Halauri sentence. Bound
   endings and clitics are written together with their host. The apostrophe remains because it represents
   the spoken consonant /ʔ/; hyphens and equals signs do not appear.
2. The *italic second line* is morphological analysis, not Halauri spelling. A **hyphen** marks the boundary
   between a stem and a bound affix; an **equals sign** marks a clitic, a phonologically attached element
   whose grammatical scope is larger than its host word.
3. The third line is a word-by-word grammatical gloss, followed by an idiomatic English translation.

The native Halauri script is now under construction in `Halauri Script - Plate 1.md`. Its runic and
operative architecture is authorized, while the exact glyph inventory remains a working visual candidate.
Consequently, “written Halauri” in this plate still means only the agreed author-facing romanization; the
examples make no claim yet about approved native glyphs, word spacing, or whether documentary hands
visually join clitics to their hosts.

## 2. Sound system

### Consonants

| | Labial | Alveolar | Postalveolar / palatal | Velar | Glottal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Stops | p b | t d | — | k g | ʔ **'** |
| Fricatives | f v | s z, th /θ/, dh /ð/ | sh /ʃ/, x /ɕ/ | — | h |
| Nasals | m | n | — | — | — |
| Liquids | — | l r | — | — | — |
| Glides | w | — | y /j/ | — | — |

The author-facing romanization is phonemic:

- **th** /θ/ and **dh** /ð/ are distinct;
- **sh** is /ʃ/;
- **x** is the voiceless alveolo-palatal /ɕ/, not /ks/;
- **y** is /j/;
- apostrophe **'** is always /ʔ/ and never decoration.

Hyphens and equals signs belong only to the italic morphological-analysis line. The apostrophe is the
exception because it represents an actual consonant in the spoken language.

The palatal **x** is uncommon in household vocabulary but productive in mathematical, alignment, and
archival roots. Its limited distribution makes it conspicuous without making it a one-name exception.

### Vowels and hiatus

| Front | Central | Back |
|---:|---:|---:|
| i | — | u |
| e | a | o |

Each vowel keeps its value. Late Open Halauri has no native phonemic diphthongs: **ae, ai, ao, ea, ei,
eo, ia, io, oa,** and other sequences are pronounced as separate syllables. Careful hiatus is one of the
features human daughter languages will later reshape.

Vowel length can arise for emphasis or across lost consonants in metropolitan dialects, but it is not
contrastive in the late civic/contact standard and is not written in this romanization.

### Word shape

The ordinary syllable is **(C)(Y)V(C)**, where **Y** is *y* or *w*. Most roots are **CV.CV, CVCV, CVC,**
or **V.CV**.

- Native onset clusters other than consonant + glide are rare.
- Ordinary lexical codas are *m, n, l, r, s, t,* and *th*.
- Other stops can appear at transparent morpheme boundaries but are released into the following syllable
  when a vowel follows.
- /n/ assimilates in place before /k/ and /g/ but remains written *n*.
- Vowels do not automatically merge at compound boundaries.
- /ʔ/ keeps two vowels separate and can mark a historically meaningful derivational boundary.

### Stress

Stress falls on the **penultimate syllable** of the complete lexical word. Grammatical case endings and
clause clitics do not attract stress, but derivational endings do.

- *arol* /ˈa.rol/ — **A**-rol
- *batae* /ba.ˈta.e/ — ba-**TA**-e
- *Kaha'an* /ka.ˈha.ʔan/ — ka-**HA**-'an
- *ravaon* /ra.ˈva.on/ — ra-**VA**-on
- *xeli* /ˈɕe.li/ — **XE**-li

This gives the learned register a markedly different rhythm from approved Early Imperial Balishan, where
stress belongs to the first lexical root and weak final vowels are absent.

## 3. Nouns and noun phrases

### Number and collectivity

A bare noun is singular or number-neutral according to meaning. **-ir** marks an explicitly individuated
plural.

- **neri** — a person; person as a category
- **neri-ir** — several distinct people

Institutional **-e** is derivational, not plural. It creates a body or office constituted to act
continuously as one thing. Its members may be singular or numerous.

- **bata** — guard, interpose, keep watch
- **bata-e** → **batae** — a commissioned guarding body; a Guard
- **bata-en** — an appointed or trained guard
- **bataen-ir** — several individual guards, considered as people rather than one constituted body

This distinction matters to *Arol Batae*: the phrase names an institution, not merely “old guards.”

### Case

The metropolitan civic standard marks core and spatial relations with endings. Number and derivation come
before case.

| Ending | Function |
|---|---|
| **—** | subject, citation form, or unmarked predicate |
| **-m** | definite or discourse-prominent object |
| **-n** | possessor, source institution, or author |
| **-i** | location in/at |
| **-ur** | motion or transfer toward |
| **-ath** | motion, origin, or derivation from |
| **-va** | accompaniment, instrument, or means |

The object ending is not required on indefinite, incorporated, or low-prominence objects. Legal prose uses
it more consistently than conversation.

- **anen dora** — my hand
- **Kaha'ani** — at the Royal Seat (*Kaha'an-i* in morphological analysis)
- **rava-n zena** — the civic body's record
- **aeva-va** — with or by means of water

### Word order inside the noun phrase

Possessors, demonstratives, quantities, and descriptive modifiers precede the head. Adjectives do not
agree with number or case.

- **ei arol doma** — this old building
- **anen sema** — my elder
- **sei neri-ir** — three people
- **kaha batae** — the sovereign guarding body

Possessor order and modifier-before-head compounding are among the structures the surface contact register
transmits most easily.

### Pronouns

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| First | **an** | **anai** inclusive; **anar** exclusive |
| Second | **ti** | **tir** |
| Third | **se** | **ser** |

Third person is genderless. Inclusive **anai** includes the listener; exclusive **anar** does not. Noun
case endings attach regularly. Consonant-final pronouns insert **e** before another consonantal ending:
**anem** “me” as object, **anen** “my,” **tin** “your,” and **seri** “at/among them.”

## 4. Clauses and verbs

### Metropolitan order

The unmarked metropolitan civic clause is **subject–object–verb**. Case marking permits variation for
topic and emphasis, but the verb normally closes the clause.

> **An sumam radatve.**<br>
> *an suma-m rada-t=ve*<br>
> I child-OBJ treat-CPL=DIRECT<br>
> “I treated the child.”

An understood subject can be omitted. Crown and contact documents prefer to state it.

### Aspect

Verbs do not agree with person or gender. A finite stem takes one aspect ending:

| Ending | Function |
|---|---|
| **-s** | ongoing, iterative, or incomplete |
| **-t** | bounded or completed |
| **-r** | intended, expected, or prospective |
| **-l** | customary, institutional, or repeatedly prescribed |

Vowel-final roots retain the vowel:

- **vena-s** → **venas** — is observing
- **zena-t** → **zenat** — recorded, has inscribed
- **rena-r** → **renar** — will return, intends to return
- **bata-l** → **batal** — guards as a standing duty

Time words locate the event. The aspect system describes its shape rather than assigning a mandatory past,
present, or future tense.

### Negation, command, and question

**ne-** negates the finite verb. The imperative replaces the aspect ending with **-o**. Open questions and
yes/no questions end in **he**.

- **Ti nerenar he?** — Will you not return? (*ti ne-rena-r he*)
- **Ei litam nelitao.** — Do not open this door. (*ei lita-m ne-lita-o*)
- **Ti munam venas he?** — What are you looking at?

Question words remain in the ordinary grammatical position: **mei** who, **muna** what, **kani** where,
**sai** how.

### Identity and existence

**ira** identifies or classifies. **sua** asserts existence, presence, or location. Both take regular
aspect endings when needed.

- **Se radaen ira.** — They are a healer.
- **Arol Batae Kaha'ani suan.** — The Old Guard is present at the Royal Seat.

The unmarked final **-n** in **suan** is the old stative form retained by a small class of positional and
existential verbs. It describes a state rather than an event and is not productive across ordinary verbs.

### Claim-source clitics

Household speech normally leaves source to context. Crown judgments, diplomatic instruments, technical
reports, and Repository-facing work close factual clauses with one of four clitics:

| Clitic | Source claimed by the writer or speaker |
|---|---|
| **=ve** | directly observed, measured, or personally verified |
| **=ri** | warranted by a named record, instrument, or authorized procedure |
| **=so** | received from another speaker or institution |
| **=ma** | derived by calculation, model, or explicit inference |

They mark the route by which a claim is offered, not guaranteed truth. A dishonest witness can misuse
**=ve**; a flawless copy of a false record still takes **=ri**.

These are syntactic clause clitics: they scope over the whole claim and attach phonologically to its final
word. SOV clauses therefore usually show them on the verb, while contact-register SVO clauses may show
them on the final object.

These clitics are a plausible historical source for the approved EIB habit of marking witnessed,
recorded, and reported claims in formal institutions, but the transmission plate must determine whether
EIB inherited, translated, or rebuilt the distinction.

### Relative and subordinate clauses

A relative clause precedes its noun. Its verb takes **-ya**, replacing finite aspect; an aspect particle
inside the clause can clarify time when needed.

> **An venaya neri**<br>
> *[an vena-ya] neri*<br>
> I witness-REL person<br>
> “the person whom I observed”

Complement clauses end in **ke** before the matrix verb:

> **Ti renar ke an theras.**<br>
> *[ti rena-r ke] an thera-s*<br>
> you return-PROSP COMP I know-IPFV<br>
> “I know that you will return.”

## 5. The surface contact register

The contact register is a taught social register of Late Open Halauri, not a separate invented
language. It favors structures that remain recoverable when speaker and listener share little context:

- explicit subjects and referents;
- **SVO** order in ordinary clauses;
- object **-m** only where ambiguity or legal precision requires it;
- separate relational words alongside the metropolitan case system;
- careful vowels, hiatus, and citation pronunciation;
- reduced household idiom and neighborhood wordplay;
- full source clitics in contracts, measurements, and witnessed exchanges.

Compare:

> **Metropolitan civic:** *An ei navam zenatri.*<br>
> *an ei nava-m zena-t=ri*<br>
> I this compact-OBJ record-CPL=RECORD

> **Surface contact:** *An zenat ei navari.*<br>
> *an zena-t ei nava=ri*<br>
> I record-CPL this compact=RECORD

Both mean “I entered this compact into the record.” The second places the verb where human learners and
mixed-language markets can recover it earlier, while retaining the same vocabulary and documentary source
claim.

Generations of human native speakers can generalize the contact order, weaken or lose cases, reduce final
vowels, and replace difficult consonants. Those outcomes belong to the Surface-Halauric transmission
plate, not to “incorrect” Halauri.

## 6. Word-building

Late Open Halauri keeps derivational boundaries audible. Compounds are **modifier–head**, while
productive endings attach to the semantic head.

### Productive endings

| Ending | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-en** | trained, appointed, or habitual role-holder | **rada-en**, healer; **netha-en**, teacher |
| **-e** | constituted office or body acting continuously | **bata-e**, Guard; **netha-e**, teaching faculty |
| **-el** | instrument, interface, or controlled means | **zena-el**, writing instrument; **xeli-el**, alignment tool |
| **-ar** | practice, discipline, or sustained activity | **ketha-ar**, classification; **thuma-ar**, maintenance |
| **-is** | abstract relation or quality | **vera-is**, truth or correctness; **nava-is**, obligation |
| **-eth** | bounded ordinary site associated with a root | **mera-eth**, exchange place or market site |
| **-on** | integrated civic, inhabited, or engineered system | **rava-on**, an integrated civic system |
| **-'an** | authorized seat in which a public office is instantiated | **esha-'an**, tribunal; **kaha-'an**, Royal Seat |

The glottal in **-'an** is part of the suffix. This is a narrow legal-institutional derivation, not a
generic ending meaning “city.” It contrasts with genitive **-n** and ordinary site **-eth**: **eshaeth**
is a courthouse or law-work site, while **esha'an** is the tribunal whose judgments have force. Plate 2
tests the full productive family. A place becomes **Kaha'an** because sovereign authority is legally
present there; mountains and villages do not take the ending merely to sound important.

### Compounds

Compounds preserve full roots more often than their human descendants will.

- **aeva thumael** — water-maintenance instrument
- **riena zenae** — archival recording body
- **kaha batae** — sovereign guarding body
- **arol batae** — old guarding body

Formal institutional phrases favor transparent modifier–head order. Rapid household compounds can
contract, but those contractions do not control Crown spelling.

## 7. The two required name histories

### Kaha'an — P0

**Source morphology:**

- **kaha** /ˈka.ha/ — sovereign authority as a continuous public office; the Crown or sovereignty, not the
  individual body of a ruler;
- **-'an** /ʔan/ — the authorized seat where an office is instantiated and can act.

**kaha + -'an** → **Kaha'an** /ka.ˈha.ʔan/, literally “Seat of Sovereign Authority,” translated **Royal
Seat**.

The semantics make the title transferable without a special exception. A city does not etymologically
become royal forever; it bears *Kaha'an* while the sovereign office is seated there. When the court moves,
the title can move.

This is the sovereign member of a productive family rather than a one-word rescue. Working Plate 2 also
derives **veda'an** “governing seat,” **esha'an** “tribunal,” **netha'an** “certifying chair,”
**ketha'an** “standards tribunal,” **vela'an** “licensing authority,” **riena'an** “archival custody
office,” **bata'an** “guard command,” and **thuma'an** “system control seat.”

The approved EIB native inventory lacks /ʔ/. The later human learned standard must therefore preserve the
spelling and careful glottal pronunciation while ordinary daughter speech adapts it. That split is required
history, not a defect in either language.

### Arol Batae — P0

**Source morphology:**

- **arol** /ˈa.rol/ — old, long-established, or belonging to an earlier order still recognized in the
  present;
- **bata** /ˈba.ta/ — guard, interpose, maintain watch between a protected thing and an approach;
- institutional **-e** → **batae** /ba.ˈta.e/ — commissioned guarding body, a Guard.

Modifier-before-head order gives **Arol Batae** /ˈa.rol ba.ˈta.e/, literally **Old Guard**.

The phrase itself is a human post-Rending formation assembled from inherited learned material. Halauri
elves need not have called an institution *Arol Batae*. Human survivors replace **Kaha Batae** “Sovereign
Guard,” conventionally translated “Imperial Guard,” with **Arol Batae** while preserving the institutional
head. The new phrase is grammatically sound Late Open/Hieratic Halauri even though it commemorates a
human political rupture that occurred after Hauren's fall.

## 9. Naming machinery

### Personal names

Late Open personal names do not grammatically mark gender. The public form usually contains an old lexical
stem plus a name-forming ending or a fossil second root. Household shortenings are expected but cannot be
designed until household structure and kinship practice are settled.

Fresh test names:

| Name | Historical material | Old association |
|---|---|---|
| **Saelen** | old combining stem *sael- + -en* | one associated with the stars |
| **Nethais** | *netha + -is* | teaching or transmission |
| **Thumarel** | *thuma + -rel* fossil name ending | continued maintenance |
| **Aevorin** | *aeva + orin* fossil second stem | water and continuity |
| **Rienath** | *riena + -th* old name ending | preservation |
| **Xelaris** | old combining stem *xel- + -ar + -is* | alignment as a discipline |
| **Veyara** | *veya + -ra* name ending | bonded relation |
| **Kethen** | old combining stem *keth- + -en* | one associated with distinctions |
| **Morael** | *mora + -el* fossilized in names | ending or completion |
| **Saivor** | opaque late-open personal stem | meaning already uncertain by this stage |

The historical readings do not assign destinies. A person called **Rienath** can be forgetful; a child
called **Nethais** is not required to become a teacher.

### Designed places and civic complexes

Halauri distinguishes an ordinary inhabited place from a designed system:

- **orena** — dwelling-place or ordinary settlement;
- **rava** — the civic community assembled there;
- **-ara** — inhabited district, terrace, or settled tract;
- **-eth** — a bounded site characterized by an activity or material;
- **-on** — an integrated civic, inhabited, or engineered system;
- **-'an** — the legally effective seat of a public office, not a settlement class.

The same activity can therefore produce two different places: **nethaeth** is a teaching site, while
**netha'an** is a chair empowered to examine and certify; **thumaeth** is a maintenance room, while
**thuma'an** is the authorized control station for the integrated system.

| **Saelion** | *saeli + -on* | star-observation or star-ordered complex |
| **Aevara** | *aeva + -ara*, with identical vowels coalesced | water settlement |

These began as internal tests rather than map replacements. The sole bounded exception is

feature can have different Halauri, human-contact, and later national names; the oldest available form is
not automatically the one printed in prose.

## 10. Register stress tests

Abbreviations: **OBJ** object, **GEN** genitive, **LOC** location, **IPFV** incomplete, **CPL** completed,
**PROSP** prospective, **HAB** customary, **IMP** imperative, **REL** relative, **REC** record-source,
**DIR** direct-source, **INF** inferred/modelled source.

### Household

> **Ti renar he?**<br>
> *ti rena-r he*<br>
> you return-PROSP Q<br>
> “Will you come back?”

> **An renar.**<br>
> *an rena-r*<br>
> I return-PROSP<br>
> “I will.”

### Civic instruction

> **Ei litam nelitao.**<br>
> *ei lita-m ne-lita-o*<br>
> this door-OBJ NEG-open-IMP<br>
> “Do not open this door.”

> **Xelim kelatve; thumao.**<br>
> *xeli-m kela-t=ve; thuma-o*<br>
> alignment-OBJ measure-CPL=DIR maintain-IMP<br>
> “The alignment has been measured; maintain it.”

### Crown record

> **Anar ei navam zenatri.**<br>
> *anar ei nava-m zena-t=ri*<br>
> we.EXCL this compact-OBJ record-CPL=REC<br>
> “We entered this compact into the record.”

The exclusive pronoun specifies that the addressee did not constitute the recording authority. It does
not imply that the addressee is excluded from the compact itself.

### Technical inference

> **Ei thumael gerema irama.**<br>
> *ei thuma-el gerema ira=ma*<br>
> this maintenance-tool damaged be=INF<br>
> “This maintenance instrument is damaged, by diagnostic inference.”

### Surface instruction

> **Ti pelas aeva meraethur.**<br>
> *ti pela-s aeva mera-eth-ur*<br>
> you carry-IPFV water market-site-ALL<br>
> “You are carrying water to the market site.”

The corresponding metropolitan order **Ti aevam meraethur pelas** (*ti aeva-m mera-eth-ur pela-s*)
remains ordinary inside the city.

### Protected institutional phrases

## 11. Relationship to approved Early Imperial Balishan

This plate deliberately supplies material that can become the approved daughter without making the two
languages cosmetically identical.

Likely transmission pressures include:

- loss of weak final vowels: *nava, mera, kela, vena* → possible *nav, mer, kel, ven*;
- shift from penultimate word stress to initial root stress;
- metropolitan SOV competing with contact-register and substrate SVO;
- loss or fossilization of case endings;
- human loss or substitution of /θ/, /ð/, /ɕ/, and /ʔ/;
- repair of vowel hiatus into contraction, glide, or diphthong;
- survival of full forms in learned texts beside reduced inherited cognates;
- institutional source clitics reanalyzed as independent particles.

Those are targets, not yet an ordered sound history. The Surface-Halauric transmission plate must apply
them to a broad test vocabulary and show where western, coastal, learned, and substrate channels diverge.
It may not invent a unique rule for each desired name.

## 13. What approval would establish

- A clear-vowel, hiatus-preserving language with phonemic /ʔ/, /θ/, /ð/, and /ɕ/.
- Predictable penultimate stress and audible derivational boundaries.
- Modifier-before-head noun phrases, case marking, and metropolitan SOV clauses.
- A derived SVO surface contact register capable of becoming a human mother tongue.
- No grammatical gender, but inclusive/exclusive first-person plural.
- Aspect suffixes and formal claim-source clitics.
- A productive contrast among individuals, constituted bodies, ordinary sites, integrated systems, and
  seats of public authority.
- The exact source semantics and morphology of *Kaha'an* and *Arol Batae*, including the productive
  institutional-locus family to which *Kaha'an* belongs.
- The first controlled cognate pool from which Surface Halauric can reach approved EIB.

It would not yet establish Foundational, Sealed, or Terminal Halauri; the human sound-change sequence;
the script's glyphs; a complete number system; elven household naming; or the reason for the sealing.

## 14. Iteration controls

- **Sound:** keep or remove rare **x** /ɕ/; increase or reduce hiatus; make metropolitan speech softer or
  more consonant-rich.
- **Rhythm:** retain strict penultimate stress or allow lexical exceptions.
- **Grammar:** keep SOV and cases; reduce either; make the contact register closer to or farther from civic
  speech.
- **Institutional texture:** keep the individual/body/system/seat distinctions or simplify them.
- **P0 pronunciation:** retain **A**-rol /ˈa.rol/ and ba-**TA**-e /ba.ˈta.e/, or revise them while preserving
  the protected spelling and meaning.
- **Founding-city compatibility:** after the Foundational city-name plate exists, test whether this sound
  system can receive its late-open descendant without a special rule. The old label *Lathion* gets no vote.

The plate should be approved only if the language itself feels like the civilization that built the city—not
merely because its P0 derivations work.
