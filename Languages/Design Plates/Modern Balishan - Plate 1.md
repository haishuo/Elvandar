# Modern Balishan — Design Plate 1

**Status:** WORKING — general system not canon; the geographic roots and toponymic forms promoted through
Plate 2 are approved in that bounded scope  
**Created:** 2026-08-22  
**Historical focus:** The national language after imperial dissolution, with the urban Kaha'ani and
learned court registers visible inside it  
**Generative test:** A native geography and place-name system for the old Province of Litrava, the coast,
and the post-Rending desert

This plate derives Modern Balishan from the approved Early Imperial Balishan foundation and the settled
history of the court's transfer to the eastern harbor. It was designed before consulting P2–P4 Balishan
names or the current supporting-canon map. Those forms have no authority here. P0 forms are tested after
the system exists; P1 forms receive a preliminary compatibility pass but do not choose the language.

The plate distinguishes three things that English narration can usually translate without displaying:

- **Modern Balishan**, the national and administrative standard;
- **Kaha'ani**, the capital's fast urban dialect, which belongs to Modern Balishan rather than standing
  beside it as another language;
- the **learned court register**, which can deliberately restore Halauri sound and rhythm in titles,
  medicine, law, and ceremony.

## 1. Intended voice

Modern Balishan sounds like a language that has spent a thousand years moving goods and obligations.
Lexical words remain compact and audible. Compounds disclose what an unfamiliar thing is. A speaker can
state a price, source, route, ownership claim, or legal standing with very little grammatical furniture.

The national standard is **clear, direct, and rhythmically firm**. Kaha'ani is the same language under
urban pressure: quicker, more elliptical, and willing to reduce anything the listener can reconstruct.
The court register moves in the other direction. It gives every old vowel its chair and makes the room
wait while it sits down.

Three voices can therefore coexist:

> **Bora do ren vo maulom, ha.**  
> “The ship has returned from the harbor—I saw it.”

> **Sei kel gon. Gev mun ma?**  
> “Three measures of grain. What price?”

> **Arol Batae do gar lo wan, zi.**  
> “The Old Guard kept that enclosure, as the record attests.”

The first is ordinary; the second is characteristically commercial; the third places an inherited
Balishan clause around a learned institutional name.

## 2. How to read the examples

1. The **bold line** is ordinary author-facing romanization of a spoken sentence.
2. When present, the *italic line* exposes morphological structure. A hyphen marks an affix boundary. An
   equals sign marks a clitic boundary. Neither sign belongs to ordinary spelling.
3. The next line is a word-by-word grammatical gloss; the final line is idiomatic English.

The surviving Imperial chancery tradition writes Modern Balishan, but its glyphs and native spacing have
not been designed. This plate establishes Latin-letter romanization only. Apostrophes occur in instructed
learned forms such as **Kaha'an**; they are not decoration, native word dividers, or House markers.

## 3. Historical layers

| Layer | Social history | Modern consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Early Imperial koine | Vartonne's river-and-market grammar is spread by court, army, trade, and record | SVO clauses, prepositions, compact roots, productive compounds, and source particles |
| Marresonnian coast | The established eastern port contributes scribal practice, shipping, finance, and coastal inherited speech | Fuller weak vowels, coastal vocabulary, and a prestige tolerance for longer words |
| Transferred Court | The throne and portable government move east without replacing the harbor population | National speech becomes coastal-facing while old imperial grammar remains intact |
| Learned renewal | The court preserves and repeatedly reborrows selected Halauri words | Penultimate stress, /θ/, /ʔ/, and hiatus remain available as instructed register features |
| Successor-state period | Balisha contracts around Kaha'an while the imperial koine develops separate daughters elsewhere | A national standard centered on Kaha'an and a recognizably local city dialect |
| The Rending | Refugees from the destroyed countryside enter the capital within one generation | Dialect leveling and neighborhood variation, not an implausible new language in twenty years |

The central historical fact is mixture without replacement. Modern Balishan is not Marresonnian with a
new flag, and Kaha'ani is not court language spoken carelessly. Both descend from the transferred koine
whose speakers already knew how to move between household, market, administrative, and ceremonial speech.

## 4. Ordinary sound system

### Consonants

| | Labial | Alveolar | Postalveolar | Velar | Glottal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Stops | p b | t d | — | k g | — |
| Affricates | — | c /ts/ | j /dʒ/ | — | — |
| Fricatives | f v | s z | sh /ʃ/, zh /ʒ/ | — | h |
| Nasals | m | n | — | — | — |
| Liquids | — | l r | — | — | — |
| Glides | w | — | y /j/ | — | — |

The ordinary inventory remains deliberately close to Early Imperial Balishan. **C** /ts/ and **j** /dʒ/
survive as living phonemes; this is one immediate contrast with the working Urathan proposal, where they
merge with /s/ and /ʒ/. Native words still lack /θ/, /x/, and phonemic /ʔ/.

Learned citation can add **th** /θ/ and apostrophe **'** /ʔ/. These sounds mark a word's route, not a
speaker's intelligence. **X** survives in a very small set of historical spellings and is learned word by
word; it has no single productive ordinary value.

### Vowels

The ordinary vowels remain **a e i o u**, with inherited diphthongs **ai, au, oi**. Vowel length is not
phonemic. Unlike Urathan, Modern Balishan does not generally smooth the diphthongs:

- **cai** /tsai/ — crossing, bridge
- **zhai** /ʒai/ — binding debt
- **mau** /mau/ — sea
- **koi** /koi/ — edge, shore, boundary

Coastal prestige helped keep unstressed vowels audible in careful speech. In fast Kaha'ani they centralize,
but ordinary spelling retains their lexical quality.

### Word shape and stress

The inherited syllable remains **(C)(R)V(V)(C)**. Modern loans and compressed compounds permit a second
coda consonant when the first is a liquid or nasal and the second is a stop or **s**. Medial compound
clusters are broader than native root-internal clusters.

Stress remains on the **first lexical root**. Derivational endings are unstressed; compound heads retain a
lighter secondary beat.

- **MAU**-lom /ˈmau.lom/ — protected harbor basin
- **UN**-dar /ˈun.dar/ — cistern, water-store
- **GON**-mer /ˈgonˌmer/ — grain market
- **TAL**-a-**RA**-va /ˈta.laˌra.va/ — river city

Learned words can announce themselves through penultimate stress, restored consonants, or separately
pronounced vowels. This plate does not select a learned technical word merely to demonstrate the sound;
the distinction must arise when a real semantic domain requires it.

## 5. Ordered development from Early Imperial Balishan

These are changes or stabilizations in inherited words. A term freshly taken from a Halauri manuscript
does not retroactively undergo them.

### B-1 — the coastal five-vowel system becomes standard

Weak **a e o** survive more often than in western Vartonnian. They may centralize in rapid speech, but a
speaker can recover the full form without consulting a book. This preserves the audible difference
between many compounds and their roots.

> Early Imperial **borapel** “boat-carrier” > careful Modern **borapel**, rapid Kaha'ani [ˈborəpəl]

### B-2 — inherited diphthongs remain distinct

**Ai, au,** and **oi** do not participate in the Urathan smoothing. Individual high-frequency words may
contract, but there is no language-wide *ai > ae* or *au > o* rule.

### B-3 — final devoicing survives

Final voiced obstruents are pronounced voiceless, while conservative spelling retains the historical
letter. Thus organized-body **-ad** is written with **d** and heard [at]. Documents and ordinary speakers
agree on the word even when writing makes its ancestry more visible than speech does.

### B-4 — ordinary hiatus is repaired

Inherited adjacent vowels contract when identical. Otherwise front vowels favor a light [j], back rounded
vowels favor [w], or one weak vowel is lost in a lexicalized word. Learned citation preserves the boundary.

The extreme high-frequency result is the capital title:

> /ka.ˈha.ʔan/ > /ka.ˈha.an/ > /ka.ˈhaːn/ > **Kaha'an** /kaˈhan/

This is categorical in the city-name because residents use it constantly; it is not a rule that deletes
every **h** in the language.

### B-5 — compound linking **a** remains productive but becomes vulnerable

Careful national speech inserts inherited **a** when a compound boundary would create an illegal cluster.
Frequent Kaha'ani words may lose it once listeners no longer need the analysis.

> **tal + rava** > **Talarava** “river city”  
> local place-name **Talarava** > rapid **Talrava**

The second change is lexical. It cannot be applied indiscriminately to invent compact-looking words.

### B-6 — urban grammar words lean on their neighbors

In Kaha'ani speech, unstressed **en** after a vowel is commonly [n], and **di** before a vowel can reduce
to [d]. Present identity **ye** is omitted whenever the reading remains clear. Official spelling normally
writes the full particles; author-facing prose need not spell the accent.

> **ai en or** “my home” may be heard [ainor]  
> **di undar** “at the cistern” may be heard [dundar]

This produces local speed without making Kaha'ani dialogue a puzzle for the reader.

## 6. Core grammar

### Clauses and ellipsis

Neutral order remains **subject–verb–object**. Verbs do not agree with person, number, or gender. Kaha'ani
market and dock speech omit recoverable material aggressively, but the full clause is always available.

> **Merin do tem gon.**  
> merchant CPL receive grain  
> “The merchant received the grain.”

> **Sei kel.**  
> three measure  
> “Three measures.”

The second is an elliptical answer, not a different grammar.

### Nouns, number, and association

Bare nouns remain number-neutral. **Li** marks explicit plurality; a numeral makes it unnecessary.
Possessors precede the head with **en**. Descriptive modifiers follow the noun, while demonstratives and
numerals precede it.

- **li bora** — ships, explicitly plural
- **sei bora mar** — three large ships
- **Larannas en mauber** — Larannas's quay / the quay belonging to Larannas
- **lo meror nea** — that new market

Relative clauses follow the noun and begin with **ke**.

**Lo** is the general demonstrative “that/the specified.” **Ne** introduces a complement clause; **va**
joins compatible elements and **o** presents a choice. The ordinary question words used in this plate
are **mun** “what,” **mei** “who,” and **kani** “where.”

### Pronouns

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| First | **ai** | **aim** inclusive; **aur** exclusive |
| Second | **tu** | **tun** |
| Third | **e** | **er** |

The inclusive/exclusive contrast remains ordinary. A cartel speaker choosing **aur** in front of a
petitioner is making a political statement with a pronoun.

### Aspect, negation, and command

| Particle | Function |
|---|---|
| **le** | ongoing or incomplete |
| **do** | completed with present relevance |
| **sha** | intended, expected, or prospective |
| **vi** | customary or repeated |
| **nu** | negation; immediately before aspect or verb |
| **a** | imperative; **a nu** forms a negative command |

Time remains lexical: **ye** “now,” **nari** “tomorrow,” and **vori** “yesterday” locate an event when the
context does not.

### Copulas, questions, and prepositions

**Ye** identifies or classifies; **su** locates or asserts existence. Present-tense identity often omits
**ye**, especially in Kaha'an. Questions close with **ma**; question words remain where an answer would.

| Form | Function |
|---|---|
| **di** | at, in, on |
| **to** | toward, into |
| **vo** | from, out of |
| **ran** | for, in exchange for |
| **ki** | with, using, by means of |

> **Su un di undar ma?**  
> exist water at water.store Q  
> “Is there water in the cistern?”

> **Tu sha pel lo gon to mauber ma?**  
> you PROSP carry that grain toward quay Q  
> “Will you carry that grain to the quay?”

### Source particles

The imperial distinction survives in court, trade, and daily argument:

| Particle | Speaker's claimed route |
|---|---|
| **ha** | directly witnessed or personally verified |
| **zi** | entered in or warranted by a record |
| **ro** | reported by another person or institution |

Kaha'ani speakers can use **zi** dryly after an overconfident claim: roughly, “so the paper says.” The
particle still marks source; tone supplies the disbelief.

## 7. Word-building and living registers

### Inherited productive morphology

| Form | Function | Modern example |
|---|---|---|
| **-in** | practitioner or habitual human agent | **merin**, merchant; **radin**, healer |
| **-or** | site regularly dedicated to an activity | **meror**, market; **falor**, travelers' lodging |
| **-el** | instrument, medium, or usable route | **falel**, road; **zenel**, writing medium |
| **-ad** | organized body acting as one | **merad**, merchant company |
| **-as** | practice, quality, or domain | **eshas**, jurisprudence |
| **-u** | relational adjective | **meru**, mercantile |
| **ra-** | cause or enable | **rahal**, keep alive |
| **na-** | mutual or reciprocal | **nanav**, bind one another by compact |

The polity relation **-an** and city relation **-i** also belong to the modern language. They occupy
different histories and meanings:

- **Balishan** — of Balisha, its people, or its national language
- **Kaha'ani** — of Kaha'an, its residents, or its urban speech

### Register is lexical, not ornamental

Ordinary speech prefers inherited roots and transparent compounds. Court speech may select a learned
Halauri term where an ordinary synonym exists, pronounce its full vowels, and give it penultimate stress.
That choice can assert exact technical meaning, institutional continuity, social rank, or merely taste.

A future ordinary/learned doublet must carry a real difference in competence, authority, method, or
institution. Two words meaning exactly the same thing, with the longer one reserved for important people,
would not pass this plate.

## 8. Geographic vocabulary generated from Balishan life

Most necessary geography can be built from the approved Early Imperial roots. Only four new roots are
introduced:

| New root | Core sense | Why the language needs it |
|---|---|---|
| **das** | loose sand; a body or drift of sand | The post-Rending landscape makes sand an unavoidable ordinary category |
| **lom** | still or protected basin of water | The deep-water harbor and smaller sheltered anchorages need a head distinct from the open sea |
| **ber** | built landing edge; quay | Port administration distinguishes engineered cargo frontage from shore |
| **pas** | narrow traversable way through an obstruction | Roads through heights, walls, ruins, and later desert barriers share this practical category |

Everything else below uses inherited roots and productive compounding:

| Modern form | Construction | Core sense |
|---|---|---|
| **dasmel** | *das + mel* | desert; sand country |
| **dasdur** | *das + dur* | dune; sand rise |
| **unlit** | *un + lit* | spring; a natural opening of water |
| **undar** | *un + dar* | cistern or reservoir; held water |
| **unpel** | *un + pel* | aqueduct or water-carrier channel |
| **tirun** | *tir + un* | canal; cut watercourse |
| **talmor** | *tal + mor* | dead river or dry channel; especially post-Rending |
| **feshmel** | *fesh + mel* | salt flat or salt ground |
| **yelas** | *yel + -as* | woodland or forest as a continuous domain |
| **gonmel** | *gon + mel* | cultivated grainland; cropland |
| **limel** | *lim + mel* | open unbroken plain; identical **m** collapses at the compound boundary |
| **maulom** | *mau + lom* | protected harbor basin; by extension a harbor |
| **mauber** | *mau + ber* | sea quay or cargo landing |
| **maukar** | *mau + kar* | reef or sea-rock |
| **durcai** | *dur + cai* | mountain crossing or high pass |
| **durpas** | *dur + pas* | narrow mountain way, especially a road rather than the whole crossing |
| **torwan** | *tor + wan* | defensive wall or fortified enclosure |
| **wanlit** | *wan + lit* | gate; an opening in an enclosure |
| **wankoi** | *wan + koi* | ward, enclosed quarter, or bounded district |
| **peldom** | *pel + dom* | warehouse or carrier's building |
| **gonor** | *gon + -or* | granary; site devoted to keeping grain |
| **meror** | *mer + -or* | market or exchange-site |
| **falor** | *fal + -or* | travelers' house, caravan lodging, or road station |

The compounds encode history without pretending the Rending rewrote the dictionary overnight. **Yelas**
and **gonmel** belong naturally to the old fertile country. **Dasmel** and especially **talmor** become
terribly frequent afterward. A pre-Rending map and a modern refugee can describe the same ground with
different compounds while speaking the same language.

## 9. Place-name machinery

Modern Balishan inherits several naming ages rather than forcing the map through one suffix.

### Layer A — opaque pre-imperial names

Old settlement names can preserve **-onne** or other no-longer-productive material. Modern speakers use
the whole name without parsing it. This layer is where Vartonne and the city's discarded pre-title name
belong; it does not license attaching **-onne** to new settlements.

### Layer B — imperial civic names

Early Imperial **-ava** still marks some deliberately founded or reorganized civic centers. Speakers know
the association with **rava** “city,” but it is no longer the only or most fashionable way to name one.

Fresh forms:

| Name | Historical formation | Founding sense |
|---|---|---|
| **Yelava** | *yel + -ava* | woodland city |
| **Unava** | *un + -ava* | water city; a civic center founded at reliable water |
| **Durava** | *dur + -ava* | high-ground city |

### Layer C — function and landscape compounds

A settlement may simply inherit the feature or work around which it grew. The name need not contain a
word meaning “city.”

| Name | Formation | Plausible origin |
|---|---|---|
| **Maulom** | protected harbor basin | a port whose anchorage was more important than its walls |
| **Feshber** | salt quay | a landing created for the salt trade |
| **Gonmer** | grain market | a market town at an agricultural exchange |
| **Durcai** | high crossing | a settlement controlling a mountain pass |
| **Unlit** | water opening | a village named for its spring |
| **Tirun** | cut water | a settlement beside an imperial canal |

### Layer D — local compression

Extremely frequent names can lose an old linking vowel or redundant head in speech while records retain
the historical form:

| Formal or historical | Local result | Development |
|---|---|---|
| **Talarava** | **Talrava** | the linking vowel disappears after the whole name lexicalizes |
| **Gonmeror** | **Gonmer** | “grain market-site” loses the redundant site ending |
| **Unlit Dren** | **Undren** | “Spring Village” compresses at the compound boundary |

These are model histories, not current-map replacements. They demonstrate how a place can become less
transparent across centuries—the same principle that lets Massalia become Marseille—without pretending
that every inconvenient spelling is a sound law.

### Layer E — post-Rending names

Refugees and road crews name what they must navigate. Such names favor transparent compounds and may be
translated for the reader: **Talmor** “Dead River,” **Dasdur** “Sand Rise,” **Wanlit Ora** “Old Gate,” or
**Falor Nea** “New Roadhouse.” They are young enough that speakers still hear the meanings.

## 10. Personal and House naming machinery

Ordinary personal names continue the inherited two-root pattern. Modern speakers may no longer know the
old reading, and names do not mark gender.

| Fresh name | Earlier material | Historical reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Lumven** | *lum + ven* | clear witness |
| **Merdel** | *mer + del* | gift of exchange |
| **Durven** | *dur + ven* | witness at the height |
| **Veshren** | *vesh + ren* | rain returned |
| **Gonpel** | *gon + pel* | grain carrier |
| **Caidor** | *cai + dor* | crossing-hand |
| **Ashvar** | *ash + var* | fire made fit / good fire |
| **Semdar** | *sem + dar* | elder-keeper |

These are generation tests, not assignments to current characters.

Inherited **mav** names an ordinary household. Canonical Early Imperial **eshnavad** names a law-bound
organized body and can include the legal category translated as a chartered House. House names share a
legal position, not a phonological ending. A House may preserve an old personal name, an opaque substrate
form, a neo-Halauri dynastic claim, a foreign name, or a translated commercial emblem. Modern Balishan
therefore generates House vocabulary without manufacturing a universal “noble sound.”

## 11. Register stress tests

### Ordinary road speech

> **Falel ora le rav to ish.**  
> road old IPFV extend toward east  
> “The old road runs east.”

> **Lo falel do su gonmel; ye le rav ki dasmel.**  
> that road CPL be.at cropland; now IPFV extend through sand.country  
> “That road used to lie in cropland; now it runs through desert.”

The contrast uses ordinary vocabulary. No ceremonial coinage is needed to state the catastrophe.

### Port speech

> **Sei bora do ren vo maulom, ha.**  
> three ship CPL return from harbor direct  
> “Three ships have returned from the harbor—I saw them.”

> **A pel gon to mauber.**  
> IMP carry grain toward quay  
> “Take the grain to the quay.”

### Kaha'ani market speech

> **Sei kel gon. Gev mun ma?**  
> three measure grain price what Q  
> “Three measures of grain. What price?”

> **Pem ran kel. Zi.**  
> five for measure REC  
> “Five per measure. It's posted.”

Everything shared by the transaction disappears. **Zi** closes the attempt to haggle by appealing to the
written price; whether the placard is honest is another matter.

### Administrative speech

> **Aur do ket ne lo merad dar peldom, zi.**  
> we.EXCL CPL recognize COMP that merchant.body hold warehouse REC  
> “We recognized that the merchant company holds the warehouse, as entered in the record.”

### Court and learned speech

> **Xion ye radin; lo eshnavad do ket e, zi.**  
> Xion IDENT healer; that chartered.House CPL recognize them REC  
> “Xion is a healer; that House recognized him, as the record shows.”

The learned proper name does not force the whole sentence into ancient syntax. Modern grammar can carry a
court-register name without ceasing to be Modern Balishan.

## 12. Protected-form test

The language was already built when these forms returned to the table.

| P0 form | Route through the plate |
|---|---|
| **Xion** /ˈzaɪ.ən/ | A learned Marresonnian–Hieratic name with conservative **x** and a lexicalized reading; the surviving **ai** diphthong is native-compatible |
| **Elara** | A dynastic learned or neo-Halauri name; its open trisyllabic shape belongs naturally to the court register without changing ordinary phonology |
| **Valanar** | A neo-Halauri founding House name retained by the dynasty; it need not be parsed as an ordinary two-root name |
| **Kemvimore** | An opaque native, substrate, or mixed charter-name; every segment and its medial clusters are available in Modern Balishan |
| **Rosik** | A fully ordinary native-compatible CVCVC personal shape; no invented etymology is required |
| **Kaha'an** | Whole learned title, ordinary native /kaˈhan/, with the full /ka.ˈha.ʔan/ restricted to instruction and ceremony |
| **Arol Batae** | A complete learned institutional name whose full vowels, hiatus, and stress deliberately sit outside ordinary inherited reduction |

The test requires no new ordinary phoneme and no one-use grammatical exception. The cost is social
stratification: the protected forms do not all pretend to come from one native naming machine.

## 13. Preliminary P1 comparison

Only after completing the plate were prose-established Balishan forms consulted.

- **Balisha / Balishan** fit the ordinary inventory and the established polity suffix without deciding
  what the realm-stem originally meant.
- **Kaha'ani** follows the whole-title plus city-local **-i** route and the same high-frequency contraction
  as the capital name.
- **Hadris, Brennek, Jorik, Tam, Keelen,** and similar compact forms fit ordinary or borrowed personal and
  House phonotactics without amendment.
- **Tor'anar, Ilareth, Aurelan,** and other visibly ceremonial forms can be tested in the learned channel;
  the apostrophe is not made productive in native naming to rescue them.
- Familiar prose-fixed forms such as **Janice, Tobias, Markus, Perrin,** and **Silvanno** do not become
  evidence for native roots. A cosmopolitan imperial capital can transmit old regional, foreign, and
  conventionalized names. Their individual routes remain open, and P1 status favors preservation unless
  later culture-specific plates expose a real contradiction.
- Earlier reconstruction words **fen, sar, len, sathen, -um, dal, seldal,** and **-annas** were not allowed
  to choose this system. Some remain plausible candidates, but none is recovered merely by fitting the
  inventory. In particular, the old proposed honorific **sar** collides with canonical Early Imperial
  **sar** “seed.” Accidental homophony is possible; preserving it solely to save the old proposal would be
  special pleading, so the honorific requires a fresh controlled test or replacement.
- No P2–P4 personal, House, district, or map name was granted authority. The freshly generated geographic
  system must be compared with those names only if this plate is approved.

## 14. Commitments and controls

If approved, this plate would commit Modern Balishan to:

- a conservative five-vowel, three-diphthong ordinary sound system retaining **c** /ts/ and **j** /dʒ/;
- inherited root-initial stress beside a socially distinct learned Halauri channel;
- SVO, prepositional, particle-based grammar with inclusive/exclusive *we* and institutional source
  marking;
- Kaha'ani reduction through cliticization, ellipsis, and lexical frequency rather than eye-dialect;
- productive modifier–head compounds capable of naming terrain, infrastructure, trade, and settlements;
- several historically layered place-name systems rather than a universal fantasy suffix;
- a sharp age contrast between opaque old names and transparent post-Rending names;
- personal names and chartered House names as related social fields, not one morphology.

It would **not** yet approve any generated place as a replacement on the map. It would not settle the
etymology of **Balisha**, the native script, dialect boundaries inside the Warrens, the complete numeral
system, currency and measurement vocabulary, maritime law, street-level slang, or the pronunciation and
history of every P1 personal name.

### Author controls for the next iteration

- **Sound:** keep affricates prominent or make them rarer; keep all diphthongs or allow one regular coastal
  merger; increase or reduce Kaha'ani compression.
- **Rhythm:** preserve the measured imperial beat or make city speech more strongly stress-timed.
- **Grammar:** keep the inherited system highly stable or add a genuinely modern Balishan innovation.
- **Geographic texture:** favor old opaque names, transparent work-and-land compounds, or a balanced
  historical map.
- **Cultural voice:** push Kaha'an further toward mercantile bluntness, courtly archaism, refugee mixture,
  or the tension among all three.

No other part of this working plate becomes canon until the author approves it or approves another bounded
subsystem. `Modern Balishan - Plate 2.md` canonizes the lost provincial names and the geographic material
required to form them; it does not silently approve the remaining sound, grammar, personal-name, or
register proposals here.
