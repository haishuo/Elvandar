# Balishan Realm and Province Names — Plate 1

**Status:** APPROVED — canonical realm/province name history; eastern suite revised 2026-08-27<br>
**Created and authorized:** 2026-08-22; **Litrava revision authorized:** 2026-08-27<br>
**Historical focus:** The pre-imperial Vartonne country, the supra-provincial name *Balisha*, and the
separately named eastern province containing Marresonne and later Kaha'an<br>
**Generative test:** Produce the two referents independently, then test whether imperial contraction can
bring them under one modern state-name without pretending they were always the same place

## 1. The correction

Before this approval, the supporting geography collapsed four different things:

1. **Kaha'an**, the transferable capital-title;
2. **Marresonne**, the older city beneath that title;
3. the province and country surrounding the city;
4. **Balisha**, the empire and later residual state.

That collapse is unnecessary. A capital ordinarily sits inside a region whose name is neither the city-name
nor the state-name, and a contracting empire can retain its political name after the region in which that
name arose has left it.

This plate therefore asks two separate questions:

- What did ***Balisha*** name before it named an empire?
- What was the eastern country around Marresonne called before political contraction made outsiders call
  the whole residual state *Balisha*?

The names are generated from different communities and different historical needs. Their later overlap is
political history, not shared etymology.

## 2. The historical requirements

Any successful pair must preserve the following:

- Vartonne is an old western river settlement and the first imperial capital.
- The river communities around it supplied grain, irrigation, transport, markets, and the grammatical
  center of Early Imperial Balishan.
- Marresonne is an old eastern harbor settlement beside the Hauren surface-contact corridor.
- Its surrounding country existed before the court transferred there in 500 BR.
- The Balishan Empire eventually contained seven provinces.
- Six provinces seceded; the province containing Kaha'an remained beneath the imperial government.
- The Crown, chancery, treaty system, and surviving state continued to call themselves *Balisha*.
- The Rending destroyed the remaining province outside shielded Kaha'an.
- Book 3 calls it only “the province” or “a green province.” No proper regional name has reached prose.

The historical solution must not rename Vartonne, make Kaha'an a permanent city-name in Old Halauri, or
turn the imperial state and its last province into the same original referent.

## 3. The Vartonne source: shared portions and common obligations

Before returning to P1 ***Balisha***, the western substrate needs a word-family appropriate to Vartonne's
material life.

### Ancestral Continental *bal*

Reconstructed western ***bal*** /bal/ means **an apportioned share of a resource held within a common
system, together with the obligation attached to that share**. Its oldest secure domains are irrigation
water, floodplain fields, seasonal labor, and stored yield.

It does not mean abstract equality. One household's *bal* may be much larger than another's. The word says
that the portion is recognized, measured, and owed within a system whose other participants also possess
portions.

That root generates an ordinary Early Imperial family before it generates the realm-name:

| Form | Formation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **bal** | inherited root | apportion a common resource; an assessed share or quota |
| **balin** | *bal + -in* | allotment officer, water-divider, or assessor |
| **balad** | *bal + -ad* | constituted allocation body; a council competent to assign shares |
| **balas** | *bal + -as* | apportionment as a legal or practical domain |
| **balel** | *bal + -el* | schedule, marked measure, or record-medium used to assign shares |

These are not necessarily modern story vocabulary. They demonstrate that *bal* belongs to a living
economic family rather than existing only inside a protected political name.

### Fossil territorial collective *-isha*

Western pre-imperial speech also possessed fossil ***-isha*** /i.ʃa/, a collective territorial ending
meaning approximately **the people and country constituted through the preceding relation**. By Year 0 it
was no longer productive. Speakers could recognize several old regional names containing it without being
able to coin a new province by attaching it to any noun.

```text
bal + -isha
apportioned.common.share + territorial.collective
    → Balisha
```

Pre-imperial ***Balisha***, **BAH-lih-shah**, /ˈba.li.ʃa/, therefore named **the share-country**: the
interdependent river and agricultural communities around Vartonne whose households held recognized parts
of water, land, yield, and work.

“Share-country” is an historical gloss, not a claim that Vartonne was egalitarian. Its entire political
history can be read as argument over who receives a share, who measures it, and who is compelled to supply
one.

## 4. How the country-name becomes the empire-name

Valan does not rename Vartonne *Balisha*. He establishes his capital at the already named city while using
the broader country-name for the polity growing from it.

The transfer proceeds by widening referent:

| Stage | Referent of *Balisha* |
|---|---|
| Before 0 BR | The river-and-field country around Vartonne; its people collectively |
| Year 0 compact | The new central polity built from that material base and Valan's coalition |
| Expansion period | The supra-provincial imperial state, regardless of a subject's household language |
| After the 500 BR transfer | The same portable Crown and state, now governed from Kaha'an |
| After Vartonne's 715 BR secession | The imperial government Vartonne has left, not a name Vartonne can take with it |
| After the other secessions | The residual state consisting of Kaha'an and its remaining eastern province |
| After the Rending | The legal and diplomatic state surviving inside Kaha'an and over the surrounding desert claim |

This is not a province-name migrating east. It is a state-name becoming independent of its birthplace.
Vartonne supplied the original referent; seven centuries of seals, armies, judgments, treaties, and
dynastic continuity transferred ownership of the name to the imperial institution.

The result also strengthens Vartonne's grievance. Kaha'an did not merely take the court and the access
economy. When Vartonne finally departed, the court retained the name of the civilization whose first
material country had been Vartonne's own.

## 5. The eastern source from pre-existing grammar

Late Open Halauri already possesses the two roots needed by the contact history:

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **lita** /ˈli.ta/ | open; expose or permit access |
| **rava** /ˈra.va/ | an assembled civic community |
| **Litarava** /li.taˈra.va/ | the civic community of the opened access; the Community of the Opening |

During Hauren's open period, ***Litarava*** first names neither a Halauri district nor a surface country.
It names the recognized human civic community around the surface access: the households and settlements
with whom Hauren permits contact, exchange, and controlled passage. The formation consequently records a
relationship rather than a Halauri claim of sovereignty over the humans above them.

The original referent can broaden naturally. After the sealing, the access community and its descendants
retain the inherited name even when the Opening itself no longer functions. Their community-name becomes
the country-name; the empire later receives that existing country as a province.

## 6. Human inheritance: Litarava to Litrava

The established Surface-Halauric changes apply without an exception:

1. lexical stress moves toward the first root;
2. post-tonic **a** weakens and disappears between **t** and **r**, because **tr** is a legal human cluster;
3. the full civic head **rava** survives through the coastal and later imperial prestige channel.

```text
Late Open Halauri     Litarava   /li.taˈra.va/
Early Surface         Litarava   /ˈli.ta.ra.va/
Coastal Surface       Litrava    /ˈlit.ra.va/
Early Imperial        Litrava    /ˈlit.ra.va/
```

***Litrava***, **LIT-rah-vah**, /ˈlit.ra.va/, is therefore already the ordinary compact human form. It
does not require a second late Kaha'ani clipping. By imperial times its original morphology is historical
knowledge rather than everyday analysis; ordinary speakers know it as the eastern country containing
Marresonne, the Great Plain, the northern woods, and the waterworks feeding the port.

Approved Imperial-Balishan territorial ***-an*** applies regularly, with the same final-vowel loss seen in
*Balisha > Balishan*:

```text
Litrava + -an → Litravan /ˈlit.ra.van/
```

***Litravan*** means “of Litrava” or a person of the province. It does not compete with ***Balishan***:
before the Rending, a resident can be Litravan provincially and Balishan politically, just as a resident of
Kaha'an can be Kaha'ani locally and Balishan nationally.

### Why Talrava remains

***Litrava*** and ***Talrava*** share a real historical element without being variants of one name.
Contact-era ***Litarava*** is “the Community of the Opening”; much later Early Imperial
***Talarava*** is *tal + rava*, “river city.” Both undergo ordinary human compression into a legal **tr**
cluster. The resemblance therefore marks the survival of Halauri ***rava*** in imperial civic naming.

The chronology keeps the forms distinct. *Litrava* is an old, largely opaque country-name by Year 0;
*Talrava* is a younger, still partly transparent city-name within that country. Talrava is not named after
Litrava, and Litrava is not named after Talrava.

## 7. The seven-province structure

At the empire's mature extent, the provinces are:

1. Vartonne;
2. Litrava, containing Kaha'an;
3. Terinok;
4. Uratha;
5. Gunastran;
6. Erulius;
7. Silaris.

***Balisha*** is the state above them, not an eighth province and not one province privileged into
synonymy with the whole.

After Terinok, Uratha, Gunastran, Vartonne, Erulius, and Silaris leave, Litrava is the only province still
administered by the Crown. At that point two statements are simultaneously true:

- geographically and provincially, the country around the capital is **Litrava**;
- internationally and constitutionally, the surviving state is **Balisha**.

Foreign maps increasingly prefer the politically useful name *Balisha*. Provincial administration,
land records, old families, and residents retain *Litrava*. Neither usage is erroneous.

## 8. The Rending and the loss of the regional name

The Rending destroys Litrava as a functioning country. Kaha'an survives; its provincial cities, farms,
forest, rivers, roads, and administrative network do not.

That catastrophe changes the lexical competition:

- **Litrava** becomes an archival and historical geography—the green province that died—and survives in
  refugee memory, inherited local phrases, and provincial records;
- **Balisha** remains the legal state-name used by the Crown, cartels, treaties, and foreign powers;
- **Kaha'an** remains the city and capital-title;
- **Kaha'ani** remains the local urban identity.

The province-name is therefore genuinely lost from ordinary geopolitical speech. It was not omitted from
history; the thing it named ceased to function, while the larger state-name still had institutions capable
of insisting on its survival.

## 9. Rejected generated routes

The generation produced several structurally possible but weaker eastern names:

| Candidate | Formation | Reason not selected |
|---|---|---|
| **Ishara** | EIB *ish* “east” + old tract *-ara* | A western administrative exonym meaning “eastern tract”; plausible on a tax map, but it gives the pre-imperial inhabitants no name of their own |
| **Mawara** | substrate *mau* “sea” + borrowed *-ara* | A regular “sea tract,” but it reduces the province to its coast and ignores the older Hauren–human interface that made the region distinctive |
| **Koyara** | Halauri *Koiara*, *koi + -ara* “boundary district” | Historically regular but rejected by the author: its **koy-yah-rah** sound silhouette reads as Terinoki/East Asian rather than Balishan continental-Mediterranean |

***Litrava*** wins because both roots and the human changes predate the provincial need, the original
referent gives the surface humans agency as a civic community, and its **tr** cluster places it firmly in
the continental Balishan sound channel.

## 10. Verified blast radius and conformance

The first approval used ***Koyara***, which appeared only in language and support-canon files. Book 3
refers only to “the province” and “a green province,” so the 2026-08-27 revision required no prose,
beat-draft, skeleton, outline, or Royal Road substitution.

The revision found the *Koyara/Koira/Koyaran* suite in supporting geography, a map label, and recent
language material. The conformance pass:

- replaced **Koyara**, **Koira**, and **Koyaran** with **Litrava** and **Litravan** as appropriate;
- retained **Balisha** as the empire and successor-state name;
- changed “all provinces were gone” to “all other provinces were gone” wherever necessary;
- preserved the approved lost-province settlements and features as places within Litrava;
- retained **Talrava** and documented its younger *tal + rava* history rather than treating the shared
  civic head as an accidental collision;
- distinguished pre-Rending Litrava from the post-imperial state of Balisha;
- preserved every generic prose reference to the province because using the proper name adds no narrative
  value there.

No approved city, landmark, or protected form needed to change.

## 11. Approval boundary

Approval establishes:

- substrate ***bal*** and its allocation family;
- fossil territorial collective ***-isha***;
- pre-imperial Vartonnese-country ***Balisha***, “share-country”;
- the referential expansion from country to empire and later contraction to successor state;
- Late Open ***Litarava*** /li.taˈra.va/, “the Community of the Opening,” from *lita + rava*;
- inherited ***Litrava*** /ˈlit.ra.va/ as the province containing Marresonne/Kaha'an;
- regular territorial ***Litravan*** /ˈlit.ra.van/;
- separate younger ***Talarava > Talrava***, “river city,” preserving the same civic head;
- Litrava as the seventh province and the only province remaining beneath the Crown at dissolution;
- the Rending as the event that removes Litrava from ordinary geopolitical speech.

It would not approve all of Late Open Halauri Plate 1 or Modern Balishan Plate 1, define precise provincial
borders in every century, require characters to use the proper name in prose, settle Valan's birthplace,
or establish the deeper history of every other provincial name.
