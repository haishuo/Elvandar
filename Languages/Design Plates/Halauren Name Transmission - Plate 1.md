# Halauren Name Transmission — Plate 1

**Status:** APPROVED — canonical city-name transmission  
**Created:** 2026-08-22  
**Approved:** 2026-08-22  
**Source suite:** Foundational *Halāqurēna* → Late Open **Halauren / Halauri**

This plate distinguishes the name the city used from the name that survives in modern Balishan legend.
It prevents a five-thousand-year-old native word from reaching Xion unchanged merely because the author
likes its sound.

## 1. The four names that can coexist

| Layer | Speakers | Function | Present form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundational endonym | The descending founding community | Dedicatory and civic foundation-name | ***Halāqurēna*** |
| Late Open endonym | Citizens during the last surface-facing centuries | Ordinary native city-name | **Halauren** |
| Human vernacular descendant | Surface communities, then Imperial Balishan daughters | Inherited name in stories, speech, and local memory | canonical **Hauren / Haureni** |
| Learned restoration | Hieratic readers, especially later Urathan scholars | Antiquarian name reconstructed from old documents | **Halauren** or **Halaurena**, provisional |

The Book 3 replacement for *Lathion* belongs in the **human vernacular descendant** row. Xion does not know
the native name from an inscription; he recognizes a legendary city from human tradition. Surface
Halauric Bridge Plate 1 now generates **Hauren** /ˈhau.rən/, **HOW-ren**, with ordinary Balishan
**Haureni** /ˈhau.re.ni/ for the people, language, and adjective. An Urathan scholar may later use a learned
form closer to **Halauren**, but that possibility is not yet a prose plan.

## 2. Native development already proposed

| Stage | Form | Regular development to test |
|---|---|---|
| Foundational | ***Halā-qur-ēna*** /ha.laː.quˈreː.na/ | “living continuity” + “beneath shelter” + refugee habitation |
| Early Open | **Hala'urena** /ha.la.ʔuˈre.na/ | /q/ weakens to /ʔ/; vowel length ceases to contrast |
| Middle Open | **Halaurena** /ha.la.uˈre.na/ | intervocalic /ʔ/ is lost; hiatus survives |
| Late Open | **Halauren** /ha.laˈu.ren/ | fossil ***-ēna*** erodes to proper-name **-en** |

These changes require validation on unrelated vocabulary before approval. The derivation earns the name;
the name does not license one-use sound laws.

## 3. The first human borrowing

Late Open **Halauren** enters surface speech during ordinary contact, not after the city becomes a legend.
The first borrowers therefore know that it is a city and hear native speakers say it repeatedly. The
borrowed form should begin conservatively:

> Late Open /ha.laˈu.ren/ → early Surface /ˈha.lau.ren/

The notation marks two ordinary accommodations already demanded by the contact history:

- human root-initial stress replaces native late stress;
- native /a.u/ hiatus becomes the human diphthong /au/.

Everything after that point must be generated by regular Surface-Halauric and Balishan changes. This plate
does not choose between vowel smoothing, syncope, consonant loss, analogy, spelling pronunciation, or
dialect borrowing until those processes have a larger lexicon to operate on.

## 4. The two-thousand-year fork

Once the city seals, the name travels by two different channels.

### Vernacular inheritance

The living spoken loan continues changing in the ancestors and daughters of Imperial Balishan. It can
lose syllables, merge vowels, acquire an opaque spelling, and diverge by region. This route produces the
short, sayable name known from childhood stories and whispered by Xion in Book 3.

### Learned preservation

Documents preserve **Halauren**, **Halaurena**, or an even more conservative spelling. Later scholars apply
their own pronunciation rules to it. This route can produce an Urathan learned name that looks visibly
closer to the endonym than the ordinary Balishan word does.

The two forms need not compete. A scholar can know that modern **[vernacular form]** and textual
**Halauren** are historically the same city while most speakers know only the vernacular name.

## 5. Required tests for the eventual modern form

The winning descendant must work in the prose slots that currently carry *Hauren*:

> the gate of **[NAME]**, the lost City of Gears

> **[NAME]** was real, and it was a city.

> “**[NAME].**”

It must also support, whether by inheritance or a learned doublet:

- a natural adjective for technology, script, architecture, and history;
- an author-facing label for the civilization and its language stages;
- regional pronunciations in Balishan, Urathan, and Vartonnian without making every difference arbitrary;
- a collision check against major real and fictional names after the sound history generates candidates.

## 6. Generated result and decision boundary

`Surface Halauric Bridge - Plate 1.md` supplies forty-one unrelated cognate tests before applying its
rules to the name. It derives:

> ***Halāqurēna*** → **Halauren** → **Hauren**

and distinguishes native **Halauri** from ordinary Balishan **Haureni**. The author approved this complete
reader-facing suite on 2026-08-22. The former project label *Lathion* was then retired in a separate
controlled prose-and-canon migration.
