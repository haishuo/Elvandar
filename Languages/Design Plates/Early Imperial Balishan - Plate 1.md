# Early Imperial Balishan — Design Plate 1

**Status:** APPROVED — canonical foundation  
**Created:** 2026-08-22  
**Approved:** 2026-08-22  
**Method:** Generated from the historical and cultural brief before corpus comparison

This plate proposes the language standardized during the first centuries of the Balishan Empire. It does
not attempt to preserve any existing Balishan word, title, house, personal name, or place-name. The five
protected forms known during generation—**Xion, Elara, Valanar, Kemvimore,** and **Rosik**—were held aside
rather than used to choose this plate's sounds. At approval, **Kaha'an** and the complete institutional
name **Arol Batae** joined them at P0. A separate historical pass must give all seven forms credible routes.
In particular, the plate's lack of a native glottal stop or apostrophe means *Kaha'an* must enter through a
learned, archaic, or otherwise non-native channel rather than forcing those features into ordinary Early
Imperial speech.

## 1. The intended voice

Early Imperial Balishan is a language people can bargain, command, reckon, swear, litigate, and flirt in.
Its ordinary words are compact. Its vowels stay clear; its consonants close a word cleanly without making
the language harsh. Compounds are easy to build and easy to hear, which suits a multilingual river-and-road
empire whose officials, soldiers, carriers, and merchants need to learn useful speech quickly.

It is not faux Latin and it is not generic apostrophe-fantasy. Prestige archaism can enter later through
the learned Halauri register, but the living imperial language must already work without that layer.

The target sound is **measured, lucid, and slightly clipped**:

> **Navdar do ket eshnavad.**  
> “Navdar recognized the chartered House.”

> **Tal le dem rava.**  
> “The river feeds the city.”

## 2. Sound system

### Consonants

| | Labial | Alveolar | Postalveolar | Velar | Glottal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Stops | p b | t d | — | k g | — |
| Affricates | — | c /ts/ | j /dʒ/ | — | — |
| Fricatives | f v | s z | sh /ʃ/, zh /ʒ/ | — | h |
| Nasals | m | n | — | — | — |
| Liquids | — | l r | — | — | — |
| Glides | w | — | y /j/ | — | — |

Native Early Imperial Balishan has no /θ/, /x/, or phonemic glottal stop. Those sounds may later belong to
a learned or foreign register, but this plate does not reserve them as marks of sophistication.

**Romanization is literal:** *c* is always /ts/, *j* is /dʒ/, *sh* is /ʃ/, *zh* is /ʒ/, and *y* is /j/.
There are no silent letters and no apostrophes in native words.

### Vowels

| Front | Central | Back |
|---:|---:|---:|
| i | — | u |
| e | a | o |

The native diphthongs are **ai, au,** and **oi**. Every other written vowel sequence crosses a morpheme or
syllable boundary. Vowel length is not phonemic.

### Word shape

The ordinary syllable is **(C)(R)V(V)(C)**, where the optional second consonant is a liquid or glide. Native
roots favor **CVC, CV, CVCV,** and **CCVC**.

- Common initial clusters: stop + *r/l*, *s* + stop, and consonant + *w/y*.
- A native syllable has at most one final consonant. Surface codas are *p, t, k, m, n, s, sh, l,* or *r*.
- Underlying voiced obstruents may occur in that position but devoice: written final *d* is heard [t],
  final *z* is heard [s], and so on.
- /n/ becomes [ŋ] before /k/ or /g/ but remains written *n*.
- Identical consonants at a compound boundary collapse: *sor + ren* → **Soren**, not **Sorren**.
- When compounding would create an illegal cluster, **a** is inserted. Two liquids cannot meet across a
  native compound boundary, so *tal + rava* → **Talarava**.

### Stress and rhythm

Stress falls on the **first syllable of the lexical root**. Derivational endings never take stress.
Compounds keep primary stress on the first member and a lighter secondary stress on the second.

- **NAV**-dar /ˈnav.dar/
- **MER**-or /ˈmer.or/
- **TAL**-a-**RA**-va /ˈta.laˌra.va/
- **ESH**-na-vad /ˈeʃ.na.vat/

Grammar particles are normally unstressed. The result is a line with firm lexical beats separated by
lighter connective syllables.

## 3. Core grammar

### Clause frame

The neutral order is **subject–verb–object**. The verb does not agree with person, number, or gender.

> **Navdar do ket eshnavad.**  
> Navdar COMPLETED recognize chartered-body  
> “Navdar recognized the chartered House.”

Objects remain unmarked. Context and word order do most of the work; formal writing repeats nouns rather
than multiplying case endings.

### Nouns and number

A bare noun is number-neutral. Number is stated only when it matters.

- **nar** — a person, people, personhood in context
- **li nar** — multiple people, explicitly
- **sei nar** — three people; the plural particle is unnecessary after a numeral

There is no grammatical gender and no noun class. Natural gender can be expressed lexically when the
speaker needs it.

### Possession and association

The possessor comes first, joined to the head by **en**. The same construction covers ownership, kinship,
authorship, institutional belonging, and material composition; context supplies the relationship.

- **ai en sava** — my sister
- **vedin en sorin** — the ruler's speaker
- **kar en dom** — a building of stone

This is not an inalienable-possession system. A hand and a house use the same grammar even though the
relationships differ.

### Modification

Demonstratives, numerals, and the explicit plural particle precede the noun. Descriptive modifiers follow
it. A modifier does not agree with its noun.

- **lo li mav ora** — those old households
- **sei kel gon** — three measures of grain
- **rava mar** — the great city / a great city

Relative clauses follow the noun and begin with **ke**. The missing role is inferred from context.

> **nar ke ai do ven**  
> person REL I COMPLETED see  
> “the person whom I saw”

### Pronouns

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| First | **ai** | **aim** inclusive; **aur** exclusive |
| Second | **tu** | **tun** |
| Third | **e** | **er** |

Third-person pronouns do not encode gender. **Aim** includes the listener; **aur** excludes them. The
distinction is ordinary speech, not ceremonial language.

### Aspect, time, and negation

Verbs do not inflect for tense. Optional particles before the verb mark how an event unfolds; time words
locate it.

| Particle | Function |
|---|---|
| — | simple, generic, or contextually complete event |
| **le** | ongoing, incomplete, or currently unfolding |
| **do** | completed event with present relevance |
| **sha** | intended, expected, or future event |
| **vi** | customary or repeated event |
| **nu** | negation; placed immediately before the aspect particle or bare verb |

- **e le mer** — they are trading
- **e do mer** — they have made the exchange
- **e sha mer nari** — they will trade tomorrow
- **e vi mer di rava** — they habitually trade in the city
- **e nu do mer** — they did not make the exchange

The imperative uses **a** before the verb. A negative command is **a nu + verb**.

- **A lit.** — Open it.
- **A nu lit.** — Do not open it.

### Copulas and existence

**Ye** identifies or classifies; **su** locates or asserts existence. Informal present-tense identity may
omit **ye**, but formal and contrastive speech retains it.

- **E ye radin.** — They are a healer.
- **E radin.** — They're a healer. (ordinary speech)
- **E su di rador.** — They are at the clinic.
- **Su un di falel.** — There is water on the road.

Descriptive roots predicate directly: **rava mar**, “the city is great,” as well as “a great city” when
used inside a noun phrase. Intonation and position distinguish the two in speech; formal writing can use
**ye** to remove ambiguity.

### Questions and subordination

Yes/no and open questions end in **ma**. Question words remain in the position occupied by the answer.

- **Tu do ven e ma?** — Did you see them?
- **Tu do ven mei ma?** — Whom did you see?
- **E su kani ma?** — Where are they?

**Ne** introduces a complement clause; **va** joins compatible elements and **o** presents alternatives.

### Source marking

Ordinary conversation may omit source. Chancery judgments, contracts, depositions, and military reports
must close a factual clause with one of three source particles:

| Particle | Claim |
|---|---|
| **ha** | directly witnessed or personally verified |
| **zi** | entered in, copied from, or warranted by a record |
| **ro** | reported by another speaker |

The particles certify the speaker's route to the claim, not whether the claim is objectively true. A liar
can misuse **ha**; a copied error can carry **zi**. The distinction gives imperial institutions a precise
tool without pretending bureaucracy makes people honest.

## 4. Word-building

Early Imperial Balishan prefers compounds and a small set of productive derivations over long inflectional
paradigms. Roots are usually stable across noun and verb uses: **mer** can mean “exchange,” “trade,” or “to
trade” according to position.

### Productive endings

| Form | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-in** | habitual human agent or practitioner | **rad-in**, healer; **sor-in**, speaker or advocate |
| **-or** | place regularly dedicated to an activity | **mer-or**, market; **rad-or**, clinic |
| **-el** | instrument, medium, or usable route | **fal-el**, road; **zen-el**, writing implement or record medium |
| **-ad** | organized body acting as one | **nav-ad**, sworn association |
| **-as** | abstract quality, practice, or domain | **esh-as**, jurisprudence; **var-as**, fitness or propriety |
| **-u** | relational adjective | **esh-u**, legal; **mer-u**, mercantile |

**-Ad** is not an ordinary plural. A crowd of merchants is **li merin**; a merchant company capable of
holding property and making a compact is **merad**.

### Productive prefixes

| Form | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ra-** | cause or make possible | **ra-hal**, keep alive; **ra-lit**, cause to open |
| **na-** | mutual, shared, or reciprocal action | **na-nav**, bind one another by agreement |

### Compounds

Compounds are **modifier–head**. The final root states what the thing is; the first narrows it.

- **esh-nav-ad** — law-bound organized body; a chartered corporation or noble House
- **gon-mer-or** — grain market
- **kar-gar** — stone watch or stone fort
- **tal-pel-in** — river carrier

Frequent compounds can become opaque over time. Speakers do not need to hear the founding metaphor every
time they say a name.

### Houses and corporate names

The ordinary household is **mav**. A legally recognized body that can hold obligations across generations
is an **eshnavad**, literally a “law-bound organized body.” That category can include a noble House, guild,
temple corporation, or chartered company; status comes from the instrument that recognizes it, not from a
special noble sound.

An ancestral corporate name may add **-ad** to a founder's name. A granted name more often uses a compact
claim-compound.

| Corporate name | Formation | Possible reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Navdarad** | *Navdar + ad* | the constituted body of Navdar |
| **Talvenad** | *Talven + ad* | the constituted body of Talven |
| **Merdar** | *mer + dar* | keeper of exchange |
| **Kardar** | *kar + dar* | stone hold |
| **Talpel** | *tal + pel* | river carrier |
| **Gonlim** | *gon + lim* | the grain made whole / full measure |

The formal phrase is **eshnavad Navdarad**; reader-facing prose could render it “House Navdarad” if that
translation proves natural in scene.

### Settlements and cities

**Rava** is the ordinary word for a politically organized city, not a mandatory place-name ending. Older
toponyms use three productive patterns:

1. landscape root + reduced civic **-ava**;
2. a head-final description of the site's original function or feature;
3. an opaque inherited local name, which later speakers use without analysis.

This produces shared regional texture without requiring every city to announce “city” in its name—just as
real political states and capitals need not share a root.

## 7. Stress tests

Abbreviations: **IPFV** ongoing, **CPL** completed, **PROSP** prospective, **NEG** negative, **GEN**
associative/genitive, **REL** relative, **Q** question, **REC** recorded source.

### Ordinary narration

> **Tal le dem rava.**  
> river IPFV nourish city  
> “The river feeds the city.”

> **Falel ora le rav to ish.**  
> travel.route old IPFV extend toward east  
> “The old road runs east.”

> **Radin do rad nal.**  
> healer CPL treat child  
> “The healer treated the child.”

### Family speech

> **Ai en sava sha ren nari.**  
> I GEN sister PROSP return tomorrow  
> “My sister will return tomorrow.”

> **Ai le ven ne tu en li dor le resh.**  
> I IPFV witness COMP you GEN PL hand IPFV tremble  
> “I can see that your hands are shaking.”

The second line can be tender, clinical, accusatory, or threatening. The grammar does not decide the
emotion for the scene.

### Political speech

> **Aur nu do ket lo eshnavad.**  
> we.EXCL NEG CPL recognize that chartered.body  
> “We did not recognize that House.”

The exclusive pronoun matters: the speaker denies the listener membership in the body that made the
decision.

### Chancery language

> **Ai, vedin en sorin, do ket eshnavad Navdarad di sei-ya ari, zi.**  
> I ruler GEN speaker CPL recognize chartered.body Navdarad at three-ORD day REC  
> “I, the ruler's speaker, recognized House Navdarad on the third day, as entered in the record.”

The sentence is structurally ordinary Balishan. Its formality comes from explicit roles, complete dating,
and mandatory source marking rather than a separate pseudo-ancient grammar.

### Market speech

> **Mun gev ran sei kel gon ma?**  
> what price for three measure grain Q  
> “What price for three measures of grain?”

> **Pem.**  
> five  
> “Five.”

The reply is grammatical because everything recoverable is omitted. Market speech rewards speed; the
chancery rewards explicitness. Both are the same language.

## 8. Canonical commitments

- Clear five-vowel native speech with no native apostrophe or /θ/.
- Compact, initially stressed roots and audible head-final compounds.
- SVO clauses, modifier-after-noun description, and possessor-before-head **en** phrases.
- Optional number, no grammatical gender, and inclusive/exclusive *we*.
- Particle-based aspect rather than tense conjugation.
- A small, productive derivational system.
- Evidential source particles that become obligatory in imperial institutions.
- Native personal, corporate, and settlement naming systems capable of generating new forms without
  consulting a list of existing fantasy names.

It does **not** yet commit to the sound of Old Halauri, the exact route of the protected names, daughter
language changes, script, larger numerals, poetry, or a complete dictionary.

## 9. Approval and future extension

The author approved this plate on 2026-08-22. Its sound, grammatical, derivational, and naming foundations
are now the canonical starting point for Early Imperial Balishan. Later work may expand missing domains,
derive historical stages and daughters, and build the learned Halauri channel required by P0 forms. It
does not silently replace these foundations; a structural revision requires a new authorial ruling.

Useful controls for daughter languages and neighboring plates remain:

- **Sound:** softer ↔ harder; more flowing ↔ more clipped; more or fewer affricates and clusters.
- **Rhythm:** initial stress ↔ penultimate stress; shorter roots ↔ longer words.
- **Grammar:** more analytic ↔ more inflected; keep or remove inclusive/exclusive *we*; keep or remove
  institutional source marking.
- **Aesthetic identity:** more intimate/mercantile ↔ more martial/imperial; more distinct from familiar
  European language textures ↔ more immediately pronounceable to an English reader.
- **Naming texture:** compound transparency ↔ historical opacity; more shared place-name morphology ↔
  more regional variety.

Corpus comparison and accommodation begin only after the relevant source and descendant plates exist.
