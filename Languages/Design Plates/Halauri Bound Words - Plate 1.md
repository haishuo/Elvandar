# Halauri Bound Words — Plate 1

**Status:** WORKING BOUND-WORD PLATE — the runic/operative architecture and short-name principle are
authorized; the exact rune inventory, frame grammar, words, joins, and failure tests below await approval<br>
**Created:** 2026-09-02<br>
**Depends on:** `Halauri Script - Plate 1.md` and `Late Open Halauri - Plate 1.md`<br>
**Purpose:** Test whether ordinary Halauri words can also form compact, intelligible Arcane control arrays
without changing their linguistic meanings merely to rescue the magic

## 1. What this plate tests

A bound word must pass two independent tests.

1. **It must be a real word.** Its pronunciation, morphology, and ordinary meaning must belong naturally
   to Late Open Halauri.
2. **It must be a real working.** In a compatible host, its rune sequence and exact joins must express a
   useful Arcane operation with no appeal to the word's meaning as a source of power.

The coincidence is designed by Halauri artificers, not by the universe. A word that says “open” is selected
for a door rail because its rune operators can also govern a safe opening cycle. Halauri engineers can coin
technical terms around useful arrays, but they cannot make an invalid operator chain execute by declaring
that its spelling means something convenient.

## 2. The standard civic bound-word frame

This plate proposes a common **governor-initial** frame for short civic words.

- The **first tessera** is the governing operation.
- Later tesserae qualify its target, condition, method, baseline, or termination according to their keyed
  joins.
- The ordinary word is read left to right. The operative reading is hierarchical rather than a claim that
  every tile fires chronologically from left to right.
- The host supplies omitted arguments that are physically fixed by the device: a door rail already knows
  which barrier it governs; a lift rail already knows its shaft and car.
- A closure key after the last tessera returns control to the host's ordinary safe state.

This is one tested frame, not the whole technical language. Longer arrays can branch, repeat, nest, run in
parallel, or address several channels. The four-letter civic frame is common because it is easy to inspect,
replace, teach, and audit.

The operator glosses below use brackets to show scope. They are author-facing descriptions of the keyed
joins, not a native written syntax.

## 3. Seven first bound words

### *lita* /ˈli.ta/ — “open; expose access”

**Tesserae:** ***Lit–Ir–Tir–Ar***<br>
**Runes:** L–I–T–A<br>
**Operator frame:** `OPEN [IDENTIFY boundary] [SEVER from PRIOR state]`<br>
**Typical host:** Door, gate, sealed case, access hatch

The host identifies the prepared boundary and temporarily separates it from its prior closed relation. Its
termination key returns the barrier to the recorded safe state after passage. A linked authorization rail
may decide *who* is admitted; ***lita*** itself does not judge the entrant.

This remains the flagship bound word because public label, spoken instruction, and machine operation agree
without pretending that the spoken word causes the opening.

### *vela* /ˈve.la/ — “permit bounded access”

**Tesserae:** ***Vel–Esh–Lit–Ar***<br>
**Runes:** V–E–L–A<br>
**Operator frame:** `AUTHORIZE [CONDITION] {OPEN against PRIOR permission state}`<br>
**Typical host:** Credential plate, restricted cabinet, civic checkpoint, Arcanum access rail

The host compares a presented key, identity, office, or other bounded qualification against its recorded
permission state. Success authorizes a linked opening operation. ***Vela*** does not move a door and cannot
decide whether someone is morally worthy; it answers a narrower engineered question supplied by the host.

### *sela* /ˈse.la/ — “transfer between established levels or stations”

**Tesserae:** ***Sel–Esh–Lit–Ar***<br>
**Runes:** S–E–L–A<br>
**Operator frame:** `TRANSFER [CONDITION] {OPEN PRIOR route}`<br>
**Typical host:** Lift, freight platform, level-to-level carrier, prepared spatial conduit

The host transfers its car or cargo only when the route and gate conditions are satisfied, opening the
previously established channel rather than inventing a destination. Fire supplies motion, Air supplies the
route relation, Earth supplies the car and mechanism, and Water may maintain or sequence the cycle.

### *zena* /ˈze.na/ — “enter into record”

**Tesserae:** ***Zen–Esh–Nav–Ar***<br>
**Runes:** Z–E–N–A<br>
**Operator frame:** `RECORD [CONDITION] {BIND to PRIOR index}`<br>
**Typical host:** Sensor log, civic register, archive instrument, maintenance recorder

When its declared trigger is met, the host records a measured state and binds it to an established index or
record chain. It cannot determine whether the record is true beyond the quality of its sensors, and it
cannot preserve information after its material or temporal storage channel fails.

### *rena* /ˈre.na/ — “return; restore”

**Tesserae:** ***Ren–Esh–Nav–Ar***<br>
**Runes:** R–E–N–A<br>
**Operator frame:** `RETURN [CONDITION] {BIND to PRIOR baseline}`<br>
**Typical host:** Safety reset, valve, alignment tool, lift brake, self-closing mechanism

When the safety condition is met, the host returns the addressed system to its recorded baseline and binds
that state long enough for ordinary control to resume. It cannot rebuild absent matter, recover an
unrecorded state, or overcome a physical obstruction merely because the word means “restore.”

### *thuma* /ˈθu.ma/ — “maintain; keep operational”

**Tesserae:** ***Thum–Ur–Mer–Ar***<br>
**Runes:** TH–U–M–A<br>
**Operator frame:** `SUSTAIN [TARGET] {EXCHANGE against PRIOR baseline}`<br>
**Typical host:** Climate loop, power regulator, ventilation system, water or heat exchanger

The host sustains a designated output by exchanging current inputs against an established baseline. The
word is not perpetual motion: its source depletes, its materials wear, and an impossible target produces a
safe shutdown or a dangerous feedback condition according to the quality of the frame.

### *bata* /ˈba.ta/ — “guard; interpose”

**Tesserae:** ***Bat–Ar–Tir–Ar***<br>
**Runes:** B–A–T–A<br>
**Operator frame:** `GUARD [PRIOR approved state] {SEVER intrusion; restore PRIOR boundary}`<br>
**Typical host:** Civic ward, sealed threshold, containment perimeter, protected conduit

The two ***Ar*** tesserae do different work because their joins give them different scope: the first points
to the approved baseline against which approach is tested; the second points to the intact boundary to be
restored after intervention. The host must receive identity or threat criteria elsewhere. ***Bata*** cannot
recognize evil, treason, or intent.

The ordinary root survives in P0 ***Arol Batae***, but this working proposal does not claim that the Old
Guard's name is itself an executable array. ***Batae*** is an institutional collective, not the bare
technical word ***bata***, and a name written on a wall remains inert in any case.

## 4. Why the family is convincing

The seven words form three visible operator families without collapsing into mechanical synonyms.

| Shared ending | Bound words | Shared machine logic | Linguistic difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **-ela** | *vela, sela* | condition an opening against a prior state | authorize access vs transfer through the route |
| **-ena** | *zena, rena* | condition and bind against a prior state | create a record vs return to the record |
| **-a** baseline closure | all seven | final *Ar* addresses an established state | the governing first rune determines the operation |

This resemblance is historical engineering pressure. Civic artificers favor short words whose inherited
or technical meanings make useful mnemonic labels, then standardize the matching safe frame. It does not
mean every Halauri word ending in ***-a*** is automatically a runeword; most ordinary words have no approved
host, joins, or useful operator closure.

## 5. Controlled one-rune substitutions

### A valid bound word installed in the wrong host: *zena* → *wena*

**Swap:** ***Zen*** / RECORD → ***Wen*** / REPEAT<br>
**New sequence:** ***Wen–Esh–Nav–Ar***<br>
**Ordinary reading:** working technical ***wena***, “cycle; turn again”<br>
**Operator frame:** `REPEAT [CONDITION] {BIND to PRIOR index}`

Both the language and the operator chain remain valid. The problem is the archival host. Instead of making
a new record, it repeatedly rebinds the previous indexed state whenever the trigger holds. A good recorder
detects the no-change cycle and opens its frame; a damaged or illicit host can consume its stored charge,
overwrite a live buffer, or lock downstream readers onto stale data.

This is the dangerous class: a valid word, a valid array, and the wrong machine.

### A valid bound word with the wrong governing operation: *sela* → *vela*

**Swap:** ***Sel*** / TRANSFER → ***Vel*** / AUTHORIZE<br>
**New sequence:** ***Vel–Esh–Lit–Ar***<br>
**Ordinary reading:** ***vela***, “permit bounded access”

In a lift rail, the substituted word can authorize use but contains no transfer command. A properly
interlocked lift remains where it is and refuses to open a gate without station confirmation. An old or
bypassed installation may unlock controls while leaving the car unmoved. Nothing explodes; the device now
does the wrong coherent thing.

### A valid array that ceases to be a word: *lita* → *kita*

**Swap:** ***Lit*** / OPEN → ***Kur*** / CLOSE<br>
**New sequence:** ***Kur–Ir–Tir–Ar***<br>
**Ordinary reading:** none in the current Late Open lexicon<br>
**Operator frame:** `CLOSE [IDENTIFY boundary] [SEVER from PRIOR state]`

The array can remain technically meaningful in a compatible maintenance frame: close the identified
boundary and separate it from its prior open relation. But ***kita*** is not presently a Halauri word, so
the sequence loses the mnemonic and civic label that made ***lita*** a bound word. A public rail rejects the
wrong governor key; an unrestricted service rail may execute it, with the obvious crushing hazard if its
safety condition has also been bypassed.

### A formally closed loop with an impossible demand: *thuma* → *thura*

**Swap:** ***Mer*** / EXCHANGE → ***Ren*** / RETURN<br>
**New sequence:** ***Thum–Ur–Ren–Ar***<br>
**Ordinary reading:** none established<br>
**Operator frame:** `SUSTAIN [TARGET] {RETURN to PRIOR baseline}`

The sequence repeatedly demands that its target return to baseline. In an alignment or regulating host it
may be useful. In a climate host facing a continuing heat load, it commands correction without supplying
the exchange relation that removes the heat. The system oscillates, reaches its cycle limit, or drains its
source. The runes do not turn contradiction into free energy.

## 6. Failure hierarchy

Halauri safety practice distinguishes failures by what remains valid.

1. **Shape failure:** cracked or badly cut rune; the frame should remain inert.
2. **Join failure:** recognizable runes with invalid scope; the frame should refuse closure.
3. **Host mismatch:** valid array in an incompatible keyed rail; physical keying should prevent insertion.
4. **Semantic mismatch:** valid array in a technically compatible but inappropriate host; the machine does
   the wrong coherent thing.
5. **Power failure:** correct host and array with insufficient or unstable charge; the host follows its
   safe-failure path if that path remains intact.
6. **Bypassed protection:** recut tesserae, unrestricted service rails, damaged frames, or deliberate
   defeat of interlocks; this is where experimentation becomes dangerous.

Random rune combinations are therefore more likely to do nothing than to produce spectacular magic.
Danger arises from a sequence that is *nearly right*, installed by someone who knows enough to defeat the
protections and not enough to understand the resulting working.

## 7. Narrative and canon boundaries

This plate creates no automatic story knowledge. Xion, Elara, and the close-third narrator do not know the
rune-names or operator readings merely because the author does. A modern scholar may recognize a public
word while misunderstanding its technical frame; a Haureni maintenance record can distinguish the two;
and Repository encoding remains beyond both.

Nothing here makes the Brass Door's marks a hidden runeword. Its apparent inscription still resolves into
teeth, bearings, and graduations. The relationship works in the other direction: the scholars mistake
machinery for writing because Halauri machinery and operative writing share a designed geometry.

Approval of this plate would establish the seven bound words, governor-initial civic frame, operator scope,
three shared families, four substitution tests, and six-level failure hierarchy. Until then, they are the
first coherent stress test of the authorized architecture rather than additional canon.
