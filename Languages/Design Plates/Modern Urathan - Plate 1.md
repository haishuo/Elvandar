# Modern Urathan — Design Plate 1

**Status:** WORKING — language system not canon; the **Theraris / Vela Theraris** service-name suite is
approved<br>
**Created:** 2026-08-22<br>
**Historical focus:** The national standard in the fourth century after independence, with ordinary
vernacular, Collegiate, and cited-Halauri registers<br>
**Generative test:** The full legal title, common formal name, and conversational name of Uratha's state
intelligence and research-enforcement service

This plate designs Modern Urathan as a daughter of Imperial Balishan. It does not use *Ilhamori*, the
current map-city names, or the existing Book 3 Urathan personal names to choose its sounds, roots, or
morphology. Those forms are compared only after the language and institutional naming machinery exist.

The exact site and local population of Urath IV's founding academy remain open. This plate therefore
designs the **academy-descended national standard**, whose Imperial-Balishan ancestry is settled, without
claiming that every rural or regional Urathan variety descends from precisely the same local mixture.

## 1. Intended voice

Urathan has two immediately recognizable public voices.

**Vernacular Urathan** is brisk, dry, and economical. It remains ordinarily intelligible to a Balishan
speaker, though its smoothed diphthongs, denser compounds, and academic idiom make it conspicuous.

**Collegiate Urathan** is deliberately placed. It restores full vowels, late stress, hiatus, old syntax,
and selected Halauri consonants because Urathan institutions equate antiquity with precision. Sometimes
the distinction is real. Sometimes the older word is merely more prestigious. A trained speaker can make
either motive sound identical.

The target contrast is:

> **E do ket lo navat zi.**<br>
> *e do ket lo navat zi*<br>
> they CPL recognize that commission REC<br>
> “They recognized that commission, as the record shows.”

> **E lo navat do ket zi.**<br>
> *e lo navat do ket zi*<br>
> they that commission CPL recognize REC<br>
> “That commission they recognized, as entered in the record.”

The second sentence is not ancient Halauri. It is modern Urathan cultivating an old verb-final cadence.

## 2. How to read the examples

The convention matches the Late Open Halauri plate:

1. The **bold line** is ordinary author-facing romanization of the spoken sentence.
2. The *italic line* exposes morphological structure. Hyphens mark affix boundaries; equals signs, if a
   later example requires one, mark clitics. Neither symbol belongs to ordinary romanization.
3. The third line is a word-by-word grammatical gloss; the fourth is idiomatic English.

Urathan uses the Imperial chancery script historically descended from the wider Balishan writing
tradition. Its glyph shapes and native spacing remain unbuilt; this plate establishes only Latin-letter
romanization.

## 3. Historical layers

| Layer | Social history | Linguistic consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Academy Imperial Balishan | The founding academy teaches, records, and governs in the imperial koine | SVO grammar, initial stress, compact roots, source particles, and modifier–head compounds |
| Provincial academy standard | Towers, examinations, offices, and a permanent scholarly population create a local norm before secession | Dense institutional vocabulary and strong written standardization |
| Independent Urathan | Separate government, schools, military, and national prestige operate for at least 349 years | Regular vernacular sound change and distinctive idiom |
| Collegiate renewal | Scholars repeatedly import, restore, and coin from Hieratic Halauri | Penultimate stress, hiatus, learned consonants, verb-final performance, and neo-Halauri compounds |
| Modern standard | Tower schooling teaches speakers when to move between registers | Register becomes a primary marker of education, authority, and exclusion |

The High Exam does not merely test facts. It tests whether a child can recognize and produce the expected
register transitions. That makes Collegiate fluency look like intelligence even when access to it was
unequal from birth.

### Kaha'an as the national pronunciation shibboleth

Modern Urathan calls the imperial capital **kah-HAH-an**, /ka.ˈha.ʔan/. This is not restricted to a
formal Halauri quotation embedded in an Urathan sentence. Repeated Collegiate renewal has replaced the
expected inherited contraction in the ordinary Urathan proper name itself, so speakers deliberately
enunciate the hiatus and glottal stop even while the rest of the sentence remains vernacular.

Kaha'ani residents say **kah-HAN**, /kaˈhan/. An Urathan using that local form is accommodating or
code-switching; an Urathan using the full form is simply speaking Urathan. The contrast makes the same
spelling a social diagnostic: Kaha'ani usage demonstrates native frequency, while Urathan usage
demonstrates learned historical control.

## 4. Vernacular sound system

### Consonants

| | Labial | Alveolar | Postalveolar | Velar | Glottal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Stops | p b | t d | — | k g | — |
| Fricatives | f v | s z | sh /ʃ/, zh /ʒ/ | — | h |
| Nasals | m | n | — | — | — |
| Liquids | — | l r | — | — | — |
| Glides | w | — | y /j/ | — | — |

Ordinary inherited speech no longer has Early Imperial **c** /ts/ or **j** /dʒ/ as independent phonemes.
They have merged with **s** and **zh**. Recent foreign words and exact citations can reintroduce them.

### Vowels

The five short vowels remain **a e i o u**. In addition, inherited stressed **ai** and **au** have
smoothed to long **ae** /eː/ and **o** /oː/ in the standard. Unstressed vowels can centralize toward [ə]
in rapid speech, but the standard spelling retains their historical quality.

| Written | Careful value | Origin or use |
|---|---|---|
| **a e i o u** | /a e i o u/ | inherited short vowels |
| **ae** | /eː/ | principally inherited stressed *ai* |
| stressed **o** in known smoothed words | /oː/ | principally inherited stressed *au* |

Learned Halauri vocabulary can retain adjacent vowels as separate syllables: **Kethae** /ke.ˈθa.e/, not
vernacular **ae** /eː/.

### Word shape and stress

Vernacular Urathan permits the inherited Early Imperial syllable shape **(C)(R)V(V)(C)** and has expanded
its medial clusters through loss of old linking vowels. Stress remains on the first lexical root.

- **NAV**-at /ˈna.vat/ — statutory commission
- **BOR**-pel /ˈbor.pel/ — boat-carrier
- **SAE**-ven /ˈseː.ven/ — old compound name, historically “crossing-witness”
- **ESH**-na-vat /ˈeʃ.na.vat/ — law-bound corporate body

Collegiate forms announce themselves by restoring penultimate stress, full vowels, or hiatus:

- ke-**THA**-e /ke.ˈθa.e/
- the-**RA**-ris /θe.ˈra.ris/
- ve-**DA**-ris /ve.ˈda.ris/

## 5. Ordered vernacular changes from Early Imperial Balishan

These changes apply to inherited vocabulary, not to words imported from a Halauri text centuries later.

### U-1 — affricates simplify

> **c** /ts/ > **s** /s/<br>
> **j** /dʒ/ > **zh** /ʒ/

The change distinguishes ordinary Urathan from the older imperial reading without threatening mutual
intelligibility.

### U-2 — stressed inherited diphthongs smooth

> **ai** /ai/ > **ae** /eː/<br>
> **au** /au/ > **o** /oː/

Thus Early Imperial *cai* “crossing” gives Urathan **sae**, *zhai* “binding debt” gives **zhae**, and
*mau* “sea” gives **mo**. Learned forms entering later do not undergo the change.

### U-3 — final voiced stops remain phonetically voiceless and become transparent in common spelling

Early Imperial organized-body **-ad**, already pronounced [at], becomes ordinary Urathan **-at**.
Conservative charters and Collegiate historical discussion may retain **-ad** when citing the older form.

> *nav-ad* > **navat** — statutory or chartered body<br>
> *esh-nav-ad* > **eshnavat** — law-bound corporate body

### U-4 — weak linking vowels disappear before legal clusters

> *bora-pel* > **borpel**<br>
> *tala-rava* > **talrava**

Deletion is blocked when it would create a cluster outside Urathan phonotactics. Written Collegiate forms
can restore the linking vowel to display a compound's analysis.

### U-5 — unstressed vowels reduce in speech but remain in the school standard

This is the source of a familiar Urathan social judgment: full-vowel vernacular speech can sound
classroom-careful, while heavy reduction can be read—often unfairly—as rural, working-class, or bakarn.
The difference is accent, not intelligence.

## 6. Vernacular grammar

Urathan preserves the Imperial-Balishan grammatical frame because four centuries are ample time for
change, not for genealogical amnesia.

### Clause order

Ordinary clauses are **subject–verb–object**. Verbs do not agree with person or gender.

> **E le zen lo nava.**<br>
> *e le zen lo nava*<br>
> they IPFV record that compact<br>
> “They are recording that compact.”

### Nouns and association

A bare noun remains number-neutral. **Li** makes plurality explicit. Possessors precede the head with
**en**; descriptive modifiers ordinarily follow it.

> **ai en zenel** — my writing instrument<br>
> **li nar ora** — the old people / the elders, when context makes the reading clear<br>
> **navat mar** — the great commission

### Pronouns

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| First | **ai** | **aem** inclusive; **or** exclusive |
| Second | **tu** | **tun** |
| Third | **e** | **er** |

The changes *aim > aem* and *aur > or* follow ordinary vowel smoothing. Collegiate recitation may restore
**aim** and **aur** when quoting imperial documents.

### Aspect, negation, and questions

| Particle | Function |
|---|---|
| **le** | ongoing or incomplete |
| **do** | completed with present relevance |
| **sa** | intended, expected, or prospective; from Imperial *sha* |
| **vi** | customary or repeated |
| **nu** | negation |

Yes/no and open questions retain final **ma**.

> **Tu sa ren nari ma?**<br>
> *tu sa ren nari ma*<br>
> you PROSP return tomorrow Q<br>
> “Will you return tomorrow?”

### Source particles

The inherited state particles remain ordinary in formal claims:

| Particle | Claimed route |
|---|---|
| **ha** | directly witnessed or personally verified |
| **zi** | entered in or warranted by a record |
| **ro** | received from another speaker or institution |
| **mae** | derived by explicit model or inference; a Collegiate renewal from Halauri *ma* |

Urathan scholarship distinguishes source from certainty. A proposition can be **mae** and strongly
supported, or **ha** and badly observed. Institutional misuse begins when listeners treat the marker as a
guarantee rather than a description of method.

## 7. Collegiate Urathan

Collegiate is not a second language. It is a cultivated vertical register built by changing several
channels at once.

### Learned sound

- Penultimate stress is restored in Halauri-derived words.
- **th** /θ/ and apostrophe /ʔ/ are restored in cited or newly coined learned material.
- Hiatus is pronounced rather than smoothed.
- Final vowels discarded by inherited speech can return in a cited source or a new neo-Halauri compound.
- Speakers may restore an etymological distinction that never existed in their own vernacular.

### Learned syntax

Definitions, findings, ceremonial rulings, and titles cultivate modifier-before-head noun phrases and
verb-final clauses. Modern particles remain available; speakers do not become native Late Open Halauri
when they enter a lecture hall.

> **E lo nava do ket zi.**<br>
> *e lo nava do ket zi*<br>
> they that compact CPL recognize REC<br>
> “That compact they recognized, as the record shows.”

To a vernacular listener, the sentence is understandable and conspicuously performed. In a finding, the
performance can signal precision. At dinner, it can signal that the speaker wants everyone to know where
they studied.

### Learned derivation

Urathan scholars use both inherited Imperial morphology and revived Halauri material:

| Form | Route | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **-en** | inherited Imperial *-in* | ordinary practitioner or habitual agent |
| **-or** | inherited | ordinary site or office dedicated to an activity |
| **-el** | inherited and reinforced by Halauri | instrument, interface, or medium |
| **-at** | inherited Imperial *-ad* | vernacular statutory or corporate body |
| **-es** | inherited Imperial *-as* | ordinary domain or practice |
| **-u** | inherited | relational adjective |
| **-ae** | revived Halauri institutional *-e* | constituted learned body; always Collegiate |
| **-ar** | revived Halauri | discipline or sustained practice |
| **-is** | revived Halauri | abstract relation or quality |
| **-eth** | revived Halauri | bounded specialist site |

Adjacent identical **a** vowels coalesce inside learned derivation: *thera + -ar > therar* and
*veda + -ar > vedar*. Unlike vowels remain audible hiatus unless a specific Collegiate tradition has
established a contraction.

A body called a **ketat** claims statutory validity in living Urathan law. A body called a **kethae**
claims continuity with the most ancient scholarly order. That contrast can be substantive, theatrical,
or both.

## 9. Naming machinery

### Ordinary personal names

Inherited two-root names remain common, but Urathan sound change can obscure their imperial analysis.
Fresh tests, coined before comparison with the cast register:

| Urathan name | Earlier material | Historical reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Saeven** | *cai + ven* | witness at the crossing |
| **Vaedor** | *vai + dor* | wind-hand |
| **Zhaedar** | *zhai + dar* | keeper of obligations |
| **Borpel** | *bora + pel* | boat-carrier |
| **Navren** | *nav + ren* | compact restored |
| **Kelsen** | *kel + sen* | measured blood; opaque as a modern name |
| **Talrien** | *tal + rien-* | river-preservation; second member refreshed from Collegiate speech |
| **Soreth** | *sor + -eth* | a name fossil no longer transparently productive |

Names do not mark gender. Their old readings do not predict personality or profession.

### Collegiate personal names

Scholarly families may choose restored or newly coined forms such as **Theraen, Netharis, Rienath,** and
**Kethaen**. The social claim is often more important than the actual antiquity: a name coined forty years
ago can sound five thousand years old if every examiner agrees to pronounce it that way.

### Institutions and settlements

Ordinary Urathan uses compact inherited compounds. Collegiate foundations prefer long transparent titles
whose head states the legal kind of body or site. A city grown around an institution may then inherit the
institution's clipped name, while maps preserve a longer ceremonial form.

This makes the current pseudo-Latinate research-city labels disposable. They must be regenerated from the
discipline, terrain, founding charter, and historical register of each settlement after this plate is
approved.

## 10. The state-service name test

### What is being named

The organization is not culturally understood as “secret police,” even when it performs that work. Its
public functions are:

- enforcing Council rulings and research protocols;
- investigating unregistered inquiry and magical practice;
- inspecting institutions, records, and active experiments;
- pursuing fugitives and containing dangerous work;
- executing exceptional mandates outside ordinary Tower jurisdiction;
- reporting directly to the Council rather than through a disciplinary hierarchy.

Its title should therefore sound like procedural guardianship, not violence. The concealment is strongest
when every word in the official name is technically true.

### Generated and approved name stack

> **Vela Theraris, Rava Eshais va Koiathu Vedaris Kethae**<br>
> *vela thera-ar-is, rava esha-is va koi-ath-u veda-ar-is ketha-e*<br>
> licensed.access demonstrated.inquiry-DISC-REL civic law-REL and boundary-from-REL govern-DISC-REL classify-BODY<br>
> **“The Constituted Commission for Licensed Inquiry, Civic Order, and Extraordinary Mandates.”**

The title is modern Collegiate Urathan, not an authentic ancient Halauri institution-name. It uses old
material to authorize a body whose exact combination of powers belongs to Urathan history.

The legal grammatical head is final: **Kethae**, “constituted body of authoritative classification and
inspection.” That does not make it the institution's social name. Uratha has many commissions; speakers
identify this one by the distinctive opening domain of its title instead:

| Register | Form | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Full legal | **Vela Theraris, Rava Eshais va Koiathu Vedaris Kethae** | Charters, warrants, oaths, and first reference in the most formal documents |
| Common formal | **Vela Theraris** | The normal complete institutional name |
| Conversational | **the Theraris** | Ordinary speech and subsequent reference |

**Vela Theraris** is not an awkward halfway abbreviation or a concession to a one-word model. It is a
complete two-word service-name, on the same structural terms as **Arol Batae** and the older **Kaha
Batae**. **The Theraris** is merely the familiar clipping used when nobody could mistake the institution.
The name therefore behaves like the surviving, distinctive portion of a much larger legal title.

Careful Collegiate pronunciation of **Theraris** is **theh-RAH-riss**, /θe.ˈra.ris/. The wider plate has
not yet established a vernacular contraction, so the spelling and three-syllable pronunciation stand in
both approved short forms.

Reader-facing prose can retain the translated rank **Inspector**:

> former **Theraris Inspector** Thalia Neth

The service-name works as organization, modifier, and institutional affiliation without pretending that
all its members form an ethnic people.

### Rejected short-name candidate — inherited legal register

**Ketat**, /ˈke.tat/, is the ordinary inherited formation *ket + -at*, “statutorily recognized
commission.” A full vernacular title could use the same dependent phrases and end in **Ketat**. This is
the cleanest Mossad-like solution—everyone calls a long-named body simply “the Commission”—but it lacks
the conspicuous archaizing prestige Uratha usually chooses for its highest institutions.

### Rejected short-name candidate — archival euphemism

**Rienae**, /ri.e.ˈna.e/, “constituted preservation body,” could head a title presenting the service as
the guardian of accumulated knowledge. It is an excellent euphemism and a weaker description of its
daily public work. It would fit an archival or continuity service better than the bureau that visibly
conducts inspections and arrests.

### The existing form *Ilhamori*

*Ilhamori* is phonotactically possible but does not arise from this plate. The inherited words for fire
and old continuity are **ash** and **ora**; the working Halauri roots are **hara** and **arol**. Neither
route produces the old “eternal flame” gloss. The apparent pieces *ilha, hamor,* and *-i* have no generated
institutional relationship, and rescuing them would require roots coined only for this one P3 outcome.

The approved **Theraris / Vela Theraris** suite therefore retires ***Ilhamori***. It does not receive a
derivation merely because it was written first.

## 12. Approval boundary

Authorial approval on 2026-08-22 establishes only the service-name stack in §10: the full legal title,
common formal **Vela Theraris**, conversational **the Theraris**, and careful pronunciation of
**Theraris**. The remainder of this plate is still working machinery, including its general sound changes,
grammar, other roots, and proposed social registers.

Approval of the wider plate would establish:

- The sound of Standard Vernacular Urathan and its first ordered changes from Early Imperial Balishan.
- Ordinary mutual intelligibility with Balishan alongside recognizable Urathan smoothing and clusters.
- The vertical relationship among vernacular, Collegiate, and cited Halauri speech.
- The retained Imperial grammar and the cultivated verb-final Collegiate alternative.
- Four formal source routes, including Urathan inferential **mae**.
- Inherited versus re-Halaurized institutional morphology.
- The first ordinary and Collegiate naming machinery.
- The wider linguistic machinery from which the already approved state-service name was generated.

It would not yet establish rural dialects, bakarn varieties, the High Exam's exact language, Tower jargon,
native script details, city names, the founding academy's local substrate, or the personal etymologies of
the existing cast.

## 13. Iteration controls

- **Vernacular sound:** retain or reject **ai > ae** and **au > o** smoothing; increase or reduce cluster
  formation; decide how audible unstressed reduction should be.
- **Collegiate distance:** make it closer to ordinary Urathan or even more conspicuously re-Halaurized.
- **Institutional grammar:** keep cultivated verb-final findings or restrict them to quotations and titles.
- **Service name:** fixed as common formal **Vela Theraris** and conversational **the Theraris**; do not
  reopen **Kethae, Ketat, Rienae,** or *Ilhamori* as competing short names while iterating the language.
- **Register politics:** decide whether ordinary speakers admire Collegiate pronunciation, mock it, fear
  it, or shift among all three responses by context.

The plate should be approved only if the vernacular sounds like a living Balishan daughter and the
Collegiate register sounds like Uratha's own magnificent, exclusionary reinvention of antiquity.
