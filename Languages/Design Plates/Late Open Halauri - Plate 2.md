# Late Open Halauri — Design Plate 2: The *-'an* Construction

**Status:** WORKING — the requirement that *-'an* be a reusable construction is author-directed; this
semantic and grammatical implementation awaits approval<br>
**Created:** 2026-08-22<br>
**Historical focus:** Late Open Halauri civic, legal, and technical language<br>
**Generative test:** Produce a full family of *-'an* words before returning to protected **Kaha'an**

## 1. The correction

**Kaha'an cannot be the only word whose apostrophe-bearing ending works this way.** If *-'an* exists only
to derive one P0 title, it is not morphology; it is an etymological stage prop.

Late Open Halauri therefore treats ***-'an*** /ʔan/ as a productive **institutional-locus construction**.
It turns an office, delegated authority, or regulated public function into the recognized seat where that
authority is legally present and able to act.

The construction is related to possession in intuition but is not the ordinary possessive:

- **kaha-n doma** — a building belonging to the Crown;
- **kaha batae** — the sovereign Guard, classified by the authority above it;
- **kaha-'an → Kaha'an** — the recognized seat where sovereign authority is constituted.

Halauri genitive **-n** answers *whose is it?* Productive ***-'an*** answers *where is this office legally
present?* The difference is why a palace, a royal guard, and a capital are not three uses of one vague
“royal” ending.

## 2. Morphological category

***-'an*** is a **bound relational head** becoming derivational in Late Open speech.

```text
OFFICE OR AUTHORITY STEM + -'an
    → legally effective seat or locus of that authority
```

The input must denote at least one of the following:

1. a public office with recognized jurisdiction;
2. a delegated body empowered to issue binding acts;
3. a regulated competence whose judgments or certifications have institutional force;
4. an integrated system whose control is formally localized.

The output is a noun. It may denote a room, building, complex, city, council, bench, chair, or control
station—but only because that physical or social site presently instantiates the relevant authority. The
construction does not predict architecture.

This makes ***-'an*** narrower than English *office* and broader than English *capital*. It names effective
institutional location.

### Why a bound head exists

The economical historical path is grammaticalization, not the spontaneous invention of a suffix. An
older Halauri relational noun beginning in /ʔ/ meant approximately “recognized standing-place” or “place
at which something is constituted.” Repeated civic phrases placed that noun after words for offices and
authorities. By Late Open speech it could no longer stand freely in this reduced form, but its glottal onset
survived as the audible boundary of the bound head ***-'an***.

The exact Foundational citation form remains uncoined. The plate commits only to the development:

```text
older AUTHORITY + “recognized standing-place”
    → Late Open AUTHORITY-'an
    → human inherited AUTHORITY-an / contracted descendant
```

That history explains why the construction behaves partly like a compound and partly like a suffix—and
why the apostrophe remains meaningful even after later readers no longer know the source noun.

## 3. Sound, stress, and spelling

The apostrophe represents the suffix's initial glottal stop /ʔ/. It is spoken and is not a punctuation
mark separating two decorative fantasy syllables.

- The suffix joins its stem in ordinary author-facing spelling: ***esha'an***, not *esha-'an*.
- The morphological-analysis line retains the boundary: *esha-'an*.
- /ʔ/ prevents vowel coalescence: /e.ˈʃa.ʔan/, not */e.ʃaːn/ in careful Late Open speech.
- Derivational ***-'an*** participates in ordinary penultimate lexical stress.
- Case and number follow the completed derived word: *esha-'an-i* “at the tribunal,” written
  ***esha'ani***.

### The useful near-collision with the genitive

The possessive intuition is not baseless, because ordinary genitive **-n** can attach to the same stem:

| Form | Careful Late Open sound | Grammar | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **kahan doma** | /ˈka.han ˈdo.ma/ | *kaha-n doma*, Crown-GEN building | a building of the Crown |
| **Kaha'an** | /ka.ˈha.ʔan/ | *kaha-'an*, Crown-SEAT | the place where the Crown is legally present |

They are not the same formation. Genitive **-n** does not attract lexical stress and requires a possessed
head; derivational ***-'an*** contains /ʔ/, attracts the regular word-level stress pattern, and produces a
complete noun. A Halauri listener hears the difference immediately.

Human glottal loss makes the two forms much easier to confuse. The title contracts to /kaˈhan/, while an
inherited genitive can produce a neighboring **kahan** shape through a different history. Later speakers
can therefore folk-analyze **Kaha'an** as “of the Crown” even though the older grammar does not support
that reading. Learned spelling preserves the apostrophe precisely where ordinary speech has eroded the
distinction.

Human inherited speech normally loses /ʔ/ and repairs the resulting hiatus. Human learned traditions can
restore the glottal stop from instruction. That later contrast is a transmission history, not part of the
Late Open construction itself.

## 4. The generated family

These forms were generated from the working Late Open Halauri lexicon before protected **Kaha'an** was
returned to the test.

| Form | Morphology | Core meaning | Likely reader translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **veda'an** | *veda-'an* | seat from which civic government is legally exercised | governing seat; council seat |
| **esha'an** | *esha-'an* | competent seat at which law is adjudicated or made binding | court; tribunal; bench |
| **netha'an** | *netha-'an* | seat empowered to teach, examine, and certify a discipline | teaching chair; certifying faculty |
| **ketha'an** | *ketha-'an* | seat empowered to establish official classifications or distinctions | classification bench; standards tribunal |
| **vela'an** | *vela-'an* | seat empowered to grant or deny bounded access | licensing office; access authority |
| **riena'an** | *riena-'an* | seat holding formal custody and recovery authority over records | archival custody office |
| **bata'an** | *bata-'an* | command locus from which a constituted watch is directed | guard command; watch seat |
| **thuma'an** | *thuma-'an* | authorized control locus for an integrated maintained system | control station; maintenance command |

The words do not all name grand institutions. A city aqueduct may have one **thuma'an** controlling its
distribution while dozens of ordinary maintenance rooms surround it. An academy may recognize several
**netha'an** chairs, each competent in one discipline. A provincial court may be an **esha'an** without
possessing sovereign authority.

## 5. The contrast with ordinary sites

The construction becomes real only if it changes meaning predictably.

| Ordinary site | Authorized *-'an* seat | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| **eshaeth** | **esha'an** | courthouse or law-work site vs the tribunal competent to render judgment |
| **nethaeth** | **netha'an** | classroom or teaching site vs a chair empowered to examine and certify |
| **rienaeth** | **riena'an** | archive facility vs the office holding formal custody of the record |
| **bataeth** | **bata'an** | guardhouse vs the command locus of the constituted watch |
| **thumaeth** | **thuma'an** | maintenance room vs the control seat for the whole maintained system |
| **velaeth** | **vela'an** | checkpoint or permit desk vs the authority competent to grant access |

The physical site ending ***-eth*** says *work of this kind regularly happens here*. ***-'An*** says *an
act issued here counts as an act of the institution*.

This distinction permits one room to be both. It also permits them to separate: a tribunal may sit
temporarily in an ordinary hall, making that hall the **esha'an** for the sitting without converting the
building permanently into an **eshaeth**.

## 6. Mobility, vacancy, and delegation

### The seat moves by law, not footsteps

An office-holder leaving a building does not automatically move the ***-'an***. The relevant institution
must transfer its recognized seat, records, or jurisdiction. An emperor visiting another city does not make
that city **Kaha'an**. Moving the court and portable government can.

### A seat can be vacant

The office may remain legally seated during a vacancy. An empty tribunal can still be the **esha'an** if
its jurisdiction and succession continue there. What matters is recognized institutional presence, not a
warm body occupying a chair at every moment.

### Authority can delegate more than one seat

Ordinary institutions may establish multiple competent **vela'an**, **netha'an**, or **bata'an** seats.
Explicit plural **-ir** is available when their individuation matters. Halauri sovereign doctrine treats
**Kaha'an** as singular: divided sovereignty would be a constitutional crisis, not an ordinary plural.

## 7. Sentence tests

Abbreviations: **OBJ** object, **LOC** location, **ALL** motion toward, **CPL** completed, **REC**
record-source, **STAT** stative presence.

### A building is not its authority

> **Ei doma eshaeth ira; esha'an ei domai nesuan.**<br>
> *ei doma esha-eth ira; esha-'an ei doma-i ne-sua-n*<br>
> this building law-SITE be; law-SEAT this building-LOC NEG-be.present-STAT<br>
> “This is a courthouse; the tribunal is not sitting here.”

The first clause identifies the site's enduring function. The second denies present institutional
competence.

### A tribunal can move

> **Esha'an ei domaur renatri.**<br>
> *esha-'an ei doma-ur rena-t=ri*<br>
> law-SEAT this building-ALL return-CPL=REC<br>
> “The tribunal returned to this building, as entered in the record.”

The sentence moves a legal seat, not architecture.

### A technical control seat

> **An thuma'ani xelim kelatve.**<br>
> *an thuma-'an-i xeli-m kela-t=ve*<br>
> I maintenance-SEAT-LOC alignment-OBJ measure-CPL=DIRECT<br>
> “I measured the alignment at the control station, directly witnessed.”

The location matters because the station's reading governs the integrated system.

### Delegated access

> **Vela'an ei navam ketatri.**<br>
> *vela-'an ei nava-m keta-t=ri*<br>
> access-SEAT this compact-OBJ ratify-CPL=REC<br>
> “The licensing office ratified this authorization, as recorded.”

## 8. Negative tests

The following are not valid ordinary formations:

- ***dura'an*** for “mountain city”: a mountain is not an office. Use settlement or landscape morphology.
- ***aeva'an*** for “water place”: water does not exercise jurisdiction. Use **Aevara**, an aqueduct term,
  or a transparent compound.
- ***mera'an*** for any marketplace: exchange activity alone does not make an authorized public seat. Use
  **meraeth** unless a particular exchange body possesses legally delegated authority.
- attaching ***-'an*** to a personal name merely to mean “house of” or “belonging to.” Possession already
  has grammar.
- attaching apostrophes to unfamiliar names to make them sound Halauri.

A treasury, academy, forge, archive, or gate can acquire an ***-'an*** formation only when the culture has
actually constituted an office there. Physical importance is insufficient.

## 9. Return to Kaha'an

Only after the family exists does the protected title return:

| Input | Derivation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **kaha** | sovereign authority as a continuous public office | eligible input to the construction |
| **-'an** | legally effective seat of the input authority | productive institutional-locus head |
| **kaha-'an** | the seat where sovereignty is legally present and can act | **Kaha'an**, “Royal Seat” |

**Kaha'an** is now special in three legitimate ways:

1. its referent is the highest and doctrinally singular seat in the political system;
2. human dynasties borrow the whole title and preserve its archaic spelling;
3. the title survives into the story after nearly every other native ***-'an*** word is confined to
   scholarship, inherited descendants, or untranslated institutional vocabulary.

It is not special in morphology. A Halauri speaker hearing **Kaha'an** recognizes the same construction
heard in **esha'an, netha'an, vela'an,** and **thuma'an**.

## 10. Human transmission

Surface Halauric and its human daughters lose phonemic /ʔ/ and repair hiatus. The predicted inherited
outcomes therefore remove the audible apostrophe:

```text
Late Open esha'an   /e.ˈʃa.ʔan/   → inherited contracted descendant
Late Open bata'an   /ba.ˈta.ʔan/  → inherited contracted descendant
Late Open Kaha'an   /ka.ˈha.ʔan/  → ordinary Kaha'ani /kaˈhan/
                                 → learned Urathan reborrowing /ka.ˈha.ʔan/
```

The exact first two human words remain uncoined until a daughter-language lexical need requires them.
Hieratic traditions can meanwhile cite **esha'an** or **bata'an** with restored /ʔ/, creating ordinary and
learned doublets without inventing a new sound law for each.

Urathan carries this restoration furthest. **Kaha'an** is conventionally **kah-HAH-an**
/ka.ˈha.ʔan/ in modern Urathan, with conspicuous articulation of the glottal boundary, even when the
surrounding sentence is vernacular. Kaha'ani **kah-HAN** /kaˈhan/ is available to an Urathan as local
accommodation, not as the national default.

The apostrophe consequently does real historical work:

- it distinguishes vowels in careful Halauri;
- its loss creates predictable human contractions;
- its learned restoration signals the transmission route;
- its survival in **Kaha'an** records an old grammatical construction, not a one-word spelling trick.

## 11. Approval boundary

Approval would establish:

- ***-'an*** /ʔan/ as productive Late Open Halauri institutional-locus morphology;
- its distinction from genitive **-n**, ordinary site **-eth**, integrated system **-on**, and settlement
  morphology;
- the eight generated family members and their semantic contrasts;
- mobility, vacancy, delegation, and inflection behavior;
- **Kaha'an** as the sovereign member of a real lexical family.

It would not yet coin the ordinary Balishan descendants of every form, establish that all Halauri dialects
used the construction identically, approve the whole of Late Open Halauri Plate 1, or analyze unrelated
apostrophes in other languages.
