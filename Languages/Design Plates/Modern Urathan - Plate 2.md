# Modern Urathan — Design Plate 2

**Status:** WORKING — not canon  
**Created:** 2026-08-22  
**Depends on:** Modern Urathan Plate 1's working phonology, morphology, and register split  
**Generative test:** The native common names and present charter titles of Uratha's Tower system, plus
the concealed naming register of the Ebony Tower and Globehall

This plate treats **Ivory Tower, Amethyst Tower, Sapphire Tower, Ruby Tower, Topaz Tower, Emerald Tower,
Pearl Tower, Onyx Tower, Ebony Tower,** and **Globehall** as reader-facing translations. Their familiar
English forms remain the names used in prose. The plate builds the Urathan underneath them; it does not
ask the novels to make readers memorize nine replacement names.

The public institutions and their cultural functions are settled history, so they determine what must be
named. Their existing English sound-shapes contribute nothing to the Urathan forms.

## 1. Translation policy

Every public Tower has three distinct names:

1. a compact **native common name**, used by Urathan speakers;
2. a transparent **reader-facing translation**, used almost exclusively in narrative prose;
3. a long **current charter title** in Collegiate Urathan, used on seals, appointments, formal findings,
   commencement rites, and documents whose authors are being paid by the word.

The translation is not an alias invented by foreigners. When the prose says **the Ruby Tower**, it is
silently translating what an Urathan says. Native forms should surface only when the sound itself matters:
an inscription, oath, seal, linguistic discussion, exact quotation from a charter, or a character making
a conspicuous display of Urathan education.

The charter titles are modern. Most have been amended, expanded, and re-Halaurized repeatedly since the
first provincial academy. They are not necessarily the words carved above the original foundations.

## 2. What a Tower is

Early Imperial Balishan **dur** means “mountain; high ground.” During the provincial academy period,
Urathan scholars apply it metonymically to the high institutional divisions embodied by their spires.
Modern Urathan therefore distinguishes:

| Form | Analysis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **dur** | inherited *dur* | height, high ground; an ordinary uncapitalized geographical word |
| **durdom** | *dur-dom*, high-building | a physical tower or tall roofed building |
| **Dur** | academy-era institutional specialization | one of Uratha's chartered academic Towers |
| **li Dur** | explicit plural + institutional noun | the Towers as governing institutions |

A **Dur** is not merely its spire. It can rebuild, relocate, occupy annexes, or administer buildings in
several cities while remaining the same legal and scholarly body. A structure can be a **durdom** without
belonging to any **Dur**.

Common Tower names are open institutional compounds: material-name before **Dur**. Their two-word shape is
ordinary and complete, on the same broad terms as **Arol Batae, Kaha Batae,** and **Vela Theraris**.

## 3. The traditional material lexicon

The Tower names preserve old jewellers', dyers', and traders' compounds rather than modern mineralogical
precision. A scholar can distinguish varieties of corundum in a paper without renaming the Sapphire
Tower. English likewise renders the traditional referent, not each Urathan root literally.

Two roots extend the working Imperial seed lexicon regularly:

| Earlier form | Modern Urathan | Meaning and route |
|---|---|---|
| **nir** | **nir** | sky, open height; the expected compact human outcome of Late Open Halauri *nira* |
| **jal** /dʒal/ | **zhal** /ʒal/ | violet-purple dye or color; continental trade vocabulary undergoing Plate 1 **j > zh** |

The complete traditional material set is generated compositionally:

| Modern material word | Historical construction | Literal history | Reader translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **palmur** | *palu-mur* > *palmur* | beast-bone, especially worked tusk | ivory |
| **zhalkar** | *jal-kar* > *zhalkar* | violet-stone | amethyst |
| **nirkar** | *nir-kar* | sky-stone | sapphire |
| **senkar** | *sen-kar* | blood-stone | ruby |
| **selkar** | *sel-kar* | sun-stone | topaz |
| **halkar** | *hal-kar* | life-stone | emerald |
| **mosar** | *mau-sar* > *mosar* | sea-seed | pearl |
| **noskar** | *nosa-kar* > *noskar* | dark-stone | onyx |
| **nosyel** | *nosa-yel* > *nosyel* | dark-wood | ebony |

These are real ordinary words, not ceremonial syllables that exist only in institutional names. An
Urathan jeweller can sell a **senkar**; a dyer can describe cloth as **zhal**; a clerk can order a
**nosyel** writing box. Later scientific vocabulary may classify the materials more narrowly.

| **Amethyst Tower** | **Zhalkar Dur** | **ZHAHL-kar door**, /ˈʒal.kar dur/ | the violet-stone tower |
| **Sapphire Tower** | **Nirkar Dur** | **NEER-kar door**, /ˈnir.kar dur/ | the sky-stone tower |
| **Ruby Tower** | **Senkar Dur** | **SEN-kar door**, /ˈsen.kar dur/ | the blood-stone tower |
| **Topaz Tower** | **Selkar Dur** | **SEL-kar door**, /ˈsel.kar dur/ | the sun-stone tower |
| **Emerald Tower** | **Halkar Dur** | **HAHL-kar door**, /ˈhal.kar dur/ | the life-stone tower |
| **Pearl Tower** | **Mosar Dur** | **MOH-sar door**, /ˈmoː.sar dur/ | the sea-seed tower |

The repetitions are deliberate. The public suite was made systematic by an institution that considers
classification a moral virtue. **Palmur** and **Mosar** reveal that the system was never literally “eight
gemstones”; material symbolism mattered more than mineral taxonomy. **Nosyel** copies the pattern while
breaking it: ebony is not stone, and the hidden ninth institution is not a lawful Tower.

## 5. Collegiate vocabulary for the charters

Plate 1 already provides **-ar** for a discipline, **-is** for an abstract relation or quality, and
revived **-ae** for a constituted learned body. Plate 2 applies them to the established Late Open roots:

| Form | Analysis | Charter sense |
|---|---|---|
| **Nethae** | *netha-e* | constituted teaching faculty or College |
| **Kethae** | *ketha-e* | constituted authority of classification and judgment |
| **Theraris** | *thera-ar-is* | disciplined inquiry governed by demonstration |
| **Vedaris** | *veda-ar-is* | civic governance as a discipline |
| **Zenaris** | *zena-ar-is* | record-making and documentary study |
| **Bataris** | *bata-ar-is* | defensive or interposing discipline |
| **Xoraris** | *xora-ar-is* | tracing relations through a complex system |
| **Mararis** | *mara-ar-is* | structural construction as a discipline |
| **Yararis** | *yara-ar-is* | practiced making or skilled fabrication |
| **Thumaris** | *thuma-ar-is* | maintenance and operational continuity |
| **Halaris** | *hala-ar-is* | study or disciplined care of living systems |
| **Melaris** | *mela-ar-is* | study of soil, land, and natural substrate |
| **Satharis** | *satha-ar-is* | diagnostic science; inference from signs |
| **Xeli** | underived learned root | calibration or the controlled correspondence of forces |

**Xeli** is the Amethyst Tower's preferred scholarly description of arcane work. It frames magic as
calibrated correspondence among natural forces, not mysterious power. That definition is culturally
Urathan: a Ruby officer or ordinary mage need not organize the practice in the same way.

## 6. Current public charter titles

The English translations prioritize institutional sense over reproducing every derivational distinction.
All titles place the legal head last in the cultivated Collegiate fashion.

### Ivory Tower — **Palmur Dur**

> **Marae Theraris va Rava Vedaris Kethae**  
> *marae thera-ar-is va rava veda-ar-is ketha-e*  
> great.public demonstrated.inquiry-DISC-REL and civic govern-DISC-REL classify-BODY  
> **“The High Commission of Demonstrated Inquiry and Civic Governance.”**

The Ivory institution ends in **Kethae**, not **Nethae**. It classifies and governs the faculties rather
than functioning as one faculty among equals. Its common material-name hides that constitutional claim.

### Amethyst Tower — **Zhalkar Dur**

> **Vela Xeli va Koiathu Theraris Nethae**  
> *vela xeli va koi-ath-u thera-ar-is netha-e*  
> licensed.access calibrated.correspondence and boundary-from-REL demonstrated.inquiry-DISC-REL teach-BODY  
> **“The Faculty of Licensed Arcane Correspondence and Extraordinary Inquiry.”**

The title makes regulation inseparable from study. Uratha does not ban a magical practice; it determines
who is qualified to investigate it.

### Sapphire Tower — **Nirkar Dur**

> **Riena Zenaris, Eshais va Arol Theraris Nethae**  
> *riena zena-ar-is esha-is va arol thera-ar-is netha-e*  
> preserve record-DISC-REL law-REL and old demonstrated.inquiry-DISC-REL teach-BODY  
> **“The Faculty of Preserved Record, Law, and Historical Inquiry.”**

Its “history” is literally inquiry into the recognized earlier order. That phrasing quietly privileges
institutionally legible history over memory that never entered a record.

### Ruby Tower — **Senkar Dur**

> **Tora Bataris va Rava Xoraris Nethae**  
> *tora bata-ar-is va rava xora-ar-is netha-e*  
> force-resistant guard-DISC-REL and civic system.trace-DISC-REL teach-BODY  
> **“The Faculty of Defensive Force and Civic Strategy.”**

The charter names defense and systemic strategy, not conquest. That reflects Uratha's actual military
strength and its preferred account of itself.

### Topaz Tower — **Selkar Dur**

> **Mararis, Yararis va Thumaris Nethae**  
> *mara-ar-is yara-ar-is va thuma-ar-is netha-e*  
> build-DISC-REL skilled.make-DISC-REL and maintain-DISC-REL teach-BODY  
> **“The Faculty of Construction, Skilled Making, and Operational Continuity.”**

The formal title is more dignified than “applied sciences” and still cannot quite conceal that Topaz
scholars make and maintain the things everyone else uses.

### Emerald Tower — **Halkar Dur**

> **Dema Halaris, Melaris va Theraris Nethae**  
> *dema hala-ar-is mela-ar-is va thera-ar-is netha-e*  
> sustain living.system-DISC-REL land-DISC-REL and demonstrated.inquiry-DISC-REL teach-BODY  
> **“The Faculty of Cultivation, Living Systems, and Natural Inquiry.”**

The ordering puts cultivation first. Other Towers may read that as a mark of low prestige; Emerald
scholars can read it as the admission that everybody eats before anybody theorizes.

### Pearl Tower — **Mosar Dur**

> **Rada Halaris va Satharis Nethae**  
> *rada hala-ar-is va satha-ar-is netha-e*  
> tend living.body-DISC-REL and diagnose-DISC-REL teach-BODY  
> **“The Faculty of Bodily Care and Diagnostic Science.”**

The title preserves the Tower's unresolved balance: **Rada Halaris** leans toward care of living systems;
**Satharis** toward analysis, diagnosis, and magical method.

### Onyx Tower — **Noskar Dur**

> **Rava Vedaris va Xoraris Nethae**  
> *rava veda-ar-is va xora-ar-is netha-e*  
> civic govern-DISC-REL and system.trace-DISC-REL teach-BODY  
> **“The Faculty of Civic Administration and Systems Analysis.”**

This is intentionally bloodless and wholly accurate. “Systems analysis” includes information networks,
cryptography, social dynamics, deception, and institutional control without acknowledging an intelligence
service. The charter lies only through taxonomy.

## 7. The institution that does not exist

The **Ebony Tower** is not a ninth chartered **Dur**. **Nosyel Dur** is a whispered analogical nickname:
if the dark stone has a Tower, then the dark wood beneath Ivory must be its forbidden counterpart. Calling
it a **Dur** is already an accusation that the concealed operation has become an institution.

It therefore has no public charter title, no Provost, no lawful seal, and no entry in the official list of
**li Dur**. Three different names circulate in three different scopes:

| Scope | Urathan form | Reader-facing form | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| whispered illicit name | **Nosyel Dur** | **the Ebony Tower** | names the hidden institution as the obscene ninth member of the Tower system |
| inner-circle technical name | **Limzhomor** | **the Globehall** | names what the facility visibly contains |
| concealed account and requisition code | **Koiathu Rienaeth** | **Extraordinary Preservation Annex** | makes funding, staff, and materials disappear into an innocent administrative category |

**Limzhomor** is regular vernacular word-building:

> Early Imperial **jom**, “vessel or container” > Urathan **zhom** by **j > zh**  
> **lim-zhom**, “whole or closed vessel” > globe, sphere  
> **lim-zhom-or** > **Limzhomor**, “globe-site; hall of globes”

The name describes glass or crystal vessels and says nothing about the minds inside them. It can therefore
have begun as a technician's neutral description and survived after neutrality became indefensible.

**Koiathu Rienaeth** uses **koiathu**, “extraordinary; specially mandated,” plus *riena-eth*, “bounded
specialist preservation site.” It is not what anyone standing inside calls the place. It is what appears
on an Ivory ledger whose reader is not cleared to learn what was preserved.

## 8. Reader-facing usage

Normal prose continues to use:

> Harkim crossed the court toward the **Topaz Tower**.  
> The **Onyx Tower** denied that any such memorandum existed.  
> Beneath the **Ivory Tower**, the **Globehall** waited.

It should not ordinarily use:

> Harkim crossed the court toward the **Selkar Dur**.

The second form is available when the sound itself is doing work: a carved lintel, an Urathan-language
oath, a seal whose letters a viewpoint character recognizes, or a contrast between an official translation
and what an insider actually said. Linguistic depth remains underneath the story instead of standing in
front of it.

## 10. Approval boundary and iteration controls

Approval of this plate would establish:

- **durdom** for a physical tower and capitalized **Dur** for a chartered academic Tower;
- the nine native common forms and their pronunciation;
- the seven faculty charters and the Ivory Commission charter;
- **Nethae** as the Collegiate legal head “constituted faculty”;
- **Nosyel Dur, Limzhomor,** and **Koiathu Rienaeth** as the three concealed naming registers;
- the policy that prose normally uses the familiar English translations.

It would not approve the remainder of Modern Urathan Plate 1, define every gem or color, establish native
script, freeze the historical wording of the original academy charters, or require any native Tower-name
to appear in a chapter.

The principal iteration controls are:

- **Tower head:** keep **Dur**, or choose a less compact institutional word while retaining the
  building/institution distinction.
- **Material sound:** judge the suite aloud—especially **Palmur, Zhalkar, Mosar,** and **Nosyel**—rather
  than approving individual favorites in isolation.
- **Charter density:** keep the current titles at seal-length, or make Urathan bureaucracy still more
  elaborate.
- **Secret register:** decide whether **Limzhomor** sounds neutral enough to have been coined before the
  horror became normalized, and whether **Koiathu Rienaeth** is sufficiently bloodless for a hidden ledger.

The plate succeeds if the reader can continue saying **the Topaz Tower** without losing the sense that an
Urathan scholar hears an inherited, politically charged native institution-name underneath it.
