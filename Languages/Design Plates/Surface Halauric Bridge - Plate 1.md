# Surface Halauric Bridge — Plate 1

**Status:** APPROVED — canonical bridge and city-name outcome  
**Created:** 2026-08-22  
**Approved:** 2026-08-22  
**Input:** working Late Open Halauri Plate 1  
**Required output:** approved Early Imperial Balishan Plate 1  
**Name test:** Late Open **Halauren**, without protecting its modern outcome

This plate derives the ordinary human speech between Late Open Halauri and approved Early Imperial
Balishan. It is not a rescue operation for the existing corpus. The daughter target was designed and
approved before this bridge, and the Late Open seed vocabulary was likewise generated without using the
city-name as a constraint. **Halauren** is run through the resulting rules only after the unrelated cognate
set passes.

## 1. Historical setting

Surface Halauric begins as the taught Halauri contact register used by human traders, craftspeople,
interpreters, officials, and mixed households. Over centuries, children acquire it from other human
speakers. It becomes a mother tongue with a Halauri genealogy and an Ancestral Continental substrate,
not permanently imperfect elven speech.

Four moments matter:

| Stage | Social condition |
|---|---|
| Contact nativization | Human children regularize the contact register around their inherited sound and grammar habits |
| Post-sealing Surface Halauric | Native correction ends; coastal and western community norms continue independently |
| Pre-imperial heartland continuum | River, road, market, and documentary networks keep related varieties mutually accessible |
| Early Imperial koine | Valan I's coalition selects widely learnable heartland forms through use in armies, markets, courts, and records |

The city seals roughly a millennium before the empire is founded. The bridge therefore has enough time
for ordinary vocabulary to become compact and opaque before the imperial koine begins.

## 2. Shared sound changes

The labels describe an ordered history. A learned or repeatedly refreshed word may enter after an early
change and therefore escape it; that is a transmission route, not an exception to the change.

### SB-1 — stress retracts to the first lexical root

Late Open penultimate stress gives way to the Ancestral Continental pattern. Prefixes may remain weak and
later compound roots retain secondary stress.

```text
Late Open       /ha.laˈu.ren/
Surface         /ˈha.la.u.ren/
```

This retraction creates the weak post-tonic syllables affected by later reduction and loss.

### SB-2 — nonfinal hiatus becomes a human diphthong or glide sequence

The ordinary western/coastal shared layer resolves adjacent vowels inside one lexical word:

```text
/a.u/ > /au/       /a.i/ > /ai/       /o.i/ > /oi/
/i.V/ > /jV/       /u.V/ > /wV/
```

The three vowel contractions become the approved Early Imperial diphthongs **au, ai, oi**. Other
sequences acquire a glide or are leveled by analogy. Learned recitation can preserve separate vowels.

### SB-3 — the ordinary glottal stop disappears

Intervocalic /ʔ/ is lost in inherited speech. The vowels it separated then undergo SB-2. Hieratic
instruction can reintroduce the consonant and the old spelling in titles such as **Kaha'an**.

### SB-4 — unfamiliar coronal consonants merge by region

| Late Open | Western tendency | Coastal tendency | Later learned reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **th** /θ/ | /t/ | /s/ | /θ/ restored |
| **dh** /ð/ | /d/ | /z/ | /ð/ restored |
| **x** /ɕ/ | /s/ | /ʃ/, with /z/ in one learned name tradition | /ɕ/ or spelling-based reading |

The Early Imperial koine contains western, coastal, and learned strata. It does not require a single
outcome for every source word, but each inherited word must belong to a demonstrated route.

### SB-5 — the final stem vowel erodes in ordinary inherited roots

After stress retraction, the final vowel of the common Late Open **CVCV/CVCi** lexical stem becomes weak
and disappears. The output is the compact CVC root favored by approved Early Imperial Balishan.

```text
*tala > tal       *nava > nav       *fali > fal
*zena > zen       *mera > mer       *rada > rad
```

This is a lexical-stem change, not a ban on all word-final vowels. Three classes escape it naturally:

- vowel sequences and short vowel-final roots whose loss would not produce the CVC class, such as
  **ari, nari, vori, koi, sei**;
- substrate vocabulary that enters Surface Halauric with its own shape;
- civic, technical, and learned forms borrowed or refreshed after the ordinary inherited change, such as
  Early Imperial **rava** beside the expected vernacular historical base ***rav***.

### SB-6 — weak medial vowels syncopate only into legal human clusters

Post-tonic /a e o/ first reduce toward [ə]. They disappear where the surrounding consonants form a cluster
permitted by the human substrate and, later, by Early Imperial Balishan.

```text
*gerema > *gerəm > grem
```

Deletion is blocked across two liquids and wherever it would create a cluster outside the receiving
language. Coastal varieties retain more weak vowels; western varieties create more clusters.

### SB-7 — the imperial koine levels the inherited outputs

The koine favors the approved Early Imperial inventory and phonotactics:

- five clear vowels and **ai, au, oi**;
- no ordinary /θ/, /ð/, /ɕ/, or /ʔ/;
- one-consonant codas, with illegal clusters repaired by **a**;
- initial root stress;
- full learned forms retained as a recognizable prestige layer rather than forced into the native root
  class.

## 4. Replacement, survival, and learned renewal

Genealogical descent does not require every meaning to retain a Halauri word. The independently designed
Early Imperial lexicon already predicts three different histories:

| History | Examples |
|---|---|
| Ordinary inherited cognate | *tala > tal, nava > nav, mera > mer* |
| Substrate or later replacement | Late Open **aeva** beside EIB **un** “water”; **vaea** beside **mau** “sea”; **meyu** beside **lun** “moon” |
| Learned or civic renewal | ordinary inherited ***rav*** beside public EIB **rava** “city”; full vowels and old consonants in Hieratic titles |

This layering keeps the daughter from looking like Late Open Halauri with its final letters clipped.
Intimate, environmental, and local words can come from the human substrate; institutional vocabulary can
be inherited or repeatedly renewed; ordinary high-frequency verbs can undergo the full sound history.

## 5. Structural transmission

The grammar changes through the same community shift:

| Late Open source | Surface development | Approved Early Imperial result |
|---|---|---|
| Metropolitan SOV; taught contact SVO | Human households generalize contact order | SVO is neutral |
| Noun case endings | endings reduce; prepositions and order carry more work | unmarked objects and analytic relations |
| Aspect suffixes | suffixes erode; substrate particles and periphrases compete | preverbal aspect particles |
| Claim-source clitics | the documentary distinction survives while its forms are renewed | independent **ha, zi, ro** source particles |
| Modifier-before-head compounds | reinforced by both source and substrate | productive modifier–head compounds |
| No grammatical gender; inclusive/exclusive *we* | both remain socially ordinary | preserved in the approved daughter |

Early Imperial Balishan is therefore a genuine daughter in vocabulary, structure, and institutional habit,
but the inherited system has been rebuilt by human speakers rather than copied whole.

## 6. Halauren enters only after the bridge passes

The old city-name now undergoes the same shared rules:

| Stage | Form | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Late Open endonym | **Halauren** /ha.laˈu.ren/ | Native hiatus and late stress |
| Nativizing Surface | **Halauren** /ˈha.la.u.ren/ | SB-1 root-stress retraction |
| Early Surface loan | **Halauren** /ˈha.lau.ren/ | SB-2 /a.u/ contracts to /au/ |
| Early Imperial | **Halauren** /ˈha.lau.ren/ | Legal EIB syllables and native diphthong; no further repair required |

The bridge does **not** wear the name down much by the imperial founding. That is a result, not a failure:
the form is already phonotactically comfortable once hiatus contracts. Most of the reader-visible change
must therefore occur in a later daughter language, or the final name should remain close to **Halauren**.

## 7. A bounded modern Kaha'ani development

The capital dialect has one later change capable of affecting the name without being invented for it:

### MK-1 — /l/ vocalizes before an unstressed rounded nucleus after /a/

```text
/a.lu/  > /au/
/a.lau/ > /au/
```

The intermediate is [w]; adjacent rounded glides and vowels coalesce. The change applies to productive
ordinary material as well as the name:

| Early Imperial | Meaning | Modern Kaha'ani |
|---|---|---|
| **palu** /ˈpa.lu/ | animal | **pau** /pau/ |
| **hal-u** /ˈha.lu/ | living, organic | **hau** /hau/ |
| **fal-u** /ˈfa.lu/ | travel-related | **fau** /fau/ |
| **Halauren** /ˈha.lau.ren/ | ancient city-name | **Hauren** /ˈhau.ren/ |

### MK-2 — unstressed /e/ reduces in ordinary speech

The spelling remains **e**, but the final syllable is [rən] in ordinary Kaha'ani:

> **Hauren** /ˈhau.rən/, **HOW-ren**

This produces the earned modern outcome approved by the author on 2026-08-22.

### The reader-facing people and adjective

The eastern city-local suffix **-i**, already independently required by approved **Kaha'ani**, attaches to
the inherited city-name before final surface syllabification:

```text
Hauren + -i > Haureni
```

**Haureni** /ˈhau.re.ni/, **HOW-reh-nee**, is the ordinary Modern Balishan exonym for the ancient people,
their language, and things characteristically made by them. It does not erase the native name **Halauri**.
The two belong to different speakers and routes:

| Form | Users | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Halauri** | the ancient people themselves; modern specialists using a restored native term | native people-name, language-name, and adjective |
| **Haureni** | ordinary Modern Balishan and reader-facing prose | human exonym for the people, language, and civilization |

This supplies the complete canonical prose replacement suite **Hauren / Haureni** without pretending that
the Halauri called themselves by a human daughter-language word.

## 9. Prose and collision tests

**Hauren** works in the principal prose frames:

> the gate of **Hauren**, the lost City of Gears

> **Hauren** was real, and it was a city.

> “**Hauren.**”

The corresponding prose adjective and people-name also remain natural:

> The **Haureni** had walked these roads for fifty centuries.

> The precision was **Haureni**.

> The **Haureni** script in the Repository had been cut into brass plates.

An initial collision screen found **Hauren** as a very rare real surname, a form occurring in Hausa phrases
including *hauren giwa* “ivory,” and a small Kano locality/street-name; **Haureni** also appears as an
unrelated local plant-name. The search found no prominent real city, historical civilization, or major
fictional setting using the suite. This is therefore a **low collision**, not a claim of global uniqueness.
**Haloren** and **Halren** have more visible fantasy-character uses and are weaker reader-facing candidates
even before sound and prose preference.
