# Modern Balishan — Design Plate 2: The Lost Province

**Status:** APPROVED — canonical bounded toponym suite<br>
**Created and authorized:** 2026-08-22<br>
**Scope:** Pre-Rending Litravan cities, landscape, infrastructure, and locally remembered names<br>
**Authority boundary:** This approval canonizes the forms, meanings, pronunciations, and referents in this
plate. It does not approve every unresolved feature of Modern Balishan Plate 1.

The old Province of Litrava is ideal ground for native toponymy. The Rending destroyed its settlements,
farmland, forest, rivers, and roads outside shielded Kaha'an. No surviving chapter needs a character to
travel through these places, and the existing support-canon labels **Deepwood Forest** and **Fort
Dinkarth** had no prose footprint. The language therefore wins completely: the lost country receives names
generated from Balishan history and word-building rather than names inherited from an earlier dossier.

This is a bounded canonical suite, not a claim that every hamlet has been recovered. It names the minimum
backbone needed to understand what Litrava was, how trade could avoid Kaha'an, what the Rending erased, and
where the Valanar Vault once lay.

## 1. Naming ages represented

| Age | Productive pattern | Place-name consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Older inherited landscape | Direct root compounds | Rivers, plains, forests, shores, and crossings sound older than the institutions later built on them |
| Imperial civic foundation | Landscape root + reduced civic **-ava** | Deliberately organized cities declare their relationship to water, woodland, or terrain |
| Work-grown settlement | Function or commodity + place head | Market towns and ports inherit the work that made them matter |
| Local lexical erosion | High-frequency compound loses recoverable material | A long official name becomes a shorter native town-name without an arbitrary spelling drift |
| Post-Rending memory | Old name plus ordinary death or ruin vocabulary | Survivors can distinguish the vanished place from the dangerous feature occupying its ground |

The suite deliberately avoids giving every location the same ending. A planned imperial city, a forest
market, a spring settlement, and a fortress should not sound as though one committee named them together.

## 2. Canonical settlements

### Talrava — the western river city

**Talrava**, **TAHL-rah-vah**, /ˈtal.ra.va/, is the locally compressed descendant of Early Imperial
***Talarava***, *tal + rava*, “river city.” It stood on Litrava's western approach to the capital where the
old river road entered the province.

Talrava was the largest inland city in the province, a carrier and warehousing center rather than a rival
capital. Westbound and inland caravans could turn north from its roads toward the forest bypass instead of
continuing southeast to Kaha'an. The Rending destroyed the city and the river course that had made it.

### Gonmer — the grain exchange

**Gonmer**, **GON-mehr**, /ˈgon.mer/, is *gon + mer*, “grain exchange” or “grain market.” The functional
name became the town-name long before the Rending.

Gonmer stood within the central grainlands and coordinated storage, contracts, wagon traffic, and seasonal
labor. It was one of the places through which the Kemvimore agricultural network became provincial rather
than merely local. This does not make the town a Kemvimore possession in every century; its market was
older than the House's mature monopoly.

### Yelava — the woodland city

**Yelava**, **YELL-ah-vah**, /ˈjel.a.va/, is *yel + -ava*, “woodland city.” It stood at the southern edge
of Dunyelas and served as the principal exchange between forest communities, lowland timber-wolf packs,
caravans, and the cultivated plain.

Yelava anchored the western entrance to the forest bypass. The city died with the forest. Kaha'ani survivors
still distinguish “the Yelava road” in inherited documents even though neither road nor destination can be
followed across the modern dunes.

### Unava — the water city

**Unava**, **OON-ah-vah**, /ˈun.a.va/, is *un + -ava*, “water city.” It grew where the northern imperial
aqueduct descended from the mountain source and divided into the trunk lines serving the capital and the
central farms.

Unava was simultaneously a town, a headworks complex, and the center of a skilled maintenance community.
The Rending destroyed the settlement and much of the lowland distribution network. Kaha'an later repaired
and extended the surviving capital line; the modern Water Cartel controls an inheritance whose old civic
center no longer exists.

### Tirun — the canal town

**Tirun**, **TEER-oon**, /ˈtir.un/, is *tir + un*, “cut water.” The word names a canal; the largest town
on the eastern irrigation canal inherited it as a proper name.

Tirun organized the eastern farms and the road toward the Erulian frontier. Its identity remained practical:
sluices, barges, surveyors, repair yards, and grain traffic. Post-Rending records sometimes specify **Tirun
Rava**, “Tirun City,” when distinguishing the settlement from the buried canal.

### Feshber — the salt landing

**Feshber**, **FESH-behr**, /ˈfeʃ.ber/, is *fesh + ber*, “salt quay.” It was a shallow-draft landing on the
western reef coast, built around salt pans, fish, and coastal redistribution.

Feshber never challenged Kaha'an's deep-water harbor. The reefs that denied large ships protected the
capital's maritime importance even before the Rending. Feshber mattered locally and commercially; that is
enough reason for a town to exist.

### Durcai — the high crossing

**Durcai**, **DOOR-tsai**, /ˈdur.tsai/, is *dur + cai*, “high crossing.” It stood below the southern
Sentinel foothills where the northern bypass left Dunyelas and returned to the eastern road system.

The place controlled roads, pack traffic, and the first safe staging ground below the pass. The crossing in
the mountains survives; the Balishan town beneath it does not. Terinoki maps use their own name for the
pass, to be generated by the Terinoki plate rather than retrofitted here.

## 3. Canonical landmarks and works

### Dunyelas — the northern forest

**Dunyelas**, **DOON-yeh-lahs**, /ˈdun.je.las/, is *dun + yel-as*, “northern woodland” or “the woodland
domain in the upland.” It replaces the disposable English placeholder **Deepwood Forest**.

Dunyelas was the thick deciduous forest between the cultivated heartland and the southern Sentinel
foothills. It held lowland timber-wolf territories, woodland settlements, hired-road stretches, and the
middle reach of the northern bypass. The Rending did not leave a dead forest standing. It erased the
ecology that made the name describe anything.

### Dunkargar — the northern stone watch

**Dunkargar**, **DOON-kar-gar**, /ˈdun.karˌgar/, is *dun + kar + gar*, “northern stone watch.” It replaces
the disposable **Fort Dinkarth**.

Dunkargar stood on the open plain immediately south of Dunyelas. It was an imperial fortress, road patrol
base, and regional secure store. Several generations before the Rending, a Valanar emperor placed the
reserve treasury now called the Valanar Vault beneath it. The fortress was erased above ground; the buried
vault may have survived.

### Limel Mar — the Great Plain

**Limel Mar**, **LEE-mell mar**, /ˈli.mel mar/, is *lim + mel mar*, “the great open/unbroken plain.” The
identical **m** at the compound boundary collapses by the inherited rule. It
was the broad central country between Kaha'an's northern approaches and Dunyelas.

The name is geographical rather than poetic. Gonmer, Dunkargar, and much of the old road network stood on
or around it. Modern dunes cover the referent so completely that **Limel Mar** survives principally in
property records and pre-Rending route descriptions.

### Gonmel Mar — the Great Grainlands

**Gonmel Mar**, **GON-mell mar**, /ˈgon.mel mar/, is *gon-mel mar*, “the great cultivated grainland.” It
names the most intensively farmed central and western belt rather than every farm in Litrava.

The distinction from Limel Mar is one of use versus landform: much of the Grainlands lay upon the Great
Plain, but neither name was interchangeable with the other. The Rending destroyed the two referents at
once and made the old cadastral distinction grotesquely academic.

### Yeltal — the Forest River

**Yeltal**, **YELL-tahl**, /ˈjel.tal/, is *yel + tal*, “woodland river.” It descended from the Sentinel
foothills through Dunyelas and fed the western irrigation works before joining lower seasonal channels.

It was not one of the continent's major boundary rivers; the Rending could destroy or divert its lower
course without contradicting the larger rivers that bounded the catastrophe. Modern survivor usage can call
the buried bed **Yeltal Mor**, “Dead Yeltal,” but that is a description of the aftermath rather than a
second ancient name.

### Yelpas — the forest passage

**Yelpas**, **YELL-pahs**, /ˈjel.pas/, is *yel + pas*, “the traversable way through woodland.” It names the
maintained road system through Dunyelas between Yelava and Durcai.

Yelpas was not one paved imperial highway from end to end. It was the administratively recognized chain of
forest roads, patrol stretches, bridges, and contracted private tracks that let caravans bypass Kaha'an's
tariffs. Its destruction is why the Rending made the capital unavoidable.

### Unpel Dun — the Northern Aqueduct

**Unpel Dun**, **OON-pell doon**, /ˈun.pel dun/, is *un-pel dun*, “the northern water-carrier.” It names
the imperial aqueduct line from the mountain source through Unava toward Kaha'an.

The source and parts of the high line survived outside or at the edge of the Rending. Unava and the
lowland branches did not. The post-Rending city repaired, redirected, and expanded the capital-facing
system, so the modern aqueduct is a surviving work with a severed geography rather than an untouched
ancient line.

### Maulom — the harbor basin

**Maulom**, **MOW-lom** (*mow* rhymes with *cow*), /ˈmau.lom/, is *mau + lom*, “protected sea basin.” It names the physical harbor
at Kaha'an, not the city, state, or capital office.

This produces the correct stack at one location:

| Referent | Name |
|---|---|
| Physical deep-water basin | **Maulom** |
| Pre-imperial coastal settlement | **Marresonne** |
| Transferable capital-title and modern city identity | **Kaha'an** |

The three names coexist because they name three different things. Acquiring the title did not rename the
water.

### Maukaras — the Reef Coast

**Maukaras**, **MOW-kar-ahs** (*mow* rhymes with *cow*), /ˈmau.kar.as/, is *mau + kar + -as*, “the domain of sea-rock.” It names the
reef-riddled southern coast on either side of Maulom.

The reefs survive the Rending. They explain why Feshber could serve shallow coastal traffic while Kaha'an
alone offered a safe deep-water harbor. Neighboring languages may translate or rename the same coast; this
is the Balishan name.

## 4. The pre-Rending route skeleton

The names form one working country rather than eleven isolated glosses:

```text
                         Sentinel foothills
                    Durcai          water source
                       \             |
                Dunyelas -- Yelpas --|-- Unava
                    |                |   |
                 Yelava         Unpel Dun
                    |                |
     west -- Talrava -- Gonmer -- Dunkargar -- Tirun -- east
                         \       Limel Mar      /
                          \    Gonmel Mar      /
                           \                  /
                         Kaha'an / Maulom
                    Feshber -- Maukaras -- coast
```

This is a relational diagram, not a scale map. Its load-bearing facts are:

1. Talrava and the western road fed the province from inland.
2. Gonmer and the Grainlands made Litrava the agricultural body of residual Balisha.
3. Yelava, Yelpas, Dunyelas, and Durcai formed a longer northern bypass around Kaha'an.
4. Unava and the Northern Aqueduct distributed mountain water.
5. Tirun organized the eastern canal country.
6. Feshber handled shallow coastal work, while Maulom alone admitted deep-water shipping.
7. Dunkargar guarded the plain and concealed the reserve treasury beneath an ordinary imperial fortress.

The Rending destroyed every node that made the bypass coherent. Kaha'an survived with Maulom and a repaired
aqueduct, but the country around them ceased to function as country.

## 5. Reader-facing use

These names are author-facing canon and may appear in records, maps, histories, survivor memory, or future
archaeological discussion. They do not create an obligation to expose the reader to a lost atlas.

- **Dunyelas** and **Dunkargar** replace the old support-canon placeholders everywhere current canon needs
  the names.
- English may still translate generic categories: *the Great Plain, the Grainlands, the Northern Aqueduct,
  the Reef Coast*.
- A character can say *the old forest road* without saying **Yelpas**.
- Kess's people can be described as coming from Dunyelas without requiring Kess herself to know or use the
  formal Balishan name.
- No place is treated as a navigable modern destination. Pre-Rending maps remain useless because the
  Rending removed the landmarks by which their named relationships could be followed.

## 6. Canonical commitments

The following forms are canonical in the bounded scope of lost Litravan geography:

- Settlements: **Talrava, Gonmer, Yelava, Unava, Tirun, Feshber, Durcai**.
- Landmarks and works: **Dunyelas, Dunkargar, Limel Mar, Gonmel Mar, Yeltal, Yelpas, Unpel Dun, Maulom,
  Maukaras**.
- Post-Rending descriptive form: **Yeltal Mor**, “Dead Yeltal.”

This approval also canonizes the necessary Modern Balishan geographic roots and compounds from Plate 1
within these forms. It does not automatically canonize every demonstration name or every proposed sound
change in Plate 1.
