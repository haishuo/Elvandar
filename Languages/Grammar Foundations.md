# Grammar Foundations

**Status:** Minimum historical grammar, established 2026-08-22. This document fixes only the structures
needed to build names, compounds, possession, location, and ordinary clauses across Late Open
Halauri, Ancestral Continental, and Early Surface Halauric. It is not a complete grammar and does not
license a free-standing dictionary.

## Why one document covers three stages

Surface Halauric did not arise by replacing one word list with another. Human communities learned a
real Halauri contact register, used it in homes as well as public institutions, and gradually made its
grammar conform to habits already present in their own speech. The useful reconstruction therefore has
to show three things together:

1. what the Halauri public register supplied;
2. what Ancestral Continental speakers carried into the shift;
3. which choices survived into the human daughter continuum.

The categories below are firmer than their sounds. A relational marker, aspect suffix, or settlement head
can be known to have existed before its exact ancient form has been responsibly reconstructed. Schematic
labels appear in small capitals for that reason; they are structural slots, not invented words.

## Late Open Halauri

### The public register and the metropolitan language

Late Open Halauri was moderately suffixing. A lexical root could take derivation, number or
collectivity, and a final role marker, while verbs carried aspect and mood. Metropolitan household speech
had more contractions, irregular high-frequency forms, and socially marked pronouns than the taught
contact register. Public speech kept the same language but preferred transparent compounds, explicit
relations, and fewer obligatory inflections.

There was **no grammatical gender** and no adjective agreement. Living beings could of course be
described by sex, age, rank, or kinship, but an ordinary noun did not force matching forms elsewhere in
the sentence. This absence survives throughout the Surface Halauric branch.

### The noun phrase

The ordinary public noun phrase was head-final internally:

```text
(demonstrative) (possessor or relational phrase) (quantity) (quality/classifier) HEAD (role marker)
```

Not every position had to be filled. A quality, material, age term, office, or institutional classifier
stood before the thing it described. The authorially fixed phrases are the first direct evidence:

```text
kaha  batae    crown/sovereign  guard-corps    “Imperial Guard”
arol  batae    old              guard-corps    “Old Guard”
arol  rhutan   old              tunnel-system  “Old Tunnels”
```

The post-Rending replacement of *kaha* by *arol* changes the first slot and leaves the head untouched.
This is ordinary grammar, not a special poetic reversal.

Number was marked when contrast mattered, not every time more than one object was meant. Numerals,
quantity words, context, and lexical collectives could make number marking redundant. In the attested
phrases, *batae* is number-neutral because duty-holder/body nominalizer *-ae* forms either an appointed
guard or the constituted guarding body. *Rhutan* is a true lexical collective, regularly descended from
LOL *re-hutan* “interconnected tunnel system”; the count noun *hutan* names one tunnel. Their English
renderings therefore differ in number without either word supplying a productive plural suffix.

### Open phrases, compounds, and bound heads

Late Open distinguished three increasingly close constructions:

1. **Open phrase:** separately inflectable modifier before head. *Arol Batae* belongs here.
2. **Lexical compound:** two roots forming one conventional word. Roots remained audible, and **a** or
   **i** could link them when direct contact would create a disfavored cluster.
3. **Bound-head formation:** a root followed by a dependent lexical head that could not normally stand in
   the same form by itself.

The working Late Open plate makes *-'an* the productive example of the third construction. It turns a
public office, delegated authority, or regulated competence into the recognized locus where that
authority is legally present and able to act: *esha'an* “tribunal,” *netha'an* “certifying chair,” and
*thuma'an* “system control seat.” LOL *kaha* supplies sovereignty understood as an office, producing
*Kaha'an*, the sovereign member of the family. The apostrophe is the consonant /ʔ/ at the boundary, not
punctuation.

This bound head is neither ordinary possession nor a general place suffix. Genitive *-n* answers “whose
is it?”; site *-eth* says that work of a kind happens there; *-'an* says that an act issued there counts as
an act of the institution. The Valanar court borrowed *Kaha'an* whole and made it transferable, but the
source construction was not unique to the title. The grammar still does not license attaching *-'an* to
mountains, villages, personal names, or every impressive place.

Linking vowels carried no independent meaning. They belonged to the phonological shape of a productive
compound and could differ by the final and initial sounds being joined. This gives Early Surface
Halauric a genuine unstressed vowel to reduce and later lose, creating the compact clusters found in
human names without requiring Late Open public speech to tolerate them.

### Possession

Possessors preceded the possessed noun. Two strategies divided the work:

- **close or classifying possession** used direct juxtaposition or compounding: kin relations, body
  relations, offices, institutional subdivisions, and names that identified what kind of thing the head
  was;
- **alienable or contrastive possession** placed an invariant relational marker between possessor and
  possessed.

The relational marker did not agree with either noun. Public speakers favored it whenever omission could
confuse ownership, origin, material, or affiliation. Its exact form remains to be reconstructed. That
choice is deliberate: the corpus proves possessor-before-head structure, not a particular syllable.

### Place expressions and settlement names

Ordinary Late Open location used a small set of final role markers or postposed relators distinguishing
place-at, motion-to, motion-from, and route-through. The contact register often pronounced these as full
syllables even where metropolitan speech reduced them.

Named settlements used a different construction:

```text
LOCAL OR FOUNDING STEM + SETTLEMENT HEAD
```

The settlement head described an inhabited or formally established place; it was not simply the case
marker meaning “at.” A linking vowel could appear before it. `Halauric Core Lexicon.md` now identifies
the head as LOL *onna*, /ˈon.na/, “established habitation,” derived from *on-* “dwell permanently” plus the
result/place nominalizer *-na*. The shared **-onne** of *Vartonne* and *Marresonne* is its later
documentary form, not a generic fantasy suffix.

### Ordinary clauses

The neutral civic clause placed the verb last:

```text
SUBJECT – OBJECT/COMPLEMENT – VERB
```

Topic and focus could move a constituent, because final role marking kept relations recoverable. The
public contact register also admitted **subject–verb–object** order when all roles were plain, especially
in instruction and conversation with surface learners. It was a native accommodation, not yet the
unmarked order of the language.

The public verb did not obligatorily agree with gender or noun class. Its most important opposition was
aspect: an action viewed as ongoing or habitual, one viewed as bounded, and a resulting state. Time could
be supplied by context or a time expression; tense marking was available when a sequence needed it.
Derivational material stayed nearest the root, followed by aspect, with tense or mood outermost.

Negation stood immediately before the verb complex. Yes/no questions used intonation in familiar speech
and a clause-final question marker in careful public speech. Present-tense identity and quality clauses
could omit the copula; existence, location, change of state, and other tenses retained an overt verb. The
exact particles, endings, pronouns, and irregular copulas remain open.

## Ancestral Continental

Ancestral Continental supplied a different grammatical rhythm even where its noun phrases happened to
agree with Halauri.

### Nouns and relations

The common reconstruction was predominantly analytic. It had no grammatical gender and little productive
case marking. Number was likewise optional when a numeral, quantity, or collective noun made it clear.
Modifiers and possessors preceded their heads:

```text
(demonstrative) (possessor) (quantity) (quality) HEAD
```

This shared order is why modifier-before-head phrasing survived language shift so securely. Speakers did
not have to relearn where *arol* stood; they only had to learn the Halauri word and the public
distinctions attached to it.

Possession used juxtaposition for close relationships and an independent linker for alienable ownership.
Location and direction used **prepositions**, not Late Open final role markers. Compounds commonly joined
compact roots directly, preserving clusters at the boundary. A settlement could be named by placing a
local stem before a generic place word, matching the semantic direction of the Late Open settlement
construction even though the two systems expressed ordinary location differently.

### Clauses

The neutral clause was **subject–verb–object**. Grammatical roles depended on order and prepositions more
than endings. Aspect, negation, and many modal meanings appeared as particles before an otherwise lightly
inflected verb. Subjects were stated when discourse did not make them recoverable; verbs did not agree
with grammatical gender.

Present identity and quality clauses could lack an overt copula, while existence and location used one.
Questions could be marked by intonation or a final particle. These similarities to the Late Open public
register made sustained bilingualism easier, while the opposing verb position and treatment of spatial
relations supplied pressure for structural change.

No actual Ancestral Continental particle or pronoun is coined here. The reconstruction may eventually
divide into several close languages, and prematurely assigning one form would make all of them identical
by accident.

## Early Surface Halauric

Early Surface Halauric kept Halauri roots and public distinctions but reorganized them through the
human substrate. The common daughter grammar is therefore neither broken Late Open nor Ancestral
Continental with prestigious nouns pasted onto it.

### Stable inheritances

Across the early continuum:

- modifiers and possessors remain before the head;
- grammatical gender remains absent and modifiers remain invariant;
- number can remain unmarked when quantity or collective meaning is recoverable;
- close possession and institutional classification favor juxtaposition or compounding;
- the old linking vowels remain audible in learned forms but reduce or disappear in ordinary inheritance;
- aspect matters at least as much as absolute tense;
- present identity and quality clauses can remain copula-less.

These commonalities explain why a later imperial register can create *Kaha Batae*, replace it with *Arol
Batae*, and form parallel *Arol Rhutan* without sounding syntactically foreign to its human speakers.

### Substrate-driven changes

Subject–verb–object order becomes neutral. The inherited verb-final order survives longest in legal,
ritual, archival, and deliberately antique prose, then becomes a learned stylistic resource rather than
ordinary syntax.

Late Open final role markers shorten, merge, or fossilize. Human prepositions and newly independent
relators take over possession, location, direction, and instrument. Some old endings survive inside names,
titles, adverbs, and technical vocabulary after speakers cease to recognize them as productive case.

The verb develops a mixed system. Frequent inherited aspect and mood endings remain attached to common
verbs, while substrate-style preverbal particles expand around them. Later daughters can simplify the
endings differently without ceasing to be Halauric languages.

The settlement construction remains head-final inside the name even while ordinary spatial grammar turns
prepositional. Its final head becomes an ideal site for reduction, assimilation, and spelling
conservatism. That combination—not a generic fantasy suffix—is the current explanation for why two old
heartland names can share **-onne**.

### Productive human affiliation

Early Imperial Balishan has a living relational suffix ***-an*** /an/ for polity-scale affiliation. It is
substrate-derived rather than a surviving Late Open role marker. Because modifiers do not agree and the
suffix itself does not inflect, one form can function according to syntax as an adjective, an inhabitant
term, a collective people-name, or a language-name:

```text
Balisha + -an  >  Balishan
Uratha  + -an  >  Urathan
```

When the base ends in /a/, the base vowel and suffix vowel coalesce. The derivation does not establish
the deeper etymology of either realm-name.

Marresonnian and the transferred eastern court also use local-affiliation ***-i*** /i/ for a settlement,
its inhabitants, and its urban speech or culture. It remains productive in Late Imperial capital usage:

```text
Kaha'an + -i  >  Kaha'ani
```

This yields an inhabitant or a city-local adjective and dialect name. It does not compete with national
*Balishan*: a person or custom can be both Balishan and Kaha'ani at different scales. The final /n/ of
*Kaha'an* resyllabifies before /i/, but the learned /ʔ/ remains in careful speech.

These are human daughter-language suffixes. Neither Imperial *-an* nor local *-i* is LOL bound *-'an*,
the governing-seat head inside *Kaha'an*. Similar spelling is not shared grammar.

### Zero-derived work and practitioner nouns

Ancestral Continental can use a compact practical-work root as a verb, activity noun, or practitioner
noun without an audible derivational suffix. Early Surface Halauric preserves this pattern where syntax
and context keep the category clear. Established ***fen*** /fen/ therefore supports:

```text
fen                 prepare plant remedies; herb-work
a / one fen         one herb-worker or apothecary
Mira Fen            Mira identified by the trade
Master Fen          translated honorific plus the trade as occupational byname
```

Number remains optional where context or a numeral supplies it, so the practitioner use does not require
a hidden singular or plural ending. Capitalization belongs to the author-facing treatment of bynames and
titles, not to the spoken morphology. The pattern does not make every compact commoner name a trade-word;
each additional zero-derived root requires evidence of its meaning and social use.

### Invariant preposed respect title

Ancestral Continental ***sar*** /sar/ survives unchanged as the ordinary Balishan honorific for a
socially established adult or a person acting in a recognized skilled capacity. It precedes the personal
name or byname it scopes, preserving modifier-before-head order:

```text
sar Fen       respectful title + occupational byname
sar Janice    respectful title + personal name
sar Keelen    respectful title + personal name
sar Keya      respectful title + personal name
```

The particle is invariant. It has no gender agreement, number agreement, or professional-grade suffix.
Reader-facing English translates the same form as *Master* or *Mistress* according to the referent. The
translation does not establish two native titles; when the referent is unknown, it may retain the public
form *Master*, as in Elara's “the original Master Fen.” *Sar* is outside the name and cannot turn a
commoner's one personal name into a granted house name.

This test is deliberately narrow. Noble *young master*, institutional *master of the Grain*, the head of
the Shadow Corps, the Long Knives' *Mistress*, and domain-specific mastery grades remain separate
untranslated categories until their own evidence requires forms.

### Medical work, practitioner, and service-site nouns

CON ***len*** /len/ follows the same zero-derivation pattern as *fen*: according to syntax it means “treat
or mend a living body,” the practice of doing so, or one practitioner. Reader-facing English therefore
uses *heal/healing/healer* without requiring a native practitioner suffix.

Ancestral Continental also has unstressed practical-service site suffix ***-um*** /um/. Root stress is
stable while the suffix vowel reduces in Surface Halauric:

```text
len-um  /ˈle.num/  >  /ˈle.nəm/   clinic; continuing patient-care site
fen-um  /ˈfe.num/  >  /ˈfe.nəm/   apothecary shop or herb-work room
```

The suffix identifies a place by recurring organized practice, not by architecture alone. This lets a
back room be a *lenum* while patients are regularly received and cease being one when the practice ends,
even though the room remains. It also keeps English *apothecary*'s person/place ambiguity out of Balishan:
*fen* is the practitioner or craft; *fenum* is the workplace.

Learned LOL ***sathen*** /ˈsa.θen/ < *sath-* “diagnose a living body's condition from signs” + specialist
***-en*** is transmitted through the technical/Hieratic register as the Modern Balishan word translated
*physician*. It denotes practiced diagnostic competence, not grammatical gender, a legal licence, or a
court appointment. Consequently Xion can be both zero-derived *len* and learned *sathen*; the palace
physicians' robes mark their imperial appointment rather than create the lexical category.

### Household, chartered House, and lineage

CON ***dal*** /dal/ is a social collective meaning a household maintained under one continuing provision,
roof, and domestic authority. It includes more than biological kin and less than everyone who happens to
occupy one building. The word names neither the physical structure nor a noble lineage.

Early Imperial Balishan forms a mixed legal compound from LOL ***sel-*** /sel/ “enter or recognize in an
authoritative public record” plus *dal*:

```text
sel-dal  >  seldal /ˈsel.dal/   registered household; chartered House
```

Modifier-before-head order remains visible: authoritative recognition classifies the older household
head. Reader-facing English capitalizes ***House*** when translating *seldal*. The compound is a legal
category, not a suffix attached to every noble name, so its members' second names remain morphologically
diverse.

Balishan House grammar keeps five axes separate: blood descent, legal membership, the second name carried
in records, heirship, and present office. Marriage can change membership and name without changing blood;
disinheritance can remove heirship without instantly erasing a name; headship is an office governed by a
House's charter rather than by grammatical gender. Which House receives a spouse and children is settled
by the marriage instrument, not a universal paternal or maternal rule.

House ***Larannas*** preserves fossil patronymic collective ***-annas*** /an.nas/, “descendants or issue of
the named forebear,” after *Lar*. The element is no longer productive: commoners do not form hereditary
second names with it, and no new House name may be generated merely by attaching the ending.

### First regional tendencies

**Marresonnian** preserves more explicit relational words, fuller compound vowels in records, and a
larger contrast between ordinary and learned forms. Its scribal tradition is the natural carrier for
conservative spellings and later Hieratic readings such as *Xion*.

**Vartonnian** relies more heavily on fixed SVO order, prepositions, direct compounds, and zero marking
when context is sufficient. It reduces unstressed grammatical material more aggressively. The imperial
koine takes much of its ordinary clause frame from this branch while drawing learned, commercial, and
documentary forms from Marresonnian practice.

These are tendencies, not claims that every western form is shorter or every coastal speaker talks like a
contract.

The Long Knives test matters: this grammar could eventually form a plausible Late Open institutional
title, but the historical fact that such a title existed does not mean the surviving order remembers it.
An author-facing reconstruction should be coined only when a useful root set exists, and it must not leak
into Xion's narration.

## Deliberately unresolved

The following remain outside this foundation:

- the sounds of Late Open and Ancestral Continental possession markers, ordinary role endings, verbal
  particles, and copulas; Imperial affiliation *-an* and local *-i* are now established;
- complete pronoun, demonstrative, numeral, case, tense, aspect, and mood paradigms;
- honorifics beyond ordinary *sar*, wider social deixis, relative clauses, subordinate clauses, and
  metropolitan irregularities;
- the grammar of Sealed or Terminal Halauri;
- the exact division among Ancestral Continental languages;
- proven segmentation of protected names;
- any translation longer than the attested phrases above.

The settlement-head, confirmed imperial-cluster, first affiliation morphology, ordinary *fen* family,
ordinary *sar* honorific, first medical register, and household/chartered-House register are complete in
`Halauric Core Lexicon.md`. The next safe step is another small, closed cluster whose meanings and social
route are independently constrained and which must survive the same ordered sound history.
