# Late Open Halauri

**Status:** First phonological foundation, established 2026-08-22. This document describes the public
contact register used during the final centuries before Hauren sealed. `Grammar Foundations.md` now
establishes its minimum contact grammar; lexical expansion, the full metropolitan system, and the
pronunciation of Sealed and Terminal Halauri remain open.

## What this language stage is

Late Open Halauri is not “Elvish for all five thousand years.” It is the last open-period stage of a
living language, used roughly two thousand years before Book 1 by the institutions that dealt routinely
with surface communities. The register described here was heard in the Great Shaft, Arrival Halls,
Diplomatic Quarter, public instruction, contracts, technical apprenticeships, and formal worship open to
visitors.

It was based on contemporary civic Halauri but deliberately teachable. Speakers articulated word
boundaries, preferred public vocabulary with stable reference, avoided the densest neighborhood
contractions, and repaired some clusters that foreign learners found difficult. That did not make it a
pidgin. A fluent surface interpreter could argue law, learn a craft, negotiate a contract, worship, joke,
and form a household in it. What the register did not automatically grant was the full intimacy of
household speech or the restricted language of the Athenaeum, Arcanum, Crown, and Repository.

The forms below use an **author-facing romanization**. Hauren did not write in the Latin alphabet. A
romanized apostrophe or doubled letter is therefore a reconstruction convention, not the shape of a
Halauri glyph.

## Vowels

The contact register has five stable vowel qualities.

| Romanization | IPA | Approximation |
|---|---:|---|
| **a** | /a/ | open *a*, without English rounding |
| **e** | /e/ | a pure vowel between English *bet* and *bait* |
| **i** | /i/ | *ee* without a glide |
| **o** | /o/ | a pure *o* without the English off-glide |
| **u** | /u/ | *oo* |

Vowel length may have existed in older or metropolitan speech, but it is not contrastive in the public
contact register reconstructed here. This is a useful historical fact rather than an assertion that
Halauri never had long vowels: length distinctions are precisely the sort of feature a taught public
standard may level when meaning is recoverable elsewhere.

### Vowel sequences

Two written vowels may be:

- a diphthong within one syllable, principally **ai, au, ei, oi**;
- two syllables across a root or suffix boundary, as in reconstructed **i.o**;
- kept explicitly separate by /ʔ/, written **'**, as in **a'an**.

Careful contact speech preserves hiatus instead of automatically turning every pair into an English-like
diphthong. This lets *Hauren* and *Xion* contain an audible boundary if their histories require one.

### Notes on the inventory

- **r** is a tap [ɾ] between vowels and may be lightly trilled [r] in careful public or ceremonial
  speech. It is never the English approximant by default.
- **th** is voiceless /θ/. Intervocalic [ð] may occur in relaxed metropolitan speech, but the contact
  spelling does not distinguish it and trained public speakers restore [θ].
- **x** represents a narrow palatal sibilant /ɕ/, roughly an *sh* made with the tongue farther forward,
  similar to the consonant conventionally romanized *sh* in Japanese. It is uncommon in public vocabulary and most frequent in old names,
  ritual language, and learned forms. Surface speakers are expected to adapt it differently.
- **'** is a consonant /ʔ/, not punctuation and not a signal that a word is generically “ancient.” It can
  begin a root or preserve a boundary between vowels.
- **b, d,** and **g** are ordinary Late Open consonants. The inherited guide's claim that they were almost
  absent is retired: locked *Balisha* and three millennia of contact give no reason to impose that
  restriction. Later *Batae* is compatible with the inventory but does not establish it by itself.
- The contact register has no native **c, j, q**. Those letters may occur only in later transliteration
  traditions or names from other languages.

## Phonotactics

### Syllable shape

The normal public syllable is:

```text
(C)(G)V(V)(C)
```

where **G** is /j/ or /w/. Metropolitan speech permits additional clusters, but the public register
usually inserts a vowel or chooses a parallel form rather than demand a difficult onset from a learner.

Common word-initial shapes are **V, CV, CGV**. A two-consonant onset not involving a glide is marked,
usually learned, a compound boundary, or foreign. Common codas are **-m, -n, -ng, -l, -r, -s, -sh,
-th**. **-t** and **-k** occur in roots and clipped professional language but are less favored in formal
names.

Three consequences follow:

1. Open, sonorous names such as *Elara* sit naturally in the prestige register.
2. Final consonants such as *Valanar* and *Hauren* are fully possible without making every Halauri word
   end in a vowel.
3. Dense forms such as *Brennek, Draskin,* and *Gunastran* are not good evidence for this contact
   phonotactic system. They belong to later human development or other families.

### Root and word shapes

Uninflected public roots tend toward **CV, CVC, CVCV,** and **CVVC**. Longer words are usually visibly
derived or compounded. Linking vowels—especially **a** and **i**—prevent clusters at productive
boundaries. This produces long words that remain syllabically clear rather than consonant-heavy.

Full personal names in metropolitan Halauri may be long, but no claim is yet made that every citizen's
name had four or five syllables. The older naming guide inferred that from aesthetic preference rather
than culture or evidence.

## Stress and rhythm

Default stress falls on the **penultimate syllable**, without reduction of the surrounding written vowels:

- *E-**la**-ra*
- *Va-**la**-nar*
- *Ba-**li**-sha*
- *La-**thi**-on*

Compound elements may retain secondary stress. Certain Crown titles, ritual forms, and ancient names have
lexical stress that must be learned, but exceptional stress is not marked in ordinary romanization.

The contact register has a syllable-timed public rhythm: unstressed vowels remain audible. This is one of
the first properties human Surface Halauric varieties are likely to alter under Ancestral Continental
influence.

## Public pronunciation and metropolitan variation

The taught standard is conservative in exactly the places that matter to strangers: word boundaries,
contractual particles, technical contrasts, numerals, and names entered into records. Metropolitan
household speech can nevertheless:

- voice /s/ and /θ/ between vowels;
- turn /i/ or /u/ before another vowel into [j] or [w];
- weaken /h/ outside stressed syllables;
- reduce repeated vowels at high-frequency word boundaries;
- use neighborhood vocabulary absent from public lessons.

These are variation channels, not yet historical sound changes. They keep the city linguistically alive
without inventing seven separate languages for seven levels.

## Contact accommodation

Human learners whose first languages belonged to the Ancestral Continental sphere regularly produced:

| Late Open feature | Common human adaptation |
|---|---|
| /θ/ **th** | /t/ in western speech; /s/ in some coastal speech |
| /ɕ/ **x** | /s/, /ʃ/, or voiced /z ~ ʒ/ in different communities |
| /ʔ/ **'** | retained carefully; otherwise lost, with adjacent vowels contracted or separated by /j, w/ |
| clear unstressed vowels | reduced toward [ə], then sometimes lost |
| vowel hiatus | contracted, glided, or divided by an inserted consonant |
| simple onsets | retained, but new clusters arise after unstressed-vowel loss |
| final vowels | retained in careful speech; often lost in ordinary human compounds and names |

Halauri teachers did not regard every adaptation as an error. The public register existed to transmit
meaning across communities. What mattered most was preserving contrasts that changed a contract,
measurement, ritual obligation, or safety instruction. A surface accent could be conspicuous and still be
fully competent.

### The first confirmed imperial phrase structure

Three authorially settled phrases establish a modifier-before-head construction in the later imperial
prestige register:

| Phrase | Secure lexical evidence |
|---|---|
| **Kaha Batae** | *kaha* “crown/sovereign authority” + *batae* “guard, guard corps” → **“Imperial Guard”** |
| **Arol Batae** | *arol* “old” + *batae* “guard, guard corps” → **“Old Guard”** |
| **Arol Rhutan** | *arol* “old” + *rhutan* “tunnels” → **“Old Tunnels”** |

The *Kaha Batae* were renamed *Arol Batae* after the Rending. This is a lexical substitution inside the
same construction, unusually clean evidence that *kaha* and *arol* occupy the same modifier position while
*batae* is the head. *Batae* is number-neutral because LOL duty-holder/body suffix *-ae* forms either the
appointed guard or the constituted guarding body; it is not a plural ending. *Rhutan* is different: the
ordinary inherited lexical collective descends from LOL *re-hutan* “interconnected tunnel system,” while
*hutan* is one tunnel. Full derivations and route distinctions are in `Halauric Core Lexicon.md`.

### First technical-medical formation

LOL ***sathen*** /ˈsa.θen/ combines diagnostic root *sath-* “examine bodily signs and distinguish the
internal condition” with skilled-practitioner nominalizer *-en*. Surface medical schools preserve it
through the technical and Hieratic tradition as the Modern Balishan term translated **physician**.
Retention of /θ/ and the full second syllable mark learned transmission. Ordinary coastal *sasen* and
western *saten* would be phonologically regular inheritances, but neither is established as a living word.

The formation names practiced diagnostic competence, not a legal licence, palace appointment, gender, or
moral standing. Only this medical use of *-en* is established; resemblance does not segment unrelated
names or license a general profession list.

### First legal-house element

LOL ***sel-*** /sel/ means “enter, recognize, or constitute in an authoritative public record.” It is a
legal and institutional verb, not the generic word for writing or naming. Early Imperial Balishan places
it before CON household head *dal* to form mixed ***seldal*** /ˈsel.dal/, “registered household; chartered
House.” The human compound takes Imperial root-initial stress rather than preserving a separate Late Open
inflectional frame.

Only this legal use is established. No Halauri noble-house system is inferred from the human borrowing,
and no broader family of *sel-* record words is coined merely because the root survives in one imperial
formation.

### The protected transmission case: Xion

Written **Xion** is compatible with a learned form containing /ɕ/: reconstructed contact or Hieratic
**/ˈɕi.on/**. The author has fixed the Modern Balishan pronunciation as **ZY-on**, /ˈzaɪ.ən/. That selects
a learned Marresonnian–Hieratic transmission rather than ordinary inheritance: the rare palatal sibilant
is read /z/ in the coastal proper-name tradition, stressed /i/ lengthens before the preserved hiatus, and
the resulting /iː/ later diphthongizes in this conservative name class.

The ordered derivation is in `Early Surface Halauric.md`. Written **x** remains fixed, and Late Open
**x** remains /ɕ/; the modern sound is evidence for the route rather than a reason to rewrite the contact
inventory. No longer form behind *Xion* is required, and the old “probably clipped” claim is retired.

### Forms that should not be forced through direct Halauri

- **Kemvimore** can be a mixed or opaque Balishan house formation. Its medial **mv** is natural across a
  human compound boundary but marked in the contact register.
- **Rosik** fits Ancestral Continental and later Balishan shapes more readily than a prestigious Late Open
  name. That is useful differentiation, not a defect.
- **Vartonne** is a mixed CON *vart* + borrowed LOL *onna* formation; **Marresonne** is the regular coastal
  descendant of LOL *marreth* + *onna*, visibly applying /θ/ > /s/. Their full derivations are in
  `Halauric Core Lexicon.md`.
- **Fen, sar, dal, Larannas, Hadris, Tam, Jorik, Brennek,** and **Draskin** belong to the human daughter history. They are not
  failed Halauri forms.
- **Terinok, Gunastran, Erulius, Sa Ko Ren,** and the Bloodsworn vocabulary belong to other families or
  remain genealogically unassigned.

## What this phonology does not establish

  *arol, kaha, bat-, -ae, hutan, re-, -'an, on-, -na, onna,* and *marreth*;
- no exact forms for the grammatical slots established in `Grammar Foundations.md`;
- no claim that public contact speech and every metropolitan neighborhood had identical inventories;
- no exact Halauri personal-name morphology;
- no Hieratic surface pronunciation two thousand years later;
- no Sealed or Terminal sound changes;
- no approved native glyph inventory or writing direction; the separately authorized runic/operative
  architecture and working forms live in `Design Plates/Halauri Script - Plate 1.md`;
- no reason for the sealing.
