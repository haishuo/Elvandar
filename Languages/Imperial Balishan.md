# Imperial Balishan

**Status:** Imperial-stage and first daughter-language foundation, established 2026-08-22. This document
fixes the social transmission, register structure, regular correspondence channels, and first productive
affiliation morphology from Early Imperial Balishan through the capital transfer and into modern
Balishan, Vartonnian, and Urathan. It does not yet supply a complete phonology or general lexicon for any
modern daughter.

## Scope and chronology

Imperial Balishan is the institutional continuation of the Surface-Halauric heartland continuum, not a
language created by decree in Year 0. Four overlapping stages matter:

| Stage | Dates | Linguistic center |
|---|---:|---|
| **Early Imperial Balishan** | 0–500 BR | Vartonne's river-and-market koine, formalized by Marresonnian scribes and the Valanar court |
| **Transferred Court Balishan** | beginning 500 BR | the court and portable government operating from Marresonne under the title *Kaha'an* |
| **Late Imperial Balishan** | approximately 500–750 BR | a coastal court standard beside a continuing common imperial koine and increasingly separate provincial norms |
| **Daughter period** | beginning with the secessions, 680 BR onward | modern Balishan/Kaha'ani, Vartonnian, Urathan, and other outcomes not yet assigned |

The stages overlap because institutions and speakers do. A Vartonnian archive does not change language on
the day the court leaves, and an Urathan scholar does not cease to speak Imperial Balishan on the day the
province declares independence.

## Early Imperial Balishan before the transfer

The first imperial koine takes its ordinary grammatical frame from Vartonne's Surface-Halauric
river-and-market speech: root-initial stress, subject–verb–object clauses, prepositions, cluster tolerance,
and substantial reduction of unstressed material. That frame spreads through supply, campaigning,
adjudication, trade, and service at the first capital.

Marresonne contributes from the beginning. Its merchants and copyists carry commercial formulas,
documentary spelling, coastal inherited vocabulary, and continuing access to Hieratic Old Halauri.
Valan's court adds portable titles, oaths, commands, and coronation language. Terinoki companies contribute
military vocabulary without surrendering their own internal languages or making Terinoki a Balishan
dialect.

By the fifth imperial century, **Imperial Balishan** can mean three related things:

1. the ordinary koine used across trade, army supply, travel, and mixed urban life;
2. the administrative standard used by officials whose household language may be different;
3. the court and learned register that renews selected forms from Hieratic Halauri.

They are not identical. A provincial clerk may write the administrative standard, speak a regional
Balishan variety to visitors, and use another language at home.

## What moved in 500 BR

The relocation begins under the legal and ceremonial fiction of a seasonal or temporary eastern court.
That fiction is useful: it lets the emperor answer maritime threats and follow trade without first
declaring that the empire's cosmological capital has failed. Successive emperors make the arrangement
permanent by continuing to govern from the coast.

The transfer is neither one household changing residence nor Vartonne emptying into Marresonne. It occurs
in waves:

| Group | Principal movement | Linguistic consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Imperial household, inner court, **Kaha Batae**, senior personal officers, and resident envoys | Move with the throne and remain around it | Court formulas and learned pronunciation become physically portable; *Kaha'an* transfers because the authority does |
| Foreign, maritime, customs, naval, and long-distance commercial offices | Move early or create coastal branches that become the working headquarters | Coastal vocabulary and merchant usage enter daily government rather than remaining a specialist register |
| Ambitious noble branches, petitioners, financiers, luxury artisans, tutors, clerks, servants, and suppliers | Follow access, appointments, and money across one to three generations | Families split by city; children raised at the coast acquire the new court speech natively |
| Hall of Records, land and grain administration, old courts, cathedral establishments, hereditary offices, estate managers, and much of the ordinary population | Remain in Vartonne | The old written norm, legal formulas, land vocabulary, and a self-conscious claim to correctness remain locally anchored |
| Merchants, messengers, scholars, military officers, and divided noble households | Continue moving between both cities | Mutual intelligibility and competing prestige survive; neither center becomes linguistically isolated |

The court does not bring enough people to replace Marresonne's population. It brings enough political and
economic weight to change which varieties meet in schools, offices, marriages, workshops, and ambitious
households. Marresonne becomes *Kaha'an* in public identity while its local speech becomes one parent of
the new capital's language.

## The transferred court koine

The result is not Vartonnian transplanted intact and not Marresonnian promoted unchanged. **Transferred
Court Balishan** keeps the common imperial grammar but changes its social and phonological center.

- **Grammar remains broadly Vartonnian-derived:** ordinary SVO clauses, prepositions, invariant
  modifiers, optional number where context supplies it, and productive direct compounds.
- **Pronunciation becomes coastal-facing:** inherited Marresonnian /s/ outcomes and fuller weak vowels
  enter prestigious urban speech beside western forms already present in the koine.
- **Spelling remains conservative and commercial:** Marresonnian scribal practice had helped form the
  chancery before the move, so the coastal court does not require a new script merely because the palace
  changes city.
- **Learned renewal intensifies:** the court now sits above the old Hauren interface and among the human
  traditions with the strongest history of copying and teaching Hieratic forms.
- **Port speech continually enters upward:** customs, credit, shipping, warehousing, foreign brokerage,
  and maritime law are central state business, so their vocabulary cannot be dismissed as dockside slang.

This explains why Late Imperial Balishan can sound coastal in ordinary use, western in inherited
administrative structures, and conspicuously Halauri in ceremony without being three languages pasted
together.

## The first controlled correspondence set

These channels were already established in the Surface-Halauric history. The imperial period turns them
into socially recognized alternatives inside one political language:

| Late Open source feature | Western/Vartonnian inheritance | Coastal/Marresonnian inheritance | Hieratic or learned renewal | Imperial consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **th** /θ/ | /t/ | /s/ | /θ/ restored | inherited western, inherited coastal, and learned words can form three historical layers rather than one irregular sound |
| **'** /ʔ/ | lost; hiatus repaired | lost; hiatus glided or contracted | restored in careful citation and titles | ordinary speech lacks the contrast, but *Kaha'an* and other instructed forms can preserve it |
| weak final **-a, -e, -o** | early reduction and deletion | longer retention, followed by variable loss | full vowel pronounced | Late Imperial speech accepts more final-vowel and final-consonant shapes than either source variety alone |
| unstressed medial vowel | stronger syncope and new clusters | less syncope where the resulting cluster is alien | full learned form | inherited compact words coexist with transparent court and technical formations |
| lexical stress | initial root stress | initial root stress, with more surviving weak syllables | Late Open penultimate stress | stress itself helps listeners identify a learned word even before they know its meaning |
| vowel hiatus | contraction or glide | glide, long vowel, or contraction | separate vowels retained | *batae* can remain visibly learned while ordinary inherited words repair the same sequence |

The table predicts doublets; it does not authorize generating them without semantic and historical
evidence. A coastal /s/ word and a learned /θ/ word are cognates only when a derivation demonstrates it.

## The first productive affiliation morphology

The existing realm and city forms preserve two different human systems of belonging. They are not
variants of one suffix:

| Form | Imperial history | Productive force |
|---|---|---|
| ***-an*** /an/ | a substrate-derived relational suffix productive by Early Imperial Balishan | relation to a polity or territorial people; by context an adjective, inhabitant term, collective people-name, or language-name |
| ***-i*** /i/ | a Marresonnian local-affiliation suffix carried into Transferred and Late Imperial capital speech | relation to a particular settlement or urban community; by context an inhabitant term, local cultural adjective, or city dialect/accent-name |

The polity-scale suffix attaches to the stem of a realm-name. After a base-final **a**, the two adjacent
low vowels coalesce and the spelling writes only one:

```text
Balisha + -an  >  *Balishaan  >  Balishan
Uratha  + -an  >  *Urathaan   >  Urathan
```

The suffix does not change category by changing shape. ***Balishan*** can mean “of Balisha,” “a person of
Balisha,” “the Balishan people,” or “the language of Balisha” because syntax and context supply the
category. ***Urathan*** behaves the same way. This is a polity relation, not a claim that citizenship,
ethnicity, birthplace, and first language must always coincide.

The eastern local suffix attaches transparently to the whole title ***Kaha'an***:

```text
Kaha'an + -i  >  Kaha'ani
```

Careful historical ***Kaha'ani*** is **kah-HAH-ah-nee**, /ka.ˈha.ʔa.ni/. The final /n/ of the title
becomes the onset of the new syllable, while the learned glottal stop and the title's stress remain.
Native city speech normally contracts this to **kah-HAH-nee**, /kaˈha.ni/. Formal historical
pronunciation and spelling retain the stop.

The semantic boundary is visible in the prose. A house, accent, or voice can be ***Kaha'ani*** because it
belongs specifically to the capital's urban culture. A crown policy, subject, army, or language can be
***Balishan*** when the intended scope is the realm. Many inhabitants are both; the forms answer different
questions.

The morphology also exposes a useful historical stack:

```text
kaha + -'an + -i
sovereignty + governing seat + urban affiliation
```

The two endings in ***Kaha'ani*** are unrelated. LOL bound ***-'an*** /ʔan/ is part of the inherited title
and means the seat in which authority is instantiated. Imperial ***-an*** /an/ is an ordinary human
polity-relational suffix. Late Imperial ***-i*** /i/ is the city-local suffix outside both of them.

The systems are productive but not automatic naming machines. A new demonym still requires a community
that actually uses it. ***Terinoki*** belongs to another language; ***Gunastran*** is an unanalyzed Old
Gunastran form; ***Erulian*** and ***Silarian*** belong to their own naming histories. ***Marresonnian***
and ***Vartonnian*** are established historical or language labels, but their visible **-ian** is not
analyzed by this test; prose-fixed ***Vartonnese*** is the ordinary demonym and cultural adjective. None
is evidence that Imperial ***-an*** or ***-i*** applies across the continent.

## The capital-title in living speech

The Hieratic source form is three syllables: ***Kaha'an***, **kah-HAH-an**, /ka.ˈha.ʔan/. It survives in
fixed coronation language, linguistic or Hieratic citation, and an Urathan classroom deliberately
reproducing the old title. It is not ordinary modern Kaha'ani pronunciation.

Ordinary human speech applies the sound history already active throughout Surface Halauric:

```text
/ka.ˈha.ʔan/  >  /ka.ˈha.an/  >  /ka.ˈhaːn/  >  /kaˈhan/
```

The native Kaha'ani result is ***Kaha'an***, **kah-HAN**, /kaˈhan/. Residents say the city-name too often
for the careful three-syllable form to survive as everyday speech. Some other varieties retain the
intermediate long vowel; the exact distribution of that variant remains for the daughter phonologies.

Origin changes the default, not the speaker's permanent capacity:

- **Native Kaha'ani speakers**, including educated and courtly residents, say /kaˈhan/ by default. The
  contraction is a local shibboleth precisely because the city-name is unavoidable.
- **Fixed ceremonial formulas and Collegiate citation** preserve /ka.ˈha.ʔan/ as an instructed historical
  reading. A Kaha'ani speaker can reproduce it without adopting it in ordinary conversation.
- **Western and other vernacular daughter varieties** favor contraction because loss of /ʔ/ and hiatus
  repair are already native rules, though educated speakers can restore the formal pronunciation.
- **Foreign speakers** may preserve or reduce the form according to which human variety taught it to
  them. Contracted pronunciation therefore does not prove local birth, but the full form in ordinary city
  conversation strongly suggests book, classroom, or foreign transmission.

The full form is historically correct but locally conspicuous outside those fixed registers—the city's
equivalent of pronouncing every visible syllable in a name residents have long compressed. It can reveal
that a speaker learned *Kaha'an* from a book, foreign teacher, or formal court tradition before hearing
Kaha'ani residents say it. ***Ka'han*** is a pronunciation aid, not a replacement spelling; the written
title remains ***Kaha'an***.

## Register architecture after the move

### Common Imperial Balishan

The widest shared koine remains the language of interregional trade, administration, travel, and mixed
military service. It favors common SVO order, prepositions, explicit vocabulary where provincial speakers
might otherwise misunderstand one another, and relatively little court ornament. It survives political
dissolution as a continuum of mutually accessible daughter varieties.

### Kaha'ani court and commercial speech

The new capital's prestige speech is shaped by proximity to the throne and the harbor at once. A noble
cannot govern without clerks, customs officials, creditors, shipowners, warehouse factors, and people who
know how goods actually move. Their usage enters the court because it is the language of the work the
court now performs.

Formal Kaha'ani remains capable of full learned pronunciation. Ordinary urban speech is faster to reduce
high-frequency formulas, accept foreign loans, and discard distinctions that documents retain. The
contrast is register, not education versus ignorance: the same speaker can use both.

### Vartonnian documentary and ceremonial speech

Vartonne keeps the Hall of Records, old legal offices, land machinery, ritual calendar, and physical rooms
in which the first five centuries of empire were administered. Its officials preserve verb-final clauses,
old final role relics, and inherited formulas longest **in documents and ceremony**.

Spoken Vartonnian does not freeze. It continues western syncope, final-vowel loss, and ordinary SVO syntax.
Its conservatism is institutional: it preserves what the old forms authorize, sometimes while pronouncing
them in ways no early emperor would recognize. This distinction prevents “conservative” from meaning that
Vartonne somehow avoided five centuries of language change.

## The first daughter split

### Modern Balishan and urban Kaha'ani

**Modern Balishan** is the national and administrative descendant centered on the surviving capital.
**Kaha'ani** is the urban dialect and cultural adjective of Kaha'an. In many contexts the same speaker can
be called both; the terms differ in scope rather than naming unrelated languages.

Modern Balishan inherits:

- the common imperial SVO and prepositional grammar;
- coastal /s/ outcomes in genuinely local inherited vocabulary;
- western compact forms already spread through the old koine;
- learned court words with full vowels, restored /θ/ or /ʔ/, and penultimate stress;
- maritime, commercial, legal, refugee, and Warrens vocabulary from communities that do not contribute
  equally to the prestige standard.

The Rending creates intense **dialect leveling**, not a new language in twenty years. Survivors from the
destroyed province crowd into Kaha'an; adults retain recognizable pre-Rending regional habits, while
children and mixed neighborhoods favor forms understood across the refugee population. The Warrens
develop occupational and neighborhood varieties under extreme density and separation. Court and noble
speech remain more continuous because their institutions, tutors, and records survive inside the shielded
city.

### Modern Vartonnian

**Vartonnian** names the language or speech variety; **Vartonnese** remains the ordinary demonym and broad
cultural adjective for people and institutions of Vartonne.

Vartonnian continues the western branch with stronger syncope, earlier final-vowel loss, and western /t/
outcomes in inherited material. Its formal written register preserves more Early Imperial formulas than
modern Kaha'ani does, while its ordinary spoken phonology can be **less** conservative. This is precisely
why a Vartonnian official may consider his usage purer even when a linguist would identify multiple local
innovations.

Vartonnian and modern Balishan remain closely related and normally mutually intelligible. Difficulty rises
in archival bureaucracy, ritual recitation, rural vocabulary, and speech performed to emphasize national
difference.

### Urathan

Urathan descends from the Imperial Balishan used by the academy community before secession. Until the
academy's founding date and local population are settled, it should not be assigned exclusively to the
Vartonnian or Kaha'ani daughter branch. Its imperial ancestry is secure; its precise regional input is not.

The first Urathan correspondences are therefore register contrasts rather than unsupported regional sound
laws:

| Vernacular Urathan | Collegiate Urathan |
|---|---|
| inherited root-initial stress | restored Late Open penultimate stress in learned words |
| ordinary loss of /ʔ/ and repaired hiatus | /ʔ/ and separate vowels restored in citation and formal coinage |
| ordinary SVO clauses and prepositions | verb-final clauses and old relational patterns cultivated in definitions, findings, and ceremony |
| inherited Imperial vocabulary | revived roots, semantic calques, and new compounds built from Hieratic material |

Collegiate Urathan is therefore an **archaizing overlay on a daughter language**, not evidence that Urathan
split directly from native Halauri. A Kaha'ani visitor can understand a market conversation and still
struggle with a Provost's address because the social register, not just the accent, has changed.

### Other former provinces

Terinoki is not a Balishan daughter merely because Terinok participated in the empire. Gunastran, Erulian,
Silarian, and other provincial languages may retain Imperial Balishan loans, administrative registers, or
bilingual communities without sharing its genealogy. Each requires its own culture-first reconstruction.

## Existing-form tests

| Form | What this stage history explains |
|---|---|
| **Vartonne** | western final-vowel loss and conservative documentary **-onne** can coexist |
| **Marresonne** | the coastal inherited name predates the court and disappears socially after the title transfer, not through a sound change |
| **Kaha'an** | a whole learned title moves with the throne and retains /ʔ/ despite ordinary loss of the contrast |
| **Balisha / Balishan** | the realm stem takes productive polity-relational *-an*; final *a + a* coalesces regularly |
| **Uratha / Urathan** | the imperial foundation-name takes the same inherited daughter-language suffix without pretending the whole name is ancient Halauri |
| **Kaha'ani** | whole-title *Kaha'an* takes the eastern city-local suffix *-i*, preserving the older internal glottal boundary |
| **Mira Fen / Master Fen / Xion Fen** | unchanged substrate *fen* supplies craft and zero-derived practitioner; three social constructions use the same common noun without turning it into a family |
| **Master Fen / Mistress Janice / Master Keelen / Mistress Keya** | unchanged substrate *sar* precedes a name or byname as ordinary respectful adult address; English alone divides it into a gendered pair |
| **healer / physician / clinic / apothecary** | substrate *len* supplies broad healing and healer; learned LOL *sathen* supplies physician; reduced site *-um* distinguishes *lenum* clinic and *fenum* apothecary workplace from practitioners |
| **household / House / Larannas** | substrate *dal* names the domestic body; mixed *seldal* adds learned legal *sel-* to name a chartered House; fossil *-annas* preserves an old patronymic lineage formation |
| **Hadris** | compact native-Balishan House name /ˈhad.ris/ fits the koine without pretending to share Valanar's prestige morphology |
| **Kaha Batae / Arol Batae** | human learned formations retain full vowels and penultimate stress across the transfer and Rending |
| **Arol Rhutan** | a pre-imperial Marresonnian hybrid enters imperial records unchanged |
| **Xion** | a learned Marresonnian–Hieratic spelling can belong naturally to modern Balishan rather than sounding foreign to it |

No protected spelling changes, and no speculative root from the inherited naming guide becomes canonical
through this test.

## What remains open

- the founding date, site, and local population of Urath IV's academy;
- the exact location and mother tongue of Valan I before Vartonne;
- the full phoneme inventories and productive morphology beyond the two affiliation suffixes, ordinary
  *sar* honorific, controlled medical forms, and household/chartered-House distinction established here;
- actual inherited/learned doublets beyond the controlled settlement and imperial clusters;
- the chancery script, spelling reforms, and degree of literacy outside institutional work;
- the internal history of refugee and Warrens varieties after the Rending;
- whether the throne-name pool expands to include the off-screen historical forms *Aurelan, Tiberan,* and
  *Varellian*, or whether any P3 bearer is renamed.

The next closed lexical test should use another corpus cluster with separately visible meanings and social
routes. No broad kinship or specialist medical list should be generated merely because *dal, seldal, len,
lenum, fenum,* and *sathen* now exist.
