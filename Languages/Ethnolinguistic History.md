# Ethnolinguistic History

**Status:** Historical and cultural foundation, begun 2026-08-21; linguistic mechanics reset 2026-08-22.
The chronology, contact, migration, institutions, and deep Halauri ancestry of Balishan remain the
design brief. Sounds, roots, grammar, etymologies, and named “tests” below belong to the superseded
corpus-accommodating reconstruction unless an approved design plate later recovers them.

The existing corpus is catalogued in `Attested Forms Register.md`. Its exposure tiers measure the blast
radius of later conformance and the tiered resistance a form receives. P0 forms are mandatory; P1 forms
are preserved whenever they do not contort the design; P2–P4 forms do not constrain it.

**Superseded first reconstruction:** the first pass proposed contact-era sound systems, ordered daughter
changes, a minimum grammar, and six controlled reconstruction tests. `Late Open Halauri.md` defines the taught public register;
`Ancestral Continental.md` defines the human substrate; `Early Surface Halauric.md` orders their
interaction; and `Halauric Core Lexicon.md` derives the settlement layer, confirmed imperial cluster,
ordinary substrate *fen* family, genderless substrate honorific *sar*, and the first mixed medical
register: substrate *len/lenum/fenum* beside learned *sathen*. The sixth test distinguishes substrate
*dal* “household” from mixed Imperial *seldal* “chartered House” and establishes fossil patronymic
*-annas*. These are retained design candidates, not a foundation the new language must preserve.

## Design premise

Languages in Elvandar are products of communities moving through time. Culture does not mechanically
determine word order or consonants, but it does determine who speaks with whom, what kinds of speech carry
prestige, who can write, what institutions preserve an older standard, what vocabulary travels, and which
forms survive after political power moves elsewhere.

The first reconstruction therefore begins with two histories already central to the series:

- **Hauren:** a civilization built roughly five thousand years ago, open to the surface for nearly three
  millennia, sealed for roughly two millennia, and alive until the Rending twenty years before Book 1.
- **Early Balisha:** a trade-based human empire founded in 0 BR, first governed from Vartonne, relocated to
  Kaha'an in 500 BR, and broken into successor states between 680 and 750 BR.

The empire itself never overlaps living surface diplomacy with Hauren: the city sealed roughly a
millennium before the Balishan Empire was founded. Its people nevertheless inherit Halauri speech as
well as knowledge, texts, and prestige. Human communities nativized contact varieties during the open
period; their Surface-Halauric descendants continued changing after the sealing and became the
pre-imperial heartland varieties from which Imperial Balishan formed. The empire did not learn its language
from living Halauri diplomats of its own period.

## Fixed chronological anchors

| Period | Event | Linguistic consequence already licensed by canon |
|---|---|---|
| ~5,000 years before Book 1 | Hauren is built beneath the future site of Kaha'an | The oldest inscriptions need not match any later Halauri stage |
| ~5,000–2,000 years before Book 1 | Hauren maintains open surface relations | Human communities acquire and eventually nativize contact Halauri while retaining an older surface substrate |
| ~2,000 years before Book 1 | Hauren seals itself and commissions the human custodial order ancestral to the Long Knives | Surface Halauric, Hieratic Old Halauri, and the living language inside Hauren follow separate histories; one institutional mandate survives while its original Halauri name and explanation disappear |
| ~1,044 years before Book 1 / 0 BR | The Balishan Empire is founded | A living Surface-Halauric heartland koine becomes institutional; learned Old Halauri remains a separate source of prestige forms |
| 500 BR | The imperial capital moves from Vartonne to the coastal city that takes the title Kaha'an | Court, chancery, and mercantile usage can begin to diverge by region and allegiance |
| 680–750 BR | The provinces secede and the empire dissolves | A former administrative standard can become several national standards, dialects, or prestige registers |
| 1024 BR | The Rending; Hauren ends | Terminal Halauri becomes a language with no surviving native community |
| 1044 BR | Book 1 begins | Modern speech exists twenty years after both demographic catastrophe and political isolation in Balisha |

The dates above describe the settled route of descent and the opportunities for particular forms to
travel. They do not by themselves determine whether an individual word is inherited Halauric, retained
from the Ancestral Continental substrate, or borrowed later from a learned standard.

## Hauren

### What the culture already gives us

Hauren was a city of approximately five hundred thousand elves, large enough to sustain social,
occupational, and neighborhood variation. Its institutions valued knowledge preservation, technical
precision, long planning horizons, controlled access, and systems designed to operate beyond an individual
life. Its open-period diplomacy and trade exposed surface communities to Halauri learning. Its sealed
period did not stop social life: generations were born, lived, and died in the city while its instruments
continued to observe the world above.

Those facts support several linguistic expectations without yet dictating a grammar:

- durable written traditions and specialized technical registers;
- vocabulary for classification, measurement, maintenance, and long institutional time;
- a distinction between language meant for insiders and language used with surface visitors;
- conservative written material existing beside ordinary spoken change;
- occupational or institutional varieties across a city with seven highly specialized levels;
- deliberate terminology created during the months in which the Destiny Engine was built.

They do **not** establish that every Halauri person spoke identically, that every inhabitant was literate,
that the language changed slowly, or that precision in engineering produced a grammatically "precise"
language. Those questions require cultural decisions rather than aesthetic inference.

**The language-facing cultural model is settled in `Halauri Culture.md`.** The Great Shaft, Arrival Halls,
and Diplomatic Quarter supported a permanent surface interface; Crown officials, interpreters, teachers,
copyists, merchants, technical instructors, apprentices, envoys, traders, and refugees used it. A taught
contact register became a human lingua franca and then a mother tongue in some communities. Hauren's
broad functional civic literacy coexisted with restricted technical and Repository access, and its seven
levels produced occupational and neighborhood variation within a shared civic language.

### Historical stages

#### 1. Foundational Halauri

The language of the city's founding centuries, roughly five thousand years before Book 1. It should not be
treated as identical to the language spoken at the end of the open period. The oldest civic inscriptions,
religious material, institutional names, and inherited technical roots may preserve this stage long after
ordinary speech has moved on.

Before reconstruction, establish where the founding population came from, whether Hauren was their only
city, what political or religious project led them underground, and whether other elven languages existed.

#### 2. Open-period Halauri

Nearly three thousand years of contact cannot be represented by a single frozen variety. At minimum the
period needs early, middle, and late stages. Surface borrowing can therefore occur at different dates and
produce apparently inconsistent forms that are actually chronological layers.

Late Open Halauri may become the basis of a learned surface standard after the sealing, but the spoken
and written traditions on the surface would continue to acquire local pronunciation, semantic drift,
copying errors, school conventions, and new coinages. A liturgical or scholarly standard can be
conservative; it cannot literally stop having a history.

The open period also had a contact register: the taught form used in diplomacy, instruction, and trade was
based on the civic language but did not reproduce the full range of Halauri household speech.

**Settled 2026-08-22:** some human communities nativized those contact varieties. Their children did not
merely speak Halauri as a foreign prestige code; they acquired Surface Halauric community languages,
reshaped by the older Ancestral Continental speech already present. This produces the branch ancestral to
Proto-Balishan. The shift may have happened in several places and periods rather than as one event. It was
driven by access to trade, apprenticeships, technical instruction, scholarship, and a shared language for
human communities that did not otherwise speak alike, not by Halauri conquest or compulsory policy.

#### 3. Sealed Halauri

For roughly two thousand years the city continues without direct surface contact. This is not simply
"modern Halauri" waiting at the far end of a time gap. It has its own internal periods, social changes,
and possible attempts at language planning. The city's strong archives may keep old texts unusually
accessible without preventing spoken change.

How far Sealed Halauri diverges depends on facts not yet settled: elven lifespan, generation length,
education, population movement between levels, attitudes toward correction and innovation, and whether
the sealing centralized or fractured civic life. Intelligibility cannot responsibly be assigned a
"Chaucer" or "different language" analogy until those mechanisms exist.

#### 4. Crisis and Terminal Halauri

The final months produce a compressed emergency register: planning documents, warnings, technical
compounds, public instructions, farewells, and terminology for a device and metaphysical operation that
had never existed before. The Destiny Engine's visibly hasty construction should have a linguistic
counterpart. Its labels and records need not resemble the polished institutional naming of the older city.

Terminal Halauri is the ordinary living language spoken on the day the civilization ended. No native
speakers survive. Elara's impressions do not make her fluent, and author knowledge of this stage must not
be laundered into Xion's narration or a scholar's comprehension.

### The former name *Lathion* and the approved *Hauren* suite

*Lathion* was a temporary project label and an author-authorized replacement target. Its former Book 3
appearance gave the rename a conformance cost, not linguistic protection. No part of the retired word is
evidence for Foundational or Open-period phonology.

The replacement began with the founding event: the original population named a deliberate underground
civic project before the later sealing, catastrophe, and surface legends existed. That source must develop
through roughly three millennia of native change before a late-open form reaches human contact speech. The
reader-facing legendary name may therefore differ from the Foundational endonym and from whatever Terminal
speakers called home two thousand years after contact ended. Coin and approve that chain before replacing
the working label in prose. That controlled conformance pass is now complete.

The approved native suite, *Halauren / Halauri*, adds one bounded historical proposition: the founders
understood the first settlement as refuge—“Life Under Shelter.” It does **not** identify the danger or make
the sealing three thousand years later a continuation of the same event. No Halauri survive, no reliable
surface account reaches the founding, and the missing cause remains deliberately unavailable. The word
spoken in Book 3 will be a human vernacular descendant of this name, not *Halauren* itself. Surface
Halauric Bridge Plate 1 generates approved **Hauren / Haureni** for ordinary Balishan, while a
manuscript-preserved *Halauren* may coexist as a learned historical doublet.

### Cultural decisions still required

- Elven lifespan, adulthood, and generation length
- Exact family, inheritance, and household structure
- Hauren's constitution and formal offices during the open and sealed periods
- Religion, death practice, and the cultural meaning of the world's "heart"
- Documentary and workshop hands, punctuation, spelling depth, exact approved glyph forms, public writing
  direction, and the historical development of the authorized monumental runic/operative tradition
- Whether Hauren was one city among other elven communities or the final center of a wider people
- The exact breadth of bilingualism and permanent cross-boundary residence during surface contact
- The internal periods of sealed speech and the strength of prescriptive movements after withdrawal

The reason for the sealing remains a genuine mystery in canon. A language history may show consequences
of the sealing but must not accidentally answer why it happened.

## Early Balisha and the empire

### What the culture already gives us

The Balishan Empire unified disparate regions through trade, bureaucracy, infrastructure, military power,
and a shared system of administration. Its first capital was Vartonne; after five centuries the court
moved to the coastal city that took the transferable title *Kaha'an*. The move was followed within two
centuries by secession, and the old standard survived in successor states that defined themselves partly
through their relationship to imperial legitimacy.

This makes a single continent-wide "Imperial Balishan language" possible only as a qualified term. It
could name a chancery standard, trade koine, court register, educational norm, native vernacular, or some
combination of them. It should not silently imply that the empire erased the languages of all seven
regions.

### Historical stages

#### 1. Pre-imperial heartland

Before 0 BR, human communities already occupied the future imperial heartland and the wider continent.
`Halauric Core Lexicon.md` now demonstrates the old naming corridor. LOL *onna* “established settlement”
appears after CON *vart* “confluence, meeting-center” in mixed western *Vartonne* and after LOL *marreth*
“sheltered deep-water sea-harbor” in regularly inherited coastal *Marresonne*. They are sister-settlements
through a shared borrowed head and scribal convention, not proof of one uniform pre-imperial language.

**Settled 2026-08-21:** the settlement and name *Vartonne* predate the empire. Valan I Valanar established his
permanent camp at that settlement in 0 BR; the camp absorbed the town and became the planned concentric
capital. He founded imperial Vartonne, not human habitation on the site. The older name, riverside
communities and surrounding rites survived beneath the monumental city and re-emerged politically when the
title *Kaha'an* moved away in 500 BR. Full foundation: `Balishan.md`.

The pre-imperial period is also where living contact with Open Hauren and the ancestry of Balishan belong.
Human Surface-Halauric varieties descend from contact speech nativized before the sealing. Direct
borrowings, substrate retentions, and ordinary inherited Halauric forms can therefore coexist long before
the empire standardizes one heartland koine. CON *fen* “prepare plant remedies; herbal medicine as skilled
work” is the first established ordinary retention: its stressed CVC shape survives the shift unchanged and
remains productive as activity, craft, and practitioner noun in Balishan. CON *sar* is the first
established social retention: the same unchanged CVC title precedes names and bynames for ordinary adult
respect, while English alone renders it as the gendered pair *Master/Mistress*. CON *len* and practical
site *-um* produce *lenum* “clinic” beside *fenum* “apothecary workplace,” while learned LOL *sathen*
survives through technical teaching as “physician.” The medical register therefore preserves the same
ordinary-substrate/learned-Halauri layering as the wider language.

The House register repeats that layering in law. CON *dal* retains the intimate human category of the
functioning household, while LOL legal *sel-* enters Imperial speech in mixed *seldal*, the
crown-recognized body translated as capitalized *House*. The language thereby keeps blood, membership,
public name, heirship, and office separable rather than treating every second name as a biological claim.
Fossil *-annas* in *Larannas* preserves an older patronymic line without remaining productive.

#### 2. Vartonne imperial period, 0–500 BR

The first five centuries contain the empire's largest language-policy questions. A trade empire can spread
a merchant koine without displacing local household languages; a bureaucracy can require a written
standard from officials without making it the native speech of their subjects; a dynasty can adopt an
older prestige language for titles while governing in another tongue.

**Valan I Valanar founded the empire in 0 BR through a coalition, not an already completed continental
conquest.** His following supplied command and arbitration; Vartonne supplied the river, agricultural, and
market base of the first capital; Marresonne supplied merchants, scribal practice, and inherited
Halauri learning. Valan won Terinok's blades by demonstrating martial worth to its independent cantons,
which joined without surrendering the Warrior's Code or internal sovereignty. Vartonne itself remained an
older settlement absorbed and rebuilt by Valan I's camp. Full cultural foundation: `Early Balishan
Culture.md`.

The resulting language can begin as Vartonnian river-and-market speech broadened into a trade and campaign
koine, then made institutional through court, chancery, and commercial use. Marresonnian practice can
shape its written and learned registers. Terinoki forces need not shift household languages: their internal
commands remain cantonal while selected martial terms travel into Imperial Balishan.

#### 3. Kaha'an imperial period, 500–680 BR

**Settled 2026-08-22:** the capital transfer is selective and multi-generational. The imperial household,
Kaha Batae, resident envoys, portable government, maritime and foreign offices, ambitious noble branches,
financiers, clerks, artisans, servants, suppliers, and other people whose position depends on access follow
the throne. Vartonne retains the Hall of Records, land and grain machinery, old courts, cathedral
establishments, hereditary offices, estate administration, and most ordinary residents.

The move establishes four interacting linguistic centers:

- the court and chancery in Kaha'an;
- the mercantile port population of former Marresonne;
- Vartonne's conservative old-capital elite;
- provincial administrations using the standard through local speech.

Transferred Court Balishan retains the common imperial SVO and prepositional frame while Marresonnian /s/
outcomes, fuller weak vowels, port vocabulary, and intensified Hieratic renewal enter prestige speech.
Vartonne preserves old syntax and role relics most strongly in documents and ceremony, even as its spoken
language continues western reduction. Difference in accent, vocabulary, etiquette, and claims of
correctness therefore predates formal dissolution. Full model: `Imperial Balishan.md`.

#### 4. Dissolution, 680–750 BR

The secessions do not automatically create new languages in seventy years. They create new institutions
with reasons to standardize existing regional varieties, purge or retain imperial terminology, and rename
offices. Each successor state's language history must distinguish political independence from linguistic
divergence already underway.

#### 5. Successor period, 750–1044 BR

For almost three centuries the former provinces develop separate courts, schools, militaries, trade
networks, and national myths. Uratha deliberately re-Halaurizes Collegiate speech; Vartonne preserves
ceremonial and documentary forms while its vernacular innovates; Kaha'an develops a commercial coastal
standard. Erulius may cultivate the language of legitimacy; Gunastran and Terinok may retain unrelated
native structures while borrowing selected imperial vocabulary.

The Rending at 1024 BR then transforms Balisha in a single generation. Refugees, lost towns, concentrated
urban populations, and new social divisions produce dialect leveling inside modern Kaha'ani rather than a
new language in twenty years. Older speakers retain distinctions children born afterward may level; the
Warrens develop dense neighborhood and occupational varieties. Court speech changes less because its
institutions, records, and teachers survive inside the shield.

The surviving names preserve the scale of those identities. Imperial polity-relational ***-an*** derives
*Balishan* and *Urathan*; the eastern local suffix ***-i*** derives *Kaha'ani* for the capital's people,
culture, and speech. A Kaha'ani can therefore also be Balishan without contradiction. LOL bound *-'an*
inside *Kaha'an* is an older and unrelated governing-seat head.

### The Urathan branch — settled 2026-08-21

Modern Urathan descends from **Imperial Balishan** and, through it, from human Surface Halauric. It is not
a sister language of Proto-Balishan and not a survival of an otherwise unrelated "Old Urathan" family. Its distinct history begins before political
independence: an imperial academy became a permanent scholarly community, then a city and province, giving
its speech time to acquire a regional and institutional identity while Uratha was still Balishan territory.

Uratha seceded in 695 BR. By the opening of Book 1 in 1044 BR, it has had **349 years as a sovereign state**
with its own schools, examinations, administration, class system, and national standard. That is ample time
for the relationship to resemble Canadian and European French, or Mexican and Peninsular Spanish: plainly
members of the same language, normally mutually intelligible, but no longer interchangeable in accent,
idiom, technical vocabulary, etiquette, or judgments about what sounds educated.

The comparison must include innovation as well as conservation. Urathan scholars preserve more imperial
and Old Halauri material than ordinary Balishan speakers do, especially in institutional language, and
they **deliberately re-Halaurize** formal speech: reviving roots from old texts, preferring learned forms,
and building new compounds in an ancient style because antiquity carries dignity and institutional
authority. Everyday Urathan has meanwhile spent centuries changing on its own. Its academy register,
common vernacular, and bakarn speech should not be flattened into one voice. The register system is
developed in `Urathan.md`.

The academy's founding date is not yet fixed. Until it is, 349 years is the **minimum** age of independent
development, not the total age of the Urathan branch. An older local substrate is possible if the academy
grew among a pre-existing population, but it is not required and should not be invented before the founding
history is placed.

## The contact chain

The historically licensed model now contains descent and repeated learned contact:

```text
Open Halauri varieties
├── metropolitan speech ── sealing ──► Sealed and Terminal Halauri
├── textual/contact standard ────────► Hieratic Old Halauri
└── human-nativized contact speech
        + Ancestral Continental substrate
        └──► Surface Halauric
              └──► Proto-Balishan heartland varieties
                    └──► Imperial Balishan
                          └──► modern daughter languages

Hieratic Old Halauri repeatedly lends learned forms into the spoken branch.
```

This model permits doublets: one Halauri root might reach modern speech by ordinary inheritance, return
through an old religious or legal text, and arrive again through an Urathan or Gunastran technical
tradition, producing several forms whose kinship is no longer obvious. It also permits false archaism: a
surface scholar's "correct Old Halauri" may be a human school tradition that no terminal Halauri
speaker would have recognized as natural speech.

Gunastran's magitech inheritance is the clearest canonical reason to develop this chain. Its technical
vocabulary may preserve Halauri concepts through several intermediary languages while its grammar and
ordinary lexicon remain unrelated.

## Existing model under review

`Languages of Elvandar.md` supplies a useful inherited sketch, but it predates this culture-first pass. Its
authorial naming rulings remain binding. Its proposed family relationships, etymologies, and phonological
signatures are working hypotheses until tested here.

Three internal seams found in the inherited guide have now been corrected:

1. **Urathan genealogy:** authorial ruling, 2026-08-21 — Urathan descends from Imperial Balishan. "Old
   Urathan" can name an earlier historical stage of that branch, not a Proto-Balishan sister language.
2. **Surface Old Halauri:** a written norm may be deliberately conserved, but the people teaching,
   pronouncing, copying, and repurposing it continue to make history. The guide now distinguishes the
   textual standard from its living surface traditions.
3. **Balishan genealogy:** authorial ruling, 2026-08-22 — Proto-Balishan and Imperial Balishan descend from
   human-nativized Surface Halauric. An older human stratum remains as Ancestral Continental substrate;
   the former model of unrelated human grammar with only Halauri prestige vocabulary is retired.

No existing etymology is discarded merely because it is provisional. Each will be tested against the
culture, chronology, protected names, and attested prose before it is retained or replaced.
