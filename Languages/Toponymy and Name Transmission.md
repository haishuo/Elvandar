# Toponymy and Name Transmission

**Status:** Active provenance ledger, established 2026-08-22  
**Authority:** Historical naming events and plate dependencies; it does not protect P2–P4 spellings

Place-names are historical objects. A city can keep one continuous civic identity while its name passes
through several languages, spellings, pronunciations, and political interpretations. The task is therefore
not to invent a modern label and write a convenient gloss beneath it. For every important place, determine:

1. who first needed to name it;
2. what the referent was at that moment;
3. which language and register supplied the source form;
4. which later communities borrowed or inherited it;
5. what regular sound changes acted on it;
6. which modern endonyms and foreign exonyms coexist;
7. what form, if any, reader-facing English actually prints.

The real-world pattern illustrated by Greek *Massalia*, Roman *Massilia*, and later Marseille is the model:
continuity of place does not imply continuity of sound. Each community receives a name through its own ears
and phonology. Elvandar's names should carry the same accumulated history.

## Protection ladder

- **P0:** the attested outcome is immovable. Construct whatever legitimate historical layering is needed
  to reach it. P0 can require a loan phoneme, fossil spelling, learned pronunciation, irregular survival,
  or source language unlike the modern local tongue.
- **P1:** avoid change whenever a natural route exists. Inheritance, borrowing, exonymy, register, and
  ordinary sound change are natural routes; a chain of unique exceptions invented solely to rescue one
  spelling is contortion. P1 loses before the language does.
- **P2–P4:** ignore the present sound-form while designing. Once the source and receiving languages exist,
  generate the historically correct name. Keep the old form only if it happens to belong.

A culturally wrong register is affirmative reason to replace a P2–P4 form. An English-looking woodland
name inside a Terinoki naming system is not made plausible merely by already appearing in a dossier.

## The name-chain record

Every derived important place-name receives a compact record in this form:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| **Referent** | What physical or political thing did the first name denote? |
| **Naming event** | Who coined or adopted the name, and why did they need one? |
| **Source form** | What was the earliest recoverable form, with morphology and pronunciation? |
| **Transmission stages** | Which languages inherited, borrowed, translated, or deliberately revived it? |
| **Sound changes** | Which regular changes produce each later form? |
| **Modern forms** | What do local speakers, neighboring peoples, scholars, and officials call it? |
| **Reader form** | Is prose printing an endonym, an exonym, or a transparent English translation? |
| **Protection** | Which part is P0/P1, and which parts remain free? |

No arrow in a chain can mean “and then the spelling drifted.” Each step must name the community and the
sound change or translation that produced it.

## Names are attached to referents, not map pins

One location can carry several different names because political referents overlap:

- the landform or harbor;
- the original settlement;
- the planned imperial city built over or around it;
- a transferable office or capital-title;
- the modern municipality;
- the surrounding province or state;
- a foreign exonym used by neighbors.

This is especially important at Kaha'an. The protected form is a transferable capital-title, not
necessarily the oldest name of the harbor, settlement, or ground beneath it. Moving the title does not
move the landform, and acquiring the title can suppress an older civic name without erasing its history.

## Plate dependency order

Place-name needs set the next language work in this order:

1. **Late Open Halauri Plate 1 — working.** The first proposal now produces the source material and
   registers capable of reaching P0 *Kaha'an* and *Arol Batae*. It does not enter the dependency chain
   until authorial approval.
2. **Foundational city-name Plate 1.** Establish the founding referent and a minimal older sound layer,
   generate the original name, and test its regular development into Late Open speech. *Hauren* is not a
   target.
3. **Surface-Halauric transmission and pre-imperial western/coastal stages.** This is the historical bridge
   into the approved EIB plate and the source for the oldest heartland settlements.
4. **Modern Balishan/Kaha'ani and Vartonnian daughters.** Modern Balishan Plate 1 remains generally working;
   Plate 2 has canonized the bounded lost-province suite. Vartonnian and the rest of the modern Balishan
   system remain to be judged.
5. **Terinoki Plate 1.** Book 3's cantons, Takama, and mountain landscape make this the first independent
   non-Halauric language needed at scale.
6. **Urathan Plate 1 plus its Collegiate extension.** Ordinary daughter-language change comes first;
   deliberate re-Halaurization is then built as a productive scholarly register rather than cosmetic Latin.
7. **Erulian Plate 1.** It must adjudicate two P1 forms and replace several free city labels.
8. **Old/Modern Gunastran plates.** They must produce the industrial state's endonyms independently of the
   P1 foreign form and replace *Mechanus* if it fails.
9. **Silarian Plate 1.** Its foundation history begins from an imperial name but its entire current native
   naming corpus is free.
10. **Frozen North languages** only when a story, character, or route requires an actual word.

This order is a dependency graph, not a promise to fill every dictionary. A plate is built when it must
produce a needed name or explain a protected one.
