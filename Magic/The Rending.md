# The Rending: Theories and Realities

## The Observable Facts

Twenty years ago (1024 BR), a catastrophic magical event known as The Rending transformed the once-fertile province surrounding Kaha'an into a vast desert. The following facts are widely agreed upon by witnesses and historians:

- The event began without warning. An initial wave of death struck and spread with terrible speed, scouring the province around Kaha'an in a matter of hours.
- This first wave, however, was not the whole of it. The desert continued to advance afterward — slowly, league by league, for the better part of a year — before its expansion finally ceased.
- Emperor Tiberan Valanar IV, Empress Lyra Valanar, and Lady Kemvimore of House Kemvimore were beyond the city walls when the Rending struck. None of the three were ever seen again, and no remains were recovered.
- Kaha'an itself survived, protected by ancient imperial wards, though witnesses agree the force of the event nearly overwhelmed them.
- The area now known as the Gray Wastes was the apparent epicenter, suffering the most complete devastation.
- Plant and animal life died almost instantly throughout the path of the first wave.
- Water sources dried up or became corrupted and undrinkable.
- Survivors describe a "wave of death" expanding outward from the epicenter — and, in the months that followed, a second, slower dying as the desert itself crept after them.
- The devastation was not uniform — some areas were completely destroyed while others were merely damaged. The first wave was visibly slowed and turned aside by mountain ranges and major rivers.
- The slow advance, when it finally stopped, stopped *abruptly* — halting mid-terrain, against no natural boundary at all, and it has not moved since.

Beyond these basic observations, accounts begin to diverge significantly, and the true nature of The Rending remains one of Elvandar's greatest mysteries.

## Official Explanations

The various successor states have each developed their own "official" explanation for The Rending, usually one that aligns with their political interests and cultural biases.

### Balisha's Official Narrative

The four cartels of Kaha'an promote a carefully crafted explanation:

- The Rending was a natural magical catastrophe, similar to earthquakes but on a magical scale
- Such events, while rare, have precedent in ancient historical records (a claim Urathan scholars dispute)
- The narrative presents the disaster as the work of a single terrible day, quietly omitting the year-long advance of the desert that followed — for a slow and partly survivable death raises questions that an act of nature does not
- The imperial wards protecting Kaha'an demonstrate the city's special status and legitimacy
- The survival of the city amid such devastation proves its divine favor and rightful place as the true imperial successor
- The loss of the Emperor and Empress was a tragic coincidence, not directly connected to the cause
- Research into The Rending is discouraged as "unnecessarily dwelling on past tragedies"

This narrative serves the cartels by discouraging investigation while reinforcing Kaha'an's claims to imperial legitimacy. It also conveniently avoids assigning any responsibility to Kaha'ani citizens or authorities.

### Uratha's Academic Theories

The scholars of Uratha have developed more complex theories, though they remain largely speculative:

- The Rending was likely anthropogenic — caused by human magical activity rather than natural forces
- Evidence suggests a massive spell or ritual gone catastrophically wrong
- The pattern of devastation indicates a single point of origin rather than a natural phenomenon
- A minority of scholars argue that the *abruptness* with which the desert's advance halted — cleanly, mid-terrain — cannot be natural exhaustion, and points to a deliberate counter-working by some unknown hand
- Ancient texts describe similar, smaller-scale magical accidents with comparable effects
- The exact spell or ritual remains unknown, as does the identity of the responsible party
- Current theories focus on failed attempts at weather control, planar manipulation, or mass transmutation

Urathan scholars continue to debate these theories, with new papers published regularly. However, their research is hampered by limited access to the affected areas and the politically sensitive nature of their findings.

### Terinok's Martial Interpretation

The warrior culture of Terinok has developed an explanation reflecting their values:

- The Rending was a deliberate attack by unknown enemies seeking to weaken the empire
- This attack succeeded because the Balishan Empire had grown soft and complacent
- The disaster represents a warning against magical dependence and decadence
- Terinok's relative isolation from the effects proves their martial superiority and cultural strength
- The true enemies remain hidden, waiting for another opportunity to strike

This perspective justifies Terinok's military culture and perpetual readiness for conflict, while casting the other successor states as naive for not recognizing the supposed threat.

### Erulian Principality's Dynastic Theory

Prince Kalden Erulius has carefully cultivated a narrative that serves his imperial ambitions:

- The Rending was a targeted magical assassination attempt aimed at the imperial family
- The perpetrators sought to eliminate the imperial line and create chaos
- The attempt partially succeeded but failed to account for the Erulian branch of the family
- This explanation positions the Erulian Principality as both victims and rightful successors
- It also creates a framework where restoring Erulian rule becomes a matter of justice and legitimate succession

While publicly presented as historical fact, this narrative is recognized by most neutral observers as politically motivated revisionism.

## Folk Beliefs

Among the common people of Elvandar, various folk explanations for The Rending have emerged:

### Divine Punishment

Many religious communities view The Rending as divine retribution:

- The catastrophe was punishment for imperial pride and excess
- The Seven Sins now embodied by the successor states reflect the very transgressions that invited divine wrath
- Only through moral reformation can Elvandar hope to reverse the effects
- Various sects disagree on exactly which transgressions triggered the punishment

### The Imperial Curse

A popular legend among the lower classes of Kaha'an:

- The dying Emperor, betrayed by court officials, used his last breath to curse the land
- The curse specifically spared the city while blighting the surrounding countryside
- This curse will only be lifted when a true heir sits upon the Ruby Throne
- The continued devastation serves as proof that the current rulers lack legitimacy

### Lathion's Vengeance

In regions closer to Gunastran, a different tale circulates:

- The legendary City of Gears (Lathion) was not actually lost but went into hiding
- The Rending was Lathion's revenge against the empire that tried to steal its technology
- The devastation was caused by a magitech weapon of unprecedented power
- Lathion remains hidden, waiting to strike again if its existence is threatened

This narrative is particularly popular among those who distrust magic and favor technological solutions to problems. It is also a precise inversion of the truth: Lathion did not end the world, but saved it.

## Isolated Truths

While most explanations contain elements of speculation or outright fabrication, certain individuals and groups possess fragments of the actual truth about The Rending.

### The Long Knives' Knowledge

The assassins' guild possesses more accurate information than most, gathered through their extensive intelligence networks:

- They know The Rending originated with a single individual's magical experiment
- They have identified the approximate location where the event began
- They understand it was an accident rather than an attack or natural disaster
- However, even they lack details about the specific magical working involved
- Their leadership keeps this information closely guarded, recognizing its value

### Urathan Sealed Archives

In the deepest vaults of Uratha's libraries, certain texts contain dangerous insights:

- Records of similar, smaller-scale magical accidents throughout history
- Theoretical work on the boundaries between life and death magic
- Documentation of failed resurrection attempts and their consequences
- Warnings about magical workings that thin the barriers between planes
- These texts are restricted to the highest levels of Urathan scholarship

### The Arol Batae's Secret

The former imperial guards who now protect Princess Elara possess a crucial piece of information:

- They know the Emperor and Empress did not ride out by chance. The imperial couple had received warnings of a dangerous magical experiment and went, personally, intending to stop it.
- They know Lady Kemvimore of House Kemvimore rode out with them — and they do not know why. Whatever business carried the wife of a cartel house into the imperial party that morning was never recorded, and died with her. It remains one of the Rending's smaller and more stubborn mysteries.
- They know the imperial party arrived too late to prevent the ritual and too close to survive its result.
- The Arol Batae keep this knowledge secret, fearing it would be twisted — used to argue that the Valanars themselves triggered the Rending by meddling, or used to manipulate the Princess with the manner of her parents' deaths.

### Lathion's Complete Record

The most comprehensive and accurate account of The Rending exists within Lathion itself, specifically in its Great Repository:

- Before activating the Destiny Engine, the inhabitants of Lathion created a final recording
- This record details what they detected happening on the surface, their analysis of the threat, the building of the Engine, and why its activation was their only option
- It likely includes firsthand observations, scientific measurements of the Gray Gate widening, and the council's deliberations
- The recording would explain both the nature of the catastrophe and the sacrifice they were about to make

The challenge of accessing this knowledge is immense. Lathion's advanced magitech would be nearly incomprehensible to even the most educated scholars from the surface. Their information storage methods — the brass "books" and data systems — would require knowledge of specific activation methods unknown to outsiders. The Lathionese language and writing systems likely evolved separately from surface languages, creating another barrier to understanding.

Even if explorers from Kaha'an reached the Great Repository, they would face the equivalent of an ancient human attempting to operate advanced technology from thousands of years in their future. The conceptual framework for understanding the devices simply wouldn't exist in their experience. Perhaps only the most exceptional Urathan scholars with access to ancient translation matrices might have a chance at deciphering fragments, but comprehensive understanding would remain beyond reach.

This creates a situation where the complete truth about The Rending exists but remains locked behind technological and linguistic barriers nearly insurmountable to those who might seek it.

## The Actual Truth

The full, unvarnished truth of The Rending is utterly unknown to modern Elvandar. No one alive has the complete picture, with even the most knowledgeable individuals and organizations possessing only fragments. What actually happened was this:

A brilliant but grief-broken mage attempted to resurrect his beloved after her untimely death. Despite knowing that true resurrection was considered impossible, his grief drove him to attempt what no one had successfully achieved before. His research led him at last to the Gray Gate — the boundary between the world of the living and the realm of the dead. The Gray Gate is not a place and not an object: it is the threshold itself, the sealed division that holds the dead on their side of existence and the living on theirs. The mage convinced himself that if he could ease it open — just a little, just once — he might draw a single beloved soul back through.

On that fateful day, he performed his ritual at the site that is now the center of the Gray Wastes. For a heartbeat, the working seemed to succeed: the Gray Gate opened. Then he lost control of it. A threshold so vast cannot be held ajar by a single mortal will, and instead of one soul slipping back, the raw essence of death itself began to pour through the opening into the living world. The mage was its first victim, unmade by the forces he had loosed — and with him died the only person who understood how the Gray Gate had been forced open, or how it might be closed again.

The Gray Gate holds back an immense pressure, the way a dam holds back a flood. The mage had not broken it — but he had forced it open, and a gate cracked ajar against that kind of pressure does not simply swing shut. Like a crack working its way through a dam, the opening widened, and went on widening.

**The first wave.** The Gray Gate's first exhalation was swift and catastrophic. A wave of necrotic energy expanded outward from the ritual site, and every living thing it touched — crop, beast, and person — died in the instant of its passing. Within hours it had scoured the province surrounding Kaha'an; the land's fertility did not wither so much as simply *stop*, all at once, as though life had been switched off. The wave's reach was uneven, slowed and turned aside by mountain ranges and the courses of great rivers, but where it ran unobstructed it ran fast.

Emperor Tiberan Valanar IV and Empress Lyra Valanar, who had been alerted to the dangerous experiment and rushed to intervene, were caught in this initial wave — and with them Lady Kemvimore, who had ridden out in the imperial party for reasons that did not survive her. All three were instantly disintegrated, leaving no bodies to recover.

Kaha'an itself should have died with its province. It did not. The ancient imperial wards woven into the city's foundations rose against the wave and held — barely, at terrible strain, but they held. The capital survived inside a shell of failing magic while everything around it went silent and gray.

**The slow death.** Had the Gray Gate fallen shut, the Rending would have been a single dreadful day. It did not. The opening went on widening, and as it widened the dying spread — no longer a wave now but a slow, patient tide. The desert crept outward, league by league, month after month, for the better part of a year. This second death was slower than the first but far more certain: the wave had been survivable, by luck or distance or geography, but the encroaching waste was not. Those who had lived through the first day understood, watching the gray horizon draw closer, that nothing would stop it on its own — and so they fled, abandoning their ruined homes and moving ahead of the advancing waste toward the one place known to have endured it: Kaha'an, behind its wards.

**Lathion.** The refugees could not have known that their salvation was already being built beneath their feet.

Deep below Kaha'an lay Lathion, the City of Gears — an elven civilization that had built itself five thousand years ago, maintained relations with the surface for nearly three millennia of open exchange, and then — for reasons since forgotten even by its own descendants — sealed itself away two thousand years before the Rending. The Great Shaft that had served as Lathion's surface gate during the open period was collapsed and filled, the magitech elevator destroyed. Only a long-disused back door (the Brass Door, behind a network of natural caves) remained as theoretical access. Sealed, but not blind: Lathionese instruments had observed the surface for the full two millennia of their isolation. Now those instruments measured the Gray Gate, modeled its widening, and returned the same answer every time. Unchecked, the Gray Gate would not stop at a province, or a continent. It would not stop at all. Every living thing on Elvandar would die.

The Lathionese had no ancient weapon waiting for such a day. What they possessed instead was five thousand years of theory — the deepest understanding of reality and of time that Elvandar had ever produced, refined for two thousand years in isolation after three thousand years of open inquiry — and, perhaps, just enough time to turn that theory into a machine. They chose to spend it. Through the long months of the slow death, while the desert climbed toward the city above them, Lathion raced to build the only thing their science said could close a wound in the world: the Destiny Engine.

(A long-tail consequence of the open period: much of what surface civilizations would later call "magitech" — particularly Gunastran's industrial magical engineering — is the degraded inheritance of Lathionese arts transmitted during those three open millennia. Gunastran's modern theorists do not know they are working with the broken-down children of Lathionese science. Lathion's instruments, watching from below, almost certainly noticed.)

It was finished. And its makers had always known its price. The Engine could reach into the fabric of reality and force the Gray Gate shut — but to do so it had to be powered by life itself, freely given, on a scale to answer the scale of the wound. Nearly every Lathionese who remained gave their existence to it. They fed themselves into the Engine, knowingly and willingly, and the Engine did what it had been built to do: the Gray Gate was sealed, and the torn boundary between the living world and the realm of the dead was mended. The slow tide stopped — abruptly, mid-desert, halting against nothing at all, as though a hand had been laid flat against the world.

In the same moment, the Engine's activation sent a metaphysical shockwave through the region. Where it met Kaha'an's failing wards, it did not break them — it reinforced them, restoring and strengthening the shell that had so nearly fallen, so that the city survived both the Gray Gate's final violence and the reality-deep backlash of its own cure.

The cost was a civilization. Lathion had been alive that morning — hundreds of thousands of people in a city of wonders. By the time the Engine fell silent, Lathion was a tomb, perfectly preserved, its tireless machines still tending empty halls. The City of Gears is not, as legend holds, "lost." It is dead — and it died to save a world that never knew it was there.

The Gray Wastes remain "thin" because the boundary between worlds, though mended, never healed cleanly — a scar in reality where the cut had been deepest. The gray coloration of the sand is the residue of minute particles of death essence that steeped the soil through the long year the Gray Gate hung open. The strange phenomena experienced by visitors are echoes of both the original catastrophe and Lathion's reality-altering countermeasure.

## Long-Term Implications

Whether or not the true cause of The Rending is ever uncovered, its effects continue to shape Elvandar:

- The environmental devastation created resource scarcities that exacerbate tensions between successor states
- The loss of the imperial family created a power vacuum that the various successor states seek to fill
- The transformation of Kaha'an from imperial capital to desert crossroads fundamentally altered trade patterns
- The year-long advance of the desert drove a flood of survivors into Kaha'an — the one surviving refuge — in the space of a single year. The capital's population swelled catastrophically, a demographic shock the city was never built to absorb. How those refugees were housed has defined the shape of modern Kaha'an ever since (see the Warrens documentation)
- The seven successor states, once united under imperial rule, have each embraced one of the seven deadly sins in the catastrophe's aftermath
- The Gray Wastes serve as a constant reminder of forces beyond human understanding or control
- The discovery of Lathion beneath the Warrens offers both promise and peril — access to advanced knowledge but also to potentially destructive power
- Princess Elara Valanar's existence represents hope for reunification, but also a threat to those who have built power in the post-Rending world

The Rending not only transformed the physical landscape of Elvandar but also its political, economic, and spiritual character. What began as one man's desperate attempt to defy death's finality ended by reshaping an entire continent — and by quietly extinguishing the civilization that paid, in full and unwitnessed, to keep that ending from being final.
