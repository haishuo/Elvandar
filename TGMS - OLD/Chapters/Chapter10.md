## Chapter 10: Desperate Times

Xion, Elara, and Farleen crouched in the narrow tunnels of Kaha'an's distribution network, the dank air pressing against them like a physical weight. Sweat trickled down Xion's neck as he strained to hear any sounds of pursuit behind them. The silence was almost more unnerving than the shouting they'd left behind.

"How far does this network extend?" Elara whispered, her eyes—currently a pale silver betraying her unspoken fear—darting to the dark passages branching off in multiple directions.

"Most of the middle districts," Xion replied, keeping his voice low. "The nobles built these passages to move goods without having to see the people who actually do the work."

Farleen snorted. "Typical. They build their fancy homes on top and hide the infrastructure that supports them underneath. Out of sight, out of mind."

Xion tensed at her words, the betrayal in the alley still a raw wound both physically and emotionally. The injury in his shoulder had been treated during his time in the Arol Batae compound, but the presence of the woman who had stabbed him—regardless of her unexpected rescue afterward—set his nerves on edge.

Elara seemed to sense his discomfort. "We need to focus on getting somewhere safe," she said firmly, cutting through the tension. "Arguments about nobility and their failings can wait until we're not being hunted."

Xion nodded gratefully. "You're right." He turned to study the junction ahead, trying to determine the best path forward. "What I can't understand is how the Water and Iron cartels coordinated like that. They've never worked together so smoothly before."

Farleen's lips tightened. "Something's changed in the power dynamics. This kind of coordination would have been unthinkable even a month ago."

A distant scraping echoed through the tunnel behind them, and all three froze. The sound came again, closer this time—boots against stone.

"They've entered the distribution network," Farleen whispered, eyes widening.

Elara's gaze snapped to her. "How do you know their security protocols?"

"Does it matter right now?" Farleen hissed back.

Xion made a quick decision, pointing to a narrow passage sloping downward. "This way. The older tunnel systems are less maintained but they're also less likely to be on current maps."

They hurried down the passage, bent nearly double in the constricted space. The construction changed noticeably as they descended—from the relatively uniform stonework of the distribution tunnels to a more haphazard mixture of older masonry, crumbling mortar, and improvised repairs.

The air grew thicker, carrying unfamiliar smells—cooking fires, human waste, the unmistakable tang of too many bodies in too little space. The sounds of pursuit faded behind them, but their relief was tempered by growing uncertainty about their location in the maze-like passages.

"Where are we?" Elara asked as the tunnel widened enough for them to stand upright.

Xion studied the wooden supports and packed earth of the walls. "These aren't city tunnels anymore. I think we're approaching the infrastructure beneath the Warrens."

"Beneath?" Elara questioned.

"Well, the lowest levels," Xion corrected himself, recalling what little he knew of the infamous district. "The Warrens isn't just built outward, but upward. These passages would connect to the base levels."

Voices drifted to them from somewhere ahead—rough, casual conversation, not the clipped tones of security forces.

"Could be residents," Farleen suggested. "They might know which ways are clear."

Xion frowned. "We have no idea who they are or where their loyalties lie."

"We also have no idea where we are," Farleen countered. "And Warrens folk generally dislike cartel security more than they dislike strangers."

Elara studied both of them, then nodded decisively. "We need information more than we need absolute safety right now. Let's approach carefully."

They moved forward, the passage gradually opening into what appeared to be a small storage area. Three men were transferring crates from a handcart to stacked shelves against the wall. They turned sharply at the sound of footsteps, hands moving instinctively to concealed weapons.

Xion stepped forward, palms raised. "We're not looking for trouble."

The burliest of the three, a scar slicing through his left eyebrow, studied them with open suspicion. "You're not from the Warrens."

"No," Xion admitted. "We need to find safe passage through."

The man's eyes narrowed further, then flicked to Xion's chest where the Long Knives medallion had slipped partially into view from beneath his torn clothing. Something in the man's posture changed subtly.

Before anyone could speak further, Farleen stepped forward, placing several small tokens on a nearby crate. "We need to find the Chimney."

Xion shot her a confused glance—he'd never heard of the "Chimney" before—but kept silent, trusting that she had some plan.

He recognized the tokens she'd placed down as Watermarks, the unofficial currency he'd heard was used in the Warrens, named for the women who issued them. Where had Farleen gotten those?

The scarred man scooped up the tokens, examining them carefully before tucking them away. "There's a Chimney waypoint three junctions east, then up two levels. Look for the crossed chisel marks on the supports." He paused, glancing nervously at the passage they'd emerged from. "Strange day for outsiders to be wandering the Warrens."

"Strange how?" Elara asked.

"Cartel security forces moving in patterns. Not just the usual shakedowns, but coordinated-like. Different cartels working together, if you can believe it." He snorted. "Never thought I'd see Water and Iron patrols moving as a unit."

Xion exchanged a glance with Elara. It wasn't just their experience at the clinic—something larger was happening throughout Kaha'an.

"Thank you," Xion said. "We'll find our way."

As they followed the man's directions, Xion pulled Farleen aside. "The Chimney? What is that?"

"Smugglers," Farleen said quietly. "They specialize in moving people and goods through the Warrens. If anyone can get us out of here safely, it's them."

"And you know about them how?" Xion pressed.

Farleen's expression closed off. "We don't all have the luxury of growing up in the Noble District, Xion." She reached into her tunic and pulled out an identical medallion to his own, hanging on a chain around her neck. "Your father gave me this one too."

Xion stopped abruptly. "What?"

"I'll explain more when we're safer," she said, tucking the medallion away again. "But you should know that these mean more than you realize. They're recognized everywhere in the Warrens."

"That man definitely reacted to it," Elara observed.

They continued in tense silence until they reached the junction marked with crossed chisel marks. Here, the tunnel quality changed dramatically—better maintained, with strange markings on the walls that Xion couldn't decipher and occasional lamps in recessed niches providing dim illumination.

"We're in Chimney territory now," Farleen murmured.

A soft whistle came from somewhere above them. Xion looked up to see a teenage boy perched on a wooden crossbeam, a small lantern hanging from his belt.

"You looking for Smoke?" the boy asked, jumping down with practiced ease.

Xion glanced at Farleen, who nodded subtly.

"Yes," Xion answered, trying to project confidence he didn't feel.

"This way." The boy led them through a series of increasingly complex passages that seemed to climb and descend in no particular pattern. Xion attempted to memorize the route but quickly lost track of the turns and junctions.

Just as he was certain they were being led in circles, they emerged into a larger chamber where a woman in her forties with close-cropped gray hair sat at a table covered with maps and scraps of paper. She looked up, sharp eyes assessing them immediately.

"Smoke," the boy announced unnecessarily.

"Three outsiders," Smoke observed, setting down what appeared to be a ledger. "One bearing Long Knives token, yet not of the Long Knives." Her gaze traveled to Elara. "One who walks like a warrior but dresses like a commoner." Finally, to Farleen. "And one who knows our paths better than she should."

The Chimney leader leaned back in her chair. "Where are you headed that you need my guidance?"

Xion hesitated, uncertain how much to reveal.

"Upper levels," Elara answered for him. "Away from cartel security."

Smoke's mouth quirked slightly. "That medallion complicates things." She gestured to Xion's chest. "The Long Knives control the eastern sections. The Slavers control the west and south. Your token gives you passage through one but makes you a target in the other."

Xion's hand moved instinctively to touch the medallion his father had given him. He'd thought it a simple protective charm with some symbolic value—not a factional marker in this vertical maze of the desperate and dangerous.

"We just need a path through neutral territory," Xion said.

"There's precious little of that today," Smoke replied. "The cartels are stirring up the Warrens like someone kicked a wasp nest. Something's got them hunting." Her gaze lingered on Elara. "I'm guessing it might have something to do with you three."

"We can pay," Farleen said.

Smoke shook her head. "Information first. Money second."

"What do you want to know?" Elara asked.

"Why are the cartels coordinating? In twenty years, I've never seen Water and Iron patrols moving together."

Elara seemed to consider her words carefully. "There may be a shift in Kaha'an's power structure soon. The cartels are...concerned."

Smoke studied her for a long moment, then nodded as if satisfied. "Fair enough. Trill will guide you through what neutral ground remains." She nodded to the boy who had brought them. "Keep to the middle levels. Avoid the Heights where cartel influences are strongest, and the Depths where...other things dwell."

As they prepared to follow Trill, Farleen pulled Xion aside. "I'm not coming with you."

"What?" Xion hissed.

"I have connections in the Warrens that could help us determine who coordinated this operation against you," she said, eyes darting briefly to Elara. "It's better if I move alone. I know these paths."

"After everything that's happened, you expect me to trust you?" Xion demanded.

Farleen's expression hardened. "I saved your life, didn't I?" Before he could respond, she pressed the remaining Watermarks into his palm. "You'll need these more than I will."

Without waiting for his reply, Farleen turned to Smoke, exchanged a few quiet words, and disappeared down a side passage, leaving Xion staring after her with a mixture of confusion and anger.

"We should go," Elara said softly at his shoulder.

They followed Trill through a bewildering vertical labyrinth that Xion would never have imagined existed within Kaha'an's walls. The Warrens were like nothing he'd ever seen—a chaotic, improvised city-within-a-city where buildings leaned against each other in precarious stacks. They climbed makeshift ladders and crossed narrow bridges spanning dizzying gaps between structures. The passages sometimes tightened to the point where they had to turn sideways to squeeze through.

As they moved, Trill provided a running commentary on territorial boundaries.

"Long Knives control most of this side," he explained, pointing to markings on the walls that Xion would never have recognized as significant. "Slavers have the other. Don't cross from one to the other unless you want trouble."

Eventually, they reached a junction where several passages met. Trill stopped. "This is as far as I go. That way—" he pointed to a passage sloping upward "—leads to the mid-levels. Safer territory, fewer questions."

He melted away into the shadows before they could thank him, leaving Xion and Elara alone at the junction.

Elara suddenly stiffened, her hand flying to her temple.

"What is it?" Xion asked, alarmed.

Her eyes had gone unfocused. "There's something...down there." She pointed to a descending passage opposite from the one Trill had indicated.

"Down where? What do you mean?"

"I don't know," she whispered, confusion evident in her voice. "I just feel...something familiar. Like at the edge of my memory."

A commotion echoed from above—shouts about cartel security pushing deeper into the Warrens. They were running out of time.

"We should go up," Xion insisted. "Trill said it was safer."

"I need to see what's down there," Elara said, already moving toward the descending passage. "It feels important. Like it's calling to me."

Cursing under his breath, Xion followed. The passage quickly became steeper, the construction older and stranger. Unlike the haphazard improvisation of the Warrens proper, these tunnels showed signs of ancient, deliberate craftsmanship. The walls were lined with an unfamiliar stone that seemed to absorb the dim light from their borrowed lantern.

Elara moved with increasing confidence despite never having been here before, turning at junctions without hesitation. Symbols appeared etched into the walls—geometric patterns and spiraling gear-like designs that seemed to shimmer slightly when the light passed over them.

"How do you know where to go?" Xion asked, struggling to keep up.

"I don't know," she admitted. "It just...feels right."

They reached a circular chamber with multiple exits. Without pausing, Elara pointed to one of the passages. "That way."

The passage widened suddenly, opening into a vast chamber that took Xion's breath away. At the far end stood an enormous circular door, easily three times the height of a man, made of what appeared to be brass or some similar metal. Its surface was covered in intricate engravings—gears, spirals, and symbols similar to those they'd seen on the walls, but infinitely more complex.

"What is this place?" Xion whispered, his voice echoing in the cavernous space.

Elara didn't answer. She approached the door as if in a trance, her hand outstretched. As she neared, the engravings began to glow with a soft blue light that grew stronger with each step she took.

When her fingers finally touched the metal surface, the glow intensified dramatically. Elara gasped, her body going rigid. Her eyes flashed rapidly through colors—blue to violet to silver—as if experiencing multiple emotions simultaneously.

"Elara!" Xion rushed forward as she stumbled backward, nearly collapsing.

He caught her just as voices echoed in the chamber. Xion pulled her into the shadows at the edge of the room as several figures entered—men and women wearing dark clothing, moving with the silent grace of trained killers. Long Knives assassins.

"The Door is active," one of them said, voice hushed with awe. "Look at the patterns."

"Search the chamber," commanded another. "Someone's been here."

Xion held his breath, pressing deeper into the shadows with Elara still half-conscious in his arms. They were trapped, with no clear escape route.

One of the assassins moved in their direction, hand on a weapon at his belt. Xion tensed, preparing for a fight he couldn't possibly win.

The assassin froze, staring directly at them. His eyes dropped to the medallion visible at Xion's chest, then back to his face.

Instead of attacking, the man took a deliberate step backward. "North passage is clear," he called to the others, his eyes never leaving Xion's.

Xion understood the message. Taking advantage of the momentary confusion, he half-carried Elara toward the northern exit, expecting at any moment to feel a blade between his ribs. But the assassins made no move to stop them.

They stumbled through dark passages, putting as much distance as possible between themselves and the chamber. When they finally stopped to catch their breath, Elara had recovered enough to stand on her own.

"What happened back there?" Xion asked. "What was that door?"

"I don't know," she said, her voice shaking slightly. "When I touched it, I felt...everything at once. Like memories that aren't mine. Images, sounds, sensations." She shook her head. "Nothing clear, just fragments."

"And those assassins—they just let us go when they saw my medallion." Xion fingered the metal disk that had now saved them twice. "This thing is more significant than I realized."

They decided to head upward, toward the middle levels of the Warrens where they might find relative safety. As they climbed through the vertical labyrinth, the spaces around them became more populated, with crude dwellings built into every available nook and cranny. Makeshift bridges connected structures at different levels, and rope ladders hung between platforms, creating a dizzying three-dimensional maze.

"We need to be careful," Xion warned as they reached a more open area. "We don't know whose territory we're in now."

Elara nodded, her color-changing eyes now concealed behind a scrap of cloth they'd found in the tunnels. The disguise was crude but necessary—color-changing eyes would attract far too much attention, even in the anything-goes environment of the Warrens.

What neither of them realized as they pressed onward was that they had crossed an invisible boundary. The subtle markings on the walls had changed from the crossed knives of the assassins' guild to the red manacle that symbolized the domain of the Slavers.

And in the Warrens, such boundaries meant the difference between life and death.
