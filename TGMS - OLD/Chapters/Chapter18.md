## Chapter 18: The Ruby Throne

Three days. That's how long it had been since Elara Valanar had claimed the Ruby Throne, since ancient ward lights had awakened at her touch and palace guards had dropped to their knees. Three days since imperial banners had unfurled from towers that had stood bare for twenty years.

Xion watched from the edge of the council chamber as Elara received a delegation from the city's middle districts. He couldn't help noticing how different she seemed from the warrior princess who had saved him in the alley near the Arol Batae compound. Her bearing remained direct, her attention focused, but something else had emerged—a natural authority that no amount of compound training could have instilled.

The Ruby Palace had transformed almost overnight. Where dusty corridors and sparsely attended chambers had stood as monuments to absence, now scurrying officials and visiting dignitaries filled every hallway. The air of neglect had been swept away, replaced by an energy that Xion imagined hadn't been felt since before The Rending.

"The flour distribution in the eastern sector has been delayed for two days now," a merchant representative was saying, his hands clutched nervously before him. "With the Grain cartel representatives claiming they await new imperial guidance..."

A hint of irritation flashed in Elara's eyes, briefly tinting them amber before she controlled the reaction. "We'll issue directives today. Existing distribution agreements remain in force until formally amended."

"As you wish, Your Majesty." The merchant bowed deeply.

Elara nodded to the palace aide who signaled the next petitioner forward. Xion marveled at her composure. For someone raised in isolation, she'd adapted to the rituals of rule with remarkable speed.

Several more petitioners presented their cases—disputes over water rights, requests for imperial intervention in trade matters, even a noble house seeking clarification on courtly protocols that hadn't been observed in two decades. Through it all, Elara maintained a measured authority that left Xion both impressed and slightly unsettled. The transition from warrior to ruler was happening before his eyes.

As the last petitioner withdrew, Commander Vesk approached the throne with a scroll bearing reports from throughout the city.

"Support continues to grow in the Iron district," he reported. "Sa Ko Ren's endorsement has been... thorough."

"And the other districts?" Elara asked, accepting the scroll.

"The Water district remains cautiously neutral, though Lady Larannas has allowed imperial symbols to be displayed near the harbor. The Grain district shows public support through Lady Melina's proclamations, but Lord Rosik himself remains conspicuously absent from all proceedings."

Elara's expression didn't change, but Xion noted a slight tensing of her shoulders. "And the Warrens?"

Vesk's weathered face darkened. "No official response. Imperial messengers have been turned away at the borders between districts."

"As expected," Elara said, rising from the throne. "We'll address each district differently. The Remembrance ceremony approaches—it provides an opportunity to strengthen our position."

---

Later that afternoon, Xion found himself in Elara's private study, surrounded by maps and documents spread across several tables. A team of researchers leaned over the materials, murmuring among themselves.

"These markings match architectural drawings found in the eastern archives," a young woman was saying, comparing two parchments. "The passages appear to have been part of the original palace design, though many were later sealed."

Elara stood before a large map of the palace foundations, her fingers tracing a line that extended beneath the throne room. "This corridor," she said, "behind the panel in the northwest corner. There's something about it..."

Xion approached, having noticed this wasn't the first time she'd returned to that particular passage. "Your instincts about the palace have been remarkably accurate for someone who'd never set foot here before."

She looked up, her expression troubled. "It's strange, Xion. When we walked these corridors for the first time, I felt as though I'd been here before. Not just learned about it from Hedrin's lessons—actually *been* here." She dropped her voice. "It's similar to what I felt at the brass door."

One of the researchers straightened, pointing to a faded outline on his document. "Your Majesty, we've identified several sealed doorways with symbols that match your descriptions from the tunnels beneath the Warrens."

"The same patterns that glowed at your touch?" Xion asked.

Elara nodded, her gaze distant. "Something connects them. Something connects *me* to them."

"It may be worth combining what Keiran told us about his interrogation with these discoveries," Xion suggested. "His captors were fixated on tunnels beneath the palace—they must have known something."

"Or suspected something," Elara corrected. "Knowledge and fear aren't always easily distinguished."

Lydia Nask entered, bowing briefly. "The security survey of the lower levels is complete, Your Majesty. We've found evidence of recent activity in several sealed passageways—fresh tool marks on old stonework."

"Related to the unusual excavation activity reported yesterday?" Elara asked sharply.

Lydia nodded. "Palace staff reported workers claiming to conduct 'structural maintenance' in areas below the throne room. When questioned now, the overseer has no record of authorizing such work."

"Increase patrols in those areas," Elara ordered. "I want to see these sealed doorways myself, but we'll proceed with caution."

---

In the Erulian Principality's war room, Prince Kalden stood with his back to the assembled commanders, staring at a massive map of Kaha'an's western border region. Red markers indicated troop positions along mountain passes and key trade routes.

"Three battalions positioned here, here, and here," he said, jabbing a finger against the parchment. "The mountain passes give us natural advantage."

A gray-haired commander cleared his throat. "Your Highness, if I may... open warfare against Kaha'an carries significant risks. Our intelligence suggests the Water and Iron cartels have already aligned with this... claimant."

Kalden turned, his face flushed with barely contained rage. "This *pretender*," he spat, "threatens everything we've built over twenty years. My father was content to wait, to collect trinkets and play at being emperor-in-waiting. I will not make the same mistake."

"The Shadow Corps operations—" another commander began.

"Need time," Kalden cut him off. "Varen's reports confirm that security around the palace remains formidable. We need to apply pressure from multiple directions." He stalked back to the map. "Border provocations will force them to divide their attention while our agents continue working inside the city."

A younger officer stepped forward. "The latest intelligence suggests growing popular support for the Valanar girl. The activation of the imperial wards has convinced many of her legitimacy."

Kalden's fist came down hard on the table. "A parlor trick! Nothing more."

"Nevertheless," the older commander said carefully, "it has proven effective. The Iron district has fully embraced her claim, and the Water cartel appears to be negotiating favorable terms rather than opposing her."

"And what of the Grain and Slavers?" Kalden demanded.

"The Grain publicly supports her through Lady Melina Kemvimore, though Lord Rosik himself has made no appearance. The Slavers maintain their typical isolation, though there are rumors Ronas Dermir himself may visit the palace soon."

Prince Kalden paced the length of the war table, his fingers drumming against the pommel of his sword. "Increase pressure on the trade routes. I want every merchant crossing our territory questioned about conditions in Kaha'an. Target the divisions between cartels—the Slavers have never worked well with the others."

He paused before a small portrait of a stern-faced older man. "My father spent two decades collecting imperial artifacts, building this replica throne, expanding our palace with imperial architectural elements. All that careful preparation while telling me to be *patient*."

His voice dropped to a dangerous whisper. "I will not lose what is rightfully mine to some hidden girl with a color-changing parlor trick."

---

The throne room had emptied for the afternoon, leaving Elara alone with Xion and Silvanno as they reviewed reports from across the city. Silvanno had become an invaluable source of intelligence on his mother's activities, though Xion sometimes wondered how much Tania Larannas allowed her son to know.

"Mother has positioned Water cartel guards at all harbor approaches," Silvanno was saying. "Any diplomatic vessels arriving by sea must pass through her screening first."

Elara frowned. "A power play?"

"Partly," Silvanno admitted. "But also practical security. The harbor remains vulnerable to infiltration."

"She strengthens her position while appearing helpful," Xion observed. "Clever."

"The question," Elara said, "is how much we concede to maintain her support versus asserting imperial authority."

A soft knock at the side entrance interrupted their discussion. Sa Ko Ren entered, her Terinok features unreadable as always.

"I bring news from the western border," she said without preamble. "Merchants report Erulian troops massing at the mountain passes. Trade caravans face increased harassment and questioning."

Elara didn't seem surprised. "Their message made their position clear enough. How serious is the military threat?"

"Mountain terrain favors defenders," Sa Ko Ren said. "But we should prepare. My Iron Guard stands ready to reinforce the western approaches."

"We'll strengthen the positions without appearing to escalate," Elara decided. "Diplomatic routes remain open for now." She turned to Xion. "What would your father do in this situation?"

The question caught him off guard. "My father would..." he paused, considering. "He'd prepare for every contingency while revealing nothing. He'd have spies in the Erulian court feeding back every word Prince Kalden speaks."

"And do we have such resources?" Elara asked.

The room fell silent.

"The Long Knives might," Xion said finally. "But that's a dangerous alliance to court openly."

Sa Ko Ren nodded slowly. "Some walls protect, some imprison. Sometimes, they do both at once."

---

The palace healing chambers were quiet as Keiran slept fitfully, still recovering from the injuries sustained during his capture and interrogation. The guards who had rescued him from the holding facility reported that he had been subjected to harsh questioning, though the rescue operation had been successful before more severe methods could be employed.

Farleen Darfi stood in the shadows of an adjoining corridor, watching the Arol Batae guard through the partially open door. Her amber eyes—the same shade as The Mistress's—remained thoughtful as she observed the healing chambers from her vantage point.

The past weeks had altered everything. Her mission to monitor Xion's reform group had evolved into something far more complex, with layers of allegiance and subterfuge even she found difficult to navigate. She touched the medallion at her throat, identical to the one Rosik had given Xion, and considered her next steps carefully.

A messenger approached, breaking her contemplation. "My lady, your father requests an immediate meeting at the usual location."

Farleen nodded, casting one last glance toward Keiran's room before following the messenger through the palace's less traveled corridors. Her father would be expecting a full report on developments within the palace—information that would ultimately find its way to Rosik Kemvimore, though through carefully filtered channels.

As she moved through the shadowed passageway, Farleen noted the increased security throughout the palace. Guards had doubled at key junctions, and even servants were being questioned about their movements. Whatever game was being played beneath the surface of Kaha'an's politics, the stakes were rising by the hour.

---

The eastern gate of Kaha'an was crowded with merchant caravans preparing for departure as the afternoon sun began its descent. Imperial messengers in newly commissioned uniforms distributed sealed proclamations to caravan masters bound for the successor states.

"Hard to believe, isn't it?" a grizzled trader said to his companion as they loaded the last crates onto their wagon. "Twenty years with that throne empty, and now there's an empress with eyes that change color."

"You've seen it yourself?" his younger partner asked skeptically.

"With my own eyes at the ceremony. Blue one moment, then violet when she spoke of Kaha'an's future. It's the Valanar blood, true as true can be."

As the merchants continued their preparations, a nondescript traveler with road-dusted clothes moved through the gate in the opposite direction, entering the city. Nothing about his appearance would have drawn attention—brown hair, average height, travel-worn boots. Only the careful observer might have noticed how his eyes constantly scanned his surroundings, or the slight bulge beneath his cloak where a message tube rested against his ribs.

Varen carried a sealed message bearing the symbol of the Erulian Principality—Prince Kalden's personal seal. Its contents would determine Kaha'an's fate in ways even its new empress couldn't imagine.

---

In the depths of the Warrens, Long Knives operatives moved with practiced invisibility through the shadows, monitoring the subtle shifts in power dynamics following Elara's claim to the throne. Two assassins met briefly in the narrow space between leaning buildings, their words barely disturbing the air around them.

"The Mistress wants eyes on all movements between districts," one murmured. "Particularly any sign of Warrens dwellers approaching the Imperial Quarter."

"What about the Erulian agent? The one spotted at the eastern gate?"

"Presumed carrying instructions. Watch the noble houses for unusual activity."

Nearby, a woman with a blue headscarf sat beside her well, seemingly occupied with her daily chores. Her ears, however, caught every word exchanged by the Long Knives operatives. The Well Mothers had their own interest in these events—particularly in the young empress whose eyes had changed color when she'd passed through the Warrens. Some things were remembered even when history tried to forget them.

---

"Your Majesty, Ronas Dermir has arrived at the palace gates with an escort of Slaver enforcers. He demands an audience."

The announcement came as Elara was reviewing security reports with Commander Vesk in the council chamber. Vesk's face darkened at the news.

"That man should not be allowed near—"

"Bring him to the throne room," Elara interrupted. "Full security protocols, limited weapons as a courtesy to his position."

Vesk looked ready to protest but bowed instead. "As you command."

The throne room seemed to shrink as Ronas Dermir strode in shortly after, flanked by six heavily armed enforcers bearing the red manacle symbol of the Slavers cartel. Unlike the other cartel leaders, Ronas made no pretense at courtly manners. His weathered face bore the scars of countless fights, and his eyes moved constantly, assessing threats with practiced precision.

Xion felt a peculiar sensation watching the Slavers' leader in this formal setting. Their encounter in the Warrens had revealed a Ronas Dermir far more calculating than his brutal reputation suggested—a man who killed his own captain to maintain order and who understood the delicate balance of power in Kaha'an with surprising depth. Here in the palace, that same intelligent brutality seemed somehow more pronounced against the refined backdrop.

"So," Ronas said, his rough voice carrying easily through the chamber, "twenty years without anyone on that chair, and now this."

Elara sat straight-backed on the Ruby Throne, her expression neutral. Commander Vesk and two elite guards stood at attention nearby, hands resting on sword hilts.

"Cartel Leader Dermir," Elara acknowledged. "You've come to my court unannounced."

Ronas barked a laugh. "Your court? Four cartels have kept this city alive since your parents vanished. The power's been balanced on a knife edge all that time." He gestured dismissively at the throne. "That chair's been empty for a reason."

The guards tensed at the disrespect, but Elara raised a hand to still them.

"A reason born of necessity, not design," she said calmly. "The cartels couldn't agree on who would sit here, so you all agreed no one would."

Something like surprise flickered across Ronas's face before settling back into its habitual scowl. "Know your history, do you? But knowing isn't understanding."

He paced a slow circle, his enforcers remaining in position. "Three days you've been sitting where no one has sat for two decades, and I've seen six imperial proclamations. Not one mentions the Warrens except to 'reaffirm existing jurisdictional boundaries.'"

"Would you prefer I started issuing orders regarding territory you clearly control?" Elara asked.

Ronas stopped his pacing. "I'd prefer clarity about your intentions. The other cartels are falling all over themselves to kiss your ring. Makes a man wonder what plans you have for those who don't."

"My intention," Elara said firmly, "is to govern Kaha'an as it exists now, not as it existed in my parents' time. The Warrens is part of this city, whatever unique circumstances created it."

"Pretty words," Ronas snorted. "Let me be plainer. Twenty thousand people live in the Warrens. When the grain shipments run short, they starve first. When disease spreads, they die first." He leaned forward, massive hands braced on the rail separating him from the throne. "What exactly do you offer them that the cartels haven't?"

The question hung in the air, its bluntness cutting through the usual diplomatic facade. Xion found himself respecting Ronas's directness despite everything he knew about the man.

"I offer equal consideration," Elara replied. "Not special treatment, not neglect—equal standing as citizens of Kaha'an."

"Empty promise," Ronas countered. "That throne sat vacant for twenty years because the cartels couldn't stomach the idea of someone else's power. You think they'll bow to you just because your eyes change color?"

"I am not asking them to bow," Elara said, her eyes briefly flashing violet. "I am asking them to serve Kaha'an, not just their own interests."

Ronas straightened, studying her with new interest. "Bold words from someone whose claim rests on an old bloodline trick." He crossed his arms. "Here's what I want: guaranteed minimum grain shipments, regardless of price fluctuations. Medical personnel assigned to the Warrens permanently, not just during outbreaks. And official recognition of my authority there."

"Demands already?" Xion couldn't help asking.

Ronas spared him a glance. "Not demands. Terms. I can make your rule impossible in the Warrens, or I can make it work. Your choice which."

Instead of responding with anger, Elara nodded thoughtfully. "Your first two points have merit. The third requires clarification. Your authority as cartel leader, I can acknowledge. Authority to continue slavery, I cannot."

"Call it what you want," Ronas shrugged. "People still need to work, and the Warrens still need order. Change the name if it makes you feel better."

"We'll discuss specific terms," Elara said, neither accepting nor rejecting his proposal. "But understand this: I will not ignore the Warrens as previous imperial regimes did, nor will I pretend the challenges there can be solved with proclamations alone."

Ronas actually smiled, though it didn't reach his eyes. "Well now, that's the first time anyone sitting in that fancy chair has acknowledged reality." He turned to leave, then paused. "One more thing. The tunnels beneath the Warrens—the ones with the brass door. Leave them alone."

Without waiting for a response, he strode from the throne room, his enforcers falling in behind him.

When the heavy doors closed, Xion moved closer to the throne. "That went... better than expected?"

"He came in person," Elara noted. "That means something."

"It means he sees value in negotiation," Xion agreed. "Though I wouldn't mistake that for loyalty."

Elara rose from the throne, stretching muscles stiffened by the formal posture required during audiences. "Loyalty is earned, not commanded. Even imperial authority has its limits."

"What will you do about his terms?"

"Consider them carefully," she replied. "The grain shipments and medical personnel make sense. His authority..." She shook her head. "We'll need to find a middle ground."

"And his warning about the brass door?" Xion asked, lowering his voice.

Elara's expression grew distant. "That's connected to what we found earlier—the excavations, the sealed doorways with matching symbols. Something important lies beneath this city, Xion. Something that connects to my heritage in ways I don't yet understand."

---

In a tavern on the edge of the Noble District, a private room hosted a meeting between Varen and a representative from one of Kaha'an's minor noble houses. The noble, draped in expensive fabrics that couldn't quite disguise his declining fortunes, leaned forward eagerly as Varen produced the sealed message tube.

"Prince Kalden was most specific in his instructions," Varen said softly, sliding the tube across the table. "House Benizar's assistance will not be forgotten when proper order is restored."

Lady Orisa Benizar's representative tucked the message tube into his robes. "The Lady sends her assurances that our house remains loyal to the true imperial legacy—not some convenient pretender."

"Good," Varen replied, his unremarkable features settling into a thin smile. "We'll be in contact soon with more specific instructions."

The noble house representative nodded, rising to leave. Neither man acknowledged the woman seated in the common room below, her blue headscarf marking her as a Well Mother from the Warrens. Her presence in such an establishment was unusual enough that it might have warranted attention—had either been paying attention to anything beyond their own schemes.

---

Night had fallen by the time Elara returned to her private chambers, exhaustion finally catching up with her. The past three days had been a whirlwind of proclamations, audiences, and strategic planning. For someone raised in the confined world of the Arol Batae compound, the sudden expansion of her world felt both exhilarating and overwhelming.

She moved to the balcony overlooking the sleeping city, watching the patchwork of lights below. The Iron district blazed with illumination, imperial banners catching the breeze above forges that worked through the night. The Water district maintained its usual moderate activity, while the Noble district showed scattered lights from estates where influential families undoubtedly plotted their responses to her rule.

To the north, the Warrens rose in its haphazard vertical sprawl, darkness broken by occasional fire pits and lanterns. Even from this distance, it seemed like another world entirely—close enough to touch, yet governed by rules the palace could never fully comprehend.

Elara heard the soft footfall behind her and knew without turning that it was Xion. After weeks in his company, she recognized the particular cadence of his steps.

"Something troubling you?" she asked, not turning from the view.

"Many things," he admitted, joining her at the balcony rail. "The excavations beneath the palace, Ronas's warning about the brass door, the Erulian troops at the border... but mostly the feeling that we're moving through a story whose beginning we haven't read."

Elara's gaze drifted to the western horizon beyond the walls where Erulian forces gathered like storm clouds. "We've stepped into a game whose rules we don't fully understand, Xion. But that doesn't mean we can't change them."

In the uncertain lamplight, her eyes shifted from calm blue to resolute violet. Whatever came next—whether from the tunnels beneath her feet or the armies beyond her borders—she would face it as Empress of Kaha'an, last heir to a legacy older than the throne itself.
