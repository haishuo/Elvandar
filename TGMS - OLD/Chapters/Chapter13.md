## Chapter 13: Mother of Blades

The two Long Knives assassins moved with unsettling ease through the labyrinthine passages of the Warrens. One male, one female, both clad in nondescript clothing that somehow managed to blend with every shadow they passed. Xion found himself constantly adjusting his stride to keep pace, his healing shoulder occasionally reminding him of Farleen's betrayal with a dull throb.

Beside him, Elara's eyes darted everywhere, taking in each rickety bridge and precariously balanced dwelling with the analytical gaze of someone mentally mapping enemy territory. Her royal upbringing had never prepared her for the chaotic vertical sprawl of the Warrens, yet she adapted with the same determination she brought to everything else.

"Stay close," the female assassin murmured, barely glancing back at them. "These passages shift with the seasons. What was safe yesterday might collapse tomorrow."

Xion nodded, noting how the woman occasionally glanced at Elara with barely concealed interest. Not hostile, exactly, but... assessing. Like she was comparing the princess against some mental standard only she understood.

They navigated downward through increasingly narrow thoroughfares, past dwellings stacked seven levels high, some leaning so precariously against their neighbors that Xion couldn't understand how they remained standing. The deeper they descended, the more the air thickened with the smell of too many lives packed into too little space. Yet despite the squalor, Xion noticed signs of community—children playing with homemade toys, an elderly couple sharing a meager meal, makeshift gardens growing from improbable containers.

"I had no idea," Elara whispered, her voice pitched for Xion's ears alone. "All those years in the compound, and I never truly understood..."

"Few from outside ever come here willingly," Xion replied softly, his own inexperience with the Warrens making him feel like an intruder. "My father's reports never captured... this." He gestured vaguely at the vertical city surrounding them.

The male assassin glanced back, his expression unreadable. "The Warrens have their own rhythm, their own rules. The cartels see only what they wish to see."

They reached what appeared to be the lowest levels—what residents called "the Depths"—where sunlight barely penetrated the narrow gaps between structures. Here, lanterns cast weak, yellowed light that did little to dispel the perpetual twilight. People moved differently here, with the cautious efficiency of those accustomed to navigating in near-darkness.

The female assassin stopped before what looked like an ordinary wall until she pressed against a specific stone. A concealed doorway swung inward, revealing a passage that couldn't possibly connect to the structures above.

"From here, we descend," she said, producing a small lantern that cast a surprisingly bright light. "These tunnels predate the Warrens. Predate much of Kaha'an, in fact."

Xion exchanged a quick glance with Elara. They were leaving the Warrens behind, entering something older, something that shouldn't exist beneath a desert city. The passage sloped downward, the construction changing from the haphazard materials of the Warrens to worked stone with precise joins that spoke of imperial craftsmanship.

"I thought these were just stories," Xion murmured, running his fingers along the smooth stone. "Tales of hidden passages beneath the city..."

"Most things dismissed as stories have seeds of truth," the male assassin replied without turning. "The Long Knives have preserved many such truths."

The air grew cooler as they descended, the oppressive heat of Kaha'an unable to penetrate this deep. Occasionally, Xion noticed Elara studying certain symbols carved into the stonework, her head tilting slightly as if trying to recall something just beyond memory's reach. The same expression she'd worn at the brass door.

After what felt like an hour of walking, the passage widened into a large underground chamber. Ancient stone archways, their surfaces bearing faded imperial insignias, supported a ceiling that disappeared into darkness above. The craftsmanship was unmistakably imperial but of a style Xion had never seen in Kaha'an's surface buildings.

"Wait here," the male assassin instructed, disappearing through an ornate iron door at the far side of the chamber.

Left with the female assassin, Xion took the opportunity to study their surroundings more carefully. Elara had drifted toward one wall, her fingers hovering just above what appeared to be decorative carvings.

"What is this place?" Xion asked, keeping his voice low despite the vast space.

The assassin considered him for a moment. "A meeting hall, from before. Before the empire as you know it, before the cartels, before much of what now stands above us."

"And the Mistress? What can we expect?" He knew he was pushing his luck with questions, but his father had taught him that knowledge was power, especially when entering negotiations.

A ghost of a smile touched the woman's lips. "The Mistress is... exactly what she needs to be. She speaks for the Long Knives, and the Long Knives speak for interests older than you can imagine."

"That's not exactly helpful," Xion muttered.

"It wasn't meant to be."

Elara had returned to his side, her eyes drawn repeatedly to the symbols on the walls. "These markings," she whispered. "They feel... familiar somehow. Like something I should recognize but can't quite place."

Before Xion could respond, the iron door opened, and the male assassin reappeared. "She will see you now."

They followed him through the door into a space that contrasted sharply with the ancient chamber. This room was well-appointed with comfortable furnishings—plush chairs, polished wooden tables, shelves lined with books and scrolls, all illuminated by magical lights that cast no shadows. It felt more like a noble's study than a hidden chamber beneath the city.

Behind a desk of dark, polished wood sat a woman who could only be the Mistress. Her silver hair was cropped close to her scalp, and her amber eyes—the same shade as Farleen's, Xion realized with a jolt—studied them with calculated interest. She wore simple but finely made clothing, with no obvious weapons or symbols of office, yet she radiated an authority that made the room feel suddenly smaller.

"Leave us," she told the assassins, who bowed slightly before withdrawing.

For a long moment, the Mistress said nothing, simply studying them. Xion fought the urge to fidget under her gaze.

"Show me," she finally said, extending her hand.

Xion hesitated only briefly before removing the medallion from around his neck and placing it in her palm. 

She examined it, turning it over in long, elegant fingers. "Rosik gave you this." Not a question.

"Yes."

"Do you understand what it means?"

Xion glanced at Elara, then back to the Mistress. "Not entirely. It's provided protection twice now—once from your people at the brass door, and again during the confrontation with Ronas's captain."

The Mistress nodded slightly. "It serves as both protection and identifier. In a system of alliances as complex as Kaha'an's, such symbols have their uses." She returned the medallion to him. "Wear it openly when you wish our attention, hidden when you wish to avoid it."

She turned her gaze to Elara, and something subtle shifted in her expression. "Princess Elara Valanar," she said, testing the name like one might test the balance of a new blade. "The last scion of House Valanar. Tell me, child, what do you know of arrangements made before your birth?"

Elara straightened, her posture becoming unmistakably royal despite her borrowed clothing. "Very little, I suspect. Commander Vesk has taught me what he could of imperial protocol and history, but the Arol Batae themselves only know what they observed as guards."

"The Arol Batae." The Mistress's lips curved slightly. "They maintain their duty admirably, if... incompletely."

"What do you mean by that?" Elara's eyes flashed blue—caution, Xion had learned.

"There are obligations that predate The Rending, obligations to powers and principles beyond current understanding. The Arol Batae protect the body and bloodline. The Long Knives protect certain knowledge." She leaned forward slightly. "You experienced something at the brass door, did you not?"

Xion saw Elara tense. This was dangerous ground—revealing too much to someone whose allegiances remained unclear.

"I..." Elara began, then seemed to reconsider. "Yes. The door responded to my touch. It felt like... like recognition. Like something awakening after a long sleep."

The Mistress nodded, as if confirming a theory. "And certain symbols in these tunnels evoke similar sensations, though weaker?"

Elara's surprise was evident. "How did you—"

"There are connections we do not fully understand, tied to bloodlines and places of power. The brass door is one such nexus point. There are others." The Mistress rose from her seat, moving with a fluid grace that belied her age. "What matters now is that your emergence has accelerated certain timelines. The cartels already move against you.  And the Erulian Principality is now taking an active interest."

"Prince Kalden," Xion said, the pieces connecting. "His entire claim to imperial heritage rests on being the closest surviving relative to the throne."

"Indeed. And he has spent twenty years carefully positioning himself and his principality. Your existence, Princess, represents an existential threat to everything he has built." The Mistress circled the desk, stopping before Xion. "Where do your loyalties lie, Xion Kemvimore? With your father? With the cartels? Or elsewhere?"

The question caught him off guard. "I serve Kaha'an," he answered carefully. "Not any particular faction within it."

"A diplomatic answer." She studied him with unnerving intensity. "You have her eyes, you know." 

The casual observation struck like a physical blow. His mother. She was talking about his mother. Xion fought to keep his expression neutral, even as questions exploded in his mind. How did the Mistress know his mother? What connection could possibly exist between the leader of the Long Knives and Lady Kemvimore?

The Mistress turned away before he could formulate a response, moving to a small cabinet from which she withdrew a token—a flat disc of some dark metal, inscribed with symbols that matched those on the walls.

"This will ensure your safe passage back to the Arol Batae compound," she said, handing it to Elara. "A different route than the one you traveled here. Less... complicated."

As Elara reached for the token, the Mistress performed a subtle gesture—a slight inclination of her head combined with a specific placement of her fingers across her heart. The movement seemed almost instinctive, as if performed without conscious thought.

"What does that mean?" Elara asked, her curiosity overcoming caution.

The Mistress paused, looking momentarily surprised, as if unaware she had made the gesture at all. "Some traditions persist even when their purpose is forgotten," she said after a moment. "It is an old acknowledgment, from times when the empire was young."

She moved to the door, opening it to reveal two different assassins waiting outside. "These guides will take you through passages that emerge near the Noble District. From there, the Arol Batae compound is a short distance."

Before they could leave, she added, "The choices made in coming days will reshape Kaha'an, regardless of who sits upon the Ruby Throne. Consider carefully who you trust and what price you are willing to pay for the future you envision."

With that cryptic warning hanging in the air, they were ushered out of the chamber and into a different tunnel system than the one they had arrived through. These passages seemed better maintained, with regular magical lighting and occasional benches built into alcoves along the way.

As they walked in silence, Xion's mind whirled with questions. The Mistress's casual reference to his mother suggested a deeper connection than he could have imagined. His father had rarely spoken of Lady Kemvimore after her death during The Rending, and the few portraits that remained showed a gentle, compassionate woman who seemed at odds with Rosik's cold pragmatism.

So lost was he in these thoughts that he barely noticed when Elara stopped abruptly, her attention caught by a particular symbol carved into a junction where three passages met. She reached out, touching it with the same familiarity she had shown at the brass door.

"Elara?" he whispered, concerned by her distant expression.

She blinked, returning to the present. "It's nothing. Just... another echo." She shook her head slightly. "Later. We'll discuss it later."

The assassins led them ever upward, the passages gradually transitioning from ancient stonework to newer construction that incorporated elements of Kaha'an's imperial architecture. Eventually, they emerged through a hidden entrance into an alley between two noble houses, the high wall of the Arol Batae compound visible at the end of the street.

"This is where we leave you," the female assassin said. "The token will get you past their sentries without incident."

Before Xion could thank them, both assassins had vanished, melting into shadows with practiced ease. He turned to find Elara already walking purposefully toward the compound gates, her posture once again that of a princess rather than a fugitive.

As they approached, guards rushed from the gatehouse, weapons drawn until they recognized Elara. Commander Vesk himself appeared moments later, relief washing over his weathered features before being replaced by urgent questions.

"Where have you been? How did you escape the Warrens? Are you injured?" The questions tumbled out in rapid succession.

"I'm fine, Commander," Elara replied, her voice steady with newfound authority. "And I have much to report, but I need time to process what we've learned first."

Vesk looked as if he wanted to argue but instead nodded sharply. "Of course, Princess. But know that we've been searching since your disappearance, and there are urgent matters—"

"Tomorrow, Commander," Elara interrupted gently. "Tonight, we rest."

As they were escorted into the compound, Xion found himself studying Elara's profile in the fading light. She had changed during their journey—not just in the confidence of her commands but in something deeper, more fundamental. Whatever connection she had to the ancient symbols and the brass door, their meeting with the Mistress had strengthened rather than explained it.

And as for himself... Xion touched the medallion beneath his shirt, his thoughts turning to his mother and the Mistress's casual claim that he had "her eyes." What other secrets lay buried in his family's past? And more importantly, how would those secrets shape the future they were racing toward?

The gates of the Arol Batae compound closed behind them with a finality that felt like the turning of a page. One chapter of their journey had ended—but the story, Xion suspected, was only beginning to unfold.
