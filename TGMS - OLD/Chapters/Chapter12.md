## Chapter 12: The Slaver King

Xion's head throbbed with each step as their captors marched them through the labyrinthine passages of the Warrens. The narrow walkways twisted between precarious structures that leaned against one another like drunken revelers afraid of falling. Shadows played across the uneven surfaces, cast by lanterns hanging at sporadic intervals from beams that seemed too frail to support even that modest weight.

"Move," growled the red-bearded captain, shoving Xion forward when he slowed to regain his balance on the uneven boards beneath his feet.

Elara caught his arm before he could stumble, her eyes shifting momentarily to silver – fear – before returning to their neutral blue. Even in these dire circumstances, she maintained remarkable control over her emotions. Xion envied her that discipline.

"Any idea where they're taking us?" Elara whispered, her voice barely audible over the ambient sounds of the Warrens – distant arguments, the crying of children, the creaking of wooden structures settling precariously against one another.

"Not exactly," Xion replied, keeping his voice equally low. "But they mentioned 'Long Knives business' and their 'boss.' I fear we're being taken to Ronas Dermir himself."

At the mention of Ronas's name, one of their captors grinned unpleasantly. "Smart man. Let's see how clever you feel after meeting the boss."

Xion's stomach tightened. He'd seen Ronas Dermir at his father's house during the cartel gathering—a lean, weathered man with cold eyes who had openly sneered at the "niceties" of noble protocol. The Slaver lord had seemed dangerous even in the controlled environment of the Kemvimore estate. Here, in his territory, surrounded by his men...

They passed through increasingly organized sections of the Warrens. Xion noticed strategic barricades at key junctions and armed men standing guard at important passageways. This wasn't the chaotic warren of desperate people he'd imagined—there was clear organization here, a system of control. In some rooms they passed, he glimpsed captives bound with rope, awaiting transport or sale.

After climbing a narrow stairway that swayed alarmingly with each step, they emerged into a wider space dominated by a central stone building. The area around it had been cleared, creating a small courtyard that served as a buffer between the structure and the surrounding chaos of the Warrens.

"An imperial granary," Xion murmured, recognizing the architecture. The sturdy stone building had clearly been built before The Rending, its solid construction standing in stark contrast to the improvised structures around it.

"What?" Elara questioned, her gaze fixed on the building.

"It's an old imperial granary. They were built throughout the city to store food reserves. This one must have been repurposed after The Rending."

Their conversation was cut short as they were marched toward the entrance, guarded by men in better armor than their captors. These guards stood straight, their weapons well-maintained—a level of discipline that spoke of proper training.

"Found these two sneaking around near the Door," the red-bearded captain explained, holding up Xion's medallion. "Got Long Knives business, by the look of this."

The guards exchanged glances before the senior one nodded and stepped aside. "The boss is with the captains. Mind your manners."

Inside, the granary had been transformed. What was once a utilitarian storage facility now resembled a crude throne room. The central space had been cleared, with a wooden table surrounded by arguing men at its center. Above them, on a raised platform where supervisors would have once monitored the grain stores, sat a man in a high-backed chair.

Ronas Dermir.

Unlike at the cartel gathering, where he'd dressed with crude approximation of nobility, here Ronas wore simple, practical clothing. His watchful eyes surveyed the arguing captains below him with the detached interest of a man accustomed to weighing human conflict as a merchant might weigh goods.

"The Iron Lady wants twenty more for her foundry," one captain insisted, thumping the table for emphasis.

"And what do we feed the rest with the shipments coming up short?" countered another, an older man with gray at his temples. "The Depths need—"

"To hell with the Depths," interrupted a third. "We fulfill Sa Ko Ren's order, we get priority on her steel. That's worth more than—"

Ronas raised his hand slightly, and the argument ceased instantly. The silence that followed was more unnerving than the shouting. His gaze shifted from the captains to the new arrivals, and recognition dawned immediately as his eyes settled on Xion.

"Well," Ronas said, his voice surprisingly cultured for a man with his reputation, "if it isn't Rosik Kemvimore's boy. Last I saw you, you were standing behind your father's chair like a good heir, looking uncomfortable with the proceedings." His lips curled into something between a sneer and a smile. "Now you're in my house, looking even more uncomfortable. What an interesting development."

The captains turned to stare at Xion, reevaluating him with this new information. Xion felt Elara tense beside him.

"Leave us," Ronas commanded, and the captains immediately began filing out. "Not you, Merreck," he added, indicating the captain with a jagged scar across his face. "And you three," he pointed to their captors, "explain why you've brought the heir to the Grain to my doorstep."

The red-bearded captain stepped forward, holding up the medallion. "Found him and the girl near the brass door, boss. He was carrying this."

Ronas leaned forward, eyes narrowing at the sight of the medallion. He studied it for a long moment before leaning back in his chair.

"Everyone out except Merreck and our... guests."

The other men filed out, leaving only Ronas, the scarred captain, Xion, and Elara in the repurposed granary. Ronas descended from his elevated seat with the careful movements of a man who had learned to never show weakness.

"So," he said, standing before Xion, "the son of the Grain Lord wanders my territory carrying a Long Knives token. Rosik's boy with an assassin's medallion." He tilted his head slightly. "I find myself curious about the story behind that."

Xion swallowed. His throat felt impossibly dry. "We needed to move through the city undetected."

"And yet, here you are, very much detected." Ronas took the medallion from the table where the captain had placed it, examining it with practiced eyes. Then, to Xion's surprise, he held it out. "Take it. It's yours, after all."

Hesitantly, Xion accepted the medallion, careful to keep his movement slow and non-threatening.

"Follow me," Ronas said, turning toward a side door. "You as well, girl," he added to Elara, who had remained silent throughout the exchange.

Merreck positioned himself behind them, his hand resting casually on his sword hilt, making it clear that refusal wasn't an option. They followed Ronas through the door into what appeared to be his private quarters—a surprisingly modest room with a simple desk, a few chairs, and shelves containing an unexpected number of books.

"Sit," Ronas instructed, indicating two chairs across from his desk.

As they took their seats, Xion noted the absence of the ostentatious displays of wealth that characterized his father's study. Everything here was practical, utilitarian—the room of a man concerned with function over appearance.

"Now," Ronas said, settling into his own chair, "let's try this again. What brings the heir to the Grain cartel and his mysterious companion to the depths of my territory?"

Xion hesitated, weighing how much to reveal. Before he could decide, Elara spoke for the first time since entering the building.

"Necessity," she answered simply. Her eyes remained steady blue, betraying none of the concern Xion knew she must feel.

Ronas studied her for a moment, then smiled—a thin, knowing expression that never reached his eyes. "Necessity drives most decisions in the Warrens. But necessity comes in many forms. Are you running toward something... or away from something?"

"Both, perhaps," Elara replied, matching his measured tone.

Ronas nodded slowly, as if her answer confirmed something. He turned his attention back to Xion. "Your father did not strike me as the type to send his heir wandering into the Warrens on a whim. Especially not carrying that." He nodded toward the medallion in Xion's hand. "So I find myself wondering what game is being played here."

Xion saw no benefit in lying—not completely, anyway. "We needed to move through the city without being detected by the cartels."

"Including your father's cartel?"

"Especially my father's cartel."

This drew a genuine, if brief, chuckle from Ronas. "Family trouble, then? Or something more complicated?"

Before Xion could respond, shouts erupted from the main hall. Merreck tensed, his hand moving to his weapon.

The door burst open as the red-bearded captain pushed his way in, ignoring Merreck's attempt to block him. "Boss, Vark's taking men to the door! Says he's tired of waiting, going to see what's behind it himself!"

Ronas's expression darkened instantly. "That ambitious fool." He stood, then paused, glancing at Xion and Elara with sudden suspicion. "You two. When my men found you, how close were you to the brass door?"

Xion and Elara exchanged glances. There was no point denying it.

"We saw it," Xion admitted.

Ronas cursed, a string of obscenities that would have made a dockworker blush. "Merreck, assemble the loyal men. Now." As the scarred captain hurried out, Ronas fixed his gaze on Xion and Elara. "You're coming with us. You've seen the door—that makes you part of this problem now."

"What is the door?" Elara asked, rising to her feet. "Why is it important?"

"It's part of an arrangement," Ronas growled, strapping on a sword belt. "An arrangement your little expedition has complicated." He pointed at them. "Stay close, do exactly as I say, and you might survive the next hour. That door is not to be trifled with."

They followed Ronas into the main hall, where Merreck had already assembled about fifteen men. Ronas quickly issued orders, organizing them into a response force. Xion was struck by the efficiency of it all—this was not the chaotic brutality he'd expected from the Slavers, but something more disciplined, more calculated.

As they moved out of the granary and back into the Warrens, Xion found himself beside Merreck. "Why does Ronas fear the door?" he asked quietly.

Merreck glanced at him, the scar on his face pulling his mouth into a permanent half-grimace. "Some say there's treasure beyond it—ancient imperial wealth or magical artifacts from before The Rending. Others claim it leads to Lathion, the City of Gears. And some believe it's a gate to something much worse."

"And what does Ronas believe?"

"The boss?" Merreck's expression grew somber. "He believes that door is best left alone. We have an understanding with the Long Knives about it—we don't touch it, they maintain a watch. It's been that way for decades."

"And this Vark is violating that understanding?"

"Vark's ambitious and greedy. He's been pushing to explore beyond the door for years. The boss always shut him down." Merreck shook his head. "But with all the rumors lately—about the princess, about changes coming—Vark must have decided to make his move while everyone's distracted."

They descended through the levels of the Warrens, passing through the increasingly precarious structures of the lower regions. The ambient light grew dimmer, the air thicker with the smell of damp and decay. Xion could feel Elara close beside him, her steps sure despite the unfamiliar territory. If she was afraid, she hid it well.

As they approached the passage leading to the brass door, the sounds of combat reached them—metal striking metal, shouts of pain and anger echoing through the narrow corridors.

Ronas held up his hand, stopping their advance. "Merreck, form up. The rest of you, weapons ready but hold unless I give the order." He turned to Xion and Elara. "Stay behind me. If things go badly, run—though I doubt you'd get far."

They rounded the corner to find a scene of chaos. Several bodies lay motionless on the ground, while others struggled in combat. Vark's men—identifiable by red bands tied around their arms—fought against black-clad figures that moved with uncanny grace.

Long Knives assassins.

At the far end of the chamber, the massive brass door gleamed in the torchlight, its intricate engravings seeming to shift and flow with the flickering flames. Before it stood the red-bearded captain—Vark himself—directing several men who were attempting to wedge metal tools into the seam of the door.

"VARK!" Ronas's voice boomed through the chamber, cutting through the sounds of battle. "STAND DOWN!"

The fighting paused momentarily as all eyes turned toward the new arrivals. Vark spun around, his face contorted with a mixture of surprise and defiance.

"Boss," he acknowledged, making no move to comply.

"I gave explicit orders that the door was not to be approached," Ronas said, stepping forward. His voice was calm, almost conversational, which somehow made it more menacing. "Yet here you are."

Vark's eyes narrowed, then widened as they locked onto Xion. "Him!" he shouted, pointing an accusatory finger. "That noble and his woman! They were here earlier, examining the door! They know what's behind it!"

All eyes turned toward Xion and Elara. The Long Knives assassins tensed, their weapons shifting slightly, preparing to strike.

"That's not—" Xion began, but Vark was already pushing past his men, charging toward them with murder in his eyes.

"You led us here!" Vark shouted, drawing a wicked-looking blade. "You know the secret!"

Before Vark could reach them, Ronas stepped into his path. The red-bearded captain skidded to a halt, suddenly unsure.

"Boss, they're working with the Long Knives! They led us here to—"

"Enough," Ronas said, his voice so quiet that it silenced the room more effectively than a shout. "You disobeyed a direct order. You've broken our arrangement with the Long Knives. And now you threaten valuable... guests."

"Guests?" Vark spat. "That's the son of Rosik Kemvimore! And who knows who the woman is! They're not guests, they're spies! There's treasure behind that door, boss. Enough to make us all rich, to make us more than just the lowly Slavers everyone looks down on!"

For a moment, Ronas's expression softened, almost sympathetic. "There's always been men like you, Vark. Always thinking the next room holds the solution to all their problems."

With a movement too quick for Xion to follow, Ronas drew his blade and plunged it into Vark's chest. The red-bearded captain's eyes widened in shock, his mouth opening and closing without sound.

"The real treasure," Ronas said quietly as he withdrew his blade and let Vark's body slump to the floor, "is knowing which doors to leave closed."

The stunned silence lasted only seconds before Vark's loyalists surged forward with cries of outrage. Merreck and Ronas's men met them head-on, steel clashing against steel in the confined space.

Xion pulled Elara back against the wall, trying to shield her from the violence. As he did, he felt the medallion slip from beneath his clothing, hanging exposed on its chain.

A Long Knives assassin broke through the melee, blade raised to strike them down—then froze, eyes locked on the medallion. The assassin's gaze flicked from the medallion to Xion's face, then to Elara, seemingly evaluating something. Without a word, the black-clad figure lowered their weapon and melted back into the fight, deliberately turning away from them.

"The medallion," Elara whispered. "It protected us."

Xion clutched the metal disk, warm against his palm. What had his father given him? And why?

The fighting grew more desperate as Vark's men realized they were outmatched. Some threw down their weapons in surrender, while others fought to the bitter end. Through it all, Ronas and Merreck moved with practiced efficiency, their blades finding targets with surgical precision.

Within minutes, it was over. Vark's loyalists lay dead or disarmed, while the Long Knives assassins stood in a loose formation before the brass door, their stance defensive but no longer actively hostile.

Ronas approached the nearest assassin, wiping blood from his blade. "Tell your Mistress that the arrangement stands. This was the action of one ambitious fool, not a change in policy."

The assassin nodded once, a barely perceptible movement.

"The witnesses?" the assassin asked, voice muffled by their mask as they glanced toward Xion and Elara.

Ronas turned to look at them, his expression calculating. "They're my responsibility now. I'll ensure they reach the Mistress safely for... consultation."

Another slight nod from the assassin, and then, as if on some silent signal, the Long Knives began withdrawing into the shadows around the door, vanishing with unsettling efficiency until only Ronas's men remained with the dead and captured.

Ronas approached Xion and Elara, studying them with renewed interest. "It seems that medallion of yours grants certain... considerations from our blade-loving friends. Interesting."

"I don't understand what just happened," Xion admitted.

"What happened," Ronas replied, sheathing his weapon, "is that you stumbled into affairs far beyond your understanding, young Kemvimore. That door and what lies beyond it are part of arrangements that predate my leadership of the Slavers. Arrangements I've honored, as did my predecessor, and his before him."

He gestured to Merreck, who was overseeing the binding of the survivors. "Take the prisoners back to the granary. I'll deal with them later."

As Merreck led the captives away, Ronas turned his attention back to Xion and Elara. "Now, I find myself with a dilemma. You've seen the door. You carry a token of the Long Knives. And you're the son of Rosik Kemvimore, one of the four most powerful men in Kaha'an." He sighed. "Complicated."

Elara stepped forward, her posture straight and confident despite the chaos they'd just witnessed. "What will you do with us?"

Ronas studied her with newfound curiosity. "You're an interesting one. Not noble-born, I'd wager, despite your companion. But trained—military, perhaps?" He shook his head. "No matter. What I'll do is precisely what I told our shadowy friends—deliver you to the Mistress of the Long Knives."

"Why?" Xion asked.

"Because she'll want to know who's wandering around with her tokens, examining forbidden doors." Ronas's expression hardened. "And because I want no part in whatever game is being played between your father and the Long Knives. That's their business."

He gestured to two of his men, who approached without a word. "These two will escort you to a meeting point. The Long Knives will take you from there."

Xion hesitated, sensing this might be their only chance to speak freely with Ronas. "Before we go... why did you kill Vark? Truly?"

Something flickered across Ronas's face—not anger, but something more complex. "Because he threatened the stability I've built here. The Warrens exist in a delicate balance, Kemvimore. Your father and the other cartel lords look down on us, but they need us. We provide the labor they require while maintaining order in a place they'd rather pretend doesn't exist."

He glanced toward the brass door, his expression momentarily distant. "Some doors remain closed for good reason. Some arrangements, no matter how unusual, serve a purpose beyond immediate gain."

Ronas turned back to them, his calculating demeanor returning. "When you see your father, tell him his grain shipments to the Warrens have been short by fifteen percent for the last three months. He thinks we don't notice such things. We do."

With that, he nodded to the two men who would be their escorts. "Take them to the eastern meeting point. The Long Knives will be waiting."

As they were led away from the chamber with its mysterious brass door, Xion glanced back at Ronas Dermir—the brutal slaver lord who had just revealed himself to be something far more complex than the monster of childhood stories. The man noticed his gaze and held it for a moment before turning away, barking orders to his remaining men about removing the bodies.

Elara's hand found Xion's in the dim light of the passage. "From one cage to another," she whispered, but there was determination in her voice rather than resignation.

Xion squeezed her hand in response, feeling the weight of the medallion against his chest. Whatever his father's game was, whatever connection existed between the Long Knives and the mysterious brass door, they were now irrevocably entangled in it. And somehow, he suspected, so was Elara.

They ascended through the twisting passages of the Warrens, following their silent escorts toward whatever awaited them next.
