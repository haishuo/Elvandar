# Chapter 17: The Ruby Throne

Dawn broke over the Arol Batae compound, golden light spilling across stone walls that had hidden secrets for two decades. Today, those secrets would step into the light. Xion watched from a window as guards moved with practiced efficiency, checking weapons and adjusting armor. The quiet bustle of preparation created a strange counterpoint to the momentous nature of what they were about to attempt.

They were going to march to the Ruby Palace. They were going to reclaim a throne.

A knock at his door pulled Xion from his thoughts. Keiran stood there, his face still bruised from his captivity, but his eyes clear and determined.

"The Commander wants to see you," he said, voice rough from healing injuries. "Something about the information from my interrogation."

Xion nodded. Keiran had insisted on resuming duties despite his wounds, refusing to rest while others prepared. "Does he want me now?"

"Everyone's gathering in the council chamber. It's... important."

The council chamber buzzed with activity. Commander Vesk stood at the center, surrounded by the Inner Circle, their faces serious. Elara had not yet arrived.

"Kemvimore," Vesk acknowledged as Xion entered. "Keiran has something we need to hear before today's march begins."

Keiran stepped forward, wincing slightly. "My interrogators were obsessed with the tunnels beneath the palace. Not just general questions—specific ones about access points, ancient passages. They knew things even I didn't know existed."

Hedrin looked up from a stack of imperial records. "There are mentions in these scrolls of passages predating the palace itself. The imperial residence was deliberately built over... something. The records are frustratingly vague."

Xion frowned. "This connects to Elara's reaction at the brass door, doesn't it? Whatever she sensed there, whatever made those markings glow..."

"The same force that made her able to navigate passages she's never seen," Vesk agreed grimly.

The conversation halted as Elara entered, flanked by Lydia and Salia. She wore formal attire that balanced imperial tradition with practical mobility—deep blue fabric with subtle embroidery that wouldn't restrict movement if trouble came. Her eyes were the calm sapphire that marked her neutral state, but Xion could see the tension in her shoulders.

"A message arrived from Farleen," Vesk said, holding up a small scroll. "Various noble houses are already positioning themselves following Sa Ko Ren's declaration. Some seek advantage, others fear change."

"And no doubt, some report to the Erulian Principality," Hedrin added. "Prince Kalden will not take news of your existence lightly."

Elara nodded, her expression composed despite the weight of what they were about to attempt. "Our course is set. What matters now is how we proceed."

Vesk moved to a large map of the city. "The procession forms here," he said, placing markers on the table. "Iron cartel guards integrated visibly with our own forces—Sa Ko Ren's public declaration of support. We maintain defensive positions without appearing overly militaristic. This is not a conquest but a return."

The rest of the morning passed in a flurry of final preparations. Runners moved between buildings carrying messages. Guards rehearsed formations. Scouts reported increasing crowds gathering along their planned route, word having spread despite attempts at secrecy.

When the time came to depart, Xion found himself standing near Elara as the gates opened. Sunlight flooded the courtyard as the procession formed, the blue and silver of the Arol Batae mixing with the dark red of Sa Ko Ren's Iron guards.

"Ready?" he asked, knowing the question was inadequate for the moment.

Elara's eyes briefly flashed violet—determination—before returning to blue. "Twenty years waiting. Yes, I'm ready."

The procession moved out into the streets of Kaha'an, and immediately Xion sensed the difference in the air. Word had spread faster than seemed possible. Citizens lined the streets, their expressions ranging from curiosity to hope to suspicion. Some appeared only to watch a spectacle; others stood with hands clasped as if witnessing something sacred.

"Look at their faces," Elara murmured. "They don't know whether to bow or run."

Xion nodded, scanning the crowd. "Most have only heard stories of the imperial family. For twenty years, the cartels have been their only reality."

They moved steadily through the Noble District, where the wealthiest citizens watched from balconies or behind guards. Here the reactions were most guarded—these were the people with the most to lose if power shifted. Yet even among them, Xion could see calculation in their eyes. Opportunity, not just threat.

The noise grew as they approached the main avenue leading to the Imperial Quarter. What had begun as scattered observers had become a genuine crowd, people pushing to get a better view.

"Her eyes!" someone shouted. "Did you see? They changed color!"

The whisper spread through the crowd like fire through dry brush. Elara had allowed a flash of emotion to show—perhaps deliberately, Xion realized—and the distinctive mark of the Valanar bloodline had been seen.

As they entered the Imperial Quarter, Xion tensed. This was traditionally where challenges would occur in formal processions—where representatives might step forward to oppose or support the claim. Sa Ko Ren's guards shifted formation subtly, preparing for potential resistance.

It came, but not as expected.

Tania Larannas stepped into their path, flanked by Water cartel representatives in blue and white. The crowd fell silent. Silvanno's mother was an imposing figure without raising her voice, her silver-streaked hair framing a face that had navigated decades of Kaha'an politics.

"Lady Valanar," Tania said, using neither princess nor empress as title—a calculated middle ground. "Kaha'an finds itself at an unexpected crossroads today."

Elara stepped forward, her posture perfect. "Mistress Larannas. Does the Water cartel come to challenge or recognize my claim?"

Tania's lips curved in a slight smile. "The Water offers no challenge today. But recognition comes with consideration of mutual interests."

Of course, Xion thought. The Water wouldn't oppose directly—not with the Iron already declared. Instead, they would negotiate.

"The Water cartel has maintained Kaha'an's maritime connections since the Rending," Tania continued. "Our ships, our trading partners, our distribution networks. The Water would recognize your claim in exchange for guarantees regarding continued control of these essential functions."

The crowd held its breath. This was politics played in public—a rarity in Kaha'an, where deals were typically struck in shadows.

Elara's response was masterful. "The throne recognizes the Water's essential service to Kaha'an and values its expertise. Formal agreements will follow formal restoration."

It wasn't quite acceptance, but neither was it rejection. Tania nodded, apparently satisfied for the moment. With a gesture, she had her representatives fall in with the procession. The Water had neither fully committed nor opposed—but their presence in the march sent a clear message.

They continued toward the Ruby Palace, its red stone gleaming in the midday sun. Palace guards stood at attention at the main gates, their faces revealing conflict. They had served the empty throne for twenty years, maintaining the fiction that the emperor and empress might someday return.

The captain stepped forward, a grizzled veteran whose armor bore the imperial crest. "Halt," he commanded. "Who seeks entrance to the imperial residence?"

The formal challenge. Elara stepped forward alone.

"I am Elara Valanar, daughter of Emperor Tiberan Valanar IV and Empress Lyra Valanar, rightful heir to the Ruby Throne of Kaha'an and the Balishan Empire."

The captain's eyes narrowed. "Many have claimed imperial lineage in twenty years. What proof do you offer?"

Elara removed the tinted spectacles she had worn during their journey. "The blood of Valanar speaks for itself."

As she spoke, emotion rippled through her—not fear or anger, but something deeper, more primal. The connection to her heritage, to the palace before her. Her eyes shifted color visibly, the blue melting into a swirl of violet that no artifice could imitate.

The captain stared, his composure cracking. He had seen those eyes before, in another life, on another face.

"The royal trait," he whispered, then louder: "Make way for her imperial highness!"

The palace gates swung open. Twenty years of emptiness ended with those simple words.

Inside, Elara navigated the corridors with strange familiarity. Though she had never set foot in these halls, she turned at junctions with confident steps, occasionally pausing to touch a wall or glance at a fresco as if confirming a half-remembered dream.

"How are you doing this?" Xion whispered as they approached the throne room. "You've never been here before."

Elara shook her head slightly. "I don't know. It feels... remembered, somehow. Like walking through a place I've seen in dreams my entire life."

Hedrin kept pace with them, providing historical context in hushed tones. "These corridors follow patterns established during the early imperial period. The layout is said to have mystical significance, though most scholars dismiss such claims as superstition."

Yet Elara's inexplicable knowledge suggested otherwise. Something connected her to this place beyond conscious memory or education.

They reached the massive doors to the throne room, guarded by elite palace sentinels. These men had maintained their posts through twenty years of emptiness, keeping watch over a throne no one claimed.

"Only essential personnel beyond this point," the lead sentinel declared.

Elara nodded and made her selections. "Xion Kemvimore, Hedrin Vall, Commander Vesk." She paused, making a politically astute addition: "And representatives from the Iron and Water cartels."

The throne room doors swung open, revealing a vast chamber that had stood mostly empty for two decades. Sunlight streamed through high windows of stained glass, painting colored patterns across marble floors. At the far end, raised on a dais of seven steps, stood the Ruby Throne itself—a massive chair carved from a single piece of deep red stone that gave the palace its name.

Cartel representatives who maintained offices in the palace appeared from side chambers, drawn by word of their arrival. They formed a loose semicircle, expressions guarded.

A thin man in formal robes stepped forward. "The palace administration requires verification of imperial claims before recognition can be formally extended."

Rosik's representative—not Rosik himself, Xion noted—stood to one side, observing without direct challenge. Melina was there too, carefully positioned to indicate support without overcommitment.

Elara ignored the bureaucrat's words and walked forward, her steps measured and deliberate. The throne room fell silent as she approached the dais. Seven steps. Seven successor states. Seven sins that had fractured the empire.

As she reached the throne, her hand extended naturally to the right armrest where an imperial seal was embedded in the stone—a small circle of gold and crystal that had been placed there generations ago.

"Princess," Hedrin murmured, "the seal is said to confirm imperial bloodline. Legend claims it responds only to true Valanar descendants."

Elara nodded once and placed her palm firmly on the seal.

For a heartbeat, nothing happened. Then the seal began to glow with a soft blue light that spread up her arm and across her body. The light intensified, brightening until it cast shadows behind the assembled witnesses. Then, in a moment that those present would speak of for years to come, ancient ward lights dormant for decades illuminated across the chamber.

Patterns of light raced along the ceiling and walls, following channels carved into the stone that had been dark for so long many had forgotten their purpose. The entire throne room came alive with blue-white light that converged above the throne in a complex pattern resembling a celestial map.

Palace guards witnessing this dropped to one knee in the traditional imperial salute—a gesture not seen in the palace for twenty years.

The guard captain stepped forward, his face transformed by wonder. "The wards recognize her. The light answers to Valanar blood." He knelt formally. "Your Imperial Highness."

Somewhere in the palace, a mechanism activated. Great horns mounted on the palace towers sounded—a sonorous call that echoed across Kaha'an. The ancient ceremonial announcement: an imperial heir had claimed the Ruby Throne.

Across the city, reactions varied with location and allegiance.

In the Iron district, celebrations erupted as Sa Ko Ren's people hung additional imperial banners beside their own symbols. Workers paused at forges and foundries, raising cups to toast the return of imperial rule—or at least, a version of it that included their leader at the table of power.

In the Water district, citizens gathered in public squares to discuss implications while Tania's officials maintained careful order. Neither celebration nor protest was encouraged; the Water would wait and observe which way the currents flowed before fully committing.

In the Grain district, Melina made public statements supporting "the return of imperial stability" while Rosik himself remained conspicuously absent. Grain supplies continued flowing through the city—business as usual even as power shifted overhead.

In the Warrens, reactions were most mixed. Some saw hope in a power beyond the cartels; others feared stricter enforcement and less freedom in their shadowy domain. The Slavers' enforcers watched carefully for signs of unrest, while Long Knives operatives slipped through crowds gathering intelligence.

In the throne room, Elara sat upon the Ruby Throne for the first time. The ward lights continued to glow around her, stronger near her presence as if responding to her very essence. Officials bowed and backed away, scurrying to spread her proclamation throughout the palace.

"I will convene a council tomorrow," she declared, her voice steady and clear. "All cartel representatives will attend. The governance of Kaha'an enters a new chapter today."

As the throne room cleared, Elara and Xion shared a brief moment while Commander Vesk organized security with the palace guards.

"The palace feels..." Elara began, then hesitated.

"Familiar?" Xion suggested.

She nodded. "Beyond what Hedrin taught me. I know places I've never seen, passages I've never walked." Her eyes drifted to a particular wall section. "There's something there. A passage that doesn't appear on any maps."

"Like your reaction at the brass door," Xion said quietly. "Whatever connection you felt there—it's here too. Something about your heritage goes deeper than anyone realized."

Vesk approached, his expression a mixture of pride and concern. "The palace is secure for now, Your Highness. We're coordinating with existing guards to establish proper protocols."

Elara nodded, her gaze returning to the throne room's vaulted ceiling where the ward lights continued to pulse gently. "Twenty years of darkness," she murmured. "Not just in these halls. In all of Kaha'an."

As sunset light filtered through the ancient stained glass, painting the Ruby Throne in crimson shadows, imperial banners unfurled from the palace towers for the first time in two decades. Their blue and silver stood stark against the deepening sky—a visible declaration to the city below that everything had changed.

Elara Valanar had come home to a place she'd never been. The Ruby Throne had found its heir.

And somewhere in the distance, invisible from the palace heights, dust rose along Kaha'an's western border.
