## Chapter 11: Desperate Measures

Xion and Elara climbed upward from the ancient tunnels, emerging into cramped spaces unlike anything either had ever experienced. The solid stonework of the tunnels gave way to a precarious maze of wooden platforms, makeshift ladders, and structures that seemed to defy both gravity and logic. Dim light filtered through cracks and gaps overhead, revealing glimpses of a vertical labyrinth that stretched upward farther than they could see.

"The Depths," Xion whispered, recalling a term he'd heard mentioned in fearful tones. "I think that's what they call the lowest levels of the Warrens."

Elara's eyes—currently a deep, cautious blue—scanned their surroundings. The air was thick with the stench of human waste, rotting food, and unwashed bodies. Wooden supports groaned ominously around them, bearing the unfathomable weight of the structures piled above.

"Which way do we go?" she asked quietly.

Xion swallowed hard. "Up, I think. Away from here. But..." he hesitated, embarrassment coloring his voice. "I don't actually know how to get anywhere. I've never been in the Warrens before."

The admission hung between them, underscoring their vulnerability. Rumors and scraps of information from patients at his clinic were all Xion had to navigate a district notorious for swallowing outsiders whole. For Elara, whose entire life had been confined to the ordered environment of the Arol Batae compound, the chaotic sprawl was entirely alien.

"Let's just... find people," Xion suggested weakly. "Maybe someone who can help us."

Elara glanced back toward the passage they'd emerged from, her expression distant. "The connection is fading," she murmured. "To the door. I can still feel it tugging, but it's growing weaker."

"We can't go back that way," Xion reminded her gently. "Not with the Long Knives searching for us."

She nodded, squaring her shoulders. "Forward, then. Or upward, I suppose."

They chose a passage at random, climbing a series of rickety platforms that seemed to serve as stairs. Every structure creaked alarmingly beneath their weight. Passages branched in all directions without pattern—some leading up, others down or sideways. Nothing marked their path; no signs, no consistent features to orient themselves.

"How does anyone find their way through this place?" Elara whispered after they'd been walking for what felt like hours, having changed direction countless times after encountering dead ends or unstable passages.

"I've heard they have guides—children called the Lantern Boys who know all the paths," Xion replied, his voice betraying his mounting anxiety. "But I have no idea how to find one."

The perpetual twilight made time impossible to judge. They'd emerged from what felt like one ramshackle dwelling only to find themselves in another, the boundaries between structures often indistinguishable. Occasionally, they would catch glimpses of residents who would quickly disappear at the sight of strangers, melting into hidden doorways or side passages.

"Our clothes," Elara observed after another group of locals vanished at their approach. "They mark us as outsiders."

Xion nodded grimly. He'd known they would stand out, but the reality was worse than he'd imagined. Even in the dim light, their garments—practical but well-made for their original excursion into the middle districts—might as well have been imperial banners announcing their foreign status.

After more aimless wandering, they heard faint sounds of activity somewhere ahead. Xion held up a hand, signaling Elara to pause.

"People," he whispered. "Maybe a gathering place. But we need to be less conspicuous."

They advanced cautiously toward the noise, hugging the shadows of the narrow passage. Rounding a corner, Xion spotted an alcove where several sets of ragged garments hung, apparently drying after being washed. The clothes were patched and filthy, bearing the unmistakable signs of Warrens life.

"There," Xion pointed. "We can borrow those."

Elara's eyebrows rose. "Borrow?"

"Take," Xion amended, then sighed. "Steal. We don't have much choice." He reached into a hidden pocket and withdrew a small silver coin. "I'll leave payment. It's probably worth more than everything hanging there."

"Nobility even in desperation," Elara observed with a hint of admiration. "You continue to surprise me, Xion Kemvimore."

They waited until the immediate area was clear before moving quickly to the alcove. Xion selected two sets of the least-ragged clothing, handing one to Elara. The awkwardness of their situation suddenly struck him as they faced the necessity of changing in such close quarters.

"I'll... turn my back," he stammered, feeling heat rise to his cheeks.

A flicker of amusement crossed Elara's face. "As will I," she replied. "Though I'd remind you that modesty is something of a luxury in combat training. The Arol Batae were practical about such matters."

They turned away from each other, maintaining what dignity the circumstances allowed. Xion changed swiftly, grimacing at the rough feel of the borrowed clothing against his skin. Before pulling on the worn shirt completely, he carefully removed the Long Knives medallion from his original clothes and tucked it safely beneath his new garment.

"Are you decent?" he asked over his shoulder.

"As decent as one can be in these," Elara replied dryly.

When Xion turned, he barely recognized her. The poised imperial heir had vanished, replaced by what appeared to be just another Warrens resident. The ill-fitting clothes hung loosely on her athletic frame. Only her eyes—currently a cautious silver-blue—might give her away if someone looked too closely.

"Keep your head down," Xion advised. "Your eyes..."

Elara nodded. "I've spent my entire life controlling them," she reminded him, her voice tight with concentration.

Xion placed the silver coin beneath the spot where they had taken the clothes, then gestured toward the sound of voices. "Let's try to blend in and find out where we are."

They moved cautiously, neither too hurried nor too hesitant, attempting to mimic the world-weary gait they'd observed in the few residents they'd encountered. The sounds grew louder as they approached what appeared to be a junction where several paths converged from different levels.

Rounding a final corner, they emerged into a surprisingly active space. Despite the perpetual twilight, dozens of people moved with purpose. Children darted between adults, carrying small parcels. Men and women traded goods or gathered in small clusters, speaking in low voices. At the center stood a large circular well—clearly an important gathering point.

"I didn't expect this," Elara murmured. "There's so much... life here."

"People adapt," Xion replied softly. "Even to the harshest conditions."

They lingered at the edge of the gathering, uncertain how to proceed. Near the well, an elderly man had attracted a small audience, his voice rising and falling in the practiced cadence of a storyteller. Curious, they edged closer.

"...and they say the Empress's eyes would shift like the seasons," the old man was saying, his gnarled hands making expansive gestures. "Blue as the summer sky when she was calm, amber as harvest wheat when angered, gray as winter storms in sorrow..."

Xion felt Elara stiffen beside him. He placed a restraining hand on her arm, silently warning her to maintain their anonymity despite this unexpected reference to her family trait.

"When the young prince displeased her," the storyteller continued, "her eyes would flash like lightning, golden and terrible! But when she gazed upon her infant daughter, they turned green as spring leaves, so great was her joy."

The crowd murmured appreciatively. Clearly, this was a familiar tale, one they had heard before but enjoyed nonetheless.

"I didn't realize knowledge of the royal trait was so widespread," Xion whispered, genuinely shocked.

"Neither did I," Elara replied, her voice barely audible. "The Arol Batae always treated it as a closely guarded secret."

"Stories persist," Xion mused. "Even when those in power would prefer them forgotten."

As the storyteller concluded his tale and the crowd began to disperse, Xion noticed several women wearing blue headwraps moving with an air of authority around the well, directing younger people carrying water vessels. Something about their demeanor suggested importance.

Before they could decide on their next move, one of the women with a blue headwrap approached them directly. She was older, her face lined with decades of hardship, but her eyes remained sharp and evaluating.

"You two," she said simply, her voice low but carrying. "You're not from here."

It wasn't a question. Xion swallowed hard, panic rising. Before he could formulate a response, the woman continued.

"Don't bother with lies, boy. Your hands have never seen a day of real labor, and despite those rags, you both stand too straight. The Depths bend people, body and spirit."

Xion sighed, recognizing the futility of further deception. "We're trying to reach the upper levels," he admitted softly. "We're lost."

The woman studied them both, her gaze lingering on Elara's downturned face. With a suddenness that startled them both, she reached forward and lifted Elara's chin, looking directly into her eyes. Something like recognition flickered across the old woman's weathered features.

"Interesting," she murmured.

Elara maintained remarkable control, her eyes remaining a steady blue despite the unexpected contact. The woman dropped her hand and stepped back.

"Juric's crossing is your best path upward," she said after a moment of consideration. "Follow the main passage until you reach the drying racks. Turn left at the red banner, then right where the support beams cross. Three levels up, there's a bridge."

She reached into a pouch at her waist and withdrew a small object, pressing it into Elara's hand. It was a token carved from bone, worn smooth from handling, with an unfamiliar symbol etched into its surface.

"Show this to anyone wearing the blue wrap," she instructed. "They'll help you. But be careful—there's more movement than usual today. Something has the spiders stirring in their webs."

"Thank you," Elara said, closing her fingers around the token. "May I ask why you're helping us?"

The woman's lips curved in the faintest suggestion of a smile. "Not all eyes see the same things," she replied enigmatically. "Now go. Staying still in the Warrens is an invitation to trouble."

With a final, penetrating look at them both, she turned and returned to her duties at the well.

"What just happened?" Xion whispered, utterly baffled.

"I'm not entirely sure," Elara replied, examining the bone token. "But I think she recognized... something about me."

"The royal trait?"

"Perhaps. Or something else entirely." Elara slipped the token into a pocket. "Either way, we should follow her directions. We're too exposed here."

They moved away from the well, trying to follow the woman's instructions. What constituted the "main passage" was far from obvious in the chaotic environment. After several false starts and growing frustration, they eventually found what appeared to be drying racks—wooden frames hung with various textiles and small food items being preserved.

"There!" Xion pointed to a faded red cloth hanging limply from a nearby support. "That must be the banner she mentioned."

They turned left, as instructed, into a passage that seemed marginally wider than the others. After walking for what felt like an eternity, they spotted massive wooden beams crossing overhead, supporting the weight of the structures above.

"Right at the crossing beams," Elara recalled, leading the way into another passage.

Their confidence in following directions quickly faded as they encountered multiple junctions and crossings. Without landmarks or experience, each choice felt like blind guesswork. More than once, they found themselves doubling back after reaching dead ends or unstable structures.

"I think we're climbing at least," Xion offered hopefully after another detour. "The air seems slightly less foul."

Eventually, they emerged onto a ledge overlooking what appeared to be a massive chasm cutting through the heart of the Warrens. Crude bridges spanned the gap at various levels, connecting sections of the vertical shantytown. The sight was dizzying—people moving across these precarious spans with practiced ease, seemingly unconcerned by the drop below.

"This must be Juric's crossing," Elara surmised, surveying the multiple bridges. "But which one do we take?"

"The nearest, I suppose," Xion replied uncertainly. The structure in question consisted of little more than wooden planks lashed together with rope, with a single guide rope serving as a flimsy handrail.

They approached cautiously, watching as others crossed with casual familiarity born of necessity. When their turn came, Xion stepped onto the bridge first, testing its stability. It swayed alarmingly but held firm.

"One at a time," he suggested. "And don't look down."

Elara nodded, waiting for him to reach the midpoint before following. The bridge creaked ominously beneath their weight. Halfway across, Elara suddenly stopped, her body going rigid. Xion, already nearing the far side, turned back in alarm.

"Elara?" he called softly.

Her face had gone pale, her eyes wide and unfocused. One hand gripped the guide rope with white-knuckled intensity, while the other pressed against her temple.

"I feel..." she began, then shook her head as if to clear it. "Something... below. Like at the brass door, but... different."

"Can you make it across?" Xion asked urgently, noticing others waiting impatiently behind Elara.

With visible effort, she nodded and continued forward, her steps measured and deliberate. When she finally reached the far side, Xion grasped her arm to steady her.

"What happened?" he asked once they had moved away from the bridge.

"I don't know how to describe it," Elara replied, her voice uncharacteristically uncertain. "A sensation, like something calling from the depths below. The same feeling I had at the brass door, but... more diffuse. Less focused."

"Another door, perhaps?"

"Maybe. Or something else entirely." She straightened, visibly collecting herself. "It's fading now. We should continue."

On the far side of the chasm, they had no idea which direction to take. The blue-wrapped woman's instructions had only gotten them this far. With no clear path, they chose a route that seemed to lead upward, hoping to eventually reach the outer edges of the Warrens.

"We should rest," Xion suggested after more aimless wandering. "We've been moving for hours."

Elara nodded, fatigue evident in the set of her shoulders despite her military training. They found a small alcove away from the main paths and settled against the wall, keeping watch on the passage before them.

"Do you think Farleen made it out?" Elara asked after a moment of silence.

Xion's expression tightened at the mention of her name. "I don't know," he admitted. "She seemed to know her way around better than we did."

"You trusted her once."

"I did," Xion agreed, his voice flat. "I won't make that mistake again."

Elara studied him, her eyes shifting slightly toward gray—sorrow, or perhaps empathy. "Yet you trusted Silvanno and Kael."

"They proved themselves loyal. Farleen..." He trailed off, the memory of her betrayal still a fresh wound.

Before Elara could respond, shouts erupted from somewhere nearby. The sudden commotion jolted them both to alertness, their brief rest instantly forgotten.

"Raid!" someone shouted, the word echoing through the cramped passages. "Slavers raid!"

People began rushing past their alcove, panic evident in their movements. Children were scooped up by adults, possessions abandoned as residents fled the approaching danger.

"We need to move," Xion urged, pulling Elara to her feet. "Join the crowd. It's our best chance."

They merged with the fleeing residents, allowing themselves to be swept along in the general panic. Without knowing the territory or safe havens, following the locals seemed their only option. The strategy seemed sound—until they rounded a corner and found their path blocked by three men in leather armor, each bearing a red manacle symbol on their chest.

"Hold there!" the largest of the three commanded, his hand resting on the hilt of a crude but effective-looking sword. "Where's everyone rushing to?"

The small crowd faltered, some turning to flee in the opposite direction, others pressing themselves against walls in submission. Xion and Elara, caught in the middle, had no choice but to stand their ground.

"You two," the leader said, his eyes narrowing as he studied them. "Don't recognize you. New to this section?"

Xion swallowed hard, forcing himself to meet the man's gaze. "Just passing through," he managed, trying to keep his voice level.

"Passing through?" The man's lip curled. "Nobody 'passes through' here without a reason." He stepped closer, his bulk intimidating in the narrow space. "Let's see what you're carrying that's worth protecting."

Elara tensed beside him, and Xion could sense her calculating the odds of fighting their way out. The cramped quarters would negate much of her combat training advantage, and the enforcers were clearly experienced in these conditions.

As the leader reached to grab Xion's arm, the sudden movement caused the Long Knives medallion to slip from beneath his borrowed shirt, the chain catching momentarily on the rough fabric. The polished metal gleamed in the dim light, immediately drawing the enforcer's attention.

"What's this?" he demanded, snatching the medallion before Xion could react. His eyes widened as he recognized the symbol. "Long Knives?" His expression shifted from suspicious to dangerous. "What are their dogs doing in our territory?"

"We're not—" Xion began, but the leader silenced him with a rough shove.

"Save it for the boss," he growled. "He'll want to know why the Knives are sniffing around his business."

He barked orders to his companions, who moved to flank Xion and Elara. The leader pocketed the medallion with a satisfied smirk.

"You picked the wrong day to cross territories," he told them. "The boss is already in a foul mood. Long Knives business will make it worse."

Xion's mind raced, comprehension dawning with sickening clarity. In their blind wandering, they had stumbled directly into Slaver territory. The medallion that had protected them from the Long Knives now marked them as enemies to their new captors. The irony was bitter—they had fled danger only to blunder into something potentially worse.

With enforcers before and behind, they had no choice but to comply. The small crowd of Warrens residents watched with expressions ranging from relief at not being selected to sympathy for those who had been. None, however, made any move to interfere.

As they were marched deeper into unknown territory, Xion felt a cold dread spreading through his chest. He had no idea where they were being taken or what awaited them there. Worse, he had drawn Elara into this danger—the very person he had sworn to protect. Without guides, without knowledge, without weapons, they had wandered like blind mice through a labyrinth, only to find themselves trapped.

The enforcer's words echoed in his mind: "Long Knives business." The medallion that his father had given him as protection now marked them for something far worse. Whatever uneasy truce existed between the factions in the Warrens, they had stumbled onto the wrong side of it through sheer ignorance.

Through gaps in the haphazard construction, Xion caught glimpses of a larger structure rising from the general chaos of the Warrens—something more permanent and imposing than the surrounding shanties. Their destination, he suspected, and whatever—or whoever—waited for them there.

The only small mercy was that their captors seemed unaware of Elara's true identity. For now, they were simply trespassers with suspicious connections, not the imperial heir and her companion. How long that ignorance would last, Xion couldn't guess.

As they were prodded forward, Elara caught his eye, a flash of violet determination momentarily coloring her irises before she regained control. The message was clear—this wasn't over. Whatever waited ahead, they would face it together.

The medallion that had once symbolized his father's unexpected protection now hung like a weight around his neck—even in its physical absence, its meaning had been transformed. Protection into peril, safety into danger. Just like everything else in this vertical maze where nothing was as it seemed, and every direction led to unknowable consequences.
