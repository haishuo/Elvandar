Chapter 4: Betrayal

Dawn crept over the spires of the Kemvimore estate, painting the stone walls with stripes of golden light as Xion sat hunched over a stack of delivery schedules in his father’s study. The parchment felt worn under his fingertips - obviously not the official copies, but rather ones Rosik had been keeping separate. Separate, and conveniently accessible. That should have been Xion’s first warning, but he was too focused on the task at hand.

The weekly grain shipment to the Arol Batae compound was scheduled for tomorrow at dawn. His father had been surprisingly thorough - not just times and quantities, but guard rotations, delivery protocols, even the names of the regular drivers. Everything Xion needed to insert himself into the delivery team unnoticed. It was almost too perfect.

He rubbed his tired eyes. He hadn’t slept much since his meeting with Berakailen and learning the truth about Princess Elara. Each night brought dreams of a crown and a silver dagger, though he could never quite remember the details upon waking.

“You look terrible.” Melina’s voice cut through his thoughts. She stood in the doorway, arms crossed, wearing a simple morning dress that still somehow managed to look elegant. “Been here all night?”

Xion straightened, trying not to look guilty. “Just studying Father’s shipping records. Thought I should know more about the family business.”

“Is that what we’re calling it now?” Melina’s eyebrow arched as she stepped into the room, closing the door softly behind her. “Interesting dinner with Sa Ko Ren the other night, wasn’t it?”

“The Iron Lady isn’t exactly what I expected,” Xion admitted, shuffling the papers to hide the Arol Batae schedules.

Melina moved to the window, looking out over the Noble District as it stirred to life. “Few people are, in this city.” She turned back to him. “You realize Father is playing some game of his own in all this?”

Xion met her gaze. “I assumed as much.”

“And yet you’re proceeding anyway.” It wasn’t a question.

Xion leaned back in the chair - his father’s chair, he realized with a start. “Do I have a choice? We’ve come too far to back out now.”

“There’s always a choice, brother.” Melina’s voice softened slightly. “Just make sure it’s yours and not one Father has orchestrated for you.”

The warning hung in the air between them. His sister had never been this direct about their father’s manipulations before.

“Have you considered,” she continued, examining her nails with false casualness, “that Farleen might not be as loyal as you think?”

Xion felt a chill that had nothing to do with the morning air. “What are you saying?”

“She’s been seen in places she shouldn’t be. Meeting people she shouldn’t know.” Melina’s eyes met his again. “Test her, Xion. Before you commit to anything that can’t be undone.”

His mouth felt dry suddenly. “You have no proof.”

“And you have no certainty.” Melina walked over and placed a hand on his shoulder. “Just… be careful. I’d hate to lose my only brother to a pretty smile and a convenient knife.”

After she left, Xion sat in silence for a long time, the warm sunlight doing nothing to dispel the cold knot in his stomach.

The teahouse in the western Merchant Quarter was Kael’s suggestion - small, unremarkable, but with private alcoves separated by thick curtains that muffled sound. Perfect for conspirators, which Xion supposed they were. The bitter herbal tea steamed in front of him as he waited, rehearsing his plans in his mind.

Kael arrived first, slipping into the alcove with the practiced ease of someone accustomed to not being noticed. His plain brown clothing was immaculate as always, but there were shadows under his eyes that hadn’t been there before.

“Cartel guards are increasing their patrols,” he said without preamble, accepting the cup Xion poured for him. “All of them - Water, Iron, even the Slavers. Something’s got them spooked.”

“Us, probably,” Xion responded, keeping his voice low. “We haven’t exactly been subtle lately.”

Kael laughed - a short, humorless sound. “Four idealistic nobles trying to improve things? Hardly revolution material.”

“Unless one of those nobles is the heir to the Grain.” Xion took a sip of his tea, the bitterness oddly comforting. “I’ve been thinking about what you said. About making sure we can trust everyone.”

Kael’s eyes narrowed slightly. “You mean Farleen.”

Xion nodded, not trusting himself to speak her name without revealing something in his voice.

“How do you propose to test her?” Kael asked.

“False information.” Xion outlined his plan quickly - telling each of them different details about the delivery schedule. “If the information gets back to my father, or if someone shows up at the wrong time…”

“We’ll know who the leak is.” Kael nodded approvingly. “Simple but effective.”

The curtain parted as Silvanno joined them, his usual finery replaced by subdued blues that wouldn’t draw attention. “Mother had me followed yesterday,” he announced, sliding into the seat beside Kael. “Had to lose two guards just to get here.”

“Tania Larannas doesn’t strike me as the type to assign ineffective guards,” Xion observed.

Silvanno grinned. “She isn’t. But I know the docks better than they do.”

They were still discussing the patrol patterns when Farleen arrived, slightly breathless, her copper hair tucked under a plain scarf. Xion’s heart did that thing it always did when she entered a room - a painful squeeze followed by warmth. He pushed aside Melina’s warnings. This was Farleen. She wouldn’t betray him.

Would she?

“Sorry I’m late,” she said, sliding in beside Xion. “Father had unexpected guests again.” The same excuse she’d used at their last meeting, Xion noted with a stab of unease.

He poured her tea, watching her face carefully. “We were just discussing the delivery.”

She met his eyes with apparent openness. “When do we move?”

“Day after tomorrow,” Xion lied smoothly. “The delivery’s been pushed back by a day.”

If she noticed anything amiss, she didn’t show it. They continued planning, with Xion assigning positions - telling Silvanno to wait at the eastern courtyard in mid-morning rather than dawn, knowing full well the delivery would be at first light. Kael would take the northern approach in case they needed a diversion. Farleen would monitor the western gate… the day after the actual delivery.

When they finished, Silvanno and Kael departed first, leaving in separate directions as agreed. Farleen lingered, her hand finding Xion’s beneath the table.

“A moment alone?” she asked, and the warmth in her eyes made Xion forget his suspicions momentarily.

When the others were gone, her demeanor changed. The warmth remained, but something sharper entered her gaze. “You don’t trust me anymore.”

The directness of it knocked the breath from Xion’s lungs. “What?”

“You’re giving me false information, aren’t you?” Her fingers tightened on his. “It’s a test, isn’t it?”

Xion tensed, ready to deny it, but something in her expression stopped him. “How did you know?”

“Because it’s what I would do.” A small smile played at her lips. “I’m not angry, Xion. In your position, I’d do the same.”

He searched her face for any sign of deception but found none. “Farleen, I—”

She pressed a finger to his lips. “No apologies needed. We live in Kaha’an, remember? Trust is a luxury we can’t afford.” She leaned closer. “But know this - whatever you believe, whatever you’ve heard… my feelings for you are real.”

The kiss she gave him was fierce, almost desperate, and left him dizzy. Before he could respond, she was gone, the curtain falling back into place behind her.

Xion sat alone, more uncertain than ever.

Evening found Xion in his chambers, packing a small satchel with necessities - a few medical supplies, a change of clothes, some coin. Things he might need if everything went wrong. Or right. At this point, he wasn’t certain which outcome frightened him more.

A soft knock at his door announced a house servant, who bowed low. “Your father requests your presence in his study, young master.”

Dread pooled in Xion’s stomach. Had Rosik somehow learned of his plans? Had the test revealed Farleen’s betrayal already? Or worse, had it been a red herring all along? He followed the servant through the quiet corridors, each step feeling heavier than the last.

Rosik sat behind his desk, scrolls and ledgers pushed to one side, a small wooden box in front of him. He looked up as Xion entered, his expression unreadable as always.

“Sit,” he said, gesturing to the chair across from him.

Xion obeyed, waiting in silence, knowing his father preferred to speak first.

“Tomorrow will be… significant,” Rosik said finally. “And potentially dangerous.”

Xion’s heart hammered in his chest. So he did know.

“I’ve arranged for you to accompany the grain delivery to the Arol Batae compound,” Rosik continued, confirming Xion’s fears. “Officially, you’ll be inspecting our distribution procedures. Unofficially…” He paused, pushing the wooden box across the desk. “You might need this.”

With cautious fingers, Xion opened the box. Inside lay a medallion on a silver chain - a complex symbol etched into dark metal that seemed to absorb the light from the room’s lamps.

“What is this?” he asked, though something about the symbol seemed vaguely familiar.

“Protection,” Rosik answered simply. “It belongs to an association older than our family’s control of the Grain. The Arol Batae will recognize it.”

Xion lifted the medallion, feeling its surprising weight. “Why would you help me do this?”

For a moment - just a moment - something like emotion flickered across Rosik’s face. “Your mother would have approved. She always acted according to her conscience, regardless of consequences.” The words sounded almost… wistful?

Before Xion could process this, Rosik’s expression hardened again. “The medallion stays hidden unless absolutely necessary. Use it only if you have no other choice.”

Xion slipped the chain around his neck, tucking the medallion beneath his shirt. Its weight felt significant against his chest, like a promise. Or a threat.

“Father,” he began, uncertain what he even wanted to ask.

“Dawn comes early,” Rosik cut him off, turning his attention back to his ledgers. “I suggest you get some rest.”

Dismissed, Xion rose to leave, the medallion a constant reminder of his father’s unexpected aid. At the door, he paused, looking back at Rosik - the man who had executed his mentor, who ruled the Grain with calculating precision, who now offered help for reasons Xion couldn’t begin to understand.

“Thank you,” he said softly.

Rosik didn’t look up. “Don’t make me regret it.”

Dawn painted the eastern sky with stripes of pink and gold as Xion approached the loading yard where the grain carts were being prepared. He’d slept little, his mind racing with possibilities, the medallion a constant weight against his skin.

The yard foreman barely glanced at the papers Xion presented - orders signed by Rosik himself, appointing Xion as inspector for this delivery. The men loading the carts gave him curious looks but asked no questions. The Grain Lord’s son was entitled to his eccentricities.

Xion climbed onto the lead cart, nodding to the driver, a grizzled man who’d been making these deliveries for years. “Morning inspection,” Xion explained, the lie tasting bitter on his tongue.

The driver shrugged. “Suit yourself, young master. It’s your time to waste.”

The small convoy of three carts trundled through Kaha’an’s awakening streets, the sounds of the city coming to life all around them. Xion’s eyes darted everywhere, searching for signs of his friends - or enemies. Nothing seemed amiss. No unusual guards, no suspicious observers. Either his plan was working perfectly, or he was walking into a trap.

The Arol Batae compound loomed ahead, its high stone walls imposing in the early light. Unlike the ornate structures of the Noble District, the compound had a functional, almost military appearance - a fortress rather than a residence. Two armored guards stood at attention beside the heavy wooden gates, their posture perfect, their expressions wary.

One stepped forward as the carts approached. “State your business.”

“Weekly grain delivery,” the driver called back, the routine familiar to him.

The guard’s eyes narrowed at the sight of Xion. “And who’s this?”

Xion produced his papers again. “Xion Kemvimore. Here to inspect the distribution procedures.”

The guards exchanged glances before the first nodded curtly. “Wait here.”

Minutes later, a tall, weathered man emerged from within the compound, his bearing unmistakably that of someone accustomed to command. Gray streaked his close-cropped hair, and a scar ran from his left temple to jaw, pale against his sun-darkened skin.

“Commander Vesk,” the driver murmured to Xion. “Best mind your manners. Man’s a legend from before The Rending.”

Vesk approached, scrutinizing Xion with eyes that missed nothing. “House Kemvimore has never felt the need to inspect our procedures before.”

Xion met his gaze steadily. “New policy. My father believes in understanding every aspect of the distribution chain. I’m simply observing today, nothing more.”

Something in Vesk’s expression suggested he didn’t believe a word of it, but after a long moment, he stepped aside. “You may enter. Stay with the carts at all times.”

The gates swung open, revealing a courtyard larger than Xion had expected. Beyond it, he glimpsed multiple buildings arranged with military precision - barracks, a training yard, what looked like an armory. Everything spotlessly maintained, everything purposeful. This was no mere hideaway; it was a fully functioning military installation hidden in plain sight.

Arol Batae members directed the carts to a storage building at the far end of the courtyard. Xion observed them carefully - all moved with the disciplined precision of trained warriors, regardless of their current tasks. These were soldiers, not guards or servants.

As the grain sacks were unloaded, Xion wandered as far as he dared, trying to take in as much as possible without appearing suspicious. How many lived here? Forty? Fifty? All warriors by the look of them, all with the same quiet determination in their eyes.

“Curious about our home, Kemvimore?”

Xion turned to find Vesk watching him, arms crossed, expression unreadable.

“It’s impressive,” Xion admitted. “I hadn’t realized the Arol Batae maintained such… extensive facilities.”

“Our duties require it.” Vesk’s tone gave nothing away. “Your father knows this better than most.”

Before Xion could question this cryptic statement, movement from a nearby building caught his eye. A young woman emerged, her stride purposeful, her bearing as martial as any of the guards. Unlike the others in their utilitarian armor, she wore simple but well-made clothing - practical rather than ornamental, but of obvious quality.

Even at a distance, something about her commanded attention. The morning sunlight caught in her dark hair, which was pulled back in a simple braid. She moved with the fluid grace of someone thoroughly trained in combat, yet there was a natural authority in her posture that seemed almost… royal.

Vesk followed Xion’s gaze and something flickered across his weathered face - pride? Concern? Both?

“Ah, our inventory manager,” he said, pitching his voice just loud enough to carry. “She oversees all deliveries.”

The woman approached, and Xion found himself straightening involuntarily under her direct gaze. Up close, her eyes were a deep, striking blue - almost too vivid to be natural.

“Commander,” she acknowledged Vesk with a nod before turning to Xion. “I don’t believe we’ve met before.”

“Xion Kemvimore,” he managed, finding his voice. “Inspecting the delivery procedure.”

“House Kemvimore suddenly concerned with how we distribute their grain?” Her tone was neutral, but Xion detected a hint of challenge beneath it.

“Just ensuring everything meets standards,” Xion replied, studying her closely. If this was the princess, she’d been raised more warrior than royal.

She gestured toward the half-unloaded carts. “And? Does our system meet with House Kemvimore’s approval?”

Something in her manner - a subtle edge - made Xion bolder than he’d intended. “It’s efficient enough. Though it seems strange that the Arol Batae needs such regular deliveries when just beyond these walls, children in the middle districts go hungry.”

It was a provocative statement, and he saw Vesk tense, but the woman merely tilted her head slightly, her gaze sharpening.

“An interesting observation from the son of the man who controls all food distribution in Kaha’an,” she replied evenly. “Perhaps you should inspect your own house’s procedures next.”

For just a moment - so briefly Xion almost missed it - her eyes shifted color, from blue to a striking violet. Determination, his mind supplied, remembering Berakailen’s words about the royal trait. This was her. This was Princess Elara Valanar.

“Perhaps I should,” Xion agreed, hoping his expression didn’t betray his revelation.

Vesk cleared his throat. “The delivery is nearly complete. You should prepare to depart, Kemvimore.”

Elara gave him one more measuring look before turning away. “Until next time, then.”

Xion watched her go, the confirmation of her existence both exhilarating and terrifying. He’d been right. She was real. And now, everything would change.

The sun had climbed higher by the time Xion left the compound, his mind racing with what he’d seen. The carts and drivers headed back toward the Grain warehouses, but Xion made his excuses and turned toward the eastern courtyard, where Silvanno should be waiting - if he’d followed the false instructions.

The small plaza was mostly empty at this hour, with only a few merchants setting up stalls for the mid-morning rush. Xion scanned the area, looking for any sign of his friend.

There - sitting casually by a small fountain, pretending to read a scroll, was Silvanno. Exactly where he was supposed to be, at exactly the time Xion had falsely specified. Relief washed over him. At least one person could be trusted.

Xion approached, taking a seat beside Silvanno on the fountain’s edge. “You’re surprisingly punctual for someone from House Larannas.”

Silvanno looked up, his practiced boredom transforming instantly into concern. “Gods, you’re alive. Did it work? Did you see her?”

“Keep your voice down,” Xion cautioned, glancing around. “But yes. To both questions.”

Silvanno leaned closer. “You’re certain? It’s really her?”

“Her eyes changed color right in front of me,” Xion confirmed. “Just as Berakailen said they would. Violet - determination.”

“So the princess lives,” Silvanno breathed, wonder in his voice. “All this time, right under our noses.”

“Commander Vesk is formidable,” Xion added. “And they have at least forty trained warriors in there. Breaking her out won’t be easy.”

“Breaking her out?” Silvanno looked alarmed. “That wasn’t part of the plan.”

Xion frowned. “What did you think we were working toward?”

“Confirmation, certainly. Perhaps even contact. But abduction?” Silvanno shook his head. “That’s suicide, Xion.”

Before Xion could argue, a flicker of movement caught his eye - a city guard patrol entering the plaza from the north. “We’ll discuss this later,” he murmured. “I need to check the northern approach for Kael.”

They separated casually, Silvanno returning to his scroll, Xion walking purposefully as if on business. As expected, there was no sign of Kael at the northern gate - he’d been given the correct information and wouldn’t arrive until tomorrow.

That left the western gate, where Farleen shouldn’t be. Unless…

A knot formed in Xion’s stomach as he made his way there. The western approach to the Arol Batae compound was less trafficked, bordered by storage buildings and workshops rather than residences or markets.

And there she was - Farleen, leaning against a wall, watching the compound gates with studied casualness. She shouldn’t be here. Not today. Not if she’d believed his lie about the delivery being tomorrow.

She straightened when she saw him, a smile lighting her face that almost - almost - seemed genuine. “Xion! You’re done already?”

He approached slowly, fighting to keep his expression neutral. “The delivery went smoothly. What are you doing here? I thought we agreed you’d come tomorrow.”

“I wanted to check the area first,” she said smoothly. “Get familiar with the layout before the actual day.”

“That’s… thoughtful of you.” Xion forced a smile. “Always the careful planner.”

She took his arm, steering him toward a narrow alley between two buildings. “Let’s talk somewhere more private. I have some concerns about our approach.”

The alley was shadowed and quiet, away from prying eyes. Any other time, Xion might have welcomed the privacy with Farleen. Now, every step felt like walking into a trap.

“So,” she said when they were deep in the alley, “how was she? The princess?”

“Exactly as described.” Xion watched her face carefully. “Though they introduced her as their inventory manager.”

“Clever cover,” Farleen nodded, then hesitated. “Xion, there’s something I need to tell you.”

Here it comes, he thought, his hand moving unconsciously to where the medallion lay hidden beneath his shirt. “I’m listening.”

“I’ve known the true delivery schedule all along.” Her amber eyes met his directly. “My father has been keeping me informed about your activities since the beginning. At your father’s request.”

The betrayal, though suspected, still felt like a physical blow. “You’ve been reporting on us? On me?”

She didn’t flinch from his accusation. “Yes. Rosik wanted someone watching his son’s little reform conspiracy.” Her voice softened slightly. “But it became more than an assignment, Xion. My feelings for you are real.”

“Real enough to spy on me?” The bitterness in his voice surprised even him.

“We all serve someone in this city,” she replied. “Your father, my father… we’re all just pieces on their board.”

Xion stepped back, suddenly needing distance between them. “So all of it was a lie? Our plans for reform? Making things better?”

“The ideas weren’t lies. Just the method.” Farleen moved closer, her expression earnest now. “Change needs to come gradually, Xion. Destroying the cartel system overnight would collapse Kaha’an into chaos.”

“That’s not what we’re trying to do,” he protested.

“Isn’t it?” Her eyes hardened. “A princess emerging from hiding after twenty years? Claiming the Ruby Throne? The delicate balance between the cartels would shatter immediately.”

As she spoke, her hand moved to her side, drawing something from beneath her cloak. The blade caught the dim light filtering into the alley - small but wickedly sharp.

“I’m sorry it has to be this way,” she said, and for a moment, her expression seemed genuinely regretful. “Truly, I am.”

Xion’s hand flew to the medallion, pulling it out as Farleen lunged forward. The dagger missed his heart but sank deep into his shoulder, white-hot pain exploding through his body. He staggered backward, blood already soaking his shirt.

“The medallion won’t save you,” Farleen said, advancing again. “It might deter the Long Knives, but I have my orders.”

“From my father?” Xion gasped, pressing his hand to the wound, already feeling light-headed.

A bitter laugh escaped her. “No. Your father would be furious if he knew what I’m doing. But some plans are bigger than individuals, Xion. Even the Grain Lord’s son.”

His back hit the wall at the end of the alley. Nowhere to run. Blood pulsed between his fingers as he tried desperately to think of a way out.

“You could have been part of it,” Farleen continued, raising the dagger again. “We could have built something together. But your obsession with the princess…” She shook her head. “You’ve forced my hand.”

As she stepped forward for the killing blow, a shadow dropped from above - a cloaked figure landing in near-perfect silence between them. Farleen’s eyes widened in shock before the figure’s elbow connected with her temple, sending her crumpling to the ground.

Xion slid down the wall, his vision beginning to blur. The figure turned to him, hood falling back to reveal a face he’d seen just hours before - but this time, those striking eyes were violet with determination.

“Princess…” he managed, his voice sounding distant to his own ears.

Elara Valanar knelt beside him, examining his wound with practiced efficiency. “It’s deep, but you’ll live if we stop the bleeding.” Her voice was calm, commanding. “Can you stand?”

Xion tried, but his legs gave way immediately. The last thing he saw before darkness claimed him was Elara’s face, set with royal determination as she lifted him as easily as a child.

“Hold on,” her voice came to him through a growing fog. “I didn’t spend twenty years in hiding just to let my first ally die in an alley.”

Then the darkness swallowed everything.
