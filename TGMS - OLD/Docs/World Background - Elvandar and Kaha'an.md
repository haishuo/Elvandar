# World Background - Elvandar and Kaha'an

## Elvandar

# The Historical Legacy of Elvandar

## The Balishan Empire: Seven Centuries of Unity

For seven hundred years, the continent of Elvandar thrived under the rule of the Balishan Empire. Drawing inspiration from both the ancient Roman Republic and Carthage, Balisha established itself as a powerful trade-based empire, with an extensive bureaucratic infrastructure connecting its diverse provinces. Throughout its long history, several dynasties rose and fell, each leaving their unique mark on the empire's culture, architecture, and political structure.

At its height, the Balishan Empire spanned the entirety of Elvandar, creating an unprecedented era of unity, prosperity, and cultural exchange. Like Carthage with its vassal state of Nubia, Balisha maintained a semi-autonomous relationship with Terinok, which provided much of the empire's military might when needed.

## The Imperial Collapse

Unlike many great empires that fall to external invasion, the Balishan Empire's demise came from within. Over time, the extensive bureaucratic infrastructure that had once been its strength began to crack under its own weight. The collapse was gradual, spanning approximately 50 years, with provinces slowly breaking away one by one.

Terinok, never formally a full province, was the first to declare independence. Like an ancient bridge finally succumbing to decades of wear, the once-mighty empire slowly crumbled, unable to maintain the connections between its increasingly autonomous regions. By the end of this period of dissolution, the unified Elvandar was no more, replaced by a patchwork of successor states, each with their own distinct character and ambitions.

## The Successor States: Embodiments of the Seven Sins

In a striking parallel, each of the successor states has come to embody one of the seven deadly sins, their national characters warped by these fundamental flaws in the absence of imperial unity.

### Terinok: The Warrior State - Wrath

The first to break from the empire, Terinok represents a fascinating blend of ancient Spartan and Aztec cultures. Though often characterized simply as a "warrior culture" obsessed with honor and duty, the reality of Terinok society is far more complex and nuanced. Having served as the military backbone of the Balishan Empire, Terinok now channels its martial tradition into maintaining its hard-won independence. 

Embodying the sin of Wrath, Terinok's culture has become increasingly defined by its martial spirit and suspicion of outsiders. Their righteous anger, once directed at enemies of the empire, now simmers beneath the surface of all their diplomatic relations. They view their former imperial masters with particular hostility, especially Kaha'an, which claims to be the true successor of Old Balisha.

Beneath the veneer of honor and duty lies a brutal system of enslavement. Drawing from both Spartan and Aztec traditions, Terinok maintains a significant population of helot-like slaves who perform all agricultural and manual labor. Young warriors undergo ritualized trials that include hunting and killing slaves who show signs of resistance, supposedly tempering their battle instincts while reinforcing the social hierarchy. Captives from border skirmishes often become sacrifices in ceremonies meant to appease their warrior deities, their hearts removed in elaborate rituals that the warriors believe channel strength to Terinok's armies.

The warrior class justifies this cruelty as necessary for maintaining their martial readiness against external threats. Most disturbing is how thoroughly this system has been normalized—even celebrated—within Terinok society, with the most successful slave-hunters earning special distinction among their peers.

### Uratha: The Land of Scholars - Pride

What began as a single Imperial Academy grew into a province entirely dedicated to scholarship and learning. The foundation of Uratha's power and independence lies in the Scholar's Crown, a semi-intelligent divine artifact loaned to the academy by an ancient emperor. This remarkable relic grants its bearer (if found worthy) the knowledge, skills, and abilities of all worthy individuals who have worn it before, with worthiness determined by the contribution of novel knowledge.

As Uratha expanded from a building to a campus to a city to a province, its academic influence grew proportionally. When the empire collapsed, Uratha refused to return the Scholar's Crown to Kaha'an, creating a bitter feud that continues to this day. 

Pride defines Uratha's national character. Their intellectual achievements have fostered an academic arrogance that prevents them from seeing value in knowledge gained through means other than their rigorous scholarly methods. They believe their continued possession of the Scholar's Crown is justified by their superior stewardship of knowledge, dismissing Kaha'an's claims with intellectual hauteur. Despite being geographically distant from Silaris, the two maintain a friendly rivalry as centers of scholarship and art respectively.

The High Exam, while presented as a meritocratic sorting mechanism, functions as an elaborate system of academic apartheid. Those who fail become *bakarn*, or "beast-kin" in the Urathan tongue, permanently relegated to a servant class with no opportunity for advancement. Children as young as twelve have their entire futures determined by a single test, with *bakarn* children automatically inheriting their parents' status regardless of intellectual potential.

While not chattel slavery in the traditional sense, the *bakarn* exist in a state of near-permanent servitude, performing all manual labor that scholars consider beneath them. They are housed in separate quarters, forbidden from entering certain areas of academic cities without explicit permission, and required to wear identifying markers at all times. The academic elite have developed elaborate philosophical justifications for this system, arguing that it represents the natural ordering of minds—a particularly cruel irony given Uratha's original founding principles of free inquiry and the advancement of knowledge.

### Balisha (Kaha'an): The Desert Crossroads - Greed

All that remains of the original Balishan Empire is the city of Kaha'an, once the imperial capital. Approximately two centuries before the empire's collapse, the imperial court relocated from landlocked Vartonne to coastal Kaha'an, establishing it as the new center of power.

Twenty years ago, a magical catastrophe known as The Rending transformed the once-lush lands surrounding Kaha'an into a vast desert. Ironically, this disaster only enhanced the city's strategic importance, making it the unavoidable crossroads of Elvandar. Like Constantinople at its height, Kaha'an now stands as the nexus of continental trade, wealthy beyond measure.

Greed has become Kaha'an's defining characteristic. As trade routes converge on the city, its merchants and rulers demand ever-increasing tolls and tariffs, accumulating vast wealth behind its walls while the surrounding desert grows more barren. As the last seat of imperial power, Kaha'an considers itself the legitimate successor to the Balishan Empire and has demanded the return of the Scholar's Crown from Uratha—a demand that has been repeatedly refused, creating ongoing tension between the two states. Their desire for the crown stems not from scholarly interest but from an insatiable need to possess all symbols of imperial authority.

Kaha'an maintains perhaps the most insidious system of bondage among the successor states, operating on two distinct levels. The Slavers cartel practices traditional chattel slavery, capturing the desperate from the Warrens and elsewhere. Alongside this runs a pervasive mythology of self-advancement that keeps the non-enslaved working class in perpetual hope despite impossible odds.

Technically, slaves can purchase their freedom, but the price is deliberately set so high that only a handful succeed each decade—just enough to maintain the illusion of possibility. These rare successes are paraded as examples of the system's fairness while obscuring its fundamental exploitation. Similarly, the cartels promote stories of common citizens rising through their ranks, though in reality such advancement is virtually impossible without connections by birth or marriage.

This dual system—outright slavery alongside wage slavery disguised as opportunity—perfectly embodies Kaha'an's manifestation of Greed. The cartels extract maximum labor while providing minimum compensation, maintaining control through both physical coercion and the careful management of false hope.

### Vartonne: Glory Faded - Sloth

Once the opulent imperial capital of the Balishan Empire, Vartonne lost its status when the imperial court moved to Kaha'an two centuries ago. Despite this loss, Vartonne stubbornly clings to its imperial past, with nobles still wearing their empty titles and elaborate parties still being held in crumbling palaces.

The sin of Sloth has thoroughly permeated Vartonne's society. Though surrounded by lush and fertile land that could support a thriving independent nation, Vartonne remains trapped in nostalgia, unwilling to expend the effort needed to forge a new identity. Its citizens prefer to languish in faded luxury rather than adapt to a changed world. The ruling class continues to make imperial proclamations that go unheeded, convinced that the provinces will eventually return to its orbit despite all evidence to the contrary—all while avoiding the difficult work of true governance.

### The Erulian Principality: The Almost-Heirs - Envy

Once merely a province governed by a distant relative of the emperor who held the largely ceremonial title of "prince," the Erulian Principality gained new significance after The Rending. With the imperial line of Balisha believed to be cut, the ruling house of Erulius suddenly found itself as the closest surviving relatives to the throne—though still not close enough to legitimately claim it.

Envy has become the driving force behind the Principality's foreign policy. This twist of fate has become a source of bitter frustration for the Erulian rulers, who covet the Ruby Throne in Kaha'an with an obsessive intensity. Every diplomatic meeting, every trade negotiation is colored by their jealousy of Kaha'an's position. Despite their ambitions, the Principality actually has the strongest foundation among all the successor states, with abundant natural resources including iron mines, fertile farmland, and a large workforce. They could potentially forge a new empire if they weren't so fixated on reclaiming the old one, constantly looking to others' possessions rather than developing their own considerable advantages.

### Silaris: The Conservatory City - Lust

Founded during the height of the Balishan Empire by a dynasty that wished to rival the scholarly prestige of Uratha, Silaris became a haven for artists of all kinds. The entire province grew to support this cultural endeavor, and the arts flourished while the empire stood.

The sin of Lust manifests in Silaris not merely as physical desire, but as an insatiable passion for beauty and sensation in all its forms. Their pursuit of aesthetic perfection has become obsessive, with citizens willing to sacrifice practical necessities for artistic transcendence. When Balisha fell, the artists and performers of Silaris lacked the practical skills needed to maintain basic infrastructure, yet they continue to create works of breathtaking beauty even as their sewers fail and their bridges crumble. Today, Silaris presents a strange dichotomy: exquisite beauty and artistic achievement set against the backdrop of a slowly decaying urban landscape. Despite these challenges, Silaris maintains a friendly rivalry with Uratha, each representing opposite but complementary aspects of Elvandar's cultural heritage.

### Gunastran: The Land of Smoke and Gears - Gluttony

The industrial powerhouse of Elvandar, Gunastran embraces magitech with unmatched enthusiasm. Constantly producing and consuming in equal measure, this nation has an insatiable appetite for resources to fuel its factories and workshops.

Gluttony defines Gunastran's national identity. Their consumption of resources far exceeds what they actually need, yet they cannot stop their ravenous industrial appetite. Forests are cleared, mountains hollowed, and rivers diverted at an alarming rate to feed the ever-growing industrial complex. Gunastran maintains diplomatic relations with its neighbors primarily to secure the raw materials needed for its endless production. Beyond these practical concerns, the nation shows little interest in the political maneuverings of the other successor states, focused entirely on its industrial pursuits. Their factories produce far more than their people can use, yet the smoke continues to rise from their chimneys day and night.

## The Balance Lost: Virtues Corrupted into Sins

When united under the Balishan Empire, these diverse regions existed in harmony, each contributing their unique strengths to the greater whole while having their weaknesses compensated for by the others. What were once virtues—Terinok's protective strength, Uratha's pursuit of knowledge, Kaha'an's commercial acumen, Vartonne's cultural refinement, Erulius's resourcefulness, Silaris's artistic passion, and Gunastran's productivity—have twisted into deadly sins in isolation.

The martial prowess of Terinok, the scholarly wisdom of Uratha, the artistic brilliance of Silaris, the industrial might of Gunastran, and the agricultural bounty of Erulius once served the imperial whole. Now, as independent nations defined by their worst excesses, they struggle to maintain self-sufficiency, leading to rising tensions and shifting alliances.

The delicate balance that once preserved peace across Elvandar has been shattered, leaving a continent ripe for conflict, diplomacy, and the potential for either a new unification or further fragmentation. Without the tempering influence of imperial unity, each nation has fallen prey to its darkest impulses, their virtues corrupted into the seven deadly sins that now define the political landscape of a fractured Elvandar.

## International Relations of Elvandar

# International Relations of Elvandar

## Introduction

Following the collapse of the Balishan Empire, the seven successor states of Elvandar have developed complex relationships with one another, shaped by geographical proximity, historical ties, resource needs, and the unique cultural characteristics each nation has developed in isolation. This document serves as a comprehensive overview of these international relations, exploring the diplomatic, economic, military, and cultural connections that bind and divide the nations of Elvandar.

## Uratha (Pride)

As a nation defined by scholarly pursuit and academic hierarchy, Uratha's foreign relations are characterized by intellectual analysis, theoretical frameworks, and a persistent sense of academic superiority.

### The Academic Hierarchy and Bakarn System

Uratha's High Exam creates a strict two-tiered society that significantly impacts its international relations. The scholars who pass form the ruling class, while those who fail—the *bakarn* or "beast-kin"—become a permanent servile class performing all manual labor. This system influences Uratha's diplomatic posture in several ways:

- Urathan diplomats are exclusively from the scholarly class, creating immediate cultural barriers with nations that value different qualities
- The bakarn rarely interact with foreign delegations but are carefully hidden away during international visits
- Uratha's foreign policy partly focuses on ensuring continued access to resources needed to maintain the scholars' comfortable lifestyle at the bakarn's expense
- The bakarn system is diplomatically defended as a "meritocratic" arrangement rather than bondage

The bakarn system creates particular tension with Terinok, whose warrior ethos values physical capability that Uratha openly denigrates, while Kaha'an privately mocks Uratha's elaborate justifications for what they see as simply another form of slavery.

### Relations with Kaha'an (Greed)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Severely strained
- **Primary Tension**: Dispute over the Scholar's Crown, which Kaha'an claims as imperial property
- **Economic Ties**: Limited but necessary trade, as Uratha requires access to Kaha'an's position as a trade crossroads
- **Cultural Perception**: Uratha views Kaha'an's mercantile focus as shallow and materialistic
- **Notable Interactions**: Diplomatic meetings are notoriously tense and filled with academic posturing by Urathan representatives
- **Current Issues**: Increasing tensions over Kaha'an's renewed demands for the return of the Scholar's Crown
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Uratha's scholars publicly denounce Kaha'an's slavery practices while carefully avoiding mention of their own bakarn system; Kaha'an's diplomats subtly highlight this hypocrisy in negotiations

### Relations with Silaris (Lust)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Friendly rivalry
- **Common Ground**: Both represent different aspects of Elvandar's cultural heritage—knowledge versus artistic expression
- **Cultural Exchange**: Regular scholarly and artistic exchanges, though Urathan participants often attempt to frame art within academic contexts
- **Academic Interest**: Urathan scholars frequently debate the relative value of scholarly versus artistic pursuits
- **Notable Concern**: Silaris' deteriorating infrastructure is viewed as a cautionary tale about prioritizing aesthetics over practical considerations
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Urathan scholars are fascinated by Silaris' relatively fluid social structure, though they still bring bakarn servants on diplomatic trips to maintain their status

### Relations with Terinok (Wrath)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Cautious mutual respect
- **Practical Cooperation**: Terinok warriors occasionally serve as mercenaries protecting Urathan expeditions to dangerous regions
- **Cultural Disconnect**: Scholarly works on combat theory from Uratha are largely ignored by Terinok's practical warriors
- **Academic Interest**: Urathan analysis of Terinok's battle tactics is extensive but often misses cultural context
- **Notable Trend**: Both nations respect each other's specializations while fundamentally misunderstanding the other's values
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Deep mutual disdain over each other's servile class systems; Terinok views the bakarn system as weakness (preventing potentially capable fighters from serving), while Uratha views Terinok's helot system as barbaric and intellectually wasteful

### Relations with Vartonne (Sloth)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Condescending observation
- **Academic Interest**: Scholars consider Vartonne a fascinating case study in cultural stagnation
- **Practical Activities**: Expeditions to recover imperial-era texts from Vartonne's libraries are common
- **Educational Connection**: Vartonne nobility occasionally send their children to study in Uratha, hoping to recapture past glories
- **Notable Characteristic**: Uratha's scholars document Vartonne's decline with academic detachment
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Uratha provides academic opportunities to Vartonne nobility while denying similar opportunities to their own bakarn, creating resentment among the servile class

### Relations with the Erulian Principality (Envy)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Cordial but distant
- **Economic Interest**: Erulian natural resources are valuable to Uratha, particularly rare materials used in magical research
- **Academic Approach**: Urathan scholars study Erulian political maneuvering with anthropological interest
- **Political Intersection**: The Principality sometimes commissions Urathan scholars to legitimize their claims to imperial heritage
- **Strategic Position**: Uratha carefully maintains neutrality in Erulius' ongoing feud with Kaha'an
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Uratha subtly employs bakarn as spies within Erulian delegations, exploiting Erulius' tendency to ignore servants

### Relations with Gunastran (Gluttony)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Professional cooperation
- **Technological Exchange**: Scholarly exchanges focus on integrating magical theory with Gunastran practical applications
- **Economic Dependency**: Uratha depends on Gunastran for certain specialized equipment used in research
- **Collaborative Efforts**: Joint research projects occasionally arise, though differences in approach often create friction
- **Academic Concern**: Urathan scholars study Gunastran's environmental impact with growing concern
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Urathan scholars sometimes "loan" bakarn to Gunastran industry for experiments, treating them as research subjects rather than people

## Terinok (Wrath)

As the first province to break from the empire and a society defined by martial traditions, Terinok's international relations are characterized by directness, honor-based diplomacy, and military pragmatism.

### The Helot System and Sacrificial Practices

Terinok's management of its large slave population influences every aspect of its foreign relations:

- The need for new slaves drives many of Terinok's border conflicts, especially with Kaha'an
- Young Terinok warriors prove themselves by hunting slaves who attempt escape, creating warriors skilled in tracking and ambush
- Human sacrifice ceremonies are conducted before major diplomatic meetings or military campaigns
- Foreign delegations are often uncomfortably aware that their failed negotiations might result in themselves becoming sacrifices or slaves
- The constant need for slave labor inhibits Terinok's ability to adopt more advanced agricultural methods from other nations

This system creates particular tensions with Kaha'an, which views Terinok's practices as wasteful (sacrificing potentially profitable slaves), while Uratha finds the entire system barbaric yet fascinating from an anthropological perspective.

### Relations with Kaha'an (Greed)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Hostile
- **Historical Context**: As the first to break from the empire, Terinok views Kaha'an's claims to imperial succession with particular hostility
- **Military Posture**: Regular border skirmishes occur, particularly over mountain passes that connect their territories
- **Economic Relationship**: Limited to essential resources, conducted largely through neutral intermediaries
- **Current Tension**: Kaha'an's attempts to impose trade tariffs on goods moving between Terinok and other nations
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Fierce competition over the same human resources; Terinok raids for slaves while Kaha'an attempts to capture potential laborers for its own systems; both nations refuse to return escaped slaves to the other

### Relations with Silaris (Lust)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Limited engagement
- **Cultural Exchange**: Terinok warriors occasionally serve as inspiration for Silaris artists, creating a one-sided cultural connection
- **Military Aspect**: Terinok occasionally provides protection for Silaris caravans, in exchange for luxury goods
- **Geographic Factor**: Limited direct contact due to distance and geographical barriers
- **Notable Interaction**: Terinok's elite occasionally commission artwork from Silaris, though they would rarely admit this publicly
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Terinok occasionally "gifts" slaves to Silaris artisans as models or servants; Silaris accepts while privately finding the practice distasteful

### Relations with Uratha (Pride)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Cautious mutual respect
- **Military Cooperation**: Terinok provides martial protection for scholarly expeditions
- **Cultural Disconnect**: Little patience for Uratha's theoretical approaches to warfare
- **Practical Exchange**: Terinok warriors value Urathan healing techniques and certain protective magics
- **Notable Tension**: Frequent disagreements about the value of knowledge versus practical experience
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Each nation considers the other's form of servitude barbaric while defending their own; Uratha particularly condemns the hunting and sacrifice of slaves, while Terinok mocks the "wasted potential" of physically capable bakarn

### Relations with Vartonne (Sloth)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Dismissive
- **Military Perception**: Terinok views Vartonne's deteriorated military capabilities with contempt
- **Historical Connection**: Some Terinok families maintain old alliances with Vartonne nobles from imperial times
- **Cultural Attitude**: Vartonne's obsession with past glories is seen as weakness by Terinok's forward-looking warriors
- **Current Interaction**: Terinok occasionally recruits disillusioned Vartonne youth seeking purpose
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Terinok warriors openly mock Vartonne's inability to maintain even a basic slave system, viewing it as further evidence of their decadence

### Relations with the Erulian Principality (Envy)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Wary alliance
- **Strategic Cooperation**: Mutual distrust of Kaha'an creates common ground
- **Military Exchange**: Terinok trains some Erulian forces in exchange for access to resources
- **Political Stance**: Terinok is indifferent to Erulius' imperial ambitions but values them as a counter to Kaha'an
- **Current Issues**: Growing concern about Erulius' increasingly aggressive territorial posturing
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Erulius occasionally purchases slaves from Terinok rather than capturing them directly, creating a morally complicated trade relationship

### Relations with Gunastran (Gluttony)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Pragmatic trade partners
- **Military Interest**: Terinok values Gunastran's weapons and armor production
- **Economic Exchange**: Terinok provides raw materials and protection for Gunastran trade routes
- **Cultural Disconnect**: Fundamental disagreement about resource use and conservation
- **Notable Tension**: Increasing concern about Gunastran's environmental impact on traditional Terinok hunting grounds
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Terinok occasionally supplies slaves to work in Gunastran's more dangerous industries; Gunastran values this arrangement as it reduces casualties among their own workforce

## Kaha'an (Greed)

As the self-proclaimed heir to the Balishan Empire and the unavoidable crossroads of continental trade, Kaha'an's international relations are defined by economic leverage, imperial claims, and strategic positioning.

### The Dual Bondage System

Kaha'an's complex system of both chattel slavery and wage slavery significantly impacts its diplomatic relations:

- The Slavers cartel requires constant influx of new slaves, influencing border policies and raids
- Kaha'an's diplomats present their system as more "humane" than others because slaves can theoretically purchase freedom (though the price is deliberately prohibitive)
- Foreign merchants visiting Kaha'an must navigate complex regulations regarding slave ownership and transportation
- The false promise of economic mobility is extended to foreign visitors and potential immigrants, creating a constant influx of desperate people
- Kaha'an uses its position as a trade hub to influence other nations' labor practices to its advantage

This system puts Kaha'an in complex relationships with both Terinok (competing for the same human resources) and Uratha (which publicly criticizes while privately envying Kaha'an's efficiency in resource extraction from its enslaved populations).

### Relations with Terinok (Wrath)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Hostile
- **Historical Context**: Bitter resentment over Terinok's role as the first province to break away
- **Economic Leverage**: Attempts to control trade routes to and from Terinok
- **Military Posture**: Maintains fortified border positions but avoids direct conflict
- **Current Strategy**: Using economic pressure to undermine Terinok's independence
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Direct competition for the same human resources; each nation refuses to return escaped slaves to the other; Kaha'an portrays its slavery as more "civilized" than Terinok's sacrificial practices

### Relations with Silaris (Lust)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Exploitative partnership
- **Economic Aspect**: Kaha'an merchants profit extensively from trading Silaris artworks
- **Cultural Leverage**: Positions itself as the patron and protector of Silaris' artistic heritage
- **Practical Support**: Provides essential resources to Silaris in exchange for exclusive trading rights
- **Current Tension**: Growing resentment in Silaris over unfavorable trade terms
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Kaha'an merchants occasionally "commission" works that require Silaris artists to work in effective indentured servitude until the piece is completed

### Relations with Uratha (Pride)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Openly antagonistic
- **Primary Conflict**: Continues to demand the return of the Scholar's Crown
- **Economic Reality**: Maintains trade despite tensions, as both nations recognize their mutual dependence
- **Political Stance**: Refuses to recognize Uratha's legitimacy as the heir to the imperial academic tradition
- **Current Tactic**: Attempting to sow discord within Uratha's academic hierarchy
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Kaha'an diplomats subtly highlight Uratha's hypocrisy in condemning slavery while maintaining the bakarn system; occasionally offers refuge to escaped bakarn as a political tool

### Relations with Vartonne (Sloth)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Dismissive patronage
- **Imperial Politics**: Tolerates Vartonne's pretensions to imperial status while undermining them behind the scenes
- **Economic Control**: Exploits Vartonne's agricultural resources through unfavorable trade agreements
- **Diplomatic Approach**: Maintains a façade of respect for Vartonne's former status while treating it as a vassal
- **Strategic Interest**: Values Vartonne primarily as a buffer between itself and other powers
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Kaha'an merchants effectively control Vartonne's economy, creating debt-bondage arrangements that extract wealth without formal slavery

### Relations with the Erulian Principality (Envy)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Cold hostility
- **Imperial Rivalry**: Views Erulius' claims to imperial heritage as a direct threat
- **Economic Warfare**: Uses trade policies to limit Erulian economic development
- **Diplomatic Effort**: Works to isolate Erulius in international relations
- **Current Concern**: Growing alliance between Erulius and Terinok
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Kaha'an actively undermines Erulius' attempts to establish its own slave trade, while spreading rumors about brutal treatment of workers in Erulian mines

### Relations with Gunastran (Gluttony)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Essential trading partners
- **Economic Interdependence**: Kaha'an distributes Gunastran products throughout Elvandar
- **Leverage Point**: Controls many of the raw materials Gunastran requires
- **Diplomatic Approach**: Maintains cordial relations while ensuring Gunastran dependence
- **Current Objective**: Securing exclusive distribution rights for new Gunastran innovations
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Kaha'an serves as the primary market for Gunastran's labor-saving devices, which ironically further entrench slave dependency in Kaha'an

## Vartonne (Sloth)

Once the imperial capital and now a nation trapped in nostalgia, Vartonne's international relations are characterized by desperate attempts to maintain relevance, invocation of past glories, and a failure to adapt to post-imperial realities.

### Relations with Kaha'an (Greed)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Subservient deference
- **Historical Context**: Bitter over the historical transfer of imperial power to Kaha'an
- **Economic Reality**: Heavily dependent on Kaha'an for trade and resources
- **Political Position**: Officially maintains that Kaha'an is a temporary capital, while practically accepting its dominance
- **Current Strategy**: Attempts to leverage historical legitimacy for better treatment

### Relations with Terinok (Wrath)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Fearful respect
- **Historical Tension**: Still views Terinok's independence as treason against the empire

- **Cultural Exchange**: Some noble families maintain traditional ties dating to imperial times
- **Current Approach**: Avoids confrontation while attempting to maintain dignity
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Vartonne nobles still remember when Terinok provided slaves for imperial projects and occasionally make unrealistic demands for "tribute" that are politely ignored

### Relations with Silaris (Lust)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Nostalgic patronage
- **Cultural Connection**: Commissions artwork celebrating Vartonne's imperial past
- **Historical Relationship**: Traditional patron of the arts, trying to maintain this role despite limited resources
- **Current Dynamic**: Silaris humors Vartonne's pretensions in exchange for what limited patronage it can still provide
- **Notable Trend**: Vartonne nobles spend beyond their means on Silaris artworks to maintain appearances

### Relations with Uratha (Pride)

- **Notable Tension**: Resentment over Uratha's academic analysis of Vartonne's decline

### Relations with the Erulian Principality (Envy)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Competitive nostalgia
- **Imperial Politics**: Both claim connection to the imperial line, creating tension
- **Practical Reality**: Vartonne's claims are more legitimate but less practically viable
- **Current Dynamic**: Each attempts to undermine the other's imperial credentials
- **Notable Trend**: Increasingly elaborate ceremonies and titles on both sides to assert legitimacy
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Both nations claim to uphold "proper" imperial treatment of servants while criticizing each other's practices

### Relations with Gunastran (Gluttony)
- **Diplomatic Status**: Reluctant modernization
- **Economic Reality**: Dependent on Gunastran goods but ideologically opposed to industrialization
- **Cultural Stance**: Views Gunastran innovations as vulgar and beneath imperial dignity
- **Practical Necessity**: Gradually adopting Gunastran technology while pretending not to
- **Current Pattern**: Disguising modern conveniences behind classical facades
- **Bondage Dynamics**: Imports Gunastran labor-saving devices while maintaining the fiction that servants operate them through traditional means

## The Erulian Principality (Envy)

As almost-heirs to the imperial throne with abundant natural resources, the Erulian Principality's international relations are shaped by imperial ambitions, resource leverage, and strategic positioning between more powerful neighbors.

### Relations with Kaha'an (Greed)

### Relations with Terinok (Wrath)

- **Military Cooperation**: Receives training and occasional support against Kaha'an
- **Economic Exchange**: Provides resources in exchange for martial expertise
- **Current Objective**: Strengthening ties without becoming dependent

### Relations with Silaris (Lust)

## Bondage Systems as Diplomatic Tools

Each nation's system of servitude has evolved beyond mere economic necessity to become a complex diplomatic instrument:

- Bakarn in Uratha observe foreign delegations and report back to their masters
- Slaves in Kaha'an serve as unwitting infiltrators when given as "gifts" to other nations

- Erulius threatens to cut off the slave trade to pressure Terinok on certain issues

- Each nation presents its bondage system as morally superior to others

- Public rituals related to servitude are calibrated to impress or intimidate foreign visitors

### Economic Interdependence
- The flow of forced labor across borders creates complex webs of economic dependency
- Specialized slave/servant skills develop regionally, leading to trade in specific types of bondage
- Resources extracted by slaves/servants in one nation often processed by slaves/servants in another
- Competition for control of bondage-related trade routes causes significant diplomatic tension

## The Human Cost

Beneath the political maneuvering and diplomatic posturing lies the profound human cost of these systems:

- Families separated by national borders and different bondage systems
- Children born into servitude with no hope of advancement or education
- Individuals caught between nations, belonging nowhere and claimed by multiple powers
- Cultural traditions and languages suppressed to maintain control
- Medical experimentation and dangerous work conditions causing preventable suffering and death
- Resistance movements brutally suppressed to maintain international stability

The international relations of Elvandar cannot be fully understood without recognizing how these bondage systems form the foundation upon which diplomatic, economic, and cultural exchanges are built. The seven deadly sins that define these nations find their most concrete expression in how each treats its most vulnerable populations—a reflection of how far they have fallen from the virtuous unity they once shared under imperial rule.

## Kaha'an

# Kaha'an: The Desert Crossroads

## Origins and Transformation

Once the imperial capital of the Balishan Empire, Kaha'an has undergone a dramatic transformation following the empire's collapse and a magical catastrophe known as The Rending. What remains of the original Balishan Empire is now just this city and its immediate surroundings.

### Pre-Rending Kaha'an

Before The Rending, Kaha'an was far more than just a city:

- A fertile, prosperous region with abundant farmland
- The seat of imperial power after the court relocated from Vartonne approximately two centuries before the empire's collapse
- Strategically positioned at the heart of Elvandar's trade routes
- Ruled by the remnants of the imperial house, giving it the strongest legitimate claim to imperial succession
- A wealthy nation enriched by trade passing through its territories
- A complete political entity, though not as powerful as the original empire at its height

### The Rending and Its Aftermath

Approximately twenty years ago, a magical catastrophe known as The Rending transformed the once-lush lands surrounding Kaha'an into a vast desert. The effects were devastating:

- Almost the entire province was transformed into desert wasteland
- The capital city survived only because of powerful magical wards befitting an imperial capital
- The emperor and empress were caught outside the city during the event
- Their bodies were never recovered, leading to their presumed death (with some plausible deniability)
- The imperial line was effectively cut, creating a major power vacuum

In the immediate aftermath, chaos reigned. Refugees flooded into the protected city, and the established power structure collapsed without its imperial leadership.

### Post-Rending Kaha'an

Ironically, The Rending actually enhanced the city's strategic importance, making it an unavoidable crossroads for continental trade. Like Constantinople at its height, Kaha'an now stands as the nexus of continental trade, wealthy beyond measure.

As the last seat of imperial power, Kaha'an considers itself the legitimate successor to the Balishan Empire. It has demanded the return of the Scholar's Crown from Uratha—a demand that has been repeatedly refused, creating ongoing tension between the two states. Kaha'an's desire for the crown stems not from scholarly interest but from an insatiable need to possess all symbols of imperial authority.

## The Rise of the Merchant Cartels

In the power vacuum that followed The Rending and the presumed loss of the imperial family, three powerful merchant houses emerged to take control of the city. These houses—Kemvimore, Larannas, and Ren—reestablished order in the chaotic aftermath and formed the foundation of what would become Kaha'an's oligarchic rule.

Initially operating as a triumvirate, these three houses faced a chronic shortage of manpower in the struggling city. This shortage created an opportunity for a fourth power to rise from the seedy criminal underbelly of the newly formed refugee district known as the Warrens. Thus, the four-cartel system that now governs Kaha'an was born.

## The Four Cartels and Their Domains

Each cartel dominates a specific district of the city, establishing their power base in locations that reflect their particular trade and focus.

### The Grain: House Kemvimore

The Grain controls the import and distribution of all food supplies in Kaha'an:

- Based in the Noble District, maintaining some semblance of their former aristocratic status
- Operates from an elegant compound that combines practical warehouses with the refined architecture befitting a noble house
- Controls all food resources entering and circulating within the city
- Not limited to just grain, they manage all food resources
- Given that securing food for a desert city is critically important, they are one of the most powerful cartels
- Led by Rosik Kemvimore, whose mysterious past is the subject of much whispered speculation
- Rosik married into House Kemvimore before The Rending, a noble house that previously owned much of the farmland outside the city
- Following The Rending and the death of his wife, Rosik emerged as a hard, calculating leader who successfully transformed the agricultural house into a merchant power
- His brutal efficiency in restoring order after The Rending earned him both fear and respect

Rosik Kemvimore took control during the chaos following The Rending because he believed someone had to restore order or the city—the last bastion of civilization in the region—would collapse. His methods were harsh and brutal, but he felt he had little choice. Over time, he has come to believe that he should rightfully sit on the Ruby Throne, if only because he doesn't believe anyone else is capable of doing what is necessary to keep Kaha'an functioning. However, he understands the political realities that prevent this, much to his frustration.

Initially, Rosik considered seizing complete control in the aftermath of The Rending, believing that decisive leadership was necessary to prevent Kaha'an's collapse. However, he wasn't the only one with this ambition—Tania Larannas and Sa Ko Ren had similar ideas. Recognizing that an open power struggle between the three would likely destroy what remained of the city, Rosik made the calculated decision to propose a triumvirate instead of fighting for sole control. At the time, this seemed the necessary compromise to ensure the city's survival, but it's a decision he has increasingly come to question as the years have passed.

Rosik finds the necessity of working with the other cartels—particularly the Slavers—deeply irritating. He views many of their decisions as shortsighted and inefficient, chafing at the need to convince rather than command. In his mind, the city would run far more efficiently under his sole authority. His original compromise with Tania and Sa Ko was difficult enough, but the later emergence of Ronas Dermir and the Slavers as a fourth power—something Rosik reportedly warned the other cartels against—has further diluted his authority and complicated Kaha'an's governance.

### The Water: House Larannas

The Water controls all water-related resources and infrastructure:

- Headquartered in the Docks district, where they directly oversee all maritime trade
- Their imposing headquarters overlooks the harbor, combining defensive architecture with ostentatious displays of wealth
- Despite their base at the Docks, their jurisdiction extends throughout the city to include:
  - The entire aqueduct system (both the expanded network and the old Imperial infrastructure)
  - All cisterns and wells within the city walls
  - The sewage network (such as it is in some parts of the city)
- Control of the port authority and all ocean/water-related commerce
- Led by matriarch Tania Larannas
- House Larannas existed before The Rending but held relatively little influence
- Rose to prominence by quickly establishing order alongside the Grain and Iron
- Their swift action during the post-Rending chaos earned them political capital

### The Iron: Sa Ko Ren

The Iron oversees all metalwork in the city:

- Based in the Merchant Quarter, in a section unofficially known as "the Ironworks" or "the Foundry"
- Their headquarters is a massive foundry and factory complex constantly belching smoke into the sky
- The air around the Ironworks is filled with the sounds of hammering and the smell of steel and sweat
- Production of weapons, including swords and other armaments
- Creation of everyday items like pots, pans, nails, and horseshoes
- Management of all metal-based crafting and manufacturing
- Operation of large factories for metal production (most similar to Gunastran in their industrial approach)
- Led by Sa Ko Ren (properly addressed as "Ren Sa Ko"), a Terinok immigrant
- Known as "The Iron Lady," a nickname that reflects both her business and her character
- Created House Ren from nothing, establishing it as a power in Kaha'an
- Displays her foreign origins through her tawny skin and fierce disposition

### The Slavers: Ronas Dermir

While slavery existed before The Rending, the Slavers as an organized cartel only emerged after the catastrophe, rising from the criminal networks that developed in the Warrens:

- Based primarily in the city's slums, known as the Warrens
- Operate from a fortress-like compound deep within the labyrinthine district
- Capture desperate and impoverished individuals to provide labor
- Essential to the city's functioning despite their brutal methods
- Led by Ronas Dermir, a cruel and crass man with a criminal background
- Gained power by providing the manpower the other three cartels desperately needed
- His organization addressed the labor crisis that threatened recovery efforts
- Embodies the harshest aspects of Kaha'an's greed-driven society

The original three houses—Kemvimore, Larannis, and Ren—do not respect Ronas or the Slavers, viewing them as a necessary evil rather than legitimate peers. They wish they didn't have to deal with him but recognize the practical impossibility of maintaining the city without his organization's labor force. For his part, Ronas is acutely aware of this disdain and harbors resentment toward the "noble" houses that pretend he doesn't exist in public while depending on his services in private.

## The Balance of Power

Each cartel's territorial domain reflects both their function and the city's social hierarchy. The Grain operates from the refined Noble District, emphasizing their connection to Kaha'an's aristocratic past. The Water commands the vital Docks, controlling both maritime trade and the city's lifeline to the outside world. The Iron dominates the industrial heart of the Merchant Quarter, transforming raw materials into valuable goods. The Slavers rule the desperate depths of the Warrens, exploiting the most vulnerable to feed the city's constant hunger for labor.

This geographic distribution creates a delicate equilibrium:

- No single cartel can overpower the others
- Each controls resources vital to the city's survival
- If the Grain threatened to cut off food, the others could retaliate by cutting off water, weapons, or labor
- This mutually assured destruction has maintained a fragile stability for two decades
- The class distinction between the original three houses and the "upstart" Slavers adds another layer of tension to this balance
- The Long Knives serve as both a wild card and a stabilizing force, their services available to all factions yet loyal to none but themselves

No cartel could function without the others—the Grain needs the Water's ships to import food, the Water requires the Iron's tools to maintain their infrastructure, the Iron depends on the Slavers for workers, and the Slavers rely on the Grain's food to feed their captives. This mutual dependency, combined with each cartel's firm grip on their respective territories, has maintained Kaha'an's fragile stability for two decades.

### The Empty Throne

The Ruby Throne of Kaha'an sits empty due to this balance of power:

- An attempt to install a regent failed when the cartels couldn't agree on which family would provide the regent
- The standoff nearly led to open conflict
- To avoid war, they maintain the fiction that the emperor and empress might still be alive
- They rule in the imperial name while acting as the de facto power
- This arrangement has lasted for approximately 20 years since The Rending

## The Fifth Power: The Long Knives

While not officially recognized as a cartel, the Long Knives represent an influential power in Kaha'an's complex ecosystem—one that deals in the commodity of death itself. This guild of assassins operates primarily from the Warrens but extends its reach throughout the city.

- Predates The Rending, having existed as a secretive organization during imperial times
- Expanded dramatically in the chaos following The Rending
- Led by a figure known only as "The Mistress" or "Mother of Blades"
- Operates through a sophisticated hierarchy of specialized killers
- Maintains a carefully negotiated truce with the Slavers to prevent bloody conflict in the Warrens
- Though officially condemned by all four cartels in public, their services are secretly employed by each in private
- Acts as an invisible balancing force in Kaha'an's power dynamics, ensuring no single faction gains overwhelming advantage
- Their very existence reinforces Kaha'an's embodiment of Greed—even death is commodified

The Long Knives and the Slavers maintain an uneasy peace within the Warrens, each recognizing that open warfare between them would be mutually destructive. This calculated coexistence is typical of Kaha'an's pragmatic approach to power—enemies work together when necessity demands it, always with an eye to profit and survival.

## The Warrens

The Warrens developed after The Rending as refugees flooded into Kaha'an:

- Comparable to Kowloon Walled City in structure and conditions
- Built vertically rather than horizontally due to the limitations of the desert
- Created when nobles pushed refugees "out of sight, out of mind"
- Characterized by winding passageways, rickety bridges, and minimal sunlight
- A dangerous area where city authorities have little control
- City guards rarely enter the Warrens, and few who do ever return
- Operating as almost a city-within-a-city with its own brutal ecosystem
- Primary hunting ground for the Slavers, who rose from its criminal networks
- The hidden domain of the Long Knives, who maintain numerous concealed safe houses throughout its labyrinthine structure

## The Sin of Greed

Among the seven successor states that embody the seven deadly sins, Kaha'an represents Greed. This manifestation is evident in several ways:

- Merchants and rulers demand ever-increasing tolls and tariffs from trade passing through the city
- Vast wealth accumulates behind Kaha'an's walls while the surrounding desert grows more barren
- The desire to possess all symbols of imperial authority, including the Scholar's Crown, stems not from legitimate interest but from an insatiable need to own
- The four merchant cartels prioritize wealth and resource control above all else
- The exploitation of desperate refugees and the poor through the Slavers demonstrates how greed has corrupted basic humanity
- Even death itself has become a commodity through the services of the Long Knives

What makes Kaha'an's embodiment of greed particularly striking is how it transformed from an imperial capital with legitimate authority to a merchant oligarchy defined by accumulation and exploitation. The city that once represented the heart of empire now represents the darkest aspects of commerce, with even human lives becoming commodities to be traded.

# The Physical Layout of Kaha'an and Surroundings

## The Balishan Desert

The magical catastrophe known as The Rending transformed the once-fertile lands surrounding Kaha'an into a vast, hostile desert. While predominantly barren, the desert contains distinct regions with their own characteristics and dangers.

### The Dunes

Immediately surrounding Kaha'an lies a vast sea of sand dunes:

- Massive, ever-shifting sand formations that can reach heights of over 300 feet
- Constantly reshaped by the harsh desert winds, making navigation treacherous
- Temperatures regularly exceed 120°F (49°C) during daylight hours
- Almost entirely devoid of visible plant or animal life (though specialized creatures survive beneath the surface)
- Water is non-existent except for rare, temporary pools after the infrequent but violent thunderstorms
- Survival beyond a single day requires extensive preparation and local knowledge
- Even experienced guides can become disoriented when sandstorms obscure all landmarks

### The Badlands

Beyond the Dunes, the terrain transitions to the somewhat less hostile but still dangerous Badlands:

- Characterized by rocky outcroppings, gravel plains, and occasional scrub vegetation
- Slightly more hospitable than the Dunes, but still extremely harsh
- Temperatures remain dangerously high but drop more significantly at night
- Scattered water sources exist, though many are brackish or poisonous
- Home to hardy desert fauna including venomous reptiles and insects
- Natural caves and rock formations provide shelter for both wildlife and human outcasts
- Several small, isolated oases support primitive settlements outside Kaha'ani authority
- Serves as a haven for bandits, exiles, and those fleeing persecution or slavery in the city

### The Golden Path

The only reliable route through the Balishan Desert is the carefully maintained and heavily patrolled Golden Path:

- A wide, deliberately flattened trade route connecting Kaha'an to the other successor states
- Named for both the golden sand that borders it and the wealth that flows along it
- Marked by regular stone cairns and tall poles visible even during moderate sandstorms
- Dotted with fortified waystations approximately one day's travel apart
- Each waystation contains wells, basic provisions, and a permanent Kaha'ani Guard detachment
- Magical wards at key points help protect travelers from the worst of the desert's elements
- The path itself is subtly enchanted to resist being reclaimed by the desert
- Regular patrols by the Kaha'ani Guard deter most bandit activity on the path itself
- Despite these precautions, merchants typically hire additional caravan guards for protection
- The Golden Path splits into three main branches: the Northern Route (to Terinok and Erulius), the Eastern Route (to Gunastran and Uratha), and the Southern Route (to Vartonne and Silaris)

### The Gray Wastes

A desolate region that bears the most visible scars of The Rending:

- Located in what locals ominously call "Ground Zero" – though none can explain why this area was affected more severely than others
- The most lifeless part of the already barren desert, where not even the hardiest desert creatures venture
- The sand itself has a distinctive grayish tint unlike the golden hues found elsewhere in the Balishan Desert
- Travelers report unusual phenomena: compasses spinning wildly, strange whispers on the wind, and shadows that seem to move against the light
- People who spend time in the area often describe inexplicable feelings of dread, sorrow, and loss
- Magical detection attempts in the region produce disturbing or incomprehensible results
- Expeditions from Uratha attempting to study the phenomena rarely return intact
- The Kaha'ani authorities forbid exploration of this region under penalty of death
- Various folk explanations range from divine punishment to ancient curses to natural phenomena
- Those with magical sensitivity sometimes claim the area feels "wrong" or "thin" – though what this means is subject to debate

## The City of Kaha'an

Protected by powerful magical wards during The Rending, Kaha'an survived the catastrophe intact. The city's layout reflects both its imperial past and the current cartel-dominated present.

### City Overview

Kaha'an's physical structure combines imperial grandeur with pragmatic adaptations to desert life:

- Built primarily of sandstone, marble, and other heat-resistant materials
- Surrounded by massive walls that serve both defensive and environmental functions
- The walls reach heights of 100 feet in some sections, with strategic watchtowers
- A complex network of aqueducts, cisterns, and wells provides water throughout the city
- Narrow streets and covered walkways offer shade from the relentless sun
- Buildings typically feature thick walls, small windows, and interior courtyards
- The architecture grows more impressive toward the city center, reflecting social hierarchies
- Underground levels beneath many districts provide cooler living and working spaces
- The entire city slopes gently toward the harbor, facilitating water flow and drainage

The city is divided into five major districts, each with its own character and controlling faction:

### The Imperial Quarter

The heart of the city and its oldest section:

- Home to the Ruby Palace, where the empty throne awaits an occupant
- Technically neutral territory under the joint administration of all four cartels
- Contains the major administrative buildings from imperial times
- Houses the Hall of Cartels, where inter-cartel councils meet
- Populated primarily by bureaucrats, diplomats, and wealthy merchants
- Architectural style is predominantly classical imperial, with grand columns and domes
- The gardens surrounding the Ruby Palace are among the few green spaces in the city
- Considered the most prestigious address in Kaha'an
- Heavily patrolled by guards from all four cartels, making it paradoxically the safest district

### The Noble District (Grain Cartel)

The domain of House Kemvimore and Kaha'an's aristocracy:

- Located adjacent to the Imperial Quarter on its western side
- Features elegant manors, private gardens, and refined architecture
- Most buildings date from the imperial era but have been carefully maintained
- Streets are wider and better maintained than in other districts
- House Kemvimore's compound combines residential grandeur with practical warehouses
- Massive granaries and food storage facilities are cleverly integrated into the district's design
- Advanced preservation magic helps maintain food supplies in the desert heat
- Guards in Grain Cartel colors patrol regularly, ensuring both security and social control
- Despite its elegance, the district has a practical undercurrent reflecting Rosik's influence
- Population consists primarily of noble families, wealthy merchants, and their servants

### The Docks District (Water Cartel)

The domain of House Larannas and Kaha'an's maritime gateway:

- Located on the eastern edge of the city, where it meets the sea
- A busy harbor protected by both natural geography and massive breakwaters
- House Larannas' headquarters dominates the waterfront, combining defensive architecture with ostentatious displays of wealth
- The district contains warehouses, shipyards, and maritime support businesses
- Networks of aqueducts and water channels visible throughout the district connect to the main water systems
- The Water Cartel maintains control stations for the city's water distribution
- Despite being predominantly commercial, the district contains residential areas for sailors, dockworkers, and maritime merchants
- The architecture is more utilitarian than the Noble District but still substantial
- The smell of salt water provides a welcome relief from the desert heat
- Guards in Water Cartel colors monitor all incoming and outgoing vessels
- The district is notably more diverse than others, with foreign merchants and sailors creating a cosmopolitan atmosphere

### The Merchant Quarter (Iron Cartel)

The domain of Sa Ko Ren and the commercial heart of the city:

- Located south of the Imperial Quarter, occupying the central portion of the city
- A dense network of markets, workshops, and commercial buildings
- The Ironworks (or Foundry) section dominates the southern part of the district
- Massive foundry complexes constantly belch smoke into the sky
- The air around the Ironworks is filled with the sounds of hammering and the smell of steel and sweat
- Beyond the industrial section, the district contains the Grand Bazaar where all manner of goods are traded
- Buildings typically combine workshops on lower floors with living quarters above
- Architecture is more functional than ornate, though successful merchants' homes display their wealth
- Streets are arranged in a more organized grid pattern than other districts, facilitating commerce
- Guards in Iron Cartel colors patrol the markets and workshop areas
- Population consists primarily of craftspeople, merchants, and their families

### The Warrens (Slavers' Domain)

The domain of Ronas Dermir and the most desperate inhabitants of Kaha'an:

- Located in the northern section of the city, furthest from the harbor
- Originally developed as temporary housing for refugees after The Rending
- Evolved into a permanent district with its own brutal ecosystem
- Built vertically rather than horizontally due to space constraints
- Structures reach seven or eight levels high in some areas
- Buildings lean against each other, creating a haphazard but surprisingly stable network
- Narrow streets and alleys create a labyrinthine environment that outsiders cannot navigate
- Minimal sunlight reaches the lower levels, creating areas of perpetual twilight
- The Slavers' fortress-like compound stands in the center of the district
- The Long Knives maintain numerous hidden safe houses throughout the area
- No official maps of the Warrens exist, as its layout constantly changes with new construction
- City guards patrol only the outermost periphery; the interior is governed by the Slavers and other factions
- Population consists primarily of refugees, the impoverished, criminals, and those hiding from authority

### Other Notable Locations

#### The Aqueduct Network

- The ancient imperial aqueduct system has been expanded significantly since The Rending
- Control stations at key junctions are operated by the Water Cartel
- The main aqueduct runs from a mountain source far to the north, supplemented by desalination facilities at the harbor
- Access to the maintenance tunnels is strictly controlled, though rumors suggest they connect to hidden passages beneath the city

#### The Temple District

- A small enclave within the Imperial Quarter containing places of worship from various traditions
- One of the few areas where citizens from all districts mix freely
- The temples maintain nominal independence from cartel politics, though all rely on cartel patronage

#### The Arena

- A large amphitheater on the border between the Merchant Quarter and Noble District
- Hosts combat sports, theatrical performances, and public announcements
- One of the few entertainment venues accessible to all classes, though seating is strictly segregated

#### The Undercity

- A network of tunnels, ancient foundations, and repurposed cisterns beneath the city
- Officially does not exist according to cartel authorities
- Rumored to connect to pre-imperial ruins and possibly to the legendary City of Gears (Lathion)
- Contains what some call the "Brass Door" – an ancient sealed entrance of unknown origin
- The Long Knives are said to use these passages to move throughout the city unseen

#### The Arol Batae Compound

- A heavily fortified complex in a quiet corner of the Noble District
- Home to the remnants of the imperial guard, now known as the Arol Batae
- Maintains strict isolation from the rest of the city
- Unknown to most, it contains the hidden Princess Elara Valanar, last surviving heir to the imperial throne

## Transportation and Movement

### Within the City

- Wealthy citizens travel by palanquin carried by slaves or servants
- Merchants and middle-class citizens typically walk or use small hand-drawn carts
- Major goods are transported on larger carts drawn by desert-adapted beasts of burden
- The narrow streets in most districts make mounted transportation impractical except in the Noble District and Imperial Quarter
- The Water Cartel operates small ferry services along the harbor front

### Desert Travel

- Caravans are the primary means of long-distance travel and commerce
- Specially bred camels and other desert-adapted beasts serve as mounts and pack animals
- Sand-ships—large wheeled vessels with sail propulsion—traverse the flatter sections of the Dunes
- Magical transport exists but is extremely expensive and reserved for the most urgent communications or wealthiest travelers
- All desert travel follows the Golden Path or its branches, as venturing into the open desert is considered suicidal

## Defenses and Security

### The City Walls

- Massive sandstone and marble walls surround the city on three sides (the fourth being the harbor)
- Enchanted during imperial times, the walls helped protect the city during The Rending
- Watchtowers at regular intervals are staffed by guards from all four cartels on a rotating basis
- The main gates are heavily fortified and closely monitored

### The Harbor Defenses

- A series of breakwaters protect the harbor from both storms and potential naval threats
- Massive chains can be raised to block the harbor entrance in times of danger
- Watchtowers equipped with ballistae and magical defenses overlook the harbor approaches

### Internal Security

- Each district is primarily patrolled by guards of its controlling cartel
- The Imperial Quarter sees guards from all cartels, operating under strict protocols to prevent conflicts
- The Warrens has minimal official security presence, with order maintained by the Slavers and other internal factions
- The Long Knives serve as an invisible security force, their mere existence deterring some forms of crime and insurrection

## Climate and Environmental Conditions

### Within the City

- Daytime temperatures regularly exceed 100°F (38°C), with nighttime providing only moderate relief
- The sea breeze provides some cooling to the Docks District and adjacent areas
- Water is strictly rationed, with access determined by social status and cartel affiliation
- Sandstorms occasionally envelop the city, forcing most activities to pause
- The rare but violent rainstorms create brief flash floods, particularly in the Warrens

### The Desert Beyond

- Extreme temperature fluctuations between day and night
- Sandstorms can appear with little warning, obscuring visibility and scouring exposed flesh
- Rare thunderstorms create brief, violent downpours that can cause flash flooding
- The Shifting Lands experience unpredictable weather phenomena, including localized climate reversions

## Resource Management

### Water

- The city's most precious resource, controlled entirely by House Larannas
- Sourced from a combination of the northern aqueduct, deep wells, and harbor desalination
- Distributed through a complex network of aqueducts, channels, cisterns, and public wells
- Access is strictly tiered by social class and cartel affiliation
- Water theft is among the most serious crimes in Kaha'an, typically punishable by death

### Food

- Almost entirely imported from other regions, as the desert provides negligible agriculture
- House Kemvimore maintains massive storage facilities throughout the Noble District
- Distribution follows a similar hierarchy to water, with quality and quantity determined by status
- The Warrens receives minimal official food distribution, relying on black markets and scavenging

### Other Resources

- Building materials are primarily local sandstone and imported wood
- Metals come through trade arrangements managed by the Iron Cartel
- Luxury goods flow through the city in abundance, many never leaving the Noble District and Imperial Quarter
- The Badlands provide some limited resources, harvested by brave or desperate individuals

## The City's Future

As tensions rise between the cartels and rumors of a surviving imperial heir circulate, Kaha'an stands at a crossroads:

- The delicate balance of power could tip in numerous directions
- External pressures from other successor states add to internal stresses
- The discovery of Princess Elara Valanar would fundamentally reshape the political landscape
- The environmental challenges of desert life grow more acute with each passing year
- Yet the city's strategic position ensures its continued importance regardless of who rules

Kaha'an—once the jewel of an empire, now a desert crossroads ruled by merchant princes—continues to adapt and survive in circumstances that would have destroyed lesser cities. Whether its future holds a restoration of imperial glory or deeper descent into cartel-driven greed remains to be seen.
