# The old draft is not a worse version of this book

*It is a different book that happens to contain the same man.*

**NON-CANON, like everything else in this folder.** This document compares `TGMS - OLD/` against the
finished Book 1. It exists because a few of the comparisons are useful craft evidence, and it lives
here so that it inherits the same exclusion as the draft it describes: **nothing below is continuity,
nothing in it was reconciled against the books, and none of it may be cited as canon.** The old draft
was read once, for this document, and set down again.

A formatted version is published at
`claude.ai/code/artifact/15fa5bcc-be71-4e1c-b97b-5c5a9cadf220`.

## 1. Rule Zero did not exist, and you can watch the book discover it

The rule the series now runs on — *the camera stays on Xion's shoulder; what he attends to is what the
reader attends to* — was written down this month. The old draft had never heard of it and broke it
three times in twenty chapters.

- **Old ch14** is a Commander Vesk chapter, start to finish.
- **Old ch19** leaves Xion for Elara *and switches into present tense to do it*: "Elara watches from a
  palace window… She feels oddly detached."
- **Old ch13** stays nominally with Xion and reaches into Elara's head anyway — "Her royal upbringing
  had never prepared her for the chaotic vertical sprawl of the Warrens."

The finished book has none. Twenty-five chapters, one consciousness, and exactly one deliberate
widening of the lens — the last two pages of ch25, when the bells go up tower by tower to the harbour.

### The Vesk inversion, which nobody planned

> **Old draft, ch14, first line:** "Commander Vesk paced the length of the Arol Batae's inner ward, his
> shadow stretching and compressing with each turn."

> **The book, ch23:** "He was not pacing. Xion had expected pacing and got instead a man standing
> perfectly still in the middle of a swept expanse of stone."

The canon line was written to characterise Vesk, not to answer a manuscript nobody intended to read
again. It is the whole argument in two sentences: the old draft tells you what Vesk was doing, and the
book tells you what Xion found when he got there.

## 2. Eleven chapters opened on the weather

Dawn crept, dawn broke, morning light filtered, the dusk painted Kaha'an in hues of amber. More than
half the old draft began by describing the time of day to a reader who had not yet been given anyone to
be. The book does it four times in twenty-five, and one of those is a fever.

---

## 3. The ending problem, in its original form

The audit of all twenty-five endings turned up thirteen that needed cutting. This is where the habit
came from.

> **Old draft ends ch1:** "The princess, if she existed, might be the cure Kaha'an needed. Or she might
> be another symptom of a deeper affliction. Either way, Xion was committed to finding out, whatever
> the cost.
>
> The winding streets of Kaha'an stretched before him… Somewhere in this layered metropolis, perhaps a
> princess waited, unaware that her very existence might rewrite the future."

> **The book ends ch1:** "Two impossible things in one afternoon… Tomorrow, he would act."

The old version leaves Xion entirely for its last sentence and forecasts, on the narrator's authority,
about a woman who has not yet appeared. It is the purest specimen of the thing the ending pass was
built to hunt, and it is sitting in the first chapter.

---

## 4. The boy is never taken

**This is the largest single difference between the two manuscripts.**

Tam exists in the old draft. He comes in with a cut hand, he does not cry because *"Can't work if I
cry,"* Xion stitches him — and then Xion presses a silver piece into his good hand, a week's wages for
a labourer, and sends him home. He is never mentioned again across nineteen further chapters.

The narration explains the scene three times on the way out:

> *"While he spent his days playing at being a healer, children like Tam couldn't afford the luxury of
> pain."*
>
> *"The silver piece he'd given Tam represented nothing to him — pocket change… Such was the grotesque
> imbalance of Kaha'an."*
>
> *"These were bandages on wounds that would not close."*

| | Old draft | The book |
|---|---|---|
| What Xion does | Reaches into his pocket. The problem is money, he has money, the scene resolves. | Nothing. Two enforcers invent a debt in an open square and take the boy, and acting would mean saying his own name aloud. |
| What the narration does | Tells you it was ironic, three times. | Nothing. |
| What happens next | Nineteen chapters that never mention him. | Nine chapters of descent, and Xion carrying the same child up out of the dark with a fever in his blood. |

The book's whole argument is the correction. Xion never rescues Tam. Korvin does, and a food vendor
does, and a Slaver's cousin does, and a guard named Varris does — because Master Fen patched them up
once and never asked what they did for a living. **None of that exists in the old draft**, because the
boy walks out of chapter one with a coin and is not thought of again.

## 5. Janice does not exist either

The weaver whose back room hides the clinic is named once in the old draft — *"Mistress Thera"* — in a
subordinate clause, and never appears again. The copper argument, the coded ledgers, the eight years of
cover, the woman who told a fifteen-year-old he could not keep his own name and who later burned the
patient records rather than let them be read: all of it is invention.

**Which makes her arc across this year's work a three-stage one**, and the middle stage was a mistake
worth recording: **absent** in the old draft, then **over-weighted** in the rewrite — 275 words of
description against 90 of speech, a narrator-surrogate standing in the protagonist's light — and now
**sound before sight**, a needle that stops and starts behind him while his hands are busy, and one
look he never asks about.

**And the division inside the naming scene is exact.** Janice supplies the instruction — *"You can't
keep that name. Not if you want to keep doing this."* **Xion supplies the name** — *"Fen. Master Fen."*
Told at fifteen that he has to stop being himself, he reaches for the surname of the woman his father
had executed, and wears it for eight years. She gave him the cover; Mira gave him everything the cover
was protecting. *(The first cut of this document said Janice invented the alias. She did not, and the
error is recorded in the changelog, because the flattening always runs in the same direction and it
costs Mira every time.)*

## 6. The Iron Lady used to be won with a speech

> **Old draft, ch16 "Trial by Combat":** Sa Ko Ren pours a dark liquid from a simple carafe and asks the
> princess for her vision of governance. Elara delivers it — artificial scarcity, exploitation, lungs
> and limbs — and Sa Ko says *"You've studied us well."* Xion is in the room. He has nothing to do in
> it.

> **The book, ch24:** Sa Ko waves the bloodline and the divine sanction away in six lines — a pretty
> mirror proves the gods like you, it doesn't prove you can hold a throne — and offers single combat
> instead. Xion cannot follow the fight, so he watches the only thing he has ever known how to watch,
> and is the first person in the hall to work out that she isn't fighting back.

The old scene is competent; the new one is a different order of thing, for a reason that has nothing to
do with sentences. The book asks what *this specific man* can perceive in a room and then refuses to
give the reader anything else. He is a healer, so he reads a rib strike that should have cost her
something and did not. The chapter's discovery belongs to him because it is the only discovery he is
equipped to make.

---

## 7. The book learned to change speed

The old draft holds one gear. Twenty chapters between 2,386 and 4,595 words, almost everything
clustered near the top, dynamic range 1.9×.

The book runs 1,453 to 3,758, dynamic range 2.6×, and moves in blocks: a compressed opening movement
(ch1–8, mean ~1,900), a doubling at ch9 when the scale changes, four consecutive chapters over 3,100
through the descent, then the sharpest contraction in the novel at ch17 for the rescue that fails.

**This turned out to be the most useful discovery of the pass and the one that saved the most good
material.** Three separate proposals to cut "talky" chapters were made and withdrawn once the length
profile was checked — including two attempts on ch8, which sits dead centre of its own movement and is
the last quiet chapter before the disaster in ch10.

*"This is talky" is not a finding.* It is now in the rulebook in those words.

---

## 8. What the old draft was actually good at

**It covered more ground.** Twenty chapters reach the coronation by ch17 and then keep going — three
further chapters of aftermath, a Remembrance ceremony, a cartel regrouping. That material is not lost.
It became Book 2, and Book 2 is the better half of the series.

**And it knew what the story was about.** The imbalance, the manufactured scarcity, the healer who is
also the son of the man causing the wounds — all of that is on the first page of the old chapter one.

What it did not yet know was that a book has to *be* somebody. It kept stepping back to explain what it
had just shown, which is a form of not trusting the reader, and it kept leaving Xion to go and look at
something more interesting.

Nothing more interesting than Xion was ever found. That is the whole finding, and it took twenty
chapters and seventy-one thousand words to establish, which is exactly what a discarded draft is for.
