# Chapter 15: Melina

**Timeline:** Week 19 post-coronation
**Characters:** Xion
**Emotional work:** The routine breaks. The cost arrives, and it has a name.

---

## Beats

1. The quiet square. The familiar low wall. Xion sits where he always sits and waits.

2. He waits.

3. He waits.

4. He notices, because he has nothing else to do, that there is a vendor arguing with a customer at the far end of the square. A dog trots past. Two children chase each other, shrieking. He notices these things in the way you notice things when you've been sitting still for longer than you expected and your attention has started casting around for somewhere to be.

5. She's not late. That's the thing. Melina is never late. Across every meeting they've had at this spot — across every argument and political update and moment of exhausted honesty — she has never once been late. She arrives, he's already there, and the meeting begins. The pattern has been as reliable as the sun. Xion realizes, sitting on the low wall, that he has never once wondered if she'd show up. It has never been in question. She always comes from the estate direction.

6. She's just late. That's all. She got held up. Maybe their father called a meeting that ran long — a Coterie matter, something institutional, the kind of thing Melina can't easily excuse herself from without raising questions she doesn't want raised. Maybe a visitor arrived at the estate, someone she couldn't send away without offense. Maybe she twisted her ankle on the uneven paving between here and the noble district — she's probably fine, probably sitting somewhere, probably cursing the road. These explanations arrive in a steady stream, and Xion lets them. He cycles through them. He is aware he's cycling through them, and he cycles through them anyway, because the alternative is to sit with the thing he's not sitting with.

7. A woman walks through from the estate direction, and Xion's attention snaps to her — holds — and then she resolves into someone who is not Melina, who has never been Melina, who continues on her way without knowing that she caused a man on a low wall half a second of hope followed by its immediate withdrawal. This keeps happening. Every figure from that direction. The hope is shameful in its automaticity and he can't stop it.

8. Perhaps it is something to do with the chaos in the city. Rosik needed her for something urgent — with Ronas's men doing what they're doing, the estate may be in a kind of crisis management that doesn't care about Melina's personal schedule. Yes. That's almost certainly it. There is a perfectly sensible reason. Xion decides this. He has decided this several times now, and each time it holds for a few minutes before the knot in his stomach tightens again and he has to decide it again.

9. He doesn't leave. He should — he should go to the estate, or send a runner, or do anything other than sit here in a public square waiting for a person who isn't coming. But leaving changes what this is. Right now this is: Xion waiting for his sister who is running late. If he leaves, if he starts *finding out*, then it becomes something else, and he doesn't know what the something else is yet and he is not ready for it. So he stays. And waits. And the square continues its insultingly ordinary afternoon around a man who hasn't moved in a very long time.

10. He sees the messenger before the messenger sees him. A man moving with the particular energy of someone carrying an errand he'd rather not be carrying — not fast, but *deliberate*, the way people move when they want to get something over with. He's looking around the square in the systematic way of someone searching for a specific person. His eyes find Xion. He slows. And then — just for a moment, just one involuntary thing — he swallows. closes his eyes briefly. The smallest flinch of a man who knows that the next sentence out of his mouth will change a life, and who has been dreading this moment since he was given the task. Xion knows that flinch. He has worn it himself, in the corridor outside a patient's room, in the moment before a family looks up and sees his face and understands from his face alone that the answer is no. It is the thing you do when you have run out of time to prepare and must simply speak. He recognizes it from the inside. He knows what it means and he cannot make himself stand up.

11. The man says something. Xion can barely hear it. There is a sound in his ears — a roaring, like the ocean, like blood — and it turns the messenger's words into fragments. "Lord Kemvimore." A pause that is doing a lot of work. "I'm so sorry." Another pause. "Your sister—"

12. No.

13. The square. The low wall. The sun at the angle it's at. A dog crosses the far end. The children are gone but the vendor is still there. The city continuing. The city continuing, the way it does, the way it does not know how to stop.

14. Xion finds that he's leaning against the wall behind him. He doesn't know when this happened. The stone is warm against his back — still holding the day's heat even as the light has begun to slant toward evening — and this detail is very vivid while everything else has gone too bright, too loud, the sounds of the square arriving at strange volumes. He's a healer. He can name what's happening to him. The narrowed edges of his vision, the way his pulse is behaving, the particular lightheadedness — he has sat with patients in exactly this state and explained it to them in calm clinical terms. *This is your body's response to shock. It will pass.* He knows what this is and the knowing does nothing at all.

15. *Same time next week?*

16. The square. The low wall. The estate direction. The vendor packs up his goods and leaves, whistling something. Xion is sitting on the low wall where he always sits. He has not moved. He does not know how long he has been here. He is still, in some way he cannot explain, waiting for her.

---

**The pattern as the death scene.** The reader has been at this spot with Xion and Melina across six chapters. They know the rhythm in their body: Xion arrives first, Melina comes from the estate direction, they sit, they argue, they love each other imperfectly. The chapter breaks the pattern before a word is spoken, and the reader feels the break — the growing wrongness of the empty space — before they understand what it means. Melina's death is not the Surface Slums scene, which happens offstage. It is the absence on the low wall. The chapter stages grief as a vacancy.

**Denial as structure, not psychology.** Beats 6–9 should feel like watching someone very carefully not look at something. Xion is intelligent enough to know what he's doing — cycling through explanations, refusing to leave, staying in the world where Melina is merely late. His intelligence makes the denial more precise, not less complete. He is engineering himself a few more minutes in the reality where this hasn't happened yet.

**The healer receiving the diagnosis.** Beat 14 deploys Xion's medical training against him. He can name every symptom with clinical accuracy and the naming changes nothing. He has been the calm voice. He is now the patient. The knowledge that should give him control gives him only the ability to narrate his own collapse with professional exactness.

**Beat 15: no context, no attribution.** *Same time next week?* is Melina's last line from Chapter 14. It arrives here without introduction. The reader remembers. That's the whole mechanism.

**What Xion does not know yet — authorial context only, not in the prose.** Melina arranged a meeting with one of Ronas's lieutenants in the Surface Slums. She went as herself, carrying the Kemvimore name, believing deals could still be made. The lieutenant killed her mid-sentence — not because she threatened him, but because the leash was off and he could. Chaos doesn't negotiate. None of this is available to Xion on the low wall. The messenger gave him fragments through the roaring in his ears. The full picture assembles later, over days, from multiple sources — and belongs to Chapter 16's aftermath, not here. Here there is only the square, and the absence.

**What this chapter does not contain.** The Surface Slums. The lieutenant. Melina's last moments. Any of the violence. The causation chain — that comes later, when Xion can hear again. This is Xion's chapter, and Xion's experience of his sister's death is an empty square and a warm stone wall and the estate direction, and Melina not coming from it.
