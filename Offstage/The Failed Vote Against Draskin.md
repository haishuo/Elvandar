# The Failed Vote Against Draskin

**After Book 8, following the rescue of Elara and Xion and the exposure and ending of the Globehall.** The end of the atrocity leaves a sitting Scholar-King accused of involvement in its use and concealment and in the false imprisonment of the Empress of Balisha and her Emperor Consort. Draskin's immunity from prosecution for conduct undertaken under claimed official authority makes his continued tenure an obstacle to trying him in Urathan courts.

Responsibility is unevenly distributed among the Towers. Ivory is deeply implicated, with Draskin at the center. Amethyst is heavily implicated by the advanced magic on which the Globehall depended; its Provost and senior magical scholars face scrutiny, without every individual's knowledge having been established. Onyx's involvement extends to active suppression: its intelligence apparatus and implicated Ilhamori officers silenced people who discovered the Globehall. Their conduct goes beyond knowing and failing to object. This does not establish that every Onyx scholar or Ilhamori officer knew.

Pearl, Topaz, Emerald, Sapphire, and Ruby seek Draskin's removal. They are variously uninvolved or horrified. Sapphire's custody of law and records means the inquiry may reach its own scholars; neither the extent of their knowledge nor particular scholars' complicity is settled. The Tower nevertheless supports removal. It distinguishes the political judgment that Draskin should leave office from the criminal findings that must be proved in court.

All five Provosts sponsor a motion of no confidence. **Kandor Belus proposes it, and the other four join him** *(author's ruling, 2026-11-21)*. Sapphire's Provost is personally away overseeing the preservation and examination of evidence for the subsequent prosecutions. Draskin schedules the vote for that absence, giving the required formal notice while knowing the Provost cannot return in time. The constitution permits no proxy or advance written ballot. Sapphire's sponsorship cannot be counted as its vote.

Amethyst's presence supplies the fifth Provost required for a valid proceeding. Whether its Provost intended to help Draskin by supplying that quorum is not established. Four votes are cast for removal and none against. The motion fails because removal requires five affirmative votes out of the full seven seats. No one has voted to retain Draskin. Nevertheless, the valid failed vote triggers the two-year prohibition on another no-confidence motion against him.

Tiberian Valanar is incensed. To him, the procedure has protected the man implicated in his parents' imprisonment and the Globehall without anyone defending his fitness to rule. Melina works to restrain him. She shares his judgment of Draskin but sees how foreign intervention could divide the five opposing Towers or give Draskin a national emergency around which to rally support.

Belus is equally angry. Draskin then orders Ilhamori officers to approach him and ask him to *reaffirm his loyalties*. Operational orders ordinarily pass through the Scholar-King, and Draskin still holds that office. The implicated Ilhamori leadership also has its own interest in his survival. His instruction reaches Belus through a service whose role in suppressing the Globehall's discovery has just been exposed.

Draskin treats the request as an assertion of administrative authority. Belus reads it as a threat against the military leadership for having voted to remove him, and as possible preparation for a purge. Loyalty to Uratha is being tested through submission to the incumbent.

Draskin also seeks a constitutional amendment requiring all seven Provosts to vote for removal. He cannot enact that change himself and does not have the assent of the five opposing Towers. The proposal is not an accomplished amendment. Together with the Ilhamori visit, it makes his determination to remain in office unmistakable: leaving office would end the immunity that prevents his prosecution.

This account ends with the failed vote, the approach to Belus, and the proposed amendment. What follows is [The Strike on the Ivory Tower](The%20Strike%20on%20the%20Ivory%20Tower.md). Neither account establishes a successful amendment, removal, or prosecution.
