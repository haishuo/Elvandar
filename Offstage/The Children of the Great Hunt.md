# The Children of the Great Hunt

**When:** The ban, among Elara's first edicts after Book 1. The conspiracy, between Book 7 and Book 8.
**Who:** Melina Valanar and Vanessa Halcin; Elara, Xion and Tiberian Valanar, and Kess Ashwood; the heirs of the
hunting houses, including Lucien Vey; an unacknowledged son of Kalden Erulius; the Long Knives; a lesser
house of assassins.

Lucien's approach to his sister Celestine and the consequences for Tiberian's courtship are recorded in
[The Marriage of the Tiger Prince](The%20Marriage%20of%20the%20Tiger%20Prince.md).

## The ban

The Great Hunt was the tradition Elara heard discussed at the Larannas masquerade in B1 ch10, and the
conversation that put her eyes out of control in front of the whole Noble District. As soon as she held the
throne she outlawed it. It was not the first privilege she stripped from the old nobility, but it was the one
that broke the camel's back. The houses that had hunted — something like **two in five of the nobility** —
called it imperial interference with traditional values.

**The old guard knew better than to press the point.** A tradition of hunting people is difficult to defend
on any ground, and they understood that in strict law she could have had the Arol Batae arrest the lot of
them. **She did not, because arresting two-fifths of a nation's nobility in a single stroke would have been
politically explosive**, and she judged that the ban was enough. They complained, and they let it go.

**Their children did not have to let anything go, because they never had anything to let go of.** Eighteen
years later they had the resentment without the background. They had not been at the masquerades and had
never seen the prey. What they knew was that their houses had once been great and that the Valanars had made
them small. **The heirs of houses that had stood with the Grain under Rosik had the furthest to fall**, and
by the end of Book 7 they had watched the last hope of the old order die on the Golden Path. *(None of them
knows that the tradition they grew up resenting the loss of was itself a revival. The original Hunt opened
the royal forests to commoners alongside the nobility. The human-prey version was born after the Rending and
was barely twenty years old when it was banned.)*

## How it formed

It began as idle talk. The young nobles saw each other at soirées and masquerades and talked the way people
in that position talk. Then someone said something dangerous as a joke, and nobody laughed. Someone asked,
obliquely, whether he was serious, and the silence was an answer. **The next gathering was a great deal more
tense than the last.**

**Lucien Vey turned the talk into a conspiracy.** The fall of the Grain cartel had cost his house a
substantial share of its wealth and much of its old influence, though the Veys remained rich. He became the circle's principal financier, began giving orders and got men
who had spent their evenings complaining to commit themselves. The money gave him the authority to do it.
When his own purse ran short he borrowed against what he expected to inherit, from lenders willing to
advance money on a promise.
Every man he ordered about was a noble in his own right, and Lucien treated them like servants. The circle
owed its ability to act to a man much of it could barely stand.

**What they wanted was not a death. It was the Coterie back, with themselves in its chairs.** For twenty years
the city had been ruled in the Valanar name by people who were not Valanars, and their houses had been great
under it. Killing one Valanar would leave the others ruling, and the order that had made their houses small
would go on exactly as before. **So the plan was to remove the dynasty, all but one of it.** Their reasoning was not stupid. It was wrong in every particular,
and each part of it was a fair inference from what the court can see.

- **Elara was out of the question.** She had made the policies and would never unmake them. On matters like
  this she was famously stubborn, and she would call it *ethical*.
- **Xion was useless.** To a watching court he was a soft-spoken healer who agreed with his formidable wife
  about everything. That he carries enormous weight with her, and that she married him for the judgment she
  takes so seriously, is invisible from the outside, **because every conversation that matters between them
  happens in private.** He was to die because he was there, not because he mattered.
- **Tiberian was his mother again, only louder.** The Tiger Prince had her fire, would keep her policies and
  might well extend them, and he was the heir.
- **And then there was Melina.** She was calm, methodical and analytical, and to them she looked reasonable.
  Surely a woman of her abilities resented standing behind a brother famous for swinging a sword. Surely she
  felt cheated that he would inherit. Surely, being cool and unreadable and her grandfather's granddaughter,
  she could be brought to trade the rest of her family for a crown.

**They wanted her twice over, and they needed her once.** First, they wanted her to do the killing. Since the
Green Cities, Kess Ashwood had not left Tiberian's side, and the Arol Batae stood around his parents. Force was
the worse road by far, and **what they wanted was someone already inside**, with access to the family's food,
rooms and habits, who could make easy what force made desperate. That meant poison, and the family table.
Second, and this they needed, **the blood.** The palace stood sealed for twenty years until a Valanar laid her
hand on its doors, and no regime they could build would get through them without one. **She was to open the
palace, and she was not to rule it.**

**Her cooperation was preferred, and it was not required.** They would much rather the princess helped them
willingly. If, as they thought unlikely, she would not, the family would die by other means, the lesser house's
means, and she would be taken and married to their man regardless. **They did not need her consent, only her
person.** A captive bride opens a door as well as a willing one.

## The face

**The man they meant to put beside her was a son of Kalden Erulius, never acknowledged, living quietly and
wanting nothing.** He had no faction, no ambition and no house behind him, which was the point. Kalden's
legitimate kin came with the creditor houses that now owned the rump attached. A bastard came with nothing
but the name, and **the name came with twenty years of legal work** that had been probably winning until Elara
appeared. They did not believe in the claim. **They wore it.**

**And the name was the point, not an embarrassment.** To the city, Kalden was the man who poisoned its empress
and besieged its walls. **To this circle he was their side's champion**, the last man who tried to bring the
old order back, allied with Rosik at the end, and they had watched him die on the Golden Path. His son was the
cause continued: the old guard's banner on a quiet man who had never asked to carry it. **He was chosen for
the one thing about himself he never chose.** That Kaha'an hated the name did not trouble them, because
popular feeling is not what decides who governs Kaha'an. He was to marry the princess, her blood would
answer the wards, his father's case would answer the lawyers, and the circle would answer for both of them.

**He did not want it.** He was told what was intended for him rather than asked, in the way the Nika crowd
took Hypatius. That is why he survives.

## The morning after, as they planned it

**None of this happened.** It is what they expected, and it is the reason the plan needed killers and not an
army. **Kaha'an has no institution that is loyal to the dynasty for its own sake.** After the Rending the
Coterie took the city without converting anyone: three cartels held what mattered, every decree went out in
the Valanar name, and Vessel Street, the customs houses and the tax rolls went on working under them for
twenty years. **The machinery serves whoever is holding it.** Some will object, some will resign, and some
will stay loyal to the dead. Those are individuals, not a veto.

**So the killing was the whole of the force required.** The poison would do its work at the family table, and
the lesser house would deal with whatever the poison could not reach. By morning the circle would present
itself as a council keeping order in a stricken city, as the Coterie once had. Melina would be proclaimed and
married, willing or not. **Decrees would go out in her name, a living Valanar this time rather than a missing one**, and the
machinery would carry on. Her blood made the regime legal as well as letting it into the palace.

**The only thing the old Coterie never had to deal with was the palace**, and that is the whole difference
between their plan and the original. The Coterie could rule in the name of a dynasty that was missing; they had
to rule in the name of one that was sitting in the throne room. **So the throne would hold puppets**: Melina to
open the doors, sit the throne and say nothing, and Kalden's son beside her to listen and sign. Both were to
behave as though they were not there, rubber-stamping whatever the council put in front of them, while the
council governed the city as the Coterie had. **It was a restoration, and what it restored was the
interregnum.**

**Their weight was real but borrowed in part.** Alongside Lucien's money, a few ringleaders who were heads of
their houses supplied resources of their own. Most were heirs, who gave the plot its conviction and were
betting that parents who had let the Hunt go without a fight would fall in line once the thing was done.
**That bet was never tested.**

**Neither was the Arol Batae.** The circle reckoned that the desert people who raised Elara guard the throne
and the blood, and that a Valanar on the throne would keep them in their barracks. It was a fair inference
from what the court can see. **Whether it was true nobody ever learned**, because the plot never got that far.

**Every part of it was wrong.** Tiberian is the elder by a few hours, he is the heir, and she has never minded.
Her drive to win is fifth in her stack and her drive to accumulate is last. He is the person she keeps almost
nothing from, and the family they meant her to sell is the one thing for which she will spend anything.

## The two approaches, and the two refusals

**They worked toward the princess obliquely**, the same way they had recruited each other, and nothing they
said to her could have been repeated as an offer. **And they went to the Orb Weaver**, because everyone in
Kaha'an knows the Orb Weaver, and a plot like this needs knowledge that cannot be had anywhere else. They knew
better than to ask for anything but information.

**They went to the Long Knives first, and the Long Knives declined.** The Mistress who had raised Rosik was
dead or retired by then. Her successor had no family reason to refuse, and needed none, **because the job was
bad business.** The Imperial House is not another client's rival. Killing the empress, her consort and her
heir is the equivalent of murdering a head of state three times over. It would have staked the guild's whole
standing with the crown on a single contract. **They did not interfere, either.** A guild that sabotaged
other houses' contracts, however ill-advised, would soon have no clients of its own. That would be worse
business still.

**Lucien found a lesser house that took the job instead.** The smaller outfit was hungry to make its name
and saw the Long Knives' refusal as an opening rather than a warning.

## How she found it

**Nobody told her. She assembled it.** It was the kind of puzzle she is best at. A question at a soirée about
her family's habits that, taken alone, meant nothing. A run of purchases from the Orb Weaver by buyers who
did not know they were buying from the same woman, each one innocent, and together the outline of a
household being studied. An inquiry, through a cut-out, into the whereabouts of a man nobody had any reason to
look for. A rival broker turning down a large job. **Their own security was what exposed them.** They had
broken the work into pieces and asked many small questions through many mouths, so that no one source would
see the whole. That works everywhere except where every source leads back to one person.

**What she reached was a probability, not a proof**: *there is a very decent chance someone is trying to kill
my family.* She could not confirm it without warning them.

## How it was taken apart

**Melina and Nessa peeled it apart from the inside.** Not everyone in it was a true believer, and the pattern
that showed her the plot also showed her the people with doubts. Lucien had already given some of them a
reason to want out. Their questions to the broker sounded like looking for an exit rather than planning a
killing. **Melina found them. Nessa worked them.** The Orb Weaver
sells nothing but information, and it did not need to sell anything else. The doubters were told, accurately
and for a price, what they were part of: that the Long Knives had turned the job down; that Kess Ashwood had
not left the prince's side since the Green Cities; that an empress who once declined to arrest two-fifths of
her nobility should not be counted on to decline twice. **It was all true, and that is why it worked.** They
drifted out, one at a time. A circle that loses members without explanation suspects its own, and nobody
looked toward a broker or her palace informant.

## The attempt

**The true believers and the lesser house went ahead anyway**, without the inside woman they had never had, on
the plan they had held in reserve: kill the three by force and take her.
Melina had resisted telling anyone until then. That was strategy, not shame: Tiberian charges, and if he had
known while the waverers were being peeled away, he would have gone straight at the circle and wrecked the
quiet work. **Once they committed to the attempt that risk was gone.** She told Tiberian first, and then the
two of them told their parents, and Kess and the Arol Batae knew within the hour.

**She told them all of it, including that she had been meant to be the poisoner and the bride.** The full
account was also the safe one. *They have been sounding me out for weeks, and I put the rest together*: both
halves are true, the second is what everyone already knows she does, and the only thing left out is that some
of the pieces came through her own market. **Tiberian was angry for her before he was concerned for himself**,
because the insult was to her. Nobody in the family doubted her for a moment.

**It came to nothing.** They had too little intelligence, their resources were crippled by every defection,
and the plan they fell back on was the one they had always known was worse. **They did not get past Kess or
the Arol Batae. They did not get within fifty feet of any of the three, and they never laid a hand on her.**

## The reckoning

**This time the security that had hidden them did not save them.** An attempt on the whole Imperial House is
not one the crown lets stop at the go-betweens, and *they sounded me out* is not evidence. **So Melina spent
the Orb Weaver.** The investigators found what they found because she told them where it was, and much of
what she told them she could only have known from her own books. **The family comes first, and this is the
first time that rule was ever called on.** She did not burn the brokerage. She used it hard enough that it
could be seen.

**The ringleaders she identified, including Lucien Vey, were tried for high treason and publicly executed.** That was
justice, plainly: they had tried to murder the empress, her consort and her heir, and no society treats
regicide lightly. **It was also convenient, and she knew it.** The men who could put the pieces together were
the men who had run the buying, and the men who had run the buying were the ringleaders. She wrote the list,
the list was correct, and it suited her. **She does not pretend to herself otherwise, and nobody else ever
learns it.**

**One of them did put it together.** In the cells he called her the Orb Weaver to her face, and a lying bitch.
*Perhaps,* she said. *But you will take that secret to the grave. Your execution is scheduled for tomorrow
morning. Enjoy your final meal, good sir.* It is the only thing anyone remembers her saying about the whole
affair.

**The lesser house was broken.**

**Kalden's son was spared, and it was done in the open.** He had never wanted any of it, and the crown would
not hang a man for what had been intended for him.

**Nessa's cover was never burned.** The secret went to the gallows with the only men who had guessed it, and
her standing to the trade as the Orb Weaver's palace informant survived intact. **It was nearer a thing than
she had ever let the game get**, and that is hers to carry.

## Why no scene

**It does not warrant one**, and the reason belongs in the entry. The attempt never came close. Attempts on the
family are not ordinary, but they are not unknown; being who they are puts a target on them, and that is a
regrettable fact of their lives. **What makes this one matter is the work that blunted it**, and none of that
work was ever visible. The court saw a failed plot and a morning of executions. It never saw her.

---

**Why this entry exists.** Two things in Book 8 rest on it.

**The Orb Weaver goes into Book 8 tested.** The narrow line set at its founding is that she spends it only for
her family. This is the first time that line meets a real plot against her family. The instrument is spent
and survives, and it is the reason the plot failed. So when her parents vanish in Uratha, she reaches without
hesitation for a network she has every reason to trust, and **that is what makes it so bad when the pieces will
not come together.**

**Book 8's ordinary morning is earned.** The family Book 8 opens on has just survived something the court knows
only by its executions. The morning is calm because Melina made it calm, and nobody in the room says so.

**What it is not.** It is not Melina's coming-of-age; Book 7 holds that. It is not a second Book 8 puzzle; told
as account and not as scene, it gives the reader the fact and leaves Book 8 the experience. **And it is not
the Taking of the Tiger Prince again.** That was force and it reached him. This is the answer to the guard
the Taking created, an attempt to get past Kess by recruiting the one person already inside, with seizing her as
the fallback. It did not reach any of them.

**What it does to Kalden's claim.** His sheet and the Book 7 outline say the claim dies with him on the Golden
Path, and as a claim it does: nobody after him believes in it. **What survives is the costume.** The circle
uses his twenty years of work as a mask on a son he never acknowledged, which is what Rosik did to Kalden
himself, now done to the case without the man. **Never point at it**, and never let it be read as the claim
reviving.

**Constraint downstream.** Ronas's Great Hunt speech (`People/Ronas Dermir.md`) indicts the Hunt in the present
tense — *"you hunt humans"*. Wherever it is placed, it must fall before the ban or be put into the past
tense.
