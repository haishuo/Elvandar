# The Strike on the Ivory Tower

**After [The Failed Vote Against Draskin](The%20Failed%20Vote%20Against%20Draskin.md).** *(Author's ruling,
2026-11-21.)* The vote has failed, and the two-year prohibition bars another motion. Draskin has sent
Ilhamori officers to ask Kandor Belus to *reaffirm his loyalties*, and has begun moving toward an amendment
requiring all seven Provosts to vote for removal.

**Belus concludes that the lawful routes are closed.** Removal has failed on the arithmetic and cannot be tried
again for two years. Prosecution is blocked by the immunity. Recall belongs to a Tower's own faculty, and
Ivory's will not recall its Scholar-King. Draskin cannot pass the amendment against five Towers, but Belus
reads the Ilhamori visit as preparation for a purge: accusations, suspensions, vacated seats, replacement
Provosts, and an amendment passed by the replacements. **If he does nothing soon, he believes, Uratha as he
knows it will vanish.**

**He chooses the smallest intervention he can design.** A small, fast strike force from the Ruby Tower moves on
the Ivory Tower with a single objective: take Draskin into custody, in irons if necessary, so that he can be
removed and tried. Belus knows Onyx and Amethyst are implicated too and could retaliate even if he succeeds,
and hopes the strike will be fast enough that Draskin is already under arrest before they can react. He has no
settled view of how the courts would treat it. **He knows he is exceeding his authority as War Provost and
expects to lose his seat when it is over.** He goes anyway.

**The strike fails.** The Ilhamori fight the Ruby force off. They are the service that arrests powerful mages
who do not want to be arrested, the Ivory Tower is their ground, and the fight is close, room-by-room work of
exactly the kind they train for. Ruby trains for the field. The force was kept small to limit bloodshed, and it
is too small to overwhelm the defenders. No leak or betrayal is established, and none is needed.

**Draskin immediately declares Belus in rebellion.** The attack gives him what the vote did not:

- **A military emergency.** An armed assault on the Ivory Tower lets the Scholar-King issue temporary orders on
  military service on the authority of the office, without a Council vote.
- **A real treason.** He no longer needs to manufacture the accusations a purge would have required.
- **A divided opposition.** The four other Towers that voted to remove him must now choose between him and a
  Provost who attacked the seat of government.

**Whether the declaration is itself lawful is contested.** The constitution gives the Scholar-King emergency
military authority, but suspension of a Provost belongs to the Council, recall to the Tower's faculty, and
the vacating of a seat to a treason conviction. Nothing in it lets the Scholar-King alone declare a sitting
Provost a rebel. **Both sides can claim the constitution, and neither has clean hands.**
