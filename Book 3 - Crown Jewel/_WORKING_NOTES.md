## Canon overhaul (handoff from prior session) — read first

A series-wide canon-consistency pass landed between sessions. The authoritative sources are `Magic/The Rending.md` and `Places/Kaha'an/Lathion_ The Lost City of Gears.md`. Summary of what changed and what it means for Book 3:

- **The Rending happened ~20 years ago.** Caused by a grief-stricken necromancer forcing open the **Gray Gate** (the metaphysical boundary between life and death — not a place). Two phases: a fast necrotic death-wave on day zero, then a slow desertification spreading over ~a year. There is **only one Rending.** Do not reintroduce "First Rending" or "Sundering."
- **Lathion was built ~5,000 years ago, sealed ~2,000 years ago, inhabited until 20 years ago.** (Updated lore: the city was NOT sealed immediately on founding — that never made narrative sense. For the first three millennia, surface contact existed via the **Great Shaft**, a magical elevator that brought people down to Lathion. ~2,000 years ago the city was deliberately sealed; the Great Shaft was filled in and the elevator destroyed. The Brass Door — what the expedition now uses — was the **service entrance** for workers and freight. That's why it is so mechanical and unceremonious; it *was* the back way. The "front door" (Great Shaft) was lost when the city sealed itself, on the reasoning that no one would brave the natural caverns to reach a door that wouldn't open anyway. **What the Warrens know as "Dead Man's Passage" was originally Lathion's service/freight route.** The city's *emptiness* is only twenty years old. *Fresh tomb, not ancient one.* Its dead were Elara's contemporaries.
- **The Destiny Engine was built 20 years ago.** Not an ancient artifact. The Lathionese built it from scratch in a few desperate months during the Rending crisis and fed themselves into it (consensual sacrifice) to shut the Gray Gate. Because it was built in haste, the Engine should look **visibly different** from the rest of Lathion — hastier, less elegant. It is the one thing that does not match.
- **The sacrifice was consensual and prepared.** Months of preparation. The Lathionese set their affairs in order — *including organizing the Repository as a bequest to whoever came later* — and closed the city deliberately. NOT Mary-Celeste, NOT Pompeii. The effect is "deliberate departure," not "interrupted life."
- **Warrens-side canon.** The Warrens are Lathion's own ancient service tunnels (ventilation, water, maintenance) — known on the pre-Rending surface as the **"Arol Rhutan"** ("the old tunnels"). They stood essentially empty before the Rending; refugees were forced down into them after. Population is **~60,000** (if you see "200,000," that was an error). No one in-world knows the tunnels are Lathionese, or that Lathion lies beyond the Brass Door at their depths.
- **Long Knives:** old assassins' guild controlling Dead Man's Passage and guarding the Brass Door (without knowing what's behind it). Leader: **the Mistress.** Book 3's expedition negotiates passage with them.

**Throughline implication for the Lathion arc:** The expedition is, without anyone realizing it, descending from Lathion's attic (the Warrens, originally Lathion's service tunnels) into Lathion itself, via the service entrance. The Brass Door is not the city's front gate; it is the freight door. (The original front gate — the Great Shaft — was destroyed when the city sealed itself.) None of this is known to the expedition or anyone on the surface.

**Verification pass done on 5,000-year language.** In our drafts: Ch5 (locked) keeps its "five thousand years" phrasing as characters' in-character imprecision (they cannot know better; the Door *is* 5,000 years old, but the seal isn't). Ch6 was edited to soften "five thousand years sealed" → "millennia" / "long before any account he has read" in Xion/Voss interiority, because the recency chapter is *about* their assumptions being wrong and we should not commit them to a *specifically wrong* number. Ch7 needed no changes. Ch8's vision was edited to use the canonical *2,000 years* of sealed observation (the vision is Engine-truth, so Elara witnesses the actual numbers).

**Canon doc verified:** `Magic/The Rending.md` lines 177 and 179 are already fully updated to the new lore (built 5K, open 3M, sealed 2K, Great Shaft destroyed, Brass Door as long-disused back door).

**Additional lore from the Rending doc — Gunastran-magitech-as-degraded-Lathionese:** Line 181 establishes that Gunastran's industrial magitech is the degraded inheritance of Lathionese arts transmitted during the three open millennia. Treat this as **backstory only** — authorial truth that makes the world internally coherent, not material that needs to be deployed on-page. Gunastran does not know it and could not realistically learn it during Book 3 (espionage can steal contracts and prototypes; it cannot reconstruct three millennia of forgotten lineage). Ch11 works on its own surface terms — Gunastran fears obsolescence — and does not require this fact to land. Leave it in the canon doc; do not seed it in the chapters.

What Gunastran *does* realistically know in Book 3: Lathion is real (leaked dispatches make full deniability impossible); Balisha is implementing Lathion-derived systems (observable as they scale); Uratha has exclusive scholarly access (treaty terms); Balisha's outputs operate at a register Gunastran's don't reach. ## Critical worldbuilding rules — do not violate

**Lathionese names:** Characters don't know them. The narrator (in Xion's POV) doesn't know them either. When a location needs a name, use the expedition's coined working name, or describe it. Never use "Arrival Halls," "The Crown" as a Lathionese name, "the Core" (as a proper noun), "the Gleaming Palace," "the Archives," "the Garden Terraces." These are author knowledge only.

**Elara's connection to Lathion:** Metaphysical, not scholarly. During The Rending, the Destiny Engine activated, the Ruby Palace sat above Lathion, and soul-stuff seeped into infant Elara's neural pathways. She experiences it as "remembering something she never learned." She cannot explain it. Urathan scholars have theories but don't know the truth. Do NOT write her as having studied this civilization.

**The conveyor:** Service infrastructure for maintenance workers, not a commuter system. The overwhelming majority of Lathionese never went to the surface tunnels. The conveyor is utilitarian — built to move workers and equipment, not to impress. Only the threshold space at the bottom transitions to civic architecture.

**Epistemic honesty:** Characters in Chapter 5 do NOT call the city "Lathion" with certainty. Even in Chapters 6-8, "Lathion" is a working hypothesis (a very strong one), not a confirmed identification. The scholars would behave like real scientists finding Atlantis — some would be dubious even with the evidence in front of them.

**Uratha's motivations:** In Chapters 6-8, the Urathan scholars are genuinely excited, overwhelmed scholars doing what scholars do. They are not yet acting politically. The political move happens in Uratha (off-screen) when the dispatches arrive and leadership realizes the magnitude of the discovery. When Xion and Elara emerge in Chapter 9, the world has changed without them.
