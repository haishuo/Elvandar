# Chapter 6: Fair Price

**Timeline:** Wk 10–12 — the road home from Uratha, and the three weeks that follow
**Characters:** Xion (POV), Elara, Farleen, Kael, Ronas Dermir, Commander Vesk, Sa Ko Ren; council and court
**Emotional work:** The bill arrives. Xion wins every part of this — the treaty holds, the objection fails, the road opens — and cannot make one part of it clean. The reader should finish the chapter unable to name anything he did wrong and unwilling to call it a good day.
**POV:** Xion

---

## Beats

1. **The road back, and what he is doing on it instead.** Going out, five days of it went into drainage channels and matched bridge-marks and way-stations at intervals somebody sat down with a map to choose. Coming back he does none of that. He runs the ninety minutes in the signing room again, from the anchor to the floor to the pen, and it tracks, and it keeps tracking, and it keeps not resolving. He is aware that a man who has been handed everything he asked for and cannot stop working at the two seconds that were free is not a man having a success.

2. **Elara has put it down.** Not because she saw less — she was signing, she was watching Draskin's hands, she named the pause before Xion could. She has simply filed it and moved to the next thing, which is what she is for. He notices the difference between them and does not raise it. *(The pen opens the chapter and stays open. It is not resolved here, in ch7, or before ch11.)*

3. **Kaha'an has not moved while they were away.** The treasury is where it was. Whatever small thing shows it — a deferred works order, a wage question, a bill the palace is carrying — it is nine to fourteen months from ch1 and now eleven to sixteen weeks of that are gone. The trip cost time as well as a claim.

4. **The council receives the treaty, and it is not received as a victory.** The instrument is read into the record: Balisha renounces the demand for the Scholar's Crown, formally and irrevocably; Uratha sends scholars. Three centuries of Balishan rulers made that demand and Elara is the first to stop.

5. **Sa Ko Ren asks the question, and she is not an enemy.** She backed Elara before anybody, she has no faction interest in embarrassing the crown, and what she wants is not a concession — it is a demonstration. The crown has traded a thing everyone can name for a thing nobody has seen. She is not asking whether it was worth it. She is asking to be shown, which is the only currency she has ever accepted from anyone, and the Door is still shut.

6. **Nobody can answer her, including Xion, and he does not pretend otherwise.** He gives the arithmetic instead: the Crown has been in Uratha for three hundred years, every demand for it failed, and a claim that has never once been enforced has no price because nothing was ever going to be paid for it. It is the same argument as ch4 — *you cannot surrender what you do not possess* — and in this room it is thinner than it was in that one, because in this room somebody is entitled to say *then it cost us nothing and it bought us nothing, and we are still broke.* The objection is not withdrawn. **This is where the chapter differs from ch1**: there the objection was not renewed, and here it stands, and Elara does not put it down for him.

7. **What Elara does instead.** She does not defend him and she does not hold up a record. She takes the decision onto herself — her signature, her instrument, her call — and lets the room keep its doubt, because the room is entitled to it. Nothing about this is a rescue and Xion does not want one.

8. **Then the practical session, and the wall is domestic.** Vesk lays out what a standing camp at the Door actually is: months, not days. Relief crews. Timber and iron and lamp oil in quantity — Sa Ko's cartel supplies most of it. She is doubting the expedition and outfitting it in the same week, and nobody in the room treats that as a contradiction, including her. And, arriving on a road from Uratha, glass-fronted manuscript cases and reference stands that unfold on articulated brass joints, which have to go down five hundred meters and come back up undamaged.

9. **Kael's question from ch1 pays.** He asked, in the middle of everything else, how deep the corridor ran and whether it would take a cart, and Xion could not answer either and thought at the time they were strange things to want to know. Kael has been working the freight problem since before there was an expedition, and he has the answer: not on a ladder, not on a spar, and not eighteen porters carrying on their backs for four months.

10. **Vesk puts the eleven days on the table himself.** Ch2 bought eleven days of four shafts and a stretch of the third tier from a lieutenant who invented the rate while Vesk stood there. That was a working party of nineteen and it expired months ago. Vesk, who bought it, is the one who says it will not scale: a lieutenant sells *time*, he can sell the same shafts again tomorrow, and if he is replaced the crown owns nothing it can point to. *It is not a country; it is a market with a king in it* — and the crown now needs the king.

11. **The exposure, and it runs both ways.** Going down it is the Empress, who has to be there because the Door answers to her and will not answer anyone else, and a foreign delegation Balisha became liable for the moment Elara's name went on the instrument — scholars dying under Kaha'an would void the thing he bought with a national claim. Coming up it is whatever is behind the Door, which by ch1's terms is the only thing that keeps Balisha solvent. He does not need a strategist for this. You do not put your warehouse at the bottom of a shaft you do not own, on one road, held by a man who sets his own tolls.

    *(He reaches this the way a grain merchant's son reaches it — routes, tolls, who holds the wharf — and nobody remarks on where he learned it, least of all him. Rosik is not named in this beat.)*

12. **Farleen argues for the Long Knives, and she has done the work.** Mapped, costed, serious: the Knives are already engaged, they kept every word of the ch2 arrangement, and no faction in the Warrens will cross a blade-mark for money. She is not wrong about any of that.

13. **She runs it to the end and it does not close, and she is the one who says so.** The route to the Door runs through Dead Man's Passage and **only the Long Knives know it** — which is precisely why it cannot become a supply line. Their two ch2 terms are not obstinacy, they are the guild's entire position: guides front and back on every trip and every load, and nobody writes the route down. At eleven days that is an escort. At four months it is her people walking every cart, forever, with the crown still holding no map of its own road — and there are men in the Passage who had maps. **Deterrence protects a body; it does not protect a supply line.** The Mistress can keep them alive on her ground and has already given them the room at the end of it. She cannot sell them the way down, because the way down is not hers.

14. **Two accounts, and only one of them is settled.** The Knives own the destination and the route to it, and that account is settled — the Mistress took her price in ch2 and took it in a story. Ronas owns the shafts from the surface and the Midlevels underneath them, and that account has never been opened, because he has not answered a summons since before ch1.

15. **Xion goes down, and Elara does not, and she hates it.** The argument is short and he wins it on a single point: refuse the First Counselor and it is a bad afternoon; refuse the Empress to her face, in his hall, in front of his people, and there is nothing left to do about it that does not involve soldiers. She was raised by the Arol Batae and every instinct she has says go yourself. She sends him. **And before he goes she settles what he is allowed to spend, and the chair is in it** — hers to grant, hers to put in his hand, and her instruction is *try the money first.* *(She does not concede gracefully and the scene does not tidy it.)*

16. **Farleen goes with him, and neither of them says why.** She does not argue this one. Book 2 ended with her arms around him on a low wall, and the man whose lieutenant put him there is at the bottom of this shaft, and that is the entire reason and it stays unspoken. She is also one of three living people who walked into the Iron Hall and walked out again.

17. **The descent, and he is not a visitor here.** Eight years of Master Fen went up and down these levels. In Book 1 he was taken through the Warrens; this time he is walking a route he knows, through the Surface Warrens above ground in the ruin field, the Tiers, the Midlevels, to the Iron Hall at a hundred and eighty-five meters. People know his face at the wrong depth for a man of his office, and greet him by the wrong name, and he answers to it. What has changed is that he is arriving as somebody.

18. **Ronas names what Xion is there for inside the first minute.** He does not do preamble and he does not do cushioning, and he is a Truth Seeker about people and about nothing else in the world. Xion said in ch1, in front of witnesses, that the chair would stay empty until somebody came down and asked — and he has come down, at last, to ask for a right of way. Ronas gets there without being told. He registers Farleen in one flat line, as Tania's, the way he did in B1 ch18, and moves on. **Neither man says Melina's name.**

19. **Money does not work, and the failure is structural rather than a bargaining position.** He won his arena twenty years ago and stopped; there is no accumulation drive to sell to and no amount that resolves into a yes. Xion tries the money first because Elara told him to, and it does not land, and Ronas tells him to come back with something he has not already got.

20. **The chair was never the offer, because the chair already exists — and Xion works out in that room why it has stayed empty.** Ch1 seats four and ch3 confirms two of them standing empty: Grain, with nobody left to send, and the Warrens, whose holder has taken four summonses and answered none. Xion has read that for six months as contempt. It is not. This is a man who forced his way onto the Coterie by shutting the labor off for two months and outwaiting three cartels who were sure he would blink, and who has never once in his life been *given* a seat — a chair the palace offers is a chair the palace can take back, and a man who comes when the palace calls is a man who can be called, and every lieutenant on those platforms will run that arithmetic before the summons is cold. **He cannot take it as a gift. He has never been able to.** Nobody in the palace has asked why in six months, including the man who stood up and said somebody ought to come down and ask.

21. **So Xion does not offer him the seat. He prices it.** *I'm not offering it to you — I'm putting it in the instrument*: same paper as the shafts, same clause, listed against what the crown pays for four months of the north four and the third tier, standing, minuted, not lapsing if he never attends. That structure is the only one under which Ronas can sit down, and Xion has understood that and built it that way deliberately.

22. **Ronas takes it as a toll, which by now it genuinely is.** Commercial language over a protective act, which is how every protective act of his life has been dressed and the only way his world permits him to hold one. Xion has read enough of his father to see the dressing for what it is, and enough sense to leave it alone. The passage is granted: months, guaranteed, in terms that survive the man who signs them.

23. **The win is filthy.** He got the Warrens their chair. He got it as a **price** — which is exactly what he said in ch1 it must never be — and what is now on the record, for anyone who reads the record, is that the way to be governed by Balisha is to hold something Balisha needs. He does not have a scene about this and nobody tells him.

24. **The camp goes in.** Vesk, the Arol Batae detail, the three-mage Tor'anar detachment granted in ch3, the Long Knives arriving on the Mistress's own bargain rather than on payment, Sa Ko's timber and iron coming down the shafts the crown now holds, Farleen on the manifest and in nobody's column. The chapter ends with it standing and lit and quiet, and the Urathans still somewhere on the road.

---

**The chapter's structural function, and it is a seam.** Ch5 ended at night in a passage in Uratha and ch7 opens on the Urathans walking onto the floor in front of the Brass Door, and the page previously did nothing at all between those two sentences. This chapter is that distance. It is built so **ch7 opens unchanged** — *"The Urathans arrived on a grey morning"* — onto a camp that is already standing, with Farleen already in it, which is what ch7's own prose assumes.

**On the ceiling, which is deliberate.** This is the lowest point in the book and that is the job. Grey, transactional, and morally uncomfortable, immediately before the Door turns. Ch5's closing unease is too thin to be the only runway into a chapter built for undiluted wonder; the contrast is the argument for the chapter existing. Do not brighten the ending to be kind. **Nothing here is foreshadowing and nothing here is dread** — the reader should be tired rather than afraid, because ch7 needs them arriving with nothing in their hands.

**Placement and weight notes, moved out of the beat bodies where the first version of this draft wrongly put them.** *(The author's body/instruction rule, and this draft broke it in six places before it was audited — worth recording, because it is the first forward draft written under the rule and the failures were all the same shape: a directive smuggled in behind a fact.)* **Beat 10 belongs early** — the *they already have a deal* objection has to be answered before the reader can raise it, not after. **Beat 16's Iron Hall credential gets one line and no more**; it is a reason she is not argued with, not a scene. **Beat 14 states the geography once and never again.** **Beat 21 does not soften and does not brighten** — no late line squares the account. And **beat 8's contradiction stays unremarked**: Sa Ko doubting the expedition and outfitting it in the same week is a fact about a person, and the moment anyone in the room notices it out loud it becomes a point being scored.

**On the chair, which already exists — and this is the correction that reshaped beats 19–21.** *(Author's catch, 2026-08-03. The first prose draft had Xion create the seat: "The council seats four. It seats three." Both halves were wrong.)* **Ch1 seats four and two of them are standing empty** — Grain, because Rosik's attention is east of the border and nobody is left to send, and the Warrens, whose holder *"had not declined… had received the summons, and had not answered it, and had not sent anyone to not answer it either."* Ch3 confirms it: *"The chair for Grain was still empty. So was the other one."* So offering Ronas a seat offers him nothing; he could have walked up any day for six months. **The live question is why he never did, and the answer is on his sheet rather than in the plot** — he forced his way onto the Coterie by withholding labor for two months, he has never been given a seat in his life, and a chair the palace grants is a chair the palace can revoke in front of every lieutenant who is watching. So Xion does not offer it. He **prices** it, into the same clause as the shafts, which is the only structure under which Ronas can sit down. **This makes the toll framing structural rather than merely Ronas's self-deception**, and it makes Xion's position worse rather than better: the only way to get the Warrens governed was to make it a purchase, and he saw that, and he did it.

**On Xion's authority, which he does not have on his own.** The office chairs the advisory body; ch1 is explicit that it *"carried no cartel… did not touch Iron or Water or Grain or the Warrens."* He cannot put a council seat into a commercial instrument by himself, and **he would not** — the one-voice principle is the thing that distinguishes him from his father, and a man who commits his sovereign without leave is doing precisely a Rosik move. So Elara authorises it before he goes. That preserves the discovery in the room (he tries the money, the money fails) while keeping the constitutional act where it belongs, and *try the money first* pays when the money does not work.

**On beat 5 and the recurrence problem.** An old house objecting and Elara demolishing it is ch1's scene and running it again would be repetition, which is this series' dominant structural failure. So the objection moves to **Sa Ko Ren**, and it changes position rather than volume. She backed Elara before anyone; she has no factional interest; and her whole paradigm is that a claim is worth nothing until it is demonstrated. *Prove it* is not an attack and cannot be answered by an argument, which is why Xion loses this exchange and why nobody's authority is on the line when he does. Check `People/Sa Ko Ren.md` before writing a line of her — Spirited Competitor primary, Truth Seeker second, blunt where the rest of the Coterie uses euphemism. **She is also supplying the camp**, and the chapter should let both be true without commenting on it. Her answer arrives in ch7 when the Door opens, and she is not in that chapter to receive it, which is correct.

**On beat 6, and what must not happen.** Xion does not win this. He does not find the sharper angle, there is no ch4-style recovery, and the chapter must not hand him a late line that retroactively squares it. He made a defensible trade under bad options and said so privately in ch4 — *"Honestly, Elara? I don't"* — and this is the room where that uncertainty gets a public bill. **Do not let Elara rescue him**, and do not let him rescue himself.

**On beats 12–14, and the reason they exist at all.** A reader who knows the Warrens will ask why the crown does not simply use the Long Knives, and the answer must be **on the page**, spoken by a character, not left to be reconstructed. Giving it to Farleen solves two problems: it is voiced by the one person with a motive to want it to be true, and her working it out anyway is her documented shape — Engineer in permanent service to Caretaker, Truth Seeker third, and she will not fake a number even to keep him out of that hall. *(Her sheet's shadow is exactly this: operational justification built on an emotional decision. Here the justification fails and she reports the failure. Do not have her conceal it and be caught.)*

**On the geography, which is a live hazard rather than a theoretical one.** Ch2 states that the lieutenant is Ronas's — *"He owns his shafts. Dermir owns him"* — and **the author still misremembered him as a Long Knife**, which means the map is not in the reader's head either. So this chapter lays it out once, cleanly, and never again: Slaver-held shafts and Midlevels from the surface down; the blade-marks at the third tier; Long Knife ground from there to Dead Man's Passage and the Door at the end of it, *"only the Long Knives know the route."* The Iron Hall sits at −185m in the Midlevels, on the way. Canon lives in `The Warrens_ Geography and Physical Layout.md` and `The Warrens_ Named Locations and Chambers.md`; the Surface Warrens are **above ground** and the underground upper level is **the Tiers**.

**On Ronas, and the one thing that will go wrong if it is not watched.** `People/Ronas Dermir.md` locks his voice and the lock exists because it has already drifted once: dropped *-ing* always, elision, *got* for *have*, *boy* and *kid* to men far above him, non-standard agreement. **The drift always happens in his longest and most serious speeches** — the temptation to let him speak properly when he is being precise is exactly backwards, and the whole point of him is that a man who talks like this out-thinks the room. His sharpest analytical speech in Book 1 is also his broadest. He is at his most dangerous when he is most casual.

**On Melina, and why neither of them touches her.** Ronas's lieutenant murdered Xion's sister and Ronas holds the responsibility for it without having ordered it, and both men know both facts walking in. Ronas will not raise it: it would blow up his own negotiation and he is not cruel for sport. Xion will not raise it: he came down to buy a road and he knows what happens to the road if he does. **The scene runs on the pressure of the thing neither of them says**, and if either says it the chapter has a different and worse ending. This is also not the place for the Book 2 confrontation to be re-litigated — that account closed when Ronas stood down, and neither man reopens it.

**On what this hands Book 4.** The Warrens get a seat at the new order's table, and they get it as the price of a right of way rather than on the merits, and **nobody ever tells Xion that this is what he did.** He is not to be handed the verdict by another character — that is the same failure mode as telling him he has become his father, and the same ruling applies. The reader sees it. He carries it without naming it. See `Series Review - Master List.md` §2, *The Warrens chair.*

**Sets up:** Ch7 directly — the standing camp, the assembled expedition, and a seam that requires no change to that chapter's opening. The Crown established as a real price paid, so that Uratha returning it in ch11 lands as the reversal it is. Sa Ko's demonstration, delivered in ch7 while she is elsewhere. And the Warrens seat, which is Book 4's inheritance.
