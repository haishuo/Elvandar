# Series Review — Open Work

Running record of the read-through review. Book 1 read in full (ch1–25). Book 2 read through ch10.

Everything here is either **outstanding** (not yet done) or a **guardrail** (a decision that a later revision could accidentally undo). Applied changes are listed at the bottom.

---

## The rule hierarchy (author's, binding)

When these conflict, the lower number wins. Everything else in this file is subordinate to them.

1. **Things must make sense.** If something becomes logically incoherent, the plot changes to fix it.
2. **Characters must behave like themselves.** No adjusting a personality to make a point. *The plot serves at the sufferance of characterization, never the reverse.*
3. **A scene designed for one emotion stays that way** — wonder, joy, triumph, despair. Resolution may happen around the edges. It must never dilute.
4. **Thematic resonance ranks last.** Making something more thematic is permitted only when it does not conflict with 1, 2, or 3.

**Reviewer's failure mode, recorded so it can be caught:** three separate suggestions this session reached for thematic shape and had to be paid for out of a higher rule — an invented watcher in ch1 that contradicted ch4's premise (rule 1), a downbeat added to ch25's closing roll call (rule 3), and a request that Rosik be given an interlocutor in Book 2 ch5 (rule 2 — Xion's silence there is in character, and Rosik facing no resistance *is* Rosik). The tell is the same each time: adding something because it *rhymes* rather than because the logic or the character requires it. "This would be more resonant" is a signal to check the three rules above it before proposing anything.

---

## Craft principles established during the review

These came out of specific problems and apply generally. They are the reason for most of the fixes below. All are subordinate to the hierarchy above.

**1. A recurrence must change someone's position, not their volume.**
The book's dominant structural habit is re-running an argument rather than escalating it. Ch2/Ch3, Ch6/Ch8, Ch15's four "why is everyone being polite" beats, Ch17's four "this feels wrong" beats, Farleen's five identical *stop-this* speeches across ch14–20. Ch22 is the correct model: Elara reaches for the same weapon she used in ch15, and this time he breaks.

**2. Some scenes take one emotion, and hedging them is a loss.**
Ch25 is triumph, full stop — the Jurassic Park reveal, not Malcolm's speech at lunch. Complication belongs in adjacent chapters, where the contrast *earns* the unmixed ending. Two shading beats have already been cut on this principle (the ch1 fruit-stall watcher, the ch25 Janice line); do not reintroduce that kind of qualifier.

**3. Do not invent a payoff for something the text already paid off better nearby.**
The ch1 watcher was cut because Janice's *"a man in the market square shouting about Master Fen's billing practices"* delivered the same consequence four paragraphs later, harder.

**4. State emotional content through action where the action already carries it.**
Roughly half the fixes so far were of this kind.

## Book 1 — outstanding work

### Tier A — changes the book

**A0. Ch18 ends with Xion holding a dangerously ill child and never treating him.** *(Promoted from scene-level on the hierarchy re-audit.)* This is a rule 2 item, not polish: a healer with a fevered boy in his arms treats him. The chapter closes on Xion's feeling instead of his hands, which is the character not behaving like himself at the exact moment his defining practice applies.

**A1. Farleen's arc has no ending.** *(hold until Book 2 is read)*
She argues in ch14, 16, 17, 19, 20 — same position, rising volume — then goes silent in ch21–25 (*"unreadable," "professionally neutral"*). She never chooses anything freely; she follows Xion five times under protest.

A possible hinge exists: **ch23's cover story**, where Xion lies to Silvanno to protect her, and she registers it (*"surprise, then gratitude, then something more complicated"*) — arguably the first time anyone has protected her rather than deployed her.

**This is a question, not a fix.** Whether Farleen would actually change position because of that is a rule-2 matter and belongs to the author, not to a structural slot that wants filling. The observation that stands on its own is only this: she holds one position at rising volume for seven chapters and then goes quiet, and a character with two more books to carry should probably not exit her first one unchanged.

This matters disproportionately: her character file has her as **Xion's closest companion and the operational core of his network through Books 2 and 3**, ending inside the imperial security apparatus. She has the longest runway of anyone and currently the flattest exit. *Deliberately deferred until her Book 2 chapters are read, so the Book 1 exit is built to match the Book 2 entrance.*

**A2. The ch16–17 passivity block.**
Xion waits outside three consecutive times (the heist, the scout, the attempt), and ch17 spends 3,069 words delivering an offstage failure. The *design* is right — the rescue fails and the ledger succeeds, which is the book proving its thesis structurally.

**Two parts, different standing.** The *length* is a straightforward craft fix: a deflation should be fast, or it reads as accident rather than intent. The *staging* change — putting Xion inside one of the three — is a **rule 2 question, not a fix**: Farleen is tactically correct that he is untrained and would be a liability, and the text says so. Making him present requires bending her judgment or his, and neither is the reviewer's to bend.

### Tier B — the repetition habit, concrete instances

**B1. Ch2 and Ch3 run the same argument.** Both are friend-skepticism scenes ending in *"with or without you."* Ch3's confrontation should be about the friends discovering what they committed to, not re-litigating whether he's sane.

**B2. Ch6 and Ch8 have the same shape** — walk, witness injustice, Xion explains, Elara vows to change everything. Ch8's fountains are the stronger sequence; ch6 has the better single idea (her name on the proclamations).

**B3. Ch3 is a montage wearing a chapter's clothes.** Four days of searching reported; only Jorik is dramatized.

**B4. Ch17's "something feels wrong"** runs four times with no new information.

### Tier C — scene level

- **C1.** Ch24's combat trial is almost entirely commentary (*"beautiful in a way Xion didn't have words for"*). The climactic test of the novel needs at least one concrete exchange before the analysis takes over.
- **C2.** Ch25 is mostly told, and the closing roll call reads like the outline leaking into the prose. *(Note: fix the flatness, not the triumph — see principle 2.)*
- **C3.** *(rule 2 question, not a fix.)* Ch5 — they agree to a life-destroying plan in ~40 lines with no resistance. But *"He didn't even really consider it"* (ch19) establishes this as how Xion decides. Fast acceptance may be characterization rather than a gap. Author's call.
- **C4.** Ch9's marriage-alliance cover is set up as emotionally loaded and never tested at the ball.
- **C5.** Ch21 raises a stake and dissolves it in the same chapter (*"You've spent time with his archrivals… that complicates your relationship with him"* — Ronas never reappears).
- **C6.** Ch23's Vesk: twenty years of fury evaporates in ten lines, and it is sequenced backwards — he concedes (*"You learned what we couldn't teach you"*) before he attacks (*"You left. You abandoned our protection"*). Reverse.
- **C7.** Ch22 — Elara reaches for the ch15 slaver argument verbatim. That is right (hurt people reuse the weapon that worked), but the text should register that she knows she's repeating herself.
- **C8.** Ch18 — Ronas's dialect wobbles between registers (*"the one what tore through my men"* / *"'till you hit surface"* against *"Interesting group"*). Pick one.


### Do not touch

- **Ch18** is the best chapter in the book. *"Did it anyway."*
- **Ch20's mirror throw** — *"Lot of good it does him."*
- **Ch22's confession** — works as written; only C7 applies.
- **Ch25's triumph** — see principle 2.

## Character guardrails

### Janice

- **She never says "Xion" or "Kemvimore."** Her knowledge is conveyed only by what she declines to ask (*"I've never asked where the money comes from"*). If she speaks the name she becomes an exposition device.
- **Mira gives him the practice; Janice gives him the cover.** Values from one, tradecraft from the other. If Janice starts supplying meaning as well as method she crowds out Mira, who carries three books.
- **Janice never absolves him.** She stays operational permanently. The moment she offers comfort about Mira she is doing Mira's work and both characters deflate.
- **Ch13's "My choice" is a gift, not a claim of authority and not noble sacrifice.** Xion's *"I can't let you do that"* must read as guilt (*whatever happens to you becomes mine to carry*), never as permission-granting. Her answer closes the five-year copper argument by conceding it: he has never let a patient walk out owing him anything, so he does not get to refuse the same from her. *"I'm giving you something. You don't get to write it down."*
- **The Rosik charge is deferred, not absent.** A reader may notice that deciding for other people is exclusively what Rosik does. Xion does not notice, and must not, in ch13. *"I have turned into my father"* is the worst realization available to him and belongs to Book 2's "Becoming His Father."
- **Unconfirmed:** her injury is failing eyesight rather than hands (hands are used three times already — Tam, Korvin, Xion's own). Explains her front-of-house role, unpicking by feel, and the existing ch1 line about squinting at the light. Awaiting a decision.

### Elara

- **The royal trait is rationed to six involuntary shifts** across ch1–11: ch1 (the sighting), ch4 (fear in the compound), ch5 (Tam), ch6 (the child at the well), ch8 (the third fountain), ch10 (the detonation). Ch9's training-scene instance is deliberately a *save* — she starts the shift and stops it — so the reader learns she can hold it, which is what makes ch10 land. Do not reintroduce casual shifts; use stillness, held breath, flat repetition, the too-careful voice.
- **Flat repetition is her signature tell** — *"A sporting chance"* in ch10, called back explicitly in ch11.

## Xion — trait watchlist for Books 2 and 3

The core trait, restated after Book 1:

> **Xion pays restitution to a dead woman, using living patients as the instrument.** It is not expenditure and not problem-solving — it is payment into a ledger designed never to close.

What to test as the series continues:

1. **Does the ledger ever change shape, and does he ever try to close it?** Book 3 taking "Fen" as a legal surname is a candidate for either answer.
2. **The recipient is irrelevant.** Ch22 reveals *why* he can't triage, and it is not a healer's ethic: *"My father decided Mira didn't deserve to live."* He refuses to judge because **judging is Rosik's act**. His entire moral practice exists to prove he is not his father — which means "you have become him" would not merely wound him, it would invalidate the practice.
3. **He asks permission.** *"Can I have a look?"* — inferred to be because Mira was never asked. It breaks for the first time in ch19: he carries a delirious Tam into Long Knife territory, and the one person present who cannot consent is the one taken into lethal danger. *"He didn't even really consider it."* **The moment he stops asking is the moment the wound is driving him instead of the practice.**
4. **Overcorrection is the predicted failure mode.** A man whose wound is *I did nothing* will eventually do **anything** rather than nothing. Visible already in ch19; Book 2's "Becoming His Father" is the expected charge point.
5. **He cannot articulate the compulsion, only the principle.** Ch1: *"There never was, at this part."* Ch15: he answers a question Elara didn't ask. Ch18: *"Because it needs treating."* Ch22 is the one place he gets to say it — that fluency is earned and should not recur casually.
6. **Everyone settles with him; he settles with no one.** Korvin, the food vendor, and Rykan's cousin all close their accounts explicitly (*"after this, we're even"*). His own never closes.

## Open questions

1. **Janice's failing eyesight** — confirm or veto.
2. **Does Janice reappear alive?** Ch23 establishes she was taken and that no record of her exists anywhere. Book 3 ch1 (Xion takes "Fen" as a surname) is the scene that most wants her alive.
3. **Who tells Xion he has become his father, and when?** Elara would be cruel in the right way; Farleen would be true; nobody — him arriving at it alone — may be worst of all.
4. **Who occupies the Janice chair in Book 2?** The ally with better information who tells Xion to stop and is right. Janice held it in ch1 and ch13, Farleen in ch14–19, Elara in ch15 and ch22. If nobody holds it in Book 2, the central trait goes unchallenged, which is functionally the same as losing it.
