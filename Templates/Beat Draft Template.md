## How to write beat drafts

**Format:** Present tense throughout. Each beat is a paragraph (or a few sentences) describing what happens in that segment of the chapter and why it matters.

**Analytical, not prose:** A beat draft is a blueprint, not a draft of the actual text. The goal is to think through the chapter's structure and meaning so that the prose draft can do the work of feeling. Write clearly about what's happening and what it communicates — but don't write the scene itself.

**Dialogue:** Describe conversations rather than scripting them. "Xion and Melina discuss the state of the Coterie — she's exhausted, he's frustrated, neither has answers" is the right register. Specific lines can be noted if a particular phrase must land, but the beat draft is not the place to write dialogue.

**Texture and specificity:** Some beats will name a specific detail — a location, a gesture, a character choice — when that detail does real narrative work. Include it when it matters; don't pad when it doesn't.

**Negative choices:** It's often worth noting what a character deliberately does NOT do in a scene, and why. These absences are frequently as revealing as actions.

**Beat count:** There's no required number. Chapters in this series typically run 12–20 beats. The right number is however many it takes to map the chapter without padding or skipping.

**Footer notes:** After the beats, add bold-header notes for anything worth flagging explicitly — structural function, character attachment work, what must land for a later chapter to pay off. Not every chapter needs them; use as many as the chapter actually warrants.
