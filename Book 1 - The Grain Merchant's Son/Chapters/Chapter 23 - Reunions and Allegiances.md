Morning light filtered through gaps in the warehouse roof, painting stripes across the dusty floor. Xion lay on his makeshift bedroll, staring at the ceiling, processing everything that had happened. Tam was home. Safe. Recovering with his family. That should have felt like victory.

Instead, it felt like the calm before something inevitable.

Across the room, Elara sat by the window keeping watch, the Spirit's Reflection propped against the wall beside her. She hadn't slept much either—Xion could tell from the way she held herself, that warrior's stillness that came from exhaustion rather than rest.

In her corner, Farleen pretended to sleep. Xion knew she was awake. He could read the tension in her shoulders, the careful control of her breathing. After last night's confrontation, after everything that had been said and not said, the space between them all felt heavy with unfinished business.

Footsteps outside. Urgent voices.

All three were instantly alert. Elara's hand went to her knife. Farleen sat up, abandoning the pretense. Xion pushed himself upright, his healing shoulder protesting.

The warehouse door crashed open.

Silvanno and Kael burst through. Silvanno's eyes locked on Xion and he froze—just for half a second, shock overriding everything else. His face cycled through emotions too fast to name: disbelief, fury, relief, all crashing together.

Then his fist flew.

The punch caught Xion across the jaw before he could even process what was happening.

The impact sent Xion staggering back, hand going to his face, more shocked than hurt. His ears rang. The warehouse spun slightly.

"You promised to meet me at midnight!" Silvanno's voice cracked. "I've been searching for days thinking you were dead!"

"THAT was for making me worry!" He was shaking now, the anger breaking apart into something rawer.

Then, before Xion could recover, Silvanno pulled him into a crushing embrace.

"Thought you were dead, you stupid bastard."

Xion's arms came up slowly, returning the hug despite his stinging jaw. The embrace lasted longer than either would normally allow—Silvanno holding on like Xion might vanish again if he let go.

Kael stood back, characteristically reserved, but Xion could see the tension draining from his shoulders. His friend's eyes were bright in a way that suggested he'd been as worried as Silvanno, just better at hiding it.

Finally, Silvanno released him, stepping back but keeping one hand on Xion's shoulder like an anchor. "Days, Xion. We've been searching for days. Ever since you vanished from my mother's party with half the city guard chasing you."

"I can explain—"

"You'd better." But Silvanno's anger was already fading, replaced by profound relief. "Farleen told me you were attacked. Then you show up at the masquerade without warning, and I think 'thank the gods, he's alive,' and then you disappear AGAIN during the biggest disaster my mother's ever hosted. Do you have any idea—"

His voice caught. He stopped himself, jaw working.

"I'm sorry," Xion said quietly. "I didn't mean—everything happened so fast—"

"Just tell me what's going on." Silvanno's hand tightened on his shoulder. "Please. Before I lose my mind."

"We started at the clinic," Kael said.

It was the first thing he'd said since coming through the door. Xion turned.

"It burned." Kael's voice was flat and careful, which was how Xion understood what was coming. "Four nights ago. The weaver's shop and two buildings either side of it, before the water crews got there."

The room tilted, very slightly.

"Janice," Xion said.

"Grain cartel men brought her out the front before it caught properly. That's what the neighbours say. Walking. Hands bound." Kael didn't look away from him. "We've asked everywhere since. The Accounting Office has no record. The debtors' halls have no record. Silvanno's mother made inquiries and was told there is no such person." A pause. "She isn't anywhere, Xion."

Xion looked down at his hands.

He had not thought about her since the smoke. Four days — the Tiers, the Crossroads, the Iron Hall, the whole black length of Dead Man's Passage with a dying boy on his shoulder — and the woman who had given him his name had not once entered his head.

"She burned the patient records," he said. "Before they got in. So they couldn't use the names."

"She what?" Silvanno said.

But Xion had already turned away.

Xion tried to find the words. How did you explain infiltrating the Arol Batae compound, Farleen's betrayal, finding the actual princess, descending into the Warrens, rescuing Tam, the divine trial, the Long Knives, all of it?

The words came out jumbled. Disjointed. He kept having to backtrack, to fill in context Silvanno and Kael didn't have.

Elara stood, stepping forward to help.

Both friends went silent. Silvanno's hand dropped from Xion's shoulder.

Kael's eyes widened—the most emotion Xion had ever seen from him. He studied her face with careful attention, taking in the dark hair, the delicate features, the eyes that were currently a steady blue.

Then those eyes shifted. Blue to amber. Just for a moment before Elara caught herself.

Both friends recoiled slightly.

"The eyes," Kael said quietly. "They really do change color."

He exhaled slowly, and something shifted in his expression—months of doubt crystallizing into certainty. "We spent months chasing rumors. Turns out the real thing was walking around with Xion the whole time."

Silvanno just stared, his earlier anger temporarily forgotten as he processed what he was seeing. What it meant.

Xion watched his friends absorb what he'd known for weeks now. The impossible truth. She was real. Not another pretender with colored lenses or trick lighting. The actual Princess Elara, last of House Valanar, heir to the Ruby Throne.

After the initial shock faded, Xion explained everything. The full journey—his desperate search, the infiltration of the compound—

"Wait." Silvanno's eyes shifted to Farleen, who'd been silent in her corner since they'd arrived. "Farleen, you told me Xion was attacked. How did you know? Were you there?"

Farleen froze. Her eyes went to Xion's, and he saw the panic there. The plea. If she explained how she knew, everything would come out. The spying. The orders. The dagger.

The moment hung in the warehouse like a held breath.

Xion made his choice.

"We were both near the compound area," he said, his voice steady despite the lie. "Wrong place, wrong time. Someone attacked me in an alley. Farleen saw it happen. Elara intervened before..." He gestured vaguely at his shoulder. "It was chaotic."

Technically true. Every word of it technically true, while omitting the one detail that mattered most.

Farleen's expression shifted—surprise, then gratitude, then something more complicated that Xion couldn't quite read. She held his gaze for a heartbeat longer than necessary before looking away.

"Are you both okay?" Silvanno asked, concern replacing suspicion.

"We survived," Xion said simply.

Silvanno seemed to accept this, though Kael's eyes lingered on Farleen for a moment—thoughtful, assessing—before he turned his attention back to Xion.

"Go on," Kael said quietly. "What happened after?"

Xion continued the explanation. Finding Elara. Their descent into the Warrens to rescue Tam. The divine trial at the brass door. The Spirit's Reflection proving the gods' sanction. The Long Knives and their mysterious Mistress. All of it.

Silvanno listened, his expression cycling between disbelief and gradual acceptance. Kael asked occasional questions—practical ones about logistics and timing that helped anchor the impossible story in concrete details.

When Xion finally finished, silence settled over the warehouse.

"So," Silvanno said eventually, his voice carefully controlled. "You found her. You actually found the princess."

"Yes."

"And nearly got yourself killed about six different ways doing it."

"Yes."

Silvanno looked at Elara, really looked at her, seeing past the simple dress and travel-worn appearance to what she represented. Then he looked back at Xion, and something complicated moved behind his eyes.

"You were right," he said quietly. "Back at The Amber Leaf, when we all thought you were losing your mind. You were right."

The admission felt weighted with more than simple acknowledgment.

Kael spoke up, his voice thoughtful. "What now?"

The question hung in the dusty air.

Elara's answer was immediate. "I need to return to the Arol Batae compound."

Xion's stomach dropped. He remembered his desperate infiltration, the guards, the close call. But she was right. She couldn't claim the throne without the royal guard's support.

"They're going to be angry," he said.

"They'll get over it." Elara's voice carried that imperial certainty Xion was learning to recognize. "They've spent twenty years preparing me to be empress. Well, I'm ready now. The question is whether they're ready to actually let me try."

---

They left the warehouse at first light, moving through back streets to avoid patrols, until the high walls of the Arol Batae compound rose ahead like a memory Xion would rather forget.

The journey felt surreal. Walking through streets Xion knew, heading toward the fortress he'd once infiltrated in disguise, but this time approaching openly. No grain inspector uniform. No false papers. Just four people—three fugitives and one minor noble—about to walk through the front gate.

Silvanno kept glancing at Elara, as if verifying she was real. Kael walked with quiet focus, already thinking ahead to what came next. Farleen stayed close to Xion but slightly apart, the distance between them still unresolved.

The compound gates stood open. Guards in deep blue imperial colors watched their approach with professional attention.

Xion's heart hammered as they crossed the threshold. Last time he'd been here, he'd been running for his life. Now—

Commander Vesk was waiting in the courtyard.

He was not pacing. Xion had expected pacing and got instead a man standing perfectly still in the middle of a swept expanse of stone, hands behind his back, in the deep blue — as though he had taken up that position on the morning she left and had merely been interrupted, occasionally, by the requirements of running a fortress.

He waited until they were close enough that he did not have to raise his voice. That was the first frightening thing.

"Fourteen days," he said.

Elara said nothing.

"We searched the Merchant Quarter twice. We searched the docks. I sent men into the Warrens who did not want to go and I sent them anyway, and two came back hurt, and one came back and could not tell me where he had been." His voice did not rise. It was worse for not rising. "On the fifth day I had a boat emptied to the boards at the river gate because a man told me a woman of your description had taken passage north. We opened forty crates of dried fish in front of a furious captain. I apologised to him personally. I would do it again tomorrow."

Xion, standing a pace behind, understood that he was hearing something prepared. Somewhere in those fourteen days Vesk had begun composing what he would say if he ever got the chance, and here it was, and he was going to deliver the whole of it.

"On the ninth day an officer of mine used the word *body*." Something moved then, under the flatness. "In the search plan. He said it because after nine days it is the correct word, it is what a competent man says, and I put him against a wall for saying it. He was right. I was wrong. I have not apologised to him yet."

He let that sit exactly as long as it needed to.

"We are the men who were standing in the room," he said. "On the night. That is what the Arol Batae are — not the guard of the imperial house. What is left of the guard that was in the room and did not stop it."

He did not say anything further about the room. Xion had the sense that nobody in this compound ever did.

"And you went out a side gate," Vesk said, "and let us live it a second time."

Then his eyes moved, for the first time, past her — to Xion — and Xion felt the full professional weight of that attention land on him like a hand.

"On a stranger's promise to show you the city."

"Not a stranger." Elara's voice was calm and did not hurry. "The man who found me when every other soul in Kaha'an had agreed I was dead. The man who showed me what you couldn't."

"What we couldn't." Vesk's jaw set. "We kept you alive. We kept you **breathing**, for twenty years, in a city that would have paid a great deal for you not to be. We prepared you—"

"For a throne I didn't understand." She cut him off, but gently. "You taught me strategy and combat and court politics and everything an empress is supposed to know. You could not teach me what it is to rule Kaha'an. Not from inside these walls. Nobody could have. That isn't a failure of yours."

"And now you know."

"Now I know enough to begin."

"That is not an answer."

"No," Elara agreed. "It's an account. You taught me to give one."

And then she did — and it was not what Xion had braced for, and from the way the courtyard went quiet it was not what anyone else had either.

"I didn't think about you," she said. "That's the part I'd take back. Not the leaving; I'd leave again, and you would hate it, and I'd do it anyway, because I was no use to anyone in here. But in fourteen days I did not once sit down and think about a hundred and forty-seven men searching a city for me in the dark. I thought about what I'd find and what I'd do with it and what sort of empress it would make me." She held his eyes. "You're mine. I didn't understand that when I went out that gate. I do now. Next time you'll be told."

Vesk's jaw worked.

Whatever he had prepared for across fourteen days, it had not been that. Xion watched it happen the way he watched everything — the breath going out through the nose, the shoulders coming down half an inch, the particular moment a body stops bracing. He had seen it a thousand times in the doorways of sickrooms, in people who had been holding a thing up alone for longer than they could manage and had just been given permission to set it down.

"Then we finish what we started twenty years ago," Vesk said.

It came out rough. It carried two decades in it — the waiting, the preparation, a life's purpose arriving at last within reach of a man who had begun to believe he would not live to see it.

Elara straightened. "I'm ready."

"We'll see." But something had gone out of him that was not going to come back today. He turned toward the main building. "Come inside. We have work to do."

---

The war room was unlike anything Xion had seen in the compound during his brief, desperate infiltration. Maps covered the walls, tactical diagrams marking districts and key positions throughout Kaha'an. Commander Vesk stood at the head of a scarred wooden table, laying out the situation with military precision.

"We have legitimacy," Vesk said, gesturing to Elara. "Valanar bloodline, confirmed by the royal trait. We have divine sanction." He nodded toward the Spirit's Reflection, which Elara had set on the table. "The gods themselves have acknowledged your worthiness through their trial."

He paused, letting that sink in.

"And we have force. The Arol Batae stand ready. One hundred and forty-seven warriors, trained since The Rending for this exact purpose. We can secure the Imperial Palace. We can hold it against any immediate response."

The certainty in his voice was absolute.

"But," Vesk continued, his tone shifting, "legitimacy and force aren't enough if every power structure in Kaha'an stands against us."

Xion felt the weight of that truth settle over the room. He thought of his father, of Rosik Kemvimore and the other cartel leaders who'd spent twenty years maintaining careful balance. They wouldn't simply accept this disruption.

"We need one of the great cartels to acknowledge your claim," Vesk said, looking directly at Elara. "Not to fight for us. Not to provide troops or resources. Just to acknowledge that you have the right to sit on the Ruby Throne."

"Why?" Elara asked.

"Because legitimacy without political foundation is just another rebellion waiting to be crushed." Vesk's voice was hard with experience. "The cartels have ruled in your family's name for two decades. If you want to reclaim that authority, you need at least one of them to admit your claim is valid. With one cartel standing behind you, the others can't credibly deny your right. Without that..." He shook his head. "We're just soldiers forcing an outcome. That's not an empress. That's a coup."

Silence followed this assessment.

Xion understood the logic. His father claimed to rule in service to the imperial line. So did Tania Larannas, and Sa Ko Ren, and Ronas Dermir. If even one of them acknowledged Elara's legitimacy, it would shatter the fiction they'd all maintained. Force the others to respond on her terms rather than dismissing her as another pretender.

But which cartel would break ranks? His father certainly wouldn't—Grain cartel had too much invested in the current system. Water cartel under Tania Larannas was equally entrenched. And Ronas... Xion thought about the Slaver King in his Iron Hall, the man who'd let them leave with Tam because Master Fen's reputation mattered to his own people. Ronas operated outside normal politics entirely, maintained power through fear and force rather than legitimacy. His acknowledgment would mean nothing to the other cartels.

That left—

"Sa Ko Ren might."

Everyone turned. Kael had been silent through most of the meeting, but now he spoke with quiet certainty.

"The Iron Lady," he continued, seeing their attention. "Leader of Iron cartel. She's Terinok. A warrior. And she respects strength over politics."

Xion had seen Sa Ko Ren at cartel meetings—his father's attempts to groom him for leadership had meant enduring countless formal gatherings where the Coterie maintained their careful alliance. She'd always stood out, the Terinok woman who spoke bluntly when others traded in euphemisms. Who supposedly evaluated everything through direct contest rather than political maneuvering.

But those had been formal occasions, carefully managed affairs. He'd never seen her in her own territory, on her own terms.

"She won't be swayed by bloodline alone," Kael said. "Or divine favor. She'll want proof that you can actually fight for what you're claiming."

"What kind of proof?" Elara asked.

Kael's expression was unreadable. "The kind that matters to Terinok warriors. Strength. Will. The refusal to surrender even when outmatched."

Vesk was nodding slowly, already seeing where this was going. "If Sa Ko Ren acknowledges Elara's claim, that's the final piece. Iron cartel under her leadership makes the ascension credible. The other cartels would have to respond on different terms entirely."

"Then that's our path," Elara said. "We secure Iron, and the requirements are met."

She made it sound simple. Xion knew it wouldn't be.

"I can arrange a meeting," Kael offered. "My family has connections to minor Iron houses. Not direct access to Sa Ko herself, but I can get word to the right people. Make sure the request reaches her."

"Do it," Vesk said. Then, to Elara: "You understand what this means? If Sa Ko Ren refuses, if she dismisses your claim—"

"Then we find another way." Elara's voice was steady. "But we try this first. Because if it works, it's the cleanest path to the throne. And if it doesn't..." She met Vesk's eyes. "Then we adapt. Like you trained me to."

Vesk studied her for a long moment. Then something that might have been pride flickered across his face.

"You've changed," he said quietly.

"I had good teachers." She paused. "All of them."

Her eyes moved to Xion for just a heartbeat before returning to Vesk.

The commander nodded once. "Then we move forward. Kael arranges the meeting. The rest of us prepare for whatever comes after." He looked around the room, his gaze settling on each of them in turn. "This is it. Everything we've worked for comes down to the next few days."

As the meeting broke up and people began discussing logistics, Xion found himself standing beside Silvanno.

"You really think this will work?" his friend asked quietly.

Xion thought about everything he'd witnessed. Elara fighting in the Warrens. Refusing to surrender during the divine trial. Leading them through Dead Man's Passage without even knowing where she was going, driven by something deeper than conscious thought.

"I think," he said slowly, "that Sa Ko Ren is going to test her. And I think Elara is very good at proving herself when tested."

Silvanno was quiet for a moment. Then: "You love her."

It wasn't a question.

Xion opened his mouth to deny it, to deflect, to say something about duty and partnership and the work they were doing together. But this was Silvanno. His best friend. The person who'd punched him and then held him like he'd never let go.

"I don't know," he said honestly. "Maybe. It's complicated."

"Because of Farleen?"

"Because of everything." Xion watched Elara across the room, deep in conversation with Vesk about something tactical. "She's going to be empress. I'm the son of her enemy. And Farleen—" He stopped. "It's all complicated."

"When has your life ever been simple?" Silvanno's voice held a trace of his usual humor. Then, more seriously: "Whatever happens next, I'm with you. You know that, right? Even after you disappeared twice and gave me a heart attack both times. I'm with you."

The words made something tight in Xion's chest loosen slightly. "Thank you."

"Don't thank me yet. Wait until after you survive whatever insane thing comes next." Silvanno clapped him on the shoulder—carefully, mindful of injuries. "But yeah. We're good. Just... next time you plan to vanish into the Warrens for a week, maybe leave a note?"

Despite everything, Xion smiled. "I'll try."

Across the room, Kael was already making arrangements, speaking quietly with one of Vesk's aides about sending messages to Iron territory. Farleen stood near the door, watching everything with that careful assessment Xion recognized from her spy training.

Elara was in the middle of it, Vesk at her shoulder, two of his officers standing by to be told things.

Xion found a place along the wall and put his back against it. Nothing in the room had needed him for several minutes.
