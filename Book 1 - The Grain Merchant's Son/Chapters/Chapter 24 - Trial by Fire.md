Kael led them through Iron cartel territory as morning sun climbed higher, turning Kaha'an's sandstone buildings the color of rust. The industrial district felt different from the Noble Quarter's ostentation or the Middle District's desperate functionality. Here, everything served a purpose. Forges lined the streets, their heat bleeding into already warm air. Workshops stood open to catch the breeze, revealing workers bent over tables, crafting with focused intensity.

No fountains wasting water. No decorative excess. Just production, efficiency, the honest work of making things.

Xion noticed the respect people showed Kael here. Workers nodded as they passed. A foreman called a greeting. His friend's minor Iron clan connections, barely relevant in the Noble District, mattered in this territory. These were his people, in a way Xion's Grain cartel connections had never felt like his.

"Not much further," Kael said, navigating them through a maze of side streets with practiced ease.

They arrived at a training hall—a long, low building that reminded Xion uncomfortably of the Arol Batae compound but starker. More utilitarian. Where the compound projected military discipline, this place projected pure function. A space designed for one purpose: teaching people to fight.

Inside, the main room stretched perhaps thirty meters across, with a hard-packed dirt floor and weapons racked along the walls. High windows let in light without making the space decorative. Everything practical. Everything necessary. Nothing wasted.

Sa Ko Ren was already waiting.

Xion understood immediately why Kael had suggested her. She stood near the center of the training floor, and even motionless she radiated controlled danger. The same intensity as the Arol Batae warriors, but with something harder underneath. Like looking at tempered steel rather than merely sharpened blade.

She was tall, leanly athletic, with the balanced carriage of someone who had trained for combat most of her life. Her dark hair was pulled back in a practical style that kept it out of her way. She wore training clothes that had clearly seen decades of use, faded but immaculately maintained.

Xion had seen her before at formal gatherings, but always from a distance, always as his father's shadow in rooms full of careful politics. Here, in her own training hall, stripped of diplomatic pretense, she was something else entirely. Raw power barely contained by discipline.

Her eyes tracked them as they entered. Assessing. Evaluating. Xion felt measured and catalogued in the space of a heartbeat—the first time she'd actually **looked** at him rather than past him toward Rosik.

Elara stepped forward, and Xion noticed she'd changed her posture. Not the casual stance of their journey or the military bearing of the Arol Batae. Something else. Something that acknowledged the woman before them as an equal worthy of respect.

"Sa Ko Ren," Elara said. "Thank you for agreeing to meet."

"Your friend made the request properly." Sa Ko's voice was rough, carrying an accent that marked her Terinok origins. "I'm curious what the supposed princess wants from Iron."

Supposed. The word hung in the air like a challenge.

Elara didn't flinch. "Recognition. I am Elara Valanar, last surviving heir of House Valanar, rightful empress of Kaha'an. I have my bloodline—" Her eyes shifted, blue to amber to violet, then back to blue. "I have divine sanction." She gestured to where the Spirit's Reflection leaned against the wall, wrapped but visible. "And I have the Arol Batae ready to support my claim."

"Bloodline." Sa Ko's tone made it sound like an insult. "Half the pretenders who've walked through Kaha'an claimed Valanar descent. Your eyes change color. So what? Could be tricks. Could be real. Doesn't matter to me either way."

She moved closer to the Spirit's Reflection, studying it with casual disinterest. "Pretty mirror. Proves the gods like you. Doesn't prove you can hold a throne."

Xion felt his heart sink. They'd come here banking everything on Sa Ko's acknowledgment, and she was dismissing their strongest evidence like it meant nothing.

But Elara straightened, meeting Sa Ko's challenging gaze without wavering. "What would prove it?"

The question made Sa Ko smile. Sharp and approving, like Elara had said exactly the right thing.

"Single combat," Sa Ko said. "First blood, tap out, or surrender. You prove you have the strength to fight for what you're claiming, and Iron will support you."

The Arol Batae warriors tensed. This wasn't part of the plan. Commander Vesk's hand went to his sword, though he didn't draw. Silvanno looked between Elara and Sa Ko with growing alarm.

Xion's medical training kicked in automatically. He'd seen Elara fight—brutal efficiency in the Warrens, the desperate flight from the masquerade. But this was different. Sa Ko Ren stood like violence given human form, decades of warrior experience radiating from every controlled movement.

Elara was going to get hurt.

"I accept," Elara said.

No hesitation. No consultation. Just immediate acceptance, like Sa Ko had offered her a gift rather than a challenge.

"Good." Sa Ko gestured to the training circle marked in the dirt floor. "We'll use the space here. No weapons. First blood, tap out, or surrender ends it."

They began stripping off outer layers. Elara removed her traveling cloak and overshirt, revealing the practical fighting clothes she'd worn underneath—loose pants and a fitted top that allowed full range of movement. Standard Arol Batae training gear.

Sa Ko pulled off her own outer tunic with unhurried economy. Her training clothes were simple, worn soft with age, speaking to countless hours of use.

Xion had never seen Elara fight like this. Not formally. Not in a proper duel with rules and boundaries. In the Warrens, she'd moved with desperate efficiency. At the masquerade, they'd fled rather than engaged. This was something else entirely.

They faced each other across the training circle. Elara settled into a balanced stance—weight evenly distributed, arms ready, controlled. Sa Ko adopted something looser, more fluid, her posture speaking to different training entirely.

For a heartbeat, neither moved.

Then Elara attacked.

Xion lost the first exchange entirely. It went past at a speed his eye had no purchase on — the same way three men in the Tiers had gone past — and when it ended the two women stood a pace from where they'd begun and he could not have said what had happened in between.

So he stopped trying to watch it as fighting, and watched it the only way he had ever known how to watch anything.

Bodies. He could read bodies.

Elara's weight stayed on the front foot a beat too long after each combination — she was committing, which meant she was trying to end it. Her guard came back a finger lower every time it came back. And her breathing had begun to climb; not badly, not yet, but he had counted enough chests in his life to catch a rate change from across a room.

Sa Ko Ren's had not moved at all.

Then Elara got through, and that one he saw whole, because it was slow enough to see. A feint high. The real thing driving up underneath the older woman's arm, into the short ribs.

It was a good strike. It was the strike you throw when you mean to end a thing.

Days ago Xion had knelt on a dirt floor in the Warrens and wrapped ribs that had met a wall with less behind them than that, and he knew — precisely, the way he knew the colour of an infection — what it ought to have cost.

Sa Ko took it. Her breath went out through her nose. Her feet did not move.

And Xion understood, and the understanding sat cold under his sternum. She was not defending herself. She had not been defending herself since the first step. She was standing in the middle of that circle letting Elara Valanar hit her, and taking the readings.

The question was whether Elara knew.

He watched her face, saw her focus intensify as the exchanges continued. Saw the exact moment frustration flashed across her expression before being controlled. Her attacks became more aggressive, trying to force Sa Ko to respond with full effort.

But Sa Ko just... adapted. Still relaxed. Still testing.

Elara couldn't win this. She had to know that. You always knew when someone was letting you win, when they were measuring you rather than fighting you. The mismatch was too obvious, too fundamental.

This was the real test.

Would she surrender? Get angry? Throw down her hands in frustration and demand to know why Sa Ko wasn't taking her seriously?

Instead, Elara attacked harder.

More aggressive now. Taking risks. Combinations that left her exposed but might create openings. Sacrificing defense for offense, pushing the pace, refusing to acknowledge the obvious mismatch.

Xion's hands clenched. She was going to get hurt. Sa Ko could end this whenever she wanted, and Elara kept pressing like she could somehow force a victory through sheer determination.

The exchanges grew more intense. Elara adapted as she fought, learning Sa Ko's patterns, making the older woman work for what she took. And Xion, who could not stop doing it any more than he could stop breathing, kept the tally: the left forearm that would be black by morning, the hip she had begun favouring by a degree she did not know she was favouring, the shoulder she had stopped raising past a certain height.

Nothing broken. Nothing that wouldn't mend.

Yet.

Minutes passed. Felt like hours. Elara was breathing hard now, sweat-soaked, but still attacking. Still refusing to quit. Still standing ready after every exchange like she believed the next strike might change everything.

Finally, Sa Ko called "Enough."

The word cut through the training hall like a blade. Elara froze mid-movement, still in fighting stance, still ready to continue.

"Enough," Sa Ko repeated, stepping back from the circle.

Elara lowered her guard slowly, breathing hard, but she stayed standing. Ready. Not surrendering, just... pausing. Waiting to see what came next.

Sa Ko circled her slowly. Xion realized he was holding his breath.

"You can't beat me," Sa Ko said, her voice carrying across the silent hall. "But you knew that and fought anyway."

She stopped circling, studying Elara's face. The determination there despite exhaustion. The refusal to quit even now.

"The throne won't be won through combat," Sa Ko continued. "It will be won through will. Through refusing to surrender even when you're outmatched." She paused. "Through fighting for what you claim because it's worth fighting for, not because victory is guaranteed."

She extended her hand.

"You have that. Iron stands with the true empress."

The handshake felt weighted with implications Xion didn't fully grasp. Kael could probably explain the cultural significance—Terinok warrior customs, the meaning of acknowledgment through combat. Vesk could detail the tactical ramifications—what Iron's support meant for controlling the city's production, for military logistics, for political leverage.

But Xion understood the essential truth: Elara had proved herself.

Not through winning. Through refusing to quit. Through demonstrating the kind of determination that couldn't be faked or inherited or granted by divine favor. The kind that could only be proven through fire.

Bloodline. Divine sanction. Military force. And now, the acknowledgment of power.

The final piece had fallen into place.

The requirements were met.

Around them, the watching Iron cartel members processed what had just happened. Workers who'd stopped to observe the challenge, guards who'd gathered when word spread that Sa Ko herself was testing someone. Their faces showed understanding—their leader had just formally recognized this woman's claim to the throne.

One of the Arol Batae exhaled audibly, relief breaking through military discipline. Commander Vesk's expression showed satisfaction—the plan had worked, despite the unexpected method.

Elara, still catching her breath, met Sa Ko's eyes with something Xion recognized. Not gratitude, exactly. More like warrior recognizing warrior. Respect earned through contest rather than given through courtesy.

"Thank you for the test," Elara said simply.

"Thank you for not wasting my time with excuses." Sa Ko released her hand, stepping back. "When you take the throne, remember this: strength without will breaks under pressure. Will without strength accomplishes nothing. You need both."

She gestured around the training hall, at the weapons on the walls, at the space dedicated to teaching people to fight.

"I'll make my support known publicly," Sa Ko said. "The other cartels will have to respond on different terms now. They can't simply dismiss you as another pretender." Her smile was sharp. "They'll have to acknowledge you're real. What they do with that acknowledgment is their problem."

Xion watched political reality shift around them. Sa Ko Ren, leader of Iron cartel, had just formally recognized Elara's claim. That meant Iron's production capacity, its military resources, its political weight—all of it now stood behind the princess.

One of the four great cartels had broken ranks. The careful balance maintained since The Rending had shattered.

"We move today," Vesk said quietly, stepping forward. "While the momentum is with us. Before the others have time to coordinate a response."

Sa Ko nodded. "Smart. Strike while they're still processing. By the time they organize opposition, you'll already control the palace."

She looked at Elara again, and something that might have been approval flickered across her face.

"I'll send word to the Imperial Palace myself," Sa Ko said. "Let them know Iron cartel acknowledges your claim and expects them to open the gates when you arrive." She paused. "Any guards who object will answer to me personally."

The casual certainty with which she said it made Xion believe her completely. Sa Ko Ren wasn't making threats. She was stating facts about what would happen to anyone stupid enough to stand in her way.

---

Walking out of the training hall, Elara was limping slightly but holding her head high. Xion moved to her side, concerned about injuries his medical training said she must have sustained.

"I'm fine," she said, but accepted his steadying hand.

"You took a lot of hits."

"Nothing broken." She managed a tired smile. "Just bruised. Everywhere."

Silvanno appeared on her other side, looking shaken. "That was terrifying to watch."

"Imagine how it felt." But Elara's voice carried satisfaction despite the exhaustion. She'd proven herself. Not through victory, but through something harder—refusing to surrender when surrender would have been easier.

Outside, morning had progressed toward afternoon. The industrial district continued its work, seemingly unchanged by what had just happened in one training hall. But everything had changed. The foundation had shifted beneath Kaha'an's political landscape.

Kael fell into step beside Xion. "That went better than I expected."

"Better?" Xion looked at his friend. "She got beaten for ten minutes."

"She proved what needed proving." Kael's voice was matter-of-fact. "Sa Ko doesn't care about winning. She cares about worth. Elara demonstrated worth."

Vesk was already organizing the next steps, speaking quietly with his commanders about timing and routes and the approach to the palace.

Ahead of them Elara had stopped limping. Xion watched what it cost her to stop — the forearm held still, the hip taken out of the stride, the shoulder kept level by deciding to keep it level.

*Nothing broken.* True, and the smallest true thing available, and she had chosen it.

He kept the rest of the list to himself.
