# Chapter 14: First Blood

The stairs from the ledge descended into the heart of the Surface Warrens, and with each step the air grew thicker—sweat, cooking fires, too many bodies in too little space. Xion had been down here before, but never this deep, never at night when the scattered lamps created more shadows than light.

Farleen moved ahead with practiced caution, choosing their path through narrow streets that wound between ramshackle buildings. People watched them pass with calculating eyes, noting their clean clothes, their uncertain movements, the packs that marked them as either fresh arrivals or easy targets.

"Stay close," Farleen murmured. "And try not to look like you're carrying anything valuable."

Too late for that. Xion caught the way eyes lingered on Elara's pack, on his medical bag. They stood out like torches in the darkness—three people who clearly didn't belong here, wandering deeper into a place that devoured the unwary.

A child darted past, bare feet silent on stone, disappearing into an alley before Xion could fully register the movement. An old woman sat in a doorway, her milky eyes tracking them with unsettling precision despite obvious blindness. Somewhere in the distance, metal rang on metal—smithwork or something more violent, impossible to tell.

"How far until we're less exposed?" Elara asked quietly.

"Not far. The deeper districts have more... complexity. Easier to disappear." Farleen navigated around a group of men passing a bottle between them. "But we need to get there first."

The street opened into a small square where a broken fountain served as a gathering point. Water no longer flowed, but people clustered around it anyway—trading, talking, conducting the business that happened after dark in places the surface pretended didn't exist.

Farleen slowed, and Xion saw why. Three men had positioned themselves across their path. Not blocking it, exactly. Just... present. Watching. Their clothes were mismatched, weapons clearly scavenged rather than issued. Not cartel colors. Just opportunists.

"Keep walking," Farleen said. "Don't make eye contact."

But one of the men stepped forward as they tried to pass. He was large, his scarred hands suggesting years of violence, and the way he moved spoke of someone comfortable with his own authority here.

"You three." His voice carried the lazy confidence of a predator who'd spotted prey. "New to the Warrens?"

"Just passing through," Farleen said, her tone carefully neutral.

"Passing through." The man smiled. "Funny thing about the Warrens—nobody just passes through. You come down here, you've got business. Or you're looking to become business."

The other two had moved to flank them. Not threatening yet, but the implication was clear. Xion's hand tightened on his medical bag, useless as a weapon but all he had.

"We're not looking for trouble," he said.

"Nobody ever is." The leader's eyes swept over them, assessing. "But you've got the look of people with coin. Clean clothes. Good packs. Three of you traveling together—that's either confidence or stupidity." He gestured to his companions. "These streets can be dangerous for outsiders. We could make sure you reach wherever you're going. Very reasonable rates."

Translation: pay or be robbed. Maybe worse.

Farleen's jaw tightened. "How much?"

"For all three?" The man pretended to consider. "Twenty silver ought to cover it. Per person."

Sixty silver. More than most Warrens families saw in a year. The price wasn't meant to be paid—it was meant to be impossible, to justify what came next.

"That's robbery," Elara said flatly.

"That's the cost of staying safe." The man's smile widened. "But if you can't afford it, well... there are other arrangements we can make. Strong girl like you, someone would pay good coin for. And your friends—" His eyes settled on Xion. "Those hands look soft. Pretty face. Could fetch a decent price."

The casual way he discussed selling them made Xion's skin crawl. This wasn't even organized crime. Just three men who'd decided they were easy prey.

"We're leaving," Farleen said, her voice tight.

"I don't think so." The leader moved to block their path entirely. "See, thing about the Warrens is, nobody's going to stop us. Nobody's going to help you. And you three—"

He reached for Elara's arm.

She moved.

Later, Xion would try to reconstruct what happened and find he couldn't. The movement was too fast, too efficient. One moment the thug was reaching for her, the next he was stumbling backward with blood streaming from a broken nose.

The second man lunged. Elara ducked under his swing, drove her elbow into his solar plexus with brutal precision, and as he folded she brought her knee up into his face. He went down hard—and Elara came up with his knife.

The third had more sense—he drew his weapon, a rusted blade that had seen better days. But Elara was already moving, using the second man's falling body as a pivot point to launch herself at the third. The stolen knife flashed once, twice. The third man's blade clattered to the ground as he clutched his slashed wrist, cursing.

The leader had recovered enough to pull his own weapon, but Elara was there first. She caught his arm, twisted with leverage that made him cry out, and drove him face-first into the broken fountain. He hit hard enough that Xion heard the impact echo across the square.

Silence.

Three armed men lay groaning or unconscious in the space of maybe ten heartbeats. Elara stood over them, breathing hard but controlled, the stolen knife still in her hand. Blood—not hers—spattered her borrowed dress.

The square had gone utterly still. Everyone who'd been conducting business, trading, talking—they all stared at the impossible thing they'd just witnessed.

"What the hell did you just do?" Farleen's voice was low, shaking with fury.

Elara turned, dropping the knife beside the leader. "I dealt with the problem."

"You *dealt with*—" Farleen reached for her arm.

Elara's hand snapped up, catching Farleen's wrist before it made contact. The movement was pure reflex, controlled but unmistakably a combat response.

For a heartbeat, they froze—Farleen's eyes wide, Elara's grip firm enough to make a point.

Then Elara released her. "Don't grab me."

"Fine." Farleen's voice was tight. "Then *move*. Now. Before every pair of eyes in this square decides we're worth investigating."

They hurried through the crowd, which parted warily now, people backing away from the young woman who'd just destroyed three grown men. Xion followed, his mind still trying to process the speed, the precision, the complete absence of hesitation.

Farleen pulled them toward a narrow street leading away from the square. "We need to find somewhere—"

"Master Fen?"

The voice came from their left. An older man stood in a doorway, his face weathered and scarred, one eye milky with blindness. But the other eye was sharp, focused on Xion with sudden recognition.

"Master Fen, is that you?"

Xion froze. He knew that face—Korvin, a dockworker he'd treated two years ago for a crushed hand. The injury had been severe enough that most healers would have amputated. Xion had spent hours reconstructing the bones, saved most of the function.

"Korvin," Xion said carefully.

"Gods above, it *is* you." The man stepped forward, his good eye taking in the three of them—Xion's obvious distress, Elara's blood-spattered dress, Farleen's tense vigilance. "What are you doing down here? And after that—" He gestured back toward the square where people were still murmuring about the fight.

"We're just—" Xion started.

"Quick, get inside!" Korvin stepped aside, gesturing urgently into the building behind him. "Before someone decides to ask more questions about that little display."

"Korvin, I can't bring trouble to your door—" Xion began.

"You saved my hand when every other healer said it was hopeless." Korvin's voice was firm. "Never asked for payment, never expected anything. My son still has a father who can work because of you." He glanced past them at the gathering crowd. "Now get inside before someone sees you three!"

Farleen was already moving, recognizing the offer for what it was—their best option. Elara followed, and Xion had no choice but to go with them.

Korvin closed the door behind them quickly, barring it with practiced efficiency. The room was small—a single space that served as both living quarters and workspace, with a ladder leading up to what was probably a sleeping loft. But it had solid walls and a door that locked.

"Thank you," Xion said. "But you need to understand what you're risking—"

"I understand that Master Fen is in trouble, and that's enough." Korvin moved to a small stove, already preparing tea with the efficiency of someone used to scarce resources. "Though I'll admit, I'm curious why the best healer in the Middle District is in the Warrens with..." He glanced at Elara, taking in the blood and the way she moved. "Interesting companions."

"It's complicated," Xion said.

"It always is." Korvin poured water into a battered kettle. "You can stay here tonight. It's not much, but it's safe. Or as safe as anywhere gets down here."

Farleen had moved to the room's single window, checking their sight lines. When she spoke, her voice carried grudging respect. "This was fortunate."

"Master Fen's reputation just saved us." Elara's voice was matter-of-fact, but the emphasis wasn't lost on anyone.

Farleen's jaw tightened, but she didn't respond.

Korvin set out cups—chipped ceramic, but clean. "The Warrens talk, Master Fen. Word's probably spreading right now about three outsiders, one who fights like a demon." He looked directly at Elara. "People will want to know who you are. What you want here."

"We're looking for someone," Xion said. "A boy named Tam. Twelve years old, taken by Slavers a few weeks ago. We heard he was sent down here."

Xion felt rather than saw Farleen go rigid beside him. Her head turned slowly toward him, and when he glanced at her, her amber eyes were wide with something between shock and fury.

"Farleen—" he started quietly.

"Not now." Her voice was perfectly controlled, but her hands had curled into fists.

Korvin's expression darkened. "The Slavers move a lot of people through the Warrens. If they sold him to the scavengers..." He shook his head. "That's dangerous work. Deadly work."

"We know," Xion said. "But we have to try."

"Then you'll need help." Korvin poured the tea, the familiar ritual seeming to calm him. "The Warrens aren't like the surface. Different rules, different powers. And after tonight—" He gestured vaguely toward the square. "You've drawn attention. That can be good or bad, depending on who's watching."

"Who would be watching?" Farleen asked, her tone carefully casual despite the tension Xion could feel radiating from her.

"Everyone." Korvin handed out cups. "Long Knives keep track of who comes and goes. Slavers too, though less organized down here than above. And there are others—information brokers, faction leaders, people who make it their business to know things."

He settled onto a worn stool, cradling his cup. "But if you're looking for someone, you'll want to go to the Crossroads. That's where people go when they need to know things—information brokers operate there. It's called that for a reason. If anyone knows where one specific boy ended up, you'll find them there."

Xion committed the name to memory. "Thank you. For everything."

"Thank *you*." Korvin flexed his scarred hand—the one Xion had saved. "My boy was five when I got hurt. If I'd lost this hand, lost my work... I don't like thinking about what would have happened to him. To my wife." He met Xion's eyes. "Whatever you're doing down here, whatever trouble you're in—you helped us when we had nothing. Least I can do is return the favor."

The simple honesty of it made Xion's throat tight. This was what couldn't be explained through political calculations. Master Fen had accumulated something more valuable than coin or connections—he'd accumulated genuine gratitude, the kind that made a poor dockworker risk his own safety to help.

"You should rest," Korvin said. "All of you. You look like you've been running."

"We have been," Elara said quietly.

"Then rest. Tomorrow you can worry about finding your boy and dealing with whoever's interested in that fight." He stood, moving toward the ladder. "I'll take the loft. You three can manage down here."

After he'd climbed up and the sounds of his settling had faded, the three of them were left in the small room's uncomfortable silence.

Farleen waited. Her eyes tracked upward, watching the loft, listening as Korvin's breathing gradually evened into the rhythm of sleep. Ten seconds. Twenty. Thirty.

Then she turned to face both of them, and Xion saw something in her expression that made his stomach drop. Not just anger. Something closer to betrayal.

"I don't even know where to start with you two." Her voice was barely above a whisper, but it vibrated with fury. "Honestly, I—" She pressed her hands to her temples, visibly trying to organize her thoughts. "Who is Tam? Because nobody mentioned this before we ran."

Xion blinked. "What?"

"Tam. The boy you mentioned to Korvin. The one we're apparently looking for." Each word was precise, controlled. "Who. Is. He?"

"His parents came to my clinic—"

"I don't care about his parents." Farleen's whisper grew sharper. "I care that we're in the Warrens to *hide*, Xion. Because you're wanted by your own father's organization. Because *she*—" A gesture at Elara. "—is being hunted by half the city. We came down here to disappear. Not to go hunting for some child I've never heard of!"

"I made a promise—"

"You made a promise to strangers!" Farleen's hands were shaking now. "To people you don't even know! And now we're—" She stopped, whirled on Elara. "And YOU. Don't think you're off the hook either."

Elara straightened, her expression carefully neutral.

"That little display back there?" Farleen's voice dropped even lower, forcing them to lean in to hear. "You just told everyone in the Surface Warrens exactly what you are. A fighter. Trained. Professional. The kind of person who destroys three armed men without breaking a sweat."

"They were going to—"

"I don't care what they were going to do!" Farleen cut her off. "We needed to be invisible. Forgettable. Just three more outsiders passing through. But now?" She laughed, sharp and bitter. "Now we're interesting. Now people are talking. Now every faction down here knows there's a girl who fights like imperial guard wandering around asking questions."

She turned back to Xion. "Do you have any idea how dangerous this is? We could have stayed in the Surface Warrens. Safe. Hidden. Waited for things to calm down on the surface. But no—" Her voice was rising and she forced it back down to a whisper. "—you want to go *deeper*. Into the Midlevels. Into Slaver territory. Looking for a boy you've never met because you made some promise to desperate parents."

"Farleen—"

"I came down here for you." The words came out raw. "I risked everything to warn you. To get you out. And this is—" She gestured helplessly at the room, at the situation. "This is insane. Both of you are insane."

"His parents were desperate," Xion said quietly. "They had nowhere else to turn."

"Neither did we!" Farleen's control was fraying. "We're fugitives, Xion! We don't have the luxury of playing hero right now!"

"I'm not playing—"

"Yes you are!" She rounded on him again. "You're playing hero because that's what you do. Master Fen, saving everyone, fixing everything. But you can't fix this. You can't save everyone. And trying to save this one boy might get us all killed."

"So what would you have me do?" Xion asked. "Just abandon him?"

"Yes! Yes, that's exactly what I'd have you do!" Farleen's voice was still a whisper but the intensity made it feel like shouting. "We have bigger problems than one enslaved child. We have—" She stopped herself, visibly struggling for control.

"We're already here," Xion said. "We might as well—"

"No." Farleen's voice went flat. "No, we are not 'already here so we might as well.' The Surface Warrens are safe enough. We don't *have* to go deeper. That's a choice. A stupid, dangerous choice that you're making for all of us."

"Then why did you come at all?" Elara's voice cut through the tension. "If you don't care about helping people, why follow us down here?"

Farleen's head snapped toward her. "I didn't say I don't care about helping people. I said we can't afford to right now. There's a difference."

"Is there?" Elara's tone was cool. "Sounds like you're just trying to justify cowardice."

"Cowardice?" Farleen laughed, the sound sharp enough to make Xion wince. "I stabbed him—" She pointed at Xion. "—to maintain my cover. I betrayed everything I believed in to protect cartel interests. And when I finally decided to do the right thing, to actually help him, I threw away my entire life. My family. My position. Everything." Her amber eyes were blazing. "So don't you dare call me a coward, princess."

"Then what do you call someone who runs away when things get difficult?"

"I call them smart!" Farleen's whisper was vicious now. "I call them someone who understands that you can't help anyone if you're dead! That sometimes the smart choice is to survive today so you can fight tomorrow!"

"Or maybe it's just an excuse to do nothing."

Farleen looked like she wanted to hit her. Instead she turned back to Xion, visibly forcing herself to calm down.

"We could stay here," she said, her voice tight but controlled again. "In the Surface Warrens. Safe. Hidden. Wait for the situation on the surface to calm down. Maybe a few weeks, maybe a month. But we'd be alive. We'd have options." She met his eyes. "But if we go deeper, if we start asking questions about Slaver operations, if we draw more attention after that—" A gesture toward where the fight had happened. "—we might not get the chance to wait anything out."

"I know," Xion said quietly.

"But you're going anyway."

"Yes."

She turned to Elara, and her voice held something desperate beneath the anger. "And you're just... fine with this?"

"Yes," Elara said simply.

Farleen stared at her for a moment, something like disbelief crossing her face. Then she turned back to Xion.

"Yes."

She stared at him for a long moment. "I hope he's worth it. I really do. Because if we die down there, it better be for a damn good reason."

Farleen claimed a corner of the room, settling with her back against the wall in a position that let her watch both the door and the window. Her movements were sharp, angry.

Elara moved to the opposite side, as far from Farleen as possible. When she spoke, her voice was quiet. "For what it's worth, I think you're right to look for him."

"I don't need your approval," Xion said, more sharply than he intended.

Elara fell silent.

Xion took the remaining spot near the cold stove. His shoulder ached where Farleen had stabbed him just days ago. The wound was healing, but it still hurt when he moved wrong.

Outside, the Warrens continued its unknowing rhythms. But somewhere in that darkness, word was spreading. Three outsiders in the Surface Warrens. One who fought like imperial guard. One who moved with purpose and knowledge. One who carried a healer's bag and was recognized by the locals.

People would be talking. Making connections. Drawing conclusions.

Xion looked at his companions—Farleen rigid with controlled fury in her corner, Elara silent and alert in hers. Both of them here because of him. Both of them angry for different reasons.

Tomorrow they would go to the Crossroads. They would ask about Tam. They would go deeper into danger, by choice, for a child they'd both promised to find.
