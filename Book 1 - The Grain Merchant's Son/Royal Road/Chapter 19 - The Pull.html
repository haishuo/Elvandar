The passages were different here. Cleaner. More organized.

Xion shifted Tam's weight against his shoulder, trying to find a position that didn't make his back scream. The boy was getting heavier—or maybe Xion was just getting weaker. Either way, every step required more effort than the last.

"Almost there," Cullen said from ahead. The Slaver moved with easy confidence through the tunnels, one hand trailing along the smooth brass walls. "We cut through here, stay clear of the Knives' territory, then it's a straight shot up to the Market of Sighs."

Xion nodded, not trusting himself to speak. Breathing took enough energy.

Farleen walked beside him, her eyes scanning the passages they passed. Alert. Professional. But there was something different in her posture now—a tension that had been wound tight for days finally starting to ease.

They were leaving. Actually leaving. With Tam. The plan had worked.

Tam mumbled something against Xion's shoulder. Fevered nonsense, half-words that didn't connect. His skin was hot through the thin fabric of his shirt, but his breathing was steady. He'd make it. Once they reached the surface, once Xion could treat him properly—

Elara stopped walking.

Not slowed. Not hesitated. Just... stopped. Mid-step, like someone had grabbed her and held her in place.

"Elara?" Farleen's voice held a question.

No response.

Xion looked back. Elara stood perfectly still in the middle of the passage, staring at something to her left. A side tunnel, narrow and dark. Her face had gone blank. Empty.

"You okay?" Xion tried to shift Tam's weight to get a better look at her.

She didn't answer. Didn't even blink.

Then her eyes changed.

Blue to amber. Amber to violet. Violet back to blue. Cycling through colors like light through stained glass, faster than Xion had ever seen before.

"Elara?" Farleen moved toward her. "What's wrong?"

Elara turned. Smooth. Mechanical. Not looking at any of them. Looking at the side passage. Then she started walking toward it.

"Hey." Cullen stopped, turned back. "Where's she going? Exit's this way."

Elara didn't respond. Just kept walking, her eyes still cycling colors, her face still empty.

Cullen followed a few steps, frowning. "Princess, you're going the wrong way."

Nothing. She walked past him like he wasn't there.

"Elara!" Farleen grabbed her arm.

Elara pulled free without seeming to notice. Without acknowledgment. Without awareness. Her eyes cycled faster, and she walked with eerie certainty down the side passage.

Away from their route. Away from the surface.

Down.

Xion felt Tam's weight shift as the boy mumbled something. Felt his own heartbeat picking up speed. This was wrong. Everything about this was wrong.

Cullen followed for another few paces, his confidence draining with each step. "What the hell is she doing?"

"I don't know." Farleen's voice was tight. She tried again to grab Elara, to turn her around. Elara walked through her grip like water.

The passage opened into a junction chamber. Wider than the tunnels, with multiple openings at different heights. And carved into the walls around some of those openings—

Blade marks. Hundreds of them. Stylized cuts forming patterns that looked almost like script.

Long Knife symbols.

Cullen stopped dead. "Oh no. No no no."

Elara walked straight toward one of the marked passages. The one that sloped downward into darkness.

"That's Long Knife territory." Cullen backed up, hands raised. "I don't go there."

"We have to stop her!" Farleen looked between the passage and Cullen, desperation creeping into her voice.

"Whatever she's doing, wherever she's going, I'm not following." Cullen kept backing away. "I got orders to get you topside, not get myself killed in Knife turf. You want to follow her into that, you're on your own, kid."

Elara crossed the invisible border without hesitation. Past the carved symbols. Into the marked passage. Disappearing into darkness that seemed thicker somehow. Older.

Xion watched her silhouette fade. Felt Tam's fevered weight against his shoulder. Saw the blade marks carved into stone.

They'd had a plan. A clean exit. Everything had finally worked out.

"Xion." Farleen turned to him, and her face had gone pale. "We need to go. Now. Up."

He looked at the dark passage. Couldn't see Elara anymore. Just darkness.

"Let her go." Farleen's voice went flat. Hard. "If she wants to walk into Long Knife territory, let her do it alone."

Xion stared at her.

"Why should you die following her?" Farleen stepped closer, grabbed his free arm. "And Tam—gods, Xion, you're going to get him killed for this?"

She meant it. He could see it in her face. The tactical calculation. The ruthless practicality that kept people alive.

"You're serious."

"Yes!" Her grip tightened. "We have an exit! We have Tam! We can leave!"

Behind them, Cullen's footsteps were already fading. Retreating back the way they'd come.

Xion looked at the dark passage. At Tam, burning with fever. At Farleen's pleading face.

At the darkness where Elara had vanished.

He couldn't leave her there. Alone in the dark. Walking deeper into territory that killed people for trespassing.

He didn't even really consider it.

Xion adjusted Tam's weight. Took a step toward the passage.

"Xion—"

Another step.

"Don't you dare—XION!"

He crossed the border. Past the blade marks. Into Long Knife territory.

"FINE!" Farleen's voice cracked with fury and something else. Fear. "Gods damn you, FINE!"

Her footsteps behind him. Quick and angry. Catching up.

Relief flooded through him despite everything. She was coming. She was—

"This is insane!" Her voice shook. "You're going to die down here, do you understand that? You're going to die!"

The passage sloped downward immediately. Steeper than the tunnels above. The walls were different here—cruder, less finished. Like someone had carved through rock without caring about precision.

"Do you even know where we are?" Farleen was right behind him now. "This is their territory! The Long Knives kill people just for being here!"

Xion's light barely penetrated the darkness ahead. But he could see movement. Elara's silhouette, still walking with that eerie certainty.

The temperature was rising. Just slightly, but enough to notice. The air felt thicker.

"She doesn't even know what she's doing!" Farleen's voice went higher, more desperate. "You can't—Xion, you can't help yourself, can you? She walks and you follow!"

Tam's weight pressed down on Xion's shoulder. The boy mumbled something. His mother's name, maybe.

"With Tam dying right there! He's burning up, Xion! Look at him!"

Her anger wasn't directed at Elara. It was directed at him. At his choice. At the fact that he was here, in this place, risking everything.

"I know." Xion's voice came out rough. "I know, Farleen."

"Then why?" Almost a scream now. "Why are you doing this?"

Because he couldn't leave her. Because even knowing it was stupid, even knowing Farleen was right, he couldn't do it.

He didn't answer. Just kept walking. Following Elara deeper into the dark.

The passage narrowed. The ceiling dropped lower. Ancient brass gave way to raw stone, and the smooth construction of the upper levels became something else. Something older. Less maintained.

Just their footsteps now. Tam's labored breathing. The sound of water dripping somewhere in the darkness.

And ahead, Elara walked on. Eyes cycling colors in the dark. Moving like she knew exactly where she was going.

Moving like something was calling her home.

---

The passages branched.

Not the simple intersections of the upper levels. These were different—three tunnels, four, sometimes five all meeting at odd angles. Some sloping up, some down, some twisting away into darkness that Xion's light couldn't reach.

Elara never hesitated. She'd walk straight to a branching point, pause for half a heartbeat, then choose a direction with absolute certainty.

Xion had no idea if her choices were right. Had no way to know. Could only follow and hope that whatever was pulling her wasn't leading them to their deaths.

"Xion, please."

Farleen's voice had lost most of its anger. Now it was just fear. Raw and desperate.

"She doesn't even know where she's going. You're trusting—what? Magic? A trance? Gods, Xion, please listen to me!"

Tam was getting heavier. Or Xion was getting weaker. Hard to tell anymore. His shoulder burned where the boy's weight pressed down. His legs felt like they were made of lead.

"You're going to die for her. Is that what you want? To die down here in the dark where no one will ever find you?"

But Elara moved through the maze like she'd walked it a thousand times. Never backtracking. Never second-guessing.

Just walking deeper.

The temperature kept rising. Xion's shirt stuck to his back with sweat. Tam's skin felt like it was on fire.

"Is she worth your life?" Farleen's voice broke. "Tell me she's worth it, Xion. Tell me you're dying for something that matters."

The passage opened into a chamber. Small, maybe ten meters across. Three exits at different heights. Water pooled on the floor—dark and still, reflecting their lights like black glass.

Elara walked straight through the water. Didn't slow. Didn't look down. Just waded through and out the other side, toward a passage that descended at a steep angle.

Xion followed. The water was cold against his legs. Deeper than it looked—up to his knees in the center.

Tam jerked against his shoulder. "Mama?"

"It's okay." Xion's voice sounded distant to his own ears. "We're almost there."

He had no idea if that was true.

Farleen waded through behind him. She'd gone quiet now. Just following. Her face pale in the lamplight.

The descending passage was narrow. Xion had to turn sideways to fit through with Tam on his shoulder. The walls pressed close. Claustrophobic.

And still Elara walked ahead. Eyes cycling. Face empty.

Going deeper.

---

Time stopped meaning anything.

Could have been an hour. Could have been three. Xion just focused on the next step. The next breath. Keeping Tam's weight balanced. Keeping his feet moving.

The passages kept branching. Kept twisting. Routes that descended would suddenly curve back upward. Tunnels that seemed to dead-end would reveal hidden continuations. The maze was three-dimensional in a way that made Xion's head hurt to think about.

But Elara never faltered.

Tam's fever was getting worse. Xion could feel it through the fabric. The boy was delirious now, mumbling constantly. Names. Fragments of conversation. Things that didn't make sense.

"He's dying." Farleen's voice barely carried over the sound of their footsteps. "You know that, right? He's dying and we're taking him deeper."

"I know."

"Every step we take, he gets worse. Every minute down here is a minute he's not getting help."

The words hit like physical blows. Because they were true. Every step deeper into this maze was another step away from salvation. Another moment Tam wasn't getting the treatment he needed.

"For what?" Farleen's voice cracked. "What are we even doing this for?"

Xion didn't answer. Couldn't answer. Just kept walking.

His legs were shaking now. Each step required conscious effort. Lift. Move. Place. Lift. Move. Place.

Tam's weight threatened to pull him down with every movement.

"Please." Barely a whisper now. "Xion, please. I can't—I can't lose you."

The words hung in the air between them.

But he couldn't stop. Couldn't turn back even if he wanted to—they'd never find their way through the maze without Elara.

They were committed now. For better or worse.

Ahead, Elara kept walking, her eyes still turning over in the dark.

Like a beacon.

Like a moth drawn to flame.

---

The air changed.

Xion noticed it first as a shift in pressure. Something in his ears. Then the temperature—it had been rising steadily, but now it leveled out. Became... controlled. Deliberate.

The walls changed too. The crude stone passages gave way to something else. Smoother. More finished.

Ancient construction. Real ancient construction, not the crude tunnels they'd been navigating.

And there was light.

Faint. Blue. Coming from the walls themselves. Not bright enough to see clearly, but enough that Xion could make out the passage ahead without his lamp.

Elara walked faster now. Pulled harder by whatever was calling her.

The passage opened up.

Xion stumbled into a chamber and stopped, taking it in.

It was massive. Maybe forty meters across, the ceiling lost in darkness overhead. The walls were perfectly smooth brass, covered in intricate symbols and geometric patterns that seemed to shift in the blue light. The floor was level—actually level, not the uneven stone of the passages above.

And it was... pristine. No debris. No dust. No signs of age or decay.

Like it had been carved yesterday.

At the far end of the chamber, a raised platform held something that made Xion's breath catch.

A door.

Five meters tall. Three meters wide. Made of brass or something like it, covered in engravings so detailed they seemed to move in the blue light. The door sat in the wall like it was waiting. Like it had always been waiting.

And beyond it—Xion couldn't hear it so much as feel it. A rhythmic pulse. Deep. Steady. Like breathing.

Like a heartbeat.

Elara walked straight to the platform. Her eyes blazing through colors now, cycling so fast they were almost a blur. She climbed the steps. Reached the center of the platform.

And stopped.

For a heartbeat, nothing happened.

Then the blue light surged. Brilliant. Blinding. Xion threw up his arm to shield his eyes.

When he could see again, Elara was on her knees on the platform. The color cycling had stopped. Her eyes were just blue now. Normal blue.

She blinked. Once. Twice.

Then looked around the chamber with complete confusion.

"What the—" Her voice was rough. Disoriented. "How did I get here?"

She saw them. Xion supporting Tam. Farleen standing beside him, face pale and exhausted.

"Where are we?" Elara looked around again, taking in the chamber. The brass walls. The door. "The last thing I remember, we were with Cullen and he was saying something about the route and then—"

Her eyes widened. "Did I—did I black out? What happened?"

Tam collapsed.

The boy's weight suddenly went completely slack. Xion barely managed to catch him as he crumpled, easing him down to the floor.

"Tam!" Xion knelt beside him, hands moving automatically to check breathing, pulse. "Tam, can you hear me?"

Nothing. The boy's eyes were closed. His skin burning. Pulse rapid and thready.

Farleen dropped to her knees beside them, already pulling medical supplies from her pack. Her hands were shaking.

And Elara stood on the platform, staring down at them with dawning horror.

"What did I do?" she whispered.

Nobody answered.
