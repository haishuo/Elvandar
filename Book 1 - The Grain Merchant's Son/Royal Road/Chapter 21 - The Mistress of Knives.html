The passages were different here.

Xion noticed it first in the way the guards moved—visible now, not hiding. They stood at regular intervals, dressed in Long Knives colors, watching the group pass with professional disinterest. No threats. No posturing. Just observation.

Control. That's what it was. The Long Knives controlled this territory absolutely.

Tam's weight pressed down on Xion's shoulder. The boy had stopped mumbling. That was either good or bad. Xion couldn't tell anymore.

"Not much further," the assassin said from ahead. First words in twenty minutes.

Farleen walked beside Xion, her eyes tracking every guard, every shadow, every possible threat. Still tense. Still calculating.

Elara followed behind, the Spirit's Reflection tucked under her arm. Silent.

The passage opened into a chamber.

Xion stopped, taking it in.

It was like the Iron Hall—same impossibly smooth brass walls, same incomprehensible symbols carved into every surface, same ancient construction that spoke of builders long gone. But where Ronas had organized his territory for efficiency, the Long Knives had done something else.

They'd made it comfortable.

Proper furniture—not salvaged wood but actual crafted pieces. Lighting that didn't smoke. Fabric partitions that looked intentional rather than desperate. The human construction inside the ancient chamber was still makeshift, still temporary in the face of that eternal brass, but it was... different.

Refined. Permanent. Like they'd been here long enough to stop just surviving and start living.

"This way." The assassin led them to a side area, partitioned off with dark fabric.

And there—Xion's breath caught—a actual bed. Clean linens. Space to work.

"Medical supplies are coming," the assassin said. Then left.

Xion moved immediately. Laid Tam down on the bed as gently as he could. The boy's skin was still too hot. His breathing too shallow.

"Water," Xion said. "I need—"

Someone was already there. Not Farleen. One of the Long Knives, holding a basin and clean cloth.

Xion took them without thinking. Started working. Cooling Tam's fever. Checking his pulse. His breathing.

Footsteps behind him. Supplies being set down. Xion turned, saw a collection of medical materials that made his chest tight. Real medicine. Not the makeshift remedies he'd been using. Powdered willow bark in quantity, clean linen for bandages, yarrow for wounds, feverfew, proper herbs he'd only dreamed of having access to in the Warrens.

Everything he needed.

He took the hand out of its sling first and did it properly — the way he had wanted to do it on the floor of the Iron Hall and hadn't been able to. Opened along its whole length this time, cleaned until the water ran clear, packed, and dressed in linen that hadn't spent a week in the bottom of a bag. It should have been done days ago, in a lit room, by someone with shelves. He did it now, because now was what he had.

Then he worked without speaking. Mixed the fever reducer with water. Got it between Tam's lips. More went down this time. The boy swallowed reflexively.

Good. That was good.

Minutes passed. Xion lost track. There was only the work. Only Tam. Only keeping the boy alive.

The fever started to break. Just slightly. But enough. Tam's breathing evened out. His color improved from that terrible gray-pale to something closer to human.

He was going to make it. Actually make it.

Xion sat back, his hands shaking. From exhaustion. From relief. From everything.

A presence entered the chamber. Xion felt it—that shift in the air that said someone important had arrived. But Tam's pulse was still too fast. The boy needed monitoring. Needed—

Someone nudged his shoulder.

Xion waved them off without looking. "Not now."

The presence didn't leave.

Another nudge. Farleen's hand, more insistent.

"I said not now." Xion leaned closer to Tam, checking the boy's breathing again. "He's not stable yet."

Silence behind him. Heavy. Waiting.

Xion ignored it. Adjusted Tam's blanket. Checked his temperature. The fever was definitely breaking. Still dangerous, but breaking.

Finally—finally—he could step back. The boy would live.

Xion stood, turned.

And froze.

An older woman stood in the chamber. Maybe late fifties, with sharp eyes and silver threading through dark hair pulled back in a severe style. She wore Long Knives colors but richer fabric. Better cut. This was someone used to authority.

The Mistress.

Had to be.

Farleen and Elara stood to the side, their faces carefully neutral. But Xion could see the tension in Farleen's shoulders. The way Elara held herself ready.

They'd been trying to warn him. And he'd shushed them.

Heat crawled up Xion's neck.

"Sorry, Mistress, but my patient—"

She waved him off. A single gesture, not unkind. Just... dismissive of the apology.

"The boy is stable?"

Her voice was measured. Controlled. The voice of someone who expected answers.

"Yes. The fever's breaking. He'll need rest and monitoring, but he should recover."

"Good." She looked at Tam for a long moment. Then back to Xion. "You're a healer."

"Yes."

"Master Fen, I'm told."

Xion's stomach dropped. Of course she knew. Everyone down here seemed to know.

"Yes."

"Interesting." She moved further into the chamber, her attention shifting to Elara. "And you. You're the one who navigated Dead Man's Passage."

Elara straightened. "I don't know how. I don't remember doing it."

"But you did." The Mistress's eyes were sharp. Assessing. "No one navigates that maze without a map. No one. But you walked through it like you knew every turn."

"I didn't. I don't." Elara's voice was steady but Xion could hear the frustration underneath. "Something pulled me. I couldn't control it. I don't even remember most of it."

"Something pulled you." The Mistress repeated the words slowly. Testing them. "To the brass door."

"Yes."

"Which responded to your touch."

Elara hesitated. Then: "Yes."

The Mistress was quiet for a long moment. Just looking at Elara. Reading her.

"Do you know what that means?" she asked finally.

"No."

"Do you know what you are?"

"I'm..." Elara stopped. Started again. "I'm the last surviving member of House Valanar. The imperial bloodline. I have the eyes. I passed the trial of the Spirit's Reflection. But beyond that..." She shook her head. "I don't know what any of this means."

The Mistress's expression didn't change. But something in her eyes dimmed. Disappointment.

She turned away. Walked to the edge of the partition, looking out at the chamber beyond.

"For a moment," she said quietly, "I thought you might be the answer."

Xion exchanged a glance with Farleen. She looked as confused as he felt.

"Answer to what?" Elara asked.

"Questions we've been asking for centuries." The Mistress's voice had lost some of its edge. Now she just sounded... tired. "What this place is. What the builders intended. Why the door is here. What it guards."

She gestured at the brass walls around them.

"We've lived in these tunnels for generations. Guarded that door. Protected the route to it. But we don't know why. We don't understand what we're protecting."

Xion looked at the walls. At the symbols he couldn't read. At the ancient construction that had outlasted empires.

"I hoped," the Mistress continued, "that someone who could navigate the passage, who could make the door respond, might finally know something. Might understand."

She looked back at Elara.

"But you're just as lost as the rest of us, aren't you?"

Elara's face was tight. "It's not my fault I don't understand."

"No. It's not." The Mistress's voice softened slightly. "You can't explain what you don't know. I just hoped... after all this time, someone would finally have answers."

The silence stretched.

Then the Mistress straightened. The vulnerability closed back up like a door slamming shut. When she spoke again, her voice was all business.

"You do realize you've created a problem for yourselves."

"What kind of problem?" Farleen asked. First words since the Mistress had entered.

"You had an agreement with Ronas Dermir. Take the boy, leave directly to the surface. Yes?"

Xion nodded slowly.

"Instead you wandered into Long Knives territory. Spent hours down here with us." The Mistress's expression was neutral. "Ronas and I are not allies. We maintain a careful balance, but we're rivals. You've spent time with his archrivals. That complicates your relationship with him."

"We didn't have a choice," Elara said. "I couldn't control—"

"I know." The Mistress cut her off. "I'm not blaming you. I'm stating facts. Ronas will see it as a breach of agreement. That creates complications."

"Such as?" Xion asked.

"Such as the fact that your journey back up has now become significantly more... interesting." The Mistress looked at him. "He gave you safe passage. You didn't use it the way he expected. That matters to men like Ronas."

Xion felt his stomach sink. They'd survived Dead Man's Passage. Survived the brass door. Tam was alive. And now—

"Still." The Mistress turned back to Elara. "The door responded to you. That means something, even if you don't know what yet."

She was quiet for a moment. Thinking.

"Which is why you'll go up with my people," she said finally. "Not out of alliance. Ronas gave you a straight road to the surface and you didn't walk it, and men like Ronas count that. You'll not get through his levels alone tonight — not the three of you, not carrying a sick child, not after this. My escort makes it somebody else's arithmetic." A brief, dry look. "And because you interest me. A woman who can make that door respond is worth the loan of four knives."

"You're letting us go?" Farleen's voice held disbelief.

"You're useless to me right now. You have no information I need. Can't explain what you don't understand." The Mistress shrugged. "Might as well let you go. See what develops."

She looked at Elara.

"When you take the throne—and you will, with divine sanction and that bloodline—if you ever figure out what you are, find me. We should talk."

"About what?" Elara asked.

"About doors that respond to your touch. About passages you navigate without maps. About whatever it is that makes you different." The Mistress's smile didn't reach her eyes. "The Long Knives will be watching. We've waited this long. We can wait longer."

She turned to leave. Paused.

"My people will guide you back through safer routes. Make sure you reach the surface alive. After that..." She glanced back. "You're on your own."

Then she was gone. The partition swaying slightly in her wake.

Xion looked at the others. Farleen's expression was carefully controlled. Elara looked shaken.

On the bed, Tam stirred slightly. Mumbled something that might have been his mother's name.

Xion moved back to the boy's side. Checked his pulse. Still steady. Still improving.

They were being released. Given safe passage. Tam would survive.

After everything, they'd made it.

So why did it feel like they'd just made everything more complicated?

---

The journey back was nothing like the descent.

The Long Knives guide—a different assassin, this one silent and efficient—led them through passages Xion had never seen. Wider. More maintained. Routes that bypassed the worst of the Warrens entirely.

Tam was being carried on a stretcher by two of the assassins. Still weak. Still recovering. But alive. His color was better. His breathing normal.

They climbed. Level by level. Past the Depths, through the Midlevels, up toward the Tiers.

Xion's legs burned. His shoulders ached from hours of carrying Tam earlier. But they were moving up. Toward light. Toward air.

Toward the surface.

Farleen walked beside him, her eyes still scanning for threats even though they had escort. Professional to the end.

Elara followed behind, the Spirit's Reflection wrapped in cloth and slung across her back. She'd been quiet since they left the Mistress's chamber. Thinking. Processing.

The passages grew lighter. Not blue ancient light, but actual daylight filtering down from above. The air changed. Less oppressive. Cooler.

They were close.

The guide stopped at a junction chamber. Pointed up at a passage that sloped toward brightness.

"That leads to the surface. Temple district, near the old Sanctuary." Their voice was neutral. "You're on your own from here."

"Thank you," Xion said.

The guide nodded once. Then they and the stretcher-bearers set Tam down carefully on the chamber floor and left. Just like that. No ceremony. No farewell.

Gone.

Xion knelt beside Tam. The boy's eyes fluttered open.

"Master Fen?" His voice was weak. Confused.

"It's me. We're almost out."

"I want to go home."

"I know. Soon."

Tam's eyes closed again. Not unconscious. Just exhausted.

Xion stood. Looked at the passage leading up. At the daylight visible at the top.

They'd made it. Through everything. Through the descent, the maze, the brass door, the Mistress's domain.

Tam was alive. They had the Spirit's Reflection. They had divine sanction.

They'd survived.

Xion lifted Tam carefully. The boy was so light. Too light. But breathing. Alive.

"Let's go home," he said quietly.

And they climbed toward the light.
