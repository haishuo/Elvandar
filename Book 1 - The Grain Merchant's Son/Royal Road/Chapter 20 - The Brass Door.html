Tam's pulse was too fast. Thready. His skin burned under Xion's fingers.

"Water." Xion's voice came out hoarse. "Farleen, water."

She was already moving, pulling their remaining flask from her pack. Her hands shook as she passed it over.

Xion wet a strip of cloth, pressed it to Tam's forehead. The boy didn't respond. Didn't even twitch.

"Come on," Xion whispered. "Come on, Tam. Stay with me."

He checked the pulse again. Still too fast. Still weak. But there. The boy was still breathing.

Farleen knelt beside him. "What do you need?"

Xion was already pulling his medical supplies from his pack, hands moving on instinct. "Hold his head steady."

She complied without a word.

He found the willow bark. Mixed it with water from their flask. Got it between Tam's lips. Most of it dribbled out, but some went down. Had to be enough.

Had to.

Behind them, Elara stood frozen on the platform. Staring down at them with her hand pressed over her mouth.

Xion didn't look at her. Couldn't. Had to focus on Tam. On keeping the boy alive.

Minutes stretched. Xion worked mechanically—checking vitals, adjusting the compress, trying to get more medicine into Tam. Farleen handed him what he needed without being asked. Professional. Efficient.

Silent.

Finally, Tam's breathing evened out. Just slightly. The fever didn't break, but it stopped climbing. The boy's pulse steadied.

Not good. But stable.

Xion sat back on his heels, suddenly aware of how much he was shaking. His hands. His legs. Everything.

"He's going to make it?" Farleen's voice was carefully neutral.

"I think so. For now."

"For now."

Xion looked up at her. Saw something in her face that made his stomach clench.

She was looking at Elara.

"Farleen—"

"Do you understand what just happened?" Farleen's voice was cold. Precise. Each word sharp enough to cut.

"You walked into a death trap." Farleen stood slowly. Deliberately. "And Xion followed you, because of course he did. He could have died in that maze. We'd have lost the boy on top of it — the whole reason any of us came down here — and you could not have told a living soul why."

Elara sat down on the platform steps.

She did it without any drama at all, which was somehow worse than if she had argued, and Xion understood watching her that she had gone past arguing some time ago.

"I know," she said.

"You don't get to just—" Farleen stopped. "You know."

"I wasn't there for any of it." Her hands hung between her knees. "I don't remember the border. I don't remember wading through water. There's a piece missing between Cullen saying something about the route and this room, and I don't know how long it is. Hours, from the state of him." A glance at Tam, and away. "I have been told what I did. I have not remembered one step of it."

Farleen said nothing.

"So ask the useful question." Elara looked up. "If it starts again — now, in this room, with him on that floor. Can you stop me?"

Farleen opened her mouth.

Xion watched her go looking for it. Three hours of maze behind them. A grip that had slid off Elara's arm like water. A Slaver captain who had backed away with his hands up rather than cross a line of blade marks.

"No," she said.

"No," Elara agreed. "Neither can I."

Farleen turned away and went to check on Tam herself, which Xion understood was not about Tam.

The chamber waited around them. Ancient. Silent. The blue light from the walls painting everything in cold colors.

And the brass door loomed. Five meters of perfectly preserved metal, covered in symbols Xion couldn't read. Waiting for something.

For someone.

---

It was Elara who broke the silence.

"What is that?"

Her voice was rough. Small. But she was looking at something across the chamber.

Xion followed her gaze.

In an alcove he hadn't noticed before—maybe ten meters from the door—something gleamed. A pedestal. And on it, a shield.

No. Not a shield. A mirror shaped like a shield.

Elara stood. Moved toward it like she was being pulled. Again. Always being pulled.

"Elara—" Farleen's voice held a warning.

"I just want to look."

"You 'just wanted to look' at the brass door and we ended up in a trance walking through a death maze."

"This is different."

"How?"

Elara didn't answer. Just kept walking toward the alcove.

Xion stood, his legs protesting. Moved to follow. Keeping distance. Watching.

The shield—the mirror—was beautiful. Ornate. The metal work around the edges was as detailed as the engravings on the door. Symbols. Geometric patterns. Things that looked almost like writing but weren't quite.

And the surface—

It was perfectly reflective. Polished to impossible smoothness. But there were two surfaces. An inner mirror facing the alcove. An outer mirror facing the chamber.

Elara stopped a meter away from it. Staring.

"Do you see that?" Her voice was barely audible.

"See what?" Farleen had moved closer despite herself.

"The... the symbols. They're glowing."

Xion looked closer. She was right. Faint blue light traced through the engravings around the mirror's edge. Pulsing. Like a heartbeat.

"Don't touch it," Farleen said.

Elara reached out.

"Elara, don't—"

Her fingers touched the frame.

And she went still.

Completely still. Like someone had turned her to stone.

"Elara?" Xion stepped closer. "Elara!"

No response. She stood there with her hand on the frame, staring into the inner mirror. Her eyes wide. Unblinking.

Not again. Not another trance.

"Elara!" Farleen grabbed her shoulder. Shook her.

Nothing.

Xion moved to her other side. Looked at her face. Saw her eyes moving. Tracking something. Seeing something he couldn't see.

Tears started down her cheeks.

"What's happening to her?" Farleen's voice was tight.

"I don't know."

They stood there. Helpless. Watching Elara stare into the mirror at something only she could see. Watching her face twist with pain. With fear. With something that looked like grief.

Her breathing quickened. Her hand trembled on the frame but didn't pull away.

Minutes passed. Felt like hours.

Then—all at once—she gasped. Her hand fell away from the frame. She stumbled back, would have fallen if Xion hadn't caught her.

"What—" She looked around wildly. "What happened? Did I—"

She saw their faces. Saw the concern. The fear.

"Again." Her voice was hollow. "I did it again."

"What did you see?" Farleen demanded.

Elara looked at the mirror. At the inner surface that had gone dark again. Then at the outer surface, which had begun to glow with soft blue light. Symbols appearing across it like writing in water.

"It was a trial." She sounded distant. Confused. "A test. From... from the gods, I think. The old gods. The Balishan pantheon."

"A test of what?"

"Worthiness." Elara's laugh was hollow. Empty. "To prove I deserve the throne. To prove I'm not just some scared girl playing at being a princess."

She looked at the glowing symbols on the outer mirror.

"And apparently..." Another hollow laugh. "Apparently I passed."

The light from the mirror seemed to pulse. Divine sanction. Proof of legitimacy by judgment of the gods themselves.

Elara stared at it for a long moment.

Then she reached out and yanked the mirror off its pedestal.

"Elara—"

She threw it.

The Spirit's Reflection hit the floor with a ringing clang. Skidded across the smooth brass surface.

The glow didn't fade.

Elara stood there breathing hard. Staring at the artifact she'd just thrown. At the proof of divine approval lying on the floor like discarded garbage.

Then she walked over. Picked it up. Reluctantly. Like it was something dead.

"Might as well keep it," she muttered. "Proof for the doubters. Evidence I'm not a fraud."

She looked at Tam. Still unconscious. Still burning with fever.

"Lot of good it does him."

The mirror gleamed in her hands. Mocking.

Xion wanted to say something. That it mattered. That divine sanction meant something. That she'd earned it.

But looking at Tam's still form, he wasn't sure he believed it either.

From the shadows near the passage entrance, a voice spoke.

"Quite the performance."

Xion spun. His heart jumped into his throat.

A figure stepped into the blue light. Dressed in dark clothes. Long Knives colors. Face obscured by a scarf.

They'd been watched. The whole time.

"We've been keeping an eye on you three." The assassin's voice was casual. Professional. "Our Mistress wanted to know what you were. What you'd do."

Farleen had moved between the assassin and Tam. Her stance ready. Dangerous.

"So you followed us." Her voice was flat.

"Followed. Observed." The assassin shrugged. "You shouldn't have made it through Dead Man's Passage. Not without a map. But here you are."

A pause.

"The Mistress wants to meet you. Now."

"And if we refuse?" Elara had straightened. The Spirit's Reflection still clutched in her hands.

The assassin gestured at the passage entrance. At the maze of Dead Man's Passage beyond.

"Then you're welcome to find your own way back. Though I should mention—even we get lost sometimes. Even with maps."

They looked at Tam. Unconscious. Burning with fever. Needing treatment they couldn't provide here.

They looked at each other.

"You're serious," Farleen said. "We don't have a choice."

"Everyone has choices." The assassin's voice held something that might have been amusement. "Some are just worse than others."

Xion knelt beside Tam. Checked his pulse again. Still too fast. Still weak. But steady.

He needed proper treatment. Medicine they didn't have. A place to rest that wasn't this ancient chamber.

He needed things only the Long Knives could provide.

"We'll come," Xion said quietly.

"Xion—" Farleen started.

"We don't have a choice." He looked up at her. "You said it yourself. We can't navigate back. And Tam needs help."

Farleen's jaw worked. Then she nodded. Once. Sharp.

Xion lifted Tam carefully. The boy's head lolled against his shoulder. Still unconscious. Still so light. Too light.

The assassin watched without comment.

"Follow me," they said finally. "And try to keep up. The Mistress doesn't like to be kept waiting."

They turned and walked into the darkness of the passage.

After a moment, Xion followed. Tam's weight pressing down on his shoulder. Farleen beside him. Elara behind, the Spirit's Reflection tucked under her arm like an afterthought.

Walking deeper into Long Knives territory. Into whatever the Mistress had planned.
