Arlo's platform afforded him a view of the entire Crossroads, and he used it well. His eyes tracked the flow of people below with the practiced ease of someone who made his living reading human behavior.

"So," he said, settling back on his cushioned seat. "Master Fen seeks a lost boy. Tam, you said? Twelve years old, taken by Slavers a few weeks ago." He tapped his fingers together thoughtfully. "That's very specific information. The kind that requires... effort to acquire."

"Can you find him?" Xion asked.

"Oh, I already know where he is." Arlo's smile was pleasant. "The Slavers sold him to a scavenger crew working the Deep Warrens. Tunnel 7, specifically—they're clearing collapsed sections for the cartel. Dangerous work. Most don't last more than a few months."

Xion's heart clenched. "Is he alive?"

"As of two days ago, yes. Whether he still is now?" Arlo shrugged. "That's harder to say. But I can tell you exactly where to find the crew, their routes, their schedules. Everything you'd need to extract one small boy from a group of very dangerous men."

"What's your price?" Farleen asked, her voice tight.

"Ah, now we come to it." Arlo leaned forward, his clever eyes moving between them. "Information like this has value. Considerable value. So the question becomes: what do you have to trade?"

"We have coin—" Xion started.

"I don't take coin." Arlo waved dismissively. "Coin is everywhere. Information is power. So tell me, what information do you possess that would be worth what you're asking?"

The three of them exchanged glances. Xion could see the calculation in Farleen's eyes—she probably knew things, intelligence gathered from her time with the cartels. But anything she shared would compromise operations, betray confidences, possibly endanger lives.

And Elara's secret was absolutely not for sale.

"We don't have information to trade," Xion said quietly.

"Pity." Arlo didn't look surprised. "Then we move to the alternative: favors. You do something for me, I give you what you want. A fair trade."

"What kind of favor?" Elara asked, suspicion clear in her voice.

Arlo stood, moving to the edge of his platform. "There's a man who operates out of the eastern quarter. Calls himself Vex. He's... a competitor, let's say. Recently came into possession of a ledger—names, debts, favors owed. Very valuable information. Information I would very much like to have."

"You want us to steal it," Farleen said flatly.

"I want you to acquire it." Arlo's smile widened. "Vex won't deal with me directly. And if I send my own people, well... that escalates things in ways that aren't productive for business. But three outsiders? Unknown quantities? If you were to obtain this ledger, Vex would have no idea who to blame."

"And if we get caught?" Xion asked.

"...Don't?" Arlo smiled, but it never reached his eyes. Seeing that this answer did not satisfy, he sighed. "I rather doubt you'll get caught. Your friend here—" He nodded at Elara. "—has already demonstrated considerable combat capability. And you—" His gaze shifted to Farleen. "—move like someone with training. Professional training."

Farleen's expression didn't change, but Xion saw her jaw tighten slightly.

"The job is simple," Arlo continued. "Vex operates from a building in the eastern quarter. Third floor, back room. He keeps the ledger in a locked box—iron, nothing fancy. The building has guards, but they're just hired muscle. Dermir's, most of them — Vex rents by the month, same as half the eastern quarter. Competent. Not exceptional." He pulled a folded paper from his robes. "Here's the layout, guard rotations, everything you need."

Farleen's eyes came up at Dermir's, and stayed on Arlo a moment, and Arlo smiled at her pleasantly and did not elaborate.

"How do you have all this if you can't get the ledger yourself?" Elara asked.

"Because information is what I do, dear girl. I know everything about everyone in the Warrens. But knowing and acting are different things. Sometimes you need... contractors."

Xion looked at his companions. Farleen was studying the paper Arlo had offered, her tactical mind already working through the problem. Elara looked tense but ready.

"And if we do this," Xion said slowly, "you'll tell us everything about where to find Tam? How to reach him?"

"Everything," Arlo confirmed. "Routes, schedules, guard patterns, weak points in their security. I'll give you the best chance you're going to get."

Farleen looked up from the paper, her amber eyes meeting Xion's. "We need to discuss this."

"Of course." Arlo gestured magnanimously. "Take your time. I'll be here."

They descended from the platform and moved to a quieter corner of the Crossroads.

Farleen spoke before anyone else could. "We don't have to do this."

"We need the information—" Xion started.

"No." Her voice was quiet but firm. "We don't need it. We could walk away right now. Stay in the Tiers. Wait for things to calm down on the surface. This—" She gestured back toward Arlo. "—is a choice. The same choice I told you was insane yesterday."

"Tam is running out of time," Xion said.

"Tam is a child you barely know." Farleen's control was impressive—her voice stayed level. "I know you promised his parents. I know you think you have to do this. But breaking into an information broker's competitor? Stealing from someone who definitely has security measures in place? That's not just dangerous, Xion. That's actively choosing to put ourselves at risk for—" She stopped herself, visibly regaining composure.

"For what?" Elara asked.

"For someone who isn't us," Farleen said simply. "Someone who isn't our responsibility."

"He's a child," Xion said quietly.

"So are a thousand other children in this city. You can't save them all." Farleen's voice softened slightly. "I know that's not what you want to hear. But it's true."

"I can save this one."

Farleen stared at him for a long moment. Then she looked down at the building layout in her hands, and something shifted in her expression—resignation, maybe, or just exhausted acceptance.

"You're really going to do this."

"Yes."

"Even knowing it's dangerous. Even knowing we don't have to."

"Yes."

She was quiet for a moment, then nodded slowly. "Fine. Then we do it right." She spread the paper on a nearby crate, her voice taking on a different quality—more focused, more professional. "The layout is straightforward. Two guards at the entrance, one roaming the second floor, likely one more near Vex's office on the third. If we time it right, we can get in and out without confrontation."

Xion felt something unknot in his chest. She was helping. Despite everything, despite her objections, despite thinking he was making a terrible choice—she was helping.

"And if we can't avoid confrontation?" Elara asked.

Farleen glanced at her. "Then we have someone who can handle it." Not quite a compliment, but an acknowledgment. "But the goal is stealth. We go in quiet, get what we need, leave before anyone knows we were there."

"This is insane," Elara said, but there was less conviction in her voice now.

"Yes," Farleen agreed. "But apparently we're doing it anyway." She looked at Xion, and there was something complicated in her expression. "Just... try not to get killed. That would make all of this rather pointless."

Farleen was already studying the building plans more carefully. "We go tonight. Late, when the streets are quieter. Xion, you'll need to stay outside—you're not trained for this, and if something goes wrong, we need someone who can get help or create a distraction."

"I'm not letting you two go alone—"

"You'll be more useful outside," Farleen said firmly. "Trust me on this."

---

They spent the afternoon preparing. Farleen acquired darker clothing for herself and Elara, studied the guard rotation times, planned three different exit routes. Xion watched her work and saw a different person emerge—focused, efficient, speaking in tactical shorthand that suggested years of training.

As evening fell, they made their way to the eastern quarter. The building Arlo had indicated was modest—three stories of weathered stone, windows dark except for a few showing lamplight. Two guards stood at the entrance, looking bored but alert.

"There," Farleen pointed to a side alley. "Service entrance. Should be less watched."

"Should be?" Elara asked.

"Nothing's certain. But the odds are better." Farleen checked the street. "Xion, stay here. If we're not back in an hour, assume something went wrong."

"And then what?"

"Then you leave. Go back to Arlo, tell him we failed. Don't try to be a hero." Farleen's amber eyes were serious. "Promise me."

"I promise," Xion said, though he wasn't sure he meant it.

Farleen nodded, then turned to Elara. "Follow my lead. Stay quiet, stay close. If I signal you to stop, you freeze. Understood?"

"Understood."

They moved into the alley, and Xion lost sight of them in the shadows.

The next forty minutes were the longest of his life. He stayed in the doorway Farleen had indicated, watching the street, watching the building, watching for any sign of trouble.

At thirty-five minutes, he heard shouting from inside the building.

His heart stopped.

Then glass shattered on the second floor. A man's voice, yelling. More shouts, the sound of furniture breaking.

Xion started toward the building, but stopped himself. Don't try to be a hero, Farleen had said.

A figure dropped from the second-floor window—Elara, landing with practiced ease. She turned back, reaching up, and Farleen dropped next, less gracefully. She stumbled when she landed, and Xion saw her clutch her side.

Two men appeared at the window, shouting. One started to climb out.

"Run!" Elara grabbed Farleen's arm, pulling her into the alley where Xion waited.

They ran. Behind them, the guards gave chase, but Farleen led them through a maze of back streets with sure instinct, taking turns that seemed random but clearly had purpose. After five minutes of hard running, she pulled them into a recessed doorway and signaled silence.

They waited, breathing hard, as heavy footsteps pounded past their hiding spot. The guards kept going, their curses fading into the distance.

"Did you get it?" Xion whispered.

Farleen pulled a small leather-bound book from inside her shirt. Even in the dim light, Xion could see she was holding her ribs with her other hand, her breathing shallow.

"You're hurt," he said.

"I'm fine."

"You're not fine, you're favoring your left side."

"It's just bruising." But her voice was tight with pain.

"Let me see."

"Not here. We need to get back to Arlo first."

They made their way back to the Crossroads cautiously, taking a longer route to avoid any potential pursuit. By the time they reached Arlo's platform, Farleen was moving stiffly, each breath careful and controlled.

Arlo looked up as they approached, his expression pleased. "I see you were successful."

Farleen handed him the ledger. "As promised."

He flipped through it quickly, his smile widening. "Excellent. This is exactly what I needed." He pulled out another paper—this one more detailed, covered in notes and diagrams. "Your payment. Everything you need to know about Tunnel 7, the scavenger crew, their routines, and the best approach for extraction."

Xion took the paper, scanning it quickly. The information was incredibly detailed—guard rotations, shift changes, even notes on individual crew members' temperaments and weaknesses.

"Thank you," he said.

"Thank you." Arlo tucked the ledger away carefully. "Pleasure doing business with you, Master Fen. Do come back if you need anything else."

As they left the Crossroads, Farleen's movements were becoming more labored. By the time they reached Korvin's building, she was leaning heavily on Elara's arm.

Korvin took one look at them and stepped aside. "Upstairs. Quietly."

Once inside, Xion turned to Farleen. "Sit down. Let me look at that."

"It's nothing—"

"Sit. Down."

Maybe it was the tone, or maybe she was hurting too much to argue, but Farleen sat on one of the low stools. Xion knelt beside her, opening his medical bag.

"What happened?" he asked.

"Guard caught us leaving Vex's office," Farleen said, her voice tight. "I thought we'd avoided them all, but one came back early from his rounds." She winced as Xion gently probed her ribs through her shirt. "He was bigger than expected. Slammed me into a wall."

"I killed him," Elara said quietly from near the window. Her voice held something complicated—not regret, exactly, but not satisfaction either.

Xion's hands stilled on the bandage.

"He was going to raise the alarm. We couldn't let him." Elara's tone was matter-of-fact, but her eyes were hard. "It was necessary."

"How old was he?"

The question landed oddly in the small room. Elara blinked.

"I don't—" She stopped. "Thirty. Maybe less."

"Will anyone come looking for him?"

"Xion." Farleen's voice, quiet, warning.

He looked down at his hands, still resting against Farleen's ribs, and made himself go on working. That morning in the Crossroads he had told Elara that nobody got to weigh the worth of a life. Now a man he had never met was dead in a stairwell in the eastern quarter, and the only questions he could find were how old, and who would miss him.

"Never mind," he said. "I need to see the bruising. Can you lift your shirt?"

She hesitated, then carefully pulled up the dark fabric, revealing her left side. The bruising was already spectacular—deep purple and black spreading across her ribs and up toward her shoulder. Xion examined it carefully, pressing gently to check for breaks.

"Nothing's broken," he said after a moment. "But you're going to be in pain for a week at least. Maybe two."

"I've had worse."

"I know." The words came out before he could stop them.

Farleen's eyes found his. "What?"

Xion pulled bandages from his bag, beginning to wrap her ribs with practiced efficiency. The position required him to be close, his hands moving around her torso, and he was acutely aware of the intimacy of it. The lamplight caught the curve of her shoulder, the line of her jaw. This close, he could see the tension in her face, the way she was holding her breath against the pain.

"Not the first time I've had to wrap your ribs," he said quietly.

Farleen's expression shifted—surprise, then something softer, almost vulnerable. "No. No, it's not."

A small smile touched her lips, and for a moment she looked younger, less guarded. "Remember? I told you I fell down the stairs in the grain warehouse."

"I remember." Xion continued wrapping, his movements gentle but firm. The familiarity of the action—the careful placement of each layer, the exact tension needed—brought back that day with sudden clarity. How worried he'd been. How she'd tried to hide the extent of the injury. "You were limping for a week."

"Two weeks, actually. But I tried to hide it." She winced as he pulled the bandage snug, but didn't pull away. "You were very concerned. Kept checking on me, making sure I was healing properly."

"You were obviously in pain."

"And you were very sweet about it." There was genuine warmth in her voice now, the walls she'd been maintaining since the argument last night coming down. Her eyes were soft in the lamplight, watching his face as he worked. "You always are. Sweet, I mean. Even when you probably shouldn't be."

From across the room, Xion heard a small sound from Elara—not quite a scoff, but close.

He tied off the bandage, but didn't immediately move away. His hands rested on her ribs, feeling the rise and fall of her breathing. This close, he could see the exact amber shade of her eyes, the small scar near her temple he'd never asked about.

This was familiar. Not just the medical care, but the intimacy of it. How many times had he patched her up over the years? Small injuries, nothing serious. Always with a plausible excuse. Always with that same look in her eyes—gratitude mixed with something else he'd never quite been able to name.

The question had been sitting in his mind for months, maybe longer. Now seemed as good a time as any to ask it.

"Did you really fall, that time?"

Farleen's smile faltered. The warmth in her eyes flickered, replaced by something guarded. Careful.

The silence stretched between them.

Xion sat back on his heels, the answer clear in that silence. "I thought so."

"Xion—"

"What was it really? A fight? An assignment?" He kept his voice quiet, but he couldn't quite keep the hurt out of it. "Did you ever tell me the truth about anything?"

Farleen looked away, her jaw tight. When she spoke, her voice was barely above a whisper. "I told you the truth about things that mattered."

"Did you?" Xion started putting his supplies back in his bag, needing something to do with his hands. "Because from where I'm sitting, I'm not sure what was real and what was just... part of the role you were playing."

"It wasn't all a role."

"Wasn't it?" He looked up at her. "The friendship, the conversations, the times you'd stop by the clinic just to talk—was any of that real? Or was it all just maintaining your cover?"

"Does it matter?" Farleen's voice was sharp now, defensive. The vulnerability from moments ago was gone, replaced by the familiar armor. "It's done. I can't change what I was, what I did. And you know why I was there."

"I know why you were placed there initially. I don't know why you stayed. Or what was true and what was lies."

Farleen met his eyes, and for a moment he saw something raw there—guilt, maybe, or regret. "Some of it was real," she said quietly. "Not all of it. But some."

Xion stood, shouldering his medical bag. The moment—whatever it had been—was broken now, shattered by the weight of everything between them.

"You should rest," he said, his tone professional now, distant. "The bruising will hurt more tomorrow. Try not to move too much."

"Xion—"

"We have the information we need to find Tam. That's what matters." He moved toward the window, needing space, needing distance from the complicated tangle of emotions Farleen always seemed to create.

Elara was watching this exchange with an unreadable expression. When Xion approached, she spoke quietly.

"Are you alright?"

"Fine," he said, though it was clearly a lie.

"That looked... painful."

"The injury isn't that serious—"

"I meant the conversation." Elara paused. "She looked like you'd stabbed her."

Xion didn't know what to say to that. He stood beside Elara, both of them looking out at the Warrens below, neither speaking.

Behind them, Farleen lay down carefully on one of the sleeping mats, her back to them, her breathing shallow and controlled.
