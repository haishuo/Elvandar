# Chapter 16: Quid Pro Quo

**Timeline:** Day 16, continuous with ch15. Midday through night.
**Characters:** Xion (POV), Elara, Farleen, Arlo Berakailen, a guard who does not survive
**Emotional work:** They buy Tam's location and the reader should feel every part of the price. A man dies offstage for it, Farleen is injured for it, and Xion — who twelve hours earlier delivered a beautiful speech about nobody having the right to weigh a life — finds he can only ask how old the man was. The chapter ends by opening a door on Farleen's history that neither of them shuts.
**POV:** Xion — close third-person limited. He is not present for the heist and the chapter must not go without him.

---

## Beats

1. Arlo's platform overlooks the whole Crossroads and he uses it, tracking the flow of people below with the ease of a man who makes his living reading behaviour. He restates the problem back to them precisely — Master Fen seeks a lost boy, twelve, taken by Slavers a few weeks ago — and notes that is very specific information, the kind that requires effort to acquire.

2. He already knows where Tam is, and says so without preamble, which is more frightening than withholding it. Sold to a scavenger crew working the Deep Warrens, Tunnel 7, clearing collapsed sections for the cartel. Dangerous work; most do not last more than a few months.

3. Xion asks if he is alive. As of two days ago, yes. Whether he still is now is harder to say. Arlo can supply the crew's location, routes and schedules — everything needed to extract one small boy from a group of very dangerous men.

4. The price. He does not take coin, because coin is everywhere and information is power. He asks what they have to trade, and the narration does the arithmetic in one beat: Farleen probably knows things from her cartel years, but anything she gives compromises operations and endangers people — and Elara's secret is absolutely not for sale. Xion says they have nothing to trade.

5. The alternative: a favour. There is a competitor called Vex in the eastern quarter who has recently come into a ledger of names, debts and favours owed, and Arlo would very much like to have it. Farleen names it flatly as theft; Arlo prefers acquire.

6. His reasoning is sound and that is what makes him dangerous. Vex will not deal with him directly, and sending his own people escalates things unproductively — but three outsiders, unknown quantities, could take it and Vex would have no idea who to blame.

7. Asked what happens if they are caught, he says *don't*, smiles without it reaching his eyes, and then — seeing that this does not satisfy — sighs and gives the real answer: he doubts they will be, because Elara has already demonstrated considerable combat capability and Farleen moves like someone with professional training. Farleen's expression does not change and her jaw tightens.

8. The brief is precise: third floor, back room, an iron box, nothing fancy. Guards are hired muscle — competent, not exceptional. Dermir's, most of them, because Vex rents by the month like half the eastern quarter. He produces a folded paper with layout and rotations.

9. Farleen's eyes come up at *Dermir's* and stay on Arlo a moment, and Arlo smiles at her pleasantly and does not elaborate. Nothing else in the chapter touches this. It is the first thread of the summons that arrives in ch17 and of Ronas knowing exactly who they are in ch18.

10. Elara asks how he has all this if he cannot get the ledger himself. Because information is what he does; he knows everything about everyone in the Warrens, but knowing and acting are different things, and sometimes you need contractors.

11. Xion confirms the terms — everything about reaching Tam, routes, schedules, guard patterns, weak points — and Arlo confirms he will give them the best chance they are going to get. Farleen says they need to discuss it. Arlo is magnanimous and will be here.

12. Farleen's objection, for the third time in two chapters, and it is the same objection and still correct. They do not have to do this. They could walk away, stay in the Tiers, wait for the surface to calm. This is a choice — the same choice she called insane yesterday. Xion's answer is that Tam is running out of time.

13. And then she does the thing that defines her: having lost the argument, she plans the job properly. Her tactical expertise comes out fully for the first time — approach, timing, contingencies, what to do if it goes wrong. She is better at this than anyone else in the room and she is doing it for a cause she has argued against twice.

---

14. The heist happens without Xion and the chapter stays with him. He is not trained for infiltration and everyone including him knows it. What the reader gets is the wait and then the return.

15. It goes wrong. A guard comes back early from his rounds — bigger than expected — and slams Farleen into a wall. They get the ledger and get out.

16. The handover at Arlo's platform. Farleen gives him the ledger; he flips through it, smile widening, and produces the payment — a detailed sheet of notes and diagrams covering Tunnel 7, the crew, their routines, the best approach for extraction, guard rotations, shift changes, even notes on individual crew members' temperaments and weaknesses. Arlo is scrupulously honest and that is the point of him. Pleasure doing business, Master Fen, do come back.

17. Leaving the Crossroads, Farleen's movements become more laboured, and by Korvin's building she is leaning heavily on Elara's arm. Korvin takes one look and steps aside — upstairs, quietly. He asks nothing, which is now the third time somebody in the Warrens has declined to ask.

18. Xion tells her to sit and let him look. She says it is nothing. He says *sit down* in the tone that ends it, and she sits, either because of the tone or because she is hurting too much to argue.

19. She reports the injury flatly while he probes her ribs. And then, from near the window, Elara says she killed the guard. Her voice holds something complicated — not regret, not satisfaction. He was going to raise the alarm and they could not let him. It was necessary.

20. Xion's hands still on the bandage, and what comes out of him is two questions. How old was he. Elara blinks, stops, says thirty, maybe less. Will anyone come looking for him. Farleen says his name quietly, warning.

21. The paragraph the chapter exists for. He looks at his hands, still resting against Farleen's ribs, and makes himself go on working — because that morning at the Crossroads he told Elara that nobody gets to weigh the worth of a life, and now a man he never met is dead in a stairwell in the eastern quarter, and the only questions he can find are how old, and who will miss him. He says never mind and asks to see the bruising.

22. The examination. Deep purple and black across her left ribs and up toward the shoulder. Nothing broken, but a week of pain at least, maybe two. She says she has had worse. He says *I know* before he can stop himself, and her eyes come up, and she asks what.

23. The wrapping, and the intimacy is produced entirely by the procedure — he has to be close, his hands moving around her torso, the lamplight on her shoulder and jaw, the tension in her face as she holds her breath against the pain. He says it is not the first time he has wrapped her ribs.

24. Her expression shifts — surprise, then something softer and almost vulnerable — and she supplies the memory herself: she told him she fell down the stairs in the grain warehouse. He remembers. She was limping for a week; she corrects it to two, and admits she tried to hide it. He remembers being very concerned and checking on her.

25. Her guard comes down entirely for the first time since the betrayal. He was very sweet about it. He always is, even when he probably should not be. From across the room, Elara makes a small sound that is not quite a scoff.

26. He ties off the bandage and does not move away, his hands resting on her ribs, feeling her breathe. This close he can see the exact amber of her eyes and a small scar near her temple he has never asked about. And the recognition: how many times has he patched her up over the years, small injuries, nothing serious, always with a plausible excuse — always with that same look, gratitude mixed with something he could never name.

27. The question that has been sitting in him for months: did she really fall, that time? Her smile falters, the warmth flickers out, and what replaces it is guarded and careful. The silence stretches. End on it.

---

**On what the chapter costs, and why the accounting must be visible:** they buy Tam's location, and the invoice is a stolen ledger, a man dead in a stairwell, and Farleen's ribs. None of it is treated as a heist's cheerful collateral. The chapter's structural claim is that the rescue is now possible and every step toward it has been paid for by somebody who did not consent — which is exactly the arithmetic Xion refuses to perform on his own behalf.

**On the two questions, which are the chapter's spine:** twelve hours after telling Elara that nobody has the right to weigh a life, Xion is confronted with a dead man and finds he has no framework left, only *how old* and *who will miss him*. **He does not accuse her and he does not forgive her.** He goes back to work with his hands on a different woman's ribs. The narration states the contradiction plainly and neither character addresses it, then or later. This is the third time the book has shown him unable to act on his own principle and the first time the principle has cost somebody else their life.

**On Farleen planning the job she argued against:** this is the most complete piece of characterisation she gets in Book 1. She tells him twice that this is insane, loses, and then executes professionally — and gets hurt doing it. **Her competence is never in question; only her willingness to be listened to.** Anyone revising the heist should keep the planning hers and keep Xion out of the building.

**On the ribs scene, and why it is not a romance beat:** it is a diagnosis. He wraps her ribs, recognises the procedure, recognises that he has done it before, and then recognises the *pattern* — repeated injuries, plausible excuses, and a look he could never name. What the scene actually reveals is that Xion has been treating an abused woman for years without letting himself assemble it, which is the same faculty that walked past the notice board ten thousand times. **The question at the end is a healer's question, not a lover's**, and the chapter must end on her silence rather than on her answer.

**On the *Dermir's* beat, which is four words and load-bearing:** Arlo mentions in passing that Vex's hired muscle belongs to Ronas, and Farleen catches it and Arlo declines to elaborate. That is why the summons arrives in ch17 and why Ronas already knows everything in ch18 — they robbed a house Ronas had men standing in, and he says so directly. **Plant it exactly this lightly.** If the reader works it out here, ch17's ending stops being a surprise; if it is not planted at all, ch18's accounting arrives from nowhere.

**On rhythm:** 3,118 words, the last of four consecutive chapters over 3,100, and then ch17 drops to 1,670 — the sharpest contraction in the book. The descent has been running long and heavy since ch13 and it is about to snap short for the failed rescue. That contrast is doing real work and neither chapter should be moved toward the other.
